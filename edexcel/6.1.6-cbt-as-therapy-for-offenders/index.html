6.1.6 CBT AS A THERAPY FOR OFFENDERS
用认知行为疗法治疗犯罪者

Topic F · Criminological psychology Treatment Spec 6.1.6
Two exam questions only: how CBT is used with offenders, and how effective it is.

What this section is really asking

  1. Punishment stops at consequences. It does not teach a different way to act.
  2. CBT says: change the thought, and the behaviour follows. Offenders are taught to spot distorted thinking and restructure it.
  3. The exam names four programmes — R&R, MRT, anger management, and social skills / problem solving / assertiveness.
  4. Effectiveness is measured by recidivism (reoffending), and almost every study is a meta-analysis — learn the names and the numbers.

1. Getting Started

Someone has been convicted of a crime. Now the court has to decide what happens to them. In most cases the answer is one of two things:

Custodial sentence（监禁刑）

Sent to prison. Their activities are restricted and they lose access to the community.

Non-custodial sentence（非监禁刑）

A fine, or community service. They stay in the community but pay or work back.

中文理解：这一章要解决的矛盾
坐牢 = 让犯罪者“付出代价”。但出狱以后呢？如果一个人偷东西是因为“不会用别的方式解决问题”， 那关他几年并没有教会他别的方式。这一章整章都在回答一个问题：除了惩罚，还能做什么？

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

For each crime, which sentence do you think is more likely? There are no wrong answers here — just notice why you chose each one.

Murder

Shoplifting（first offence, low value）

Driving without a licence

You probably sorted these by how serious they felt. Courts do roughly the same — but seriousness is not the only thing they weigh. They also ask: is this person likely to do it again? That second question is where treatment comes in.

2. Why Punishment Is Not Enough

Punishment is the most common response to crime in the criminal justice system, and it does one job well.

What punishment does do

It teaches offenders that there are consequences to their offending behaviour. It aims to act as a deterrent by restricting their activities and — if they are sent to prison — removing access to the community.

What punishment does not do

It does not directly teach offenders how to act in a different way.

Think about why that gap matters. The textbook gives two reasons why an offence happens in the first place:

Reason 1 · a missing skill

The offender is lacking in skills to deal with different situations. Punishment never fills that gap — the person leaves prison as unskilled as they went in.

Reason 2 · a learned payoff

The offender has learned to associate offending with certain positive outcomes — money, status, excitement. Punishment adds a cost but never removes the reward.

中文理解：把惩罚想成“罚款单”
你因为乱停车被罚 200 块。罚款让你知道“这事有代价”——但你下次还是不知道该把车停哪里。 惩罚告诉你“别做”，但没告诉你“该做什么”。这就是 treatment（治疗）存在的理由：它教的是替代行为。

So: punishment cannot be the only solution to reduce reoffending.

3. What Treatment Means

The aim of treatment

To identify problem behaviours and the root causes of those behaviours, and to teach offenders non-offending ways to deal with their problems.

QuestionAnswer in the specification
Who delivers it?Mainly forensic psychologists or staff trained in the skills required. If the staff are not psychologists, they are usually supervised by psychologists.
Where?In the community or in prisons.
Does the setting change it?No. The principles for each treatment are the same, irrespective of where it is delivered.
Does everyone get the same amount?No. The extent of treatment differs depending on the severity of the offence and the risk of future reoffending.

The one treatment you need to know

The majority of treatment for offenders focuses on psychological techniques and behavioural treatments. The one named in the specification is cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT).

4. What CBT Is

Cognitive behavioural therapy is a treatment that helps the offender develop insight into their thoughts and feelings and how these influence their behaviour. That sentence is worth unpacking slowly, because the whole chapter sits on top of it.

CBT works on a premise — a starting assumption. For every situation we experience:

The CBT chain. The situation is fixed; the thought is the part a therapist can reach.
中文理解：整章最重要的一张图
同一件事（有人撞到你、把你的咖啡撞洒了）——
想法 A：“她是故意的” → 生气 → 骂她。
想法 B：“她可能没看见我” → 平静 → 算了。
事情没变，想法变了，行为就变了。CBT 做的就是第二步。考试只要问“CBT 的原理”，就写这一句。

What the therapy is actually trying to produce

It is possible to change an offender's reactions to the same experience by developing their awareness of their own thoughts, and changing their thoughts so that their behaviour in the same situation is more prosocial. It also encourages the offender to consider alternative ways they could act in situations that are not criminal.

prosocial behaviour（亲社会行为）： behaviour that takes account of the welfare of others and avoids harming others deliberately.

CBT can be delivered one-to-one with a therapist, or in a group setting with other offenders and a therapist guiding the discussions.

5. One Example, All the Way Through

The textbook gives one worked example, and it is the clearest way to see the whole chain in action. An offender on an anger management course keeps an anger diary. This is one entry.

ColumnWhat was writtenWhich link in the chain
DateTues 1 July—
What happened when I started to feel angry (trigger)Someone bumped into me and I spilt my coffeeSituation
What physiological symptoms did I have?Heart racing, fast breathingFeelings
On a scale of 1–10, how angry did I feel?5 / 10Feelings
What did I think about the situation?“How dare she?” “She did that on purpose.”Thoughts
How did I deal with the situation?I shouted at her and told her to get me another drinkBehaviour

Now look at what the therapist has to work with. The situation is over — nobody can change it. But the thought is written down in black and white, and that is something they can examine.

🎯 Restructure the thought Think first

The offender wrote: “She did that on purpose.” Which of these would a CBT therapist be trying to build towards?

Why the diary keeps growing

The diary is completed every time they feel angry. With support from the therapist, they start to recognise their triggers, and can then evaluate which techniques work for them in specific situations. Additional columns are added as they become more insightful — this is a scaffolding approach: it avoids giving the offender too many expectations at the start, when they still need to build understanding before they can use the skills.

中文理解：scaffolding（脚手架）
就像学骑车先装辅助轮。一开始只要求记“发生了什么 / 我想了什么”两栏； 等他看得懂自己的模式了，才加“我当时身体有什么反应”“我用了什么技巧”。 不是一次给完，是慢慢加。

6. Criminal Thinking (Beck, 1999)

CBT has a sound theoretical basis for treating offenders because the way a criminal thinks has been firmly linked to their offending behaviour — that link is Beck (1999).

Criminals have been shown to display distorted thinking. There are four patterns, and you should be able to name and recognise each one:

1 · Displacing the blame

Blaming the victim or another source external to themselves. “It was her fault for walking there.”

2 · Justifying the offending

Explaining the crime as fair or necessary. “The shop is insured — nobody really lost anything.”

3 · Misreading social cues

Interpreting neutral social cues as potential threats, then responding to a threat that was never there.

4 · Schemas of dominance

Deep beliefs about self-dominance and personal entitlement — “I take what I am owed.”

中文理解：四种“想歪了”的方式
① 甩锅给别人（怪受害者）· ② 给自己找理由（我没错）· ③ 把别人中性的一眼看错成威胁 · ④ 骨子里觉得“我说了算、我该拿”。
关键一句：这些被认为是学来的（learned） —— 既然是学来的，就能被改掉。这正是 CBT 敢接手的前提。
schema（图式 / 核心信念）： a deeply held belief about yourself and how the world works. Beck argued offenders hold schemas about their own dominance and entitlement.

7. What CBT Tries to Change

Because the distorted patterns above are assumed to be learned, CBT aims to do three things:

TargetWhat that means in practice
Identify and restructureHelp offenders identify and restructure these faulty patterns of thinking.
Victim empathyDevelop victim empathy — actually considering the impact of the behaviour on others.
Challenge self-justificationChallenge their tendency to self-justify their offending.
cognitive restructuring（认知重构）： the process of identifying and challenging maladaptive/negative thinking patterns and replacing them with more objective and adaptive ones.
Socratic questioning（苏格拉底式提问）： a thoughtful method of questioning used in CBT to identify values and beliefs. The therapist asks questions rather than giving answers, so the offender reaches the contradiction themselves.

What a CBT programme typically contains

CBT typically involves cognitive training skills, anger management and other components such as social skills training and relapse prevention.

  • Cognitive skills training teaches thinking skills in order to better problem solve. Role-play is often used for critical thinking and perspective taking, to practise new ways of coping with situations that would typically cause aggressive or criminal behaviour.
  • Anger management focuses on teaching offenders to monitor their thoughts where they typically react with anger — to identify anger triggers and reconsider their interpretation of those triggers as non-hostile explanations.

Delivery differs — that is the point

CBT treatments differ in their delivery: some focus on anger management, some on conflict resolution, others on moral reasoning and preventing relapse. When the exam says “one type of CBT programme”, it wants you to name one of these and describe it.

8. Reasoning and Rehabilitation (R&R)

The identity card

Developed in Canada by Robert Ross and Elizabeth Fabiano (1985). It is a branded CBT programme — a fixed package with a name.

What it teaches: critical thinking, social skills, assertiveness skills, and how to negotiate and get along with others.

Why those skills? They help to restructure the offender's rigid, illogical thinking and make them consider the consequences of their behaviour. Offenders learn to consider alternative ways of behaving and to think about the impact of their behaviour on others.

The main purpose, in one sentence

To equip offenders with the thinking skills to make prosocial behaviour choices that will allow them to navigate out of a criminal lifestyle.

FeatureDetail to learn
Sessions36 group sessions, each lasting two hours.
FrequencyBetween two and four times per week.
MethodsGroup discussions, Socratic questioning, role playing and thinking games.
Purpose of the methodsAll designed to challenge the offender's beliefs and develop better thinking, emotional regulation and problem-solving skills.
中文理解：R&R 在教什么
不是教“不要犯罪”，而是教思考的工具：遇到事先停一下、想后果、想别人的感受、想有没有别的做法。 36 次、每次 2 小时、每周 2–4 次 —— 这个数字容易考，记住 36。

9. Moral Reconation Therapy (MRT)

The identity card

Established by Greg Little and Ken Robinson (1986). Where R&R teaches thinking skills, MRT targets morality.

What it is: a series of group and workbook exercises to target reducing criminal thinking.

The assumption behind it: criminals have an underdeveloped morality, so the treatment is designed to teach moral reasoning and judgement. Offenders are taught about social rules and the importance of considering others.

The feature that makes MRT distinctive

The programme is a staged workbook. Each offender must complete a series of homework assignments in order to pass to the next stage.

FeatureDetail to learn
FormatGroup sessions plus a structured workbook.
ProgressionStaged — homework must be completed to move to the next stage.
Content of the exercisesEncourages moral thinking: awareness of beliefs around criminal activity, and developing tolerance to frustration.
AssumptionCriminals have underdeveloped morality; the fix is to teach moral reasoning and judgement.
中文理解：MRT 和 R&R 的区别（考试最爱考这个对比）
R&R = 教思考技巧（会不会想）。
MRT = 教道德水平（该不该做），而且像游戏通关：做完作业才能进下一关。
两个都要写“创始人 + 年份”：Ross & Fabiano (1985) / Little & Robinson (1986)。

10. Anger Management

A common application of CBT for offenders, endorsed by Raymond Novaco since the 1970s.

How Novaco defines anger： a strong emotion that has an impact on a person's physiology, behaviour and cognition. Notice — anger hits all three, so the treatment has to hit all three too.
Part of angerWhat the treatment uses
PhysiologyRelaxation techniques to deal with the physiological response (for example, increased heart rate).
CognitionCognitive restructuring to retrain thought patterns.
Behaviour“Time out” or assertiveness training to deal with the behavioural element.
The three steps of anger management (Novaco).
中文理解：三步就是“先认、再学、后练”
① 认：什么时候我会炸？（写愤怒日记找 trigger）
② 学：学放松、学拒绝、学表达（给工具）
③ 练：在安全的环境里角色扮演练这些工具
还有一个反直觉的点：不要求他们“不生气”——生气是正常的，目标是“能控制”。

🎯 Which step? No notes

A. An offender keeps a diary to work out which situations set them off.

B. An offender is taught breathing and relaxation techniques.

C. An offender role-plays being stuck in a queue while staying calm.

Where and how long

Anger management can be used in prisons or with offenders serving a probationary period in the community. Courses are usually run in small groups and last for approximately ten sessions, although some can last for a number of months.

11. Social Skills, Problem Solving and Assertiveness

Not every CBT programme has a brand name. Typically CBT also incorporates social skills training, problem-solving skills and assertiveness training. As a package it is used to encourage offenders to consider their existing social skills and develop them further.

assertiveness（自信表达 / 坚定表达）： communicating in a confident, non-confrontational way to minimise conflict and increase the likelihood the offender will be responded to positively.

The pattern it replaces

An offender who usually interacts in an aggressive manner — communicating in a forceful, threatening or intimidating style.

What it builds instead

The same person considering how to get the same outcome in a more assertive way — and then practising and reflecting on how successfully they used the skills.

Why practice beats instruction here

This is a supported approach to developing skills: by giving greater guidance it is more effective than simply telling the offender what they should do. The offender learns from personal experience how beneficial it may be to be polite in a situation, which also helps them feel more confident using the skills — so they are more likely to keep using them in the future.

中文理解：这一部分的核心逻辑
直接说教（“你要有礼貌”）没用。让他在安全环境里真的试一次，发现“有礼貌居然真的管用”—— 这个亲身体会才会让他以后继续用。考试写“为什么有效”时，这一点非常加分。

13. How Do We Know It Works?

Before the studies, you need one term and one problem. Both come up in almost every evaluation answer.

recidivism（再犯率）： the rate of reoffending — the tendency of a convicted criminal to reoffend. This is the standard measure of whether a treatment worked.

The problem: it is very hard to run a proper experiment

Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) are designed to evaluate whether a therapy works: a sample is recruited and then randomly assigned to the treatment or control group. With offenders this is difficult, because they cannot be randomly allocated to treatment or no-treatment conditions. So most evaluations use non-randomised participants.

Why that matters for the conclusion

If random allocation is not used, participant variables may prevent cause-and-effect relationships being drawn. We cannot be sure the improvement is due to the treatment itself, or to other factors — for example that the participant was selected for treatment because they were likely to benefit most, that they were highly motivated, that they volunteered, or that they were more likely to complete the programme.

Consequence: it is unsurprising that studies which do not use randomised controlled trials often find that a treatment was effective.

中文理解：为什么“有效”这个结论要打折扣
理想的实验是随机分组。但现实中，能进治疗项目的往往是本来就更想改的人。 所以“治疗后更少再犯”可能不是治疗的功劳，而是“这批人本来就不太会再犯”。
评价题里这是最稳的一个扣分点，一定要写。
Two things CBT is not. CBT is not a form of counselling — it does not focus on overcoming emotional reactions to past difficulties. Its focus is mainly on present thinking. And it does not work immediately: it requires commitment from the offender, because as a talking therapy it requires them to talk about their thoughts and experiences.

14. Evidence · R&R

Tong and Farrington (2006)

A meta-analysis on the implementation of R&R in four countries: Canada, USA, UK and Sweden.

FindingDetail
The headlineR&R reduced reoffending by 15 per cent compared to the control groups.
SettingEffective in both prison and community settings.
WhoEffective with volunteers and non-volunteers, and in all countries studied.
Risk levelEffective with high and low risk offenders, but much more effective with low risk offenders.
Their own caveatThe researchers noted that the European research included in the meta-analysis did not employ randomised controlled trials.

What this means for your answer

Overall, the R&R programme seems to be an effective treatment for offenders — the finding holds across countries, settings and risk levels. But the caveat matters: because much of the European evidence is not from RCTs, we cannot rule out participant variables as the real cause.

中文理解：这条证据怎么用
写“支持”时用：15%、四国、监狱和社区都有效 —— 说明不是只在某一种条件下成立，推广性较好。
写“限制”时用：欧洲部分没有随机分配；而且对低风险者效果明显更好，高风险者不能指望同样效果。

15. Evidence · MRT

Ferguson and Wormith (2013)

A meta-analysis of 33 published studies comparing MRT with a control group, following up offenders over an average of three years.

FindingDetail
The headlineRecidivism was reduced by up to a third compared to those who received no treatment.
AgeOutcomes were better for adult offenders compared to juvenile offenders.
Setting mattersThe treatment seems to be only beneficial when delivered in a prison context.

Blonigen et al. (2023) — the boundary condition

They found no difference in improvement between MRT and usual care in a residential mental health setting. The same programme that works in prison produced nothing extra there.

中文理解：MRT 的“但是”
Ferguson & Wormith：再犯率最多降 1/3，成年人比青少年好。

16. Evidence · CBT Overall

Three more studies sit under “CBT in general” rather than one named programme. Learn all three — the specification expects evidence for the effectiveness of CBT, not just for one brand.

Wilson, Bouffard and McKenzie (2005)

Meta-analysis of 20 group-oriented CBT research studies. CBT reduced recidivism by up to 30 per cent more than control groups that did not receive CBT. Specifically, MRT and R&R were most effective.

Pearson et al. (2002)

Other meta-analyses have found CBT more effective than behavioural techniques in reducing reoffending.

The honest caveat from the same meta-analyses

Some of the studies within each meta-analysis showed a greater effect than others, so it is not yet certain what aspect of the therapy works on what type of offender. There may be moderator variables that decide whether the effect on reoffending is small or large.

Lipsey, Landenberger and Wilson (2007) — the answer to that problem

A review of 58 CBT studies. They scrutinised possible moderator variables — type of offender in the programme, methodology of the study, and nature of the intervention. Only one thing affected the likelihood of success: the amount and quality of the CBT programme.

This suggests that any CBT programme is beneficial — what matters is how much of it you deliver and how well you deliver it.

中文理解：58 项研究找出的唯一答案
大家一直在问“到底是哪一套 CBT 最管用”。Lipsey 他们查了犯人类型、研究方法、干预形式——全都不是关键。
唯一有影响的是：做了多少、做得好不好。
这一句是评价题里非常漂亮的“总结句”：效果差异主要来自实施质量，不是方案本身。

Three headline numbers keep coming up. Learn them as a set — an evaluation answer that compares them is far stronger than one that lists a single study.

The three headline reductions. Each comes from a meta-analysis, and each compares treated offenders with untreated ones.
中文理解：怎么读这张图

18. Evidence · Anger Management

First, the boundary: it only applies to some offenders

Anger management programmes are only effective for those offenders who have problems with anger control. Not all offences — even violent ones — are committed because the offender was unable to control their anger. Before attending treatment for anger, offenders are interviewed to assess suitability.

Howells et al. (2005) — the motivation finding

Anger management was effective only for those motivated to change their behaviour, who are committed to the programme.

Ireland (2004) — the improvement finding

Assessed 50 young male prisoners on an anger management course and 37 control prisoners. It was found that 92 per cent of the prisoners showed an improvement in their management of their anger, suggesting such programmes are effective.

But read that 92 per cent carefully

It is possible, however, that offenders may be dishonest on questionnaire assessment measuring anger, and may try to show that they have either improved their anger management skills or minimised the anger they experienced in the first place.

This remains a difficulty with any intervention that relies on self-report data, particularly from offenders — there may be a greater incentive to lie, including looking good for parole (if in prison) or trying to have their restrictions reduced by demonstrating they are no longer a risk to the public.

中文理解：92% 这个数字为什么不可靠
量表是自己填的。犯人有很强的动机说“我已经不生气了”——为了假释、为了减少限制。
所以高分答案会写：数据来自自我报告，且存在明显说谎动机，因此 92% 可能被高估。 这一句比单纯引用 92% 强得多。

19. Social Skills, Problem Solving and Young Offenders

Pearson et al. (2002) — the difficulty of isolating a technique

They reviewed the impact of social skills training on reoffending rates and found that CBT programmes in general had a positive effect on reducing reoffending. However, it is difficult to assess the effectiveness of specific techniques — social skills training, problem solving and assertiveness — because they are often used alongside other techniques. In a careful analysis of the techniques used within 58 CBT programmes, cognitive skills training and interpersonal problem solving were found to have a modest positive effect on reducing recidivism.

The Enhanced Thinking Skills (ETS) programme — mixed findings

ETS helps juvenile offenders learn critical reasoning skills, problem solving and self-control. It is a 20-session programme, focused specifically on the cognitive skills that younger people may not have developed, which may have contributed to the offending.

StudyFindingVerdict
Friendship et al. (2002)Initial findings suggested the programme lowered juvenile recidivism in low-, medium- and high-risk offenders.positive
Falshaw et al. (2003)A reanalysis of the same data did not show any significant effect in reducing reoffending.negative
Cann et al. (2003)Significant effects were shown only after one year; reoffending rates after two years were not improved.short-term only
中文理解：ETS 三连问怎么记
Friendship：有效。→ Falshaw：同一批数据重新分析，无效。→ Cann：一年有效，两年无效。
合起来的结论：认知训练对青少年可能只有短期效果。写评价时用这三条说明“结果不稳定”。

The criticism from outside the therapy room

CBT has been criticised for focusing on thinking skills and therefore failing to take account of social factors such as poverty and employment. These social factors are important predictors of reoffending and act as environmental stressors which are overlooked by the treatment.

stressor（压力源）： any stimuli, internal or external, which places demands on the individual that require a physical or psychological change in order to cope.

20. Evaluating the Evidence

The specification asks for effectiveness, so every evaluation answer needs both sides. Open each card and notice that the strengths are about evidence, while the limitations are about how that evidence was collected.

▶ Strengths — why the evidence is worth taking seriously
  • SIZE 效果量大：Wilson, Bouffard and McKenzie (2005) found CBT reduced recidivism by up to 30% more than untreated controls — a large practical difference, not a marginal one.
  • META 元分析支撑：Findings come from meta-analyses pooling many studies (20 studies; 33 studies; 58 programmes), so they are not dependent on one small sample.
  • GENERAL 跨情境成立：Tong and Farrington (2006) found R&R worked in prison and community, with volunteers and non-volunteers, across four countries — good external validity.
  • THEORY 有理论依据：Beck (1999) links criminal thinking directly to offending, so there is a clear reason to expect changing thinking to change behaviour.
  • MECHANISM 知道什么在起作用：Lipsey et al. (2007) isolated the amount and quality of delivery as the key factor, which is actionable.
▶ Limitations — what the evidence cannot tell us
  • RCT 缺随机分配：Offenders cannot be randomly allocated to treatment or no treatment, so most evaluations use non-randomised participants and participant variables may explain the improvement — motivated volunteers, or those selected because they were likely to benefit.
  • MEASURE 测量本身有问题：Success is measured by recidivism. If the therapy aims to change thinking, a better measure would be whether thinking changed. Official statistics only capture arrests and convictions, so unreported crime is missed and CBT may look more effective than it is.
  • SELF-REPORT 自我报告不可靠：Ireland (2004) relied on questionnaires, and offenders have a strong incentive to lie — parole, or reduced restrictions.
  • QUALITY 元分析内部差异大：There are significant issues with using meta-analyses because of variation between programmes in quality of delivery.
  • MIXED 结果不稳定：The ETS data were positive (Friendship 2002), then non-significant on reanalysis (Falshaw 2003), then short-term only (Cann 2003).
  • BOUNDED 有条件才成立：MRT works better for adults and appears beneficial only in a prison context (Blonigen 2023 found no advantage in a residential mental health setting).
  • GENDER 性别偏差：Most data concern male offenders, who are the largest group of convicted criminals. Programmes were designed with men in mind and may not reflect the needs of female offenders, so less is known about women.
  • REDUCTIONIST 忽视社会因素：CBT focuses on thinking and overlooks poverty and employment, which are important predictors of reoffending and act as environmental stressors.
中文理解：八分的评价题怎么凑四段
① 支持：Wilson 30% / Tong 15% 四国有效（有量、能推广）。
② 方法限制：无法随机分配，参与变量可能才是原因。
③ 测量限制：只看再犯率，漏报犯罪；自我报告有说谎动机。
④ 范围限制：男性样本、青少年结果不稳、忽视贫困与就业。
最后一句要给平衡结论：CBT 值得用，但效果好坏取决于实施质量与适用人群。

22. Exam Lab

Q1. Outline how cognitive behavioural therapy might be used as a treatment for offenders. (4 marks)

Needed: the premise, plus one named programme described in enough detail to be recognisable.

¶Job of that point
1The premise: situation → thoughts → feelings → behaviour; change the thought and behaviour changes.
2Beck (1999): offenders show distorted thinking — blame, justification, misread cues, dominance schemas.
3Name and describe one programme (R&R: 36 two-hour sessions, Socratic questioning, role play).
4What the offender actually does: diary, homework, practice; victim empathy and relapse prevention.

CBT works on the premise that for every situation we experience we have thoughts about it, emotional reactions to it, and those thoughts then influence our behaviour. It is therefore possible to change an offender's reaction to the same experience by developing awareness of their own thoughts and restructuring them, so that their behaviour in that situation is more prosocial.

This matters because Beck (1999) showed criminals display distorted thinking — displacing blame onto the victim, justifying offending, misinterpreting social cues as threats, and holding schemas about self-dominance and entitlement. CBT aims to help offenders identify and restructure these patterns, develop victim empathy, and challenge self-justification.

One programme is the Reasoning and Rehabilitation programme (Ross and Fabiano, 1985), typically 36 group sessions of two hours, two to four times a week. Sessions use group discussion, Socratic questioning, role play and thinking games to challenge beliefs and develop emotional regulation and problem solving, equipping offenders to make prosocial choices.

Offenders may also keep anger diaries, completing them each time they feel angry, so that with the therapist's support they recognise their triggers and evaluate which techniques work for them; additional columns are added over time in a scaffolding approach.

Q2. Evaluate the effectiveness of CBT as a therapy for offenders. (8 marks)

Plan four paragraphs first:

¶Job of that paragraph
1Supporting evidence: Wilson et al. (2005) up to 30%; Tong and Farrington (2006) 15%, four countries.
2Methodological limit: no random allocation, participant variables.
3Measurement limit: recidivism misses unreported crime; self-report incentive to lie.
4Scope limit: gender bias, mixed juvenile findings, ignores poverty and employment — balanced conclusion.

¶1. A strength is that there is strong supporting evidence from meta-analyses. Wilson, Bouffard and McKenzie (2005) combined 20 group-oriented CBT studies and found CBT reduced recidivism by up to 30 per cent more than untreated controls, with MRT and R&R the most effective. Tong and Farrington (2006) found R&R reduced reoffending by 15 per cent across four countries, in prison and community, with volunteers and non-volunteers. Because findings hold across settings and countries, the effect is not limited to one unusual sample.

¶2. However, the evidence is methodologically weak. Offenders cannot be randomly allocated to treatment or no treatment, so most evaluations use non-randomised participants. Without random allocation, participant variables may explain the improvement: those selected for treatment may be more motivated, may have volunteered, or may be more likely to complete the programme. This makes it unsurprising that non-randomised studies often report positive effects.

¶3. The way success is measured is also a problem. Effectiveness is typically measured by recidivism, yet if a therapy is designed to change thinking, a better measure would be whether thinking actually changed. Official recidivism statistics only capture arrests and convictions, so unreported crime is missed and CBT may appear more effective than it really is. Where self-report is used, such as Ireland (2004) where 92 per cent improved, offenders may be dishonest because they have an incentive to lie — looking good for parole or having restrictions reduced.

¶4. Finally, the findings are limited in scope. Most data concern male offenders and programmes were designed with men in mind, so less is known about female offenders. Juvenile findings are mixed: Friendship et al. (2002) reported lower recidivism, Falshaw et al. (2003) found no significant effect on reanalysis, and Cann et al. (2003) found effects only after one year, suggesting short-term effects only. CBT has also been criticised for overlooking social factors such as poverty and employment, which predict reoffending and act as environmental stressors. On balance CBT appears beneficial — Lipsey et al. (2007) found the amount and quality of delivery was what mattered — but its effectiveness depends on who receives it and where.

Q3. Outline how anger management is used with offenders. (4 marks)

Anger management, endorsed by Novaco since the 1970s, treats anger as a strong emotion affecting physiology, behaviour and cognition, so all three are targeted. It is used in prisons or in the community on probation, usually in small groups for about ten sessions.

Step one is cognitive preparation: offenders identify the situations that provoke anger so they can recognise when an outburst is likely, thought patterns are challenged — for example, if they become angry when laughed at, they work through alternative conclusions such as that people are laughing at the behaviour and not at them — and they consider the negative consequences of their anger on others.

Step two is skill acquisition: new coping skills such as relaxation, avoidance, and social skills including assertiveness and conflict resolution. Because anger is a normal emotion, offenders are not taught to fear becoming angry; the emphasis is on giving them skills to control it.

Step three is application practice: offenders role-play scenarios to practise the new skills in a controlled environment where they feel safe and untrained individuals are not exposed to risk. They may also keep anger diaries to identify triggers and evaluate which techniques work for them.

Exam tip from the textbook. You may be asked to justify the use of CBT for offenders. Offer evidence which justifies its effectiveness — so it is important to learn the study evidence, names and numbers.
Two things CBT is not. It is not counselling — the focus is mainly on present thinking, not on overcoming emotional reactions to past difficulties. And it does not work immediately: it requires commitment, because as a talking therapy the offender has to talk through their thoughts and experiences.

23. Key Terms

cognitive behavioural therapy 认知行为疗法
A treatment building insight into thoughts and feelings and how these influence behaviour; works by changing the thought to change the behaviour.
custodial sentence 监禁刑
A sentence served in prison — restricts activities and removes access to the community.
non-custodial sentence 非监禁刑
A sentence served in the community, such as a fine or community service.
forensic psychologist 司法心理学家
The professional who mainly delivers treatment; where staff are not psychologists, they are usually supervised by one.
prosocial behaviour 亲社会行为
Behaviour that takes account of the welfare of others and avoids harming others deliberately.
cognitive restructuring 认知重构
Identifying and challenging maladaptive thinking patterns and replacing them with more objective and adaptive ones.
Socratic questioning 苏格拉底式提问
A thoughtful method of questioning used in CBT to identify values and beliefs — the offender reaches the contradiction themselves.
schema 图式 / 核心信念
A deeply held belief about self and world — Beck identified schemas of self-dominance and personal entitlement in offenders.
distorted thinking 扭曲思维
Beck (1999): displacing blame, justifying offending, misreading social cues as threats, dominance and entitlement schemas.
victim empathy 受害者共情
One of the three aims of CBT for offenders — genuinely considering the impact of their behaviour on others.
Reasoning and Rehabilitation (R&R) 推理与康复项目
Ross and Fabiano (1985): 36 two-hour group sessions teaching critical thinking, social skills, assertiveness and negotiation.
Moral Reconation Therapy (MRT) 道德重建疗法
Little and Robinson (1986): a staged workbook with homework, teaching moral reasoning and judgement.
moral reasoning 道德推理
Judging what is right and wrong — MRT assumes offenders have underdeveloped morality and teaches this directly.
anger management 愤怒管理
Novaco: three steps — cognitive preparation, skill acquisition, application practice.
trigger 触发因素
The situation that starts the anger — identified through anger diaries during cognitive preparation.
scaffolding 脚手架式教学
Adding diary columns gradually so the offender is not given too many expectations before they are ready.
assertiveness 自信表达
Communicating in a confident, non-confrontational way to minimise conflict and be responded to positively.
recidivism 再犯率
The rate of reoffending — the standard measure of whether treatment worked.
randomised controlled trial 随机对照试验
Participants randomly assigned to treatment or control; difficult to do with offenders, so most evaluations are not RCTs.
moderator variable 调节变量
A factor that changes the size of the effect — Lipsey et al. (2007) found only amount and quality of delivery mattered.
stressor 压力源
Any internal or external stimulus placing demands on the individual requiring change in order to cope — poverty and unemployment act this way.
relapse prevention 预防复发
A component of CBT packages aimed at stopping a return to offending after treatment ends.
