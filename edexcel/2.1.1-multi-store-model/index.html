2.1.1 THE MULTI-STORE MODEL OF MEMORY
Atkinson & Shiffrin (1968) · sensory register → short-term → long-term · 多重记忆系统模型

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Content Spec 2.1.1 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.8 · pp.81–87

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Describe the route information takes through the three stores, and what happens at each step if it is not processed.
  • Define encoding, capacity and duration, and use each to tell the three stores apart.
  • State the evidence for each store and say what each piece of evidence actually proves (not just who found what).
  • Evaluate the model by weighing the evidence for separate stores against the criticisms (dual task, rehearsal, Tulving).
  • Apply the model to a revision scenario, explaining each step of a person's learning through the stores.

What this page is really asking

  1. How does information travel through memory? Atkinson and Shiffrin's answer: through three stores in a fixed order, and at every step it is either processed and passed on, or lost.
  2. What makes the three stores different? Three measurable things: what form the information is stored in (encoding), how much fits (capacity), and how long it lasts (duration).
  3. Why believe there are three stores at all? Because they can be damaged separately, and because one memory experiment produces a curve whose two ends behave differently.
  4. And why does the model fall short? It cannot explain dual-task performance, it overrates rehearsal, and it treats long-term memory as one box when it is several.

Start Here: The Number You Lost

背景一个朋友念给你一串陌生的电话号码，你要走到桌前把它打进手机。
角色你是那个把号码装进脑子、一路默念、准备打字的人。
任务你小声重复着走回去。这时一个同学喊了你一声，你回了一句话，回头再看手机，号码没了。
目标解释这个号码为什么消失。先猜下面这题，猜完别走，再往下追两层。整页内容都是为了验证你的猜测而安排的。

先猜如果没人打断、你一直重复这个号码，你能把它保持多久？

追问猜完先别走：这两个问题没有选项，先自己想，再点开提示。

追问一：号码为什么一被打断就没了？你那一遍遍的「重复」，到底在做什么？

提示

重复（复述）是在给号码的痕迹续命：短时记忆里的痕迹本来几十秒就会消失，每重复一遍，就等于把它从消失边缘再拉回来一次。同学喊你那一声，占掉了本该用来复述的那点注意，痕迹就没人维持了。所以真正杀死号码的不是时间，是复述被打断。

追问二：如果这个号码你特别想记一辈子，光靠一直重复，够吗？

提示

不够。复述能把信息从短时送进长时，但长时记忆按「意思」来存，机械重复不一定会留下长期痕迹；而且复述根本不是唯一的路。这个疑问正是本页评价部分要解决的，它最后会变成批评这个模型的一条理由。带着它往下读。

带着这两个追问往下读：第一节先给你整张地图，后面每一节回答地图上的一个具体问题，最后回到这个丢掉的号码，你能用模型完整解释它。

1. Memory Is a Route, Not a Box

Richard Atkinson and Richard Shiffrin (1968) set out to answer one question: what has to happen for a piece of information to be remembered? Their answer is that memory is not one container, but a route information travels along, passing through three stores in a fixed order. At each store it is either processed and passed on, or it is lost.

They separated what is fixed from what we control. The structural features（结构特征：记忆的固定部分，即三个存储单元本身） are the stores themselves: built in, and not something we can change. The control processes（控制过程：我们主动对信息做的操作，如编码、复述、提取） are the things we choose to do with information — how we encode it, rehearse it and retrieve it.

To tell the three stores apart, the model gives each of them three measurable properties:

  • Encoding（编码：信息以什么形式被存下来）: the format in which the store holds information.
  • Capacity（容量：这个单元能装多少）: how much information it can hold.
  • Duration（时长：信息在这里能留多久）: how long information lasts in it.

Back to the number: the friend's voice entered the first store, you attended to it, it moved to the second — and the three stores plus their three properties are the whole map you will now walk through.

中文理解：先记住这条单向路线：信息进来 → 感官登记处 →（被注意）→ 短时记忆 →（被复述）→ 长时记忆。每一关都有淘汰出口：没被注意的、没被复述的，都直接丢。三个单元靠三把尺子区分：编码（存成什么形式）、容量（装多少）、时长（留多久）。后面三节就是依次把每个单元用这三把尺子量一遍。结构特征是固定的单元，控制过程是你自己做的操作，考试别把这两类混在一起。

2. Stop One: The Sensory Register

The first question the model has to answer is: before we even pay attention, how much does memory hold on to, and for how long? The model proposes a sensory register for each of the five senses; the visual one is the most studied.

The question leads straight to a measurement problem: the information vanishes almost at once, so to measure it you must flash it for an instant and ask immediately. George Sperling (1960, 1963) did exactly that — he used a tachistoscope（速示器：精确控制视觉材料显示时长的仪器） to flash a visual array（视觉阵列：一组排好的字母或数字） of twelve letters for a moment, then asked people to recall what they had seen.

The result: participants recalled on average 4.32 letters of the twelve. That single number proves two things at once. The register holds only a fraction of what the senses take in, so its capacity is small; and whatever is not reported must have already faded by the time they finished answering, so its duration is a few hundred milliseconds at most.

Back to the number: this first store held the friend's voice for an instant, and it survived only because you paid attention to it — attention is the process that moves information on to the next store. Everything unattended was lost right there.

中文理解：感官登记处要回答的问题是「还没注意之前，信息能留多久」。测它的难点在于信息消失太快，所以 Sperling 用速示器一闪 12 个字母，让人马上报，结果平均只报出 4.32 个。这一个数字推出两个结论：装得少（容量小）、留得短（几百毫秒）。能被注意到的信息继续往前走，其余当场丢掉。

3. Stop Two: The Short-Term Store

Information you attend to enters the short-term store. Three separate questions need answering here — how long it lasts, how much it holds, and what form it is stored in — and each question has a matching experiment.

Duration: why the counting task?

To measure how long short-term memory lasts, you hit an obstacle: people rehearse, and rehearsing keeps refreshing the memory, so you would never observe its natural lifetime. Peterson and Peterson (1959) got around this by blocking rehearsal. They read participants a trigram（三连字母组：三个辅音组成的一组） of three consonants, then made them count backwards in threes from a number for 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 or 18 seconds before recalling it.

The result: after a 3-second count, recall was good; after 15 to 18 seconds, it had dropped sharply. Because the counting task stopped rehearsal, the forgetting that remained can only be decay — so the short-term store's duration is roughly 20 seconds without rehearsal.

Back to the number: counting backwards in threes is the laboratory version of the classmate calling your name. Both take over the part of attention that was doing the rehearsal, and once rehearsal stops, the trace decays within about 20 seconds.

Capacity: five to eight items

George Miller (1956) put the short-term store at around five to eight items, which he summarised as "the magical number seven, plus or minus two". Because the space is limited, forgetting happens by displacement（置换：新信息把旧信息从有限的槽位里挤出去）: when new items arrive, older or weaker ones are knocked out.

Encoding: held by sound

The third question — what form is the information in? — is answered by the phonological similarity effect（语音相似效应：发音相近的字母更难回忆）. Conrad and Hull (1964) found that letters which sound alike are harder to recall than letters that sound different. The reasoning is the key point: sounds can only confuse each other if the store is holding information by sound, so the short-term store must use acoustic encoding（声学编码：按声音形式储存）.

中文理解：短时记忆这一节要讲清「为什么实验这么设计」。测时长时，障碍是人会复述、复述会续命，所以 Peterson 用倒着数数把复述堵死，剩下的遗忘就只能是自然衰减，推出约 20 秒。容量 Miller 说是 5 到 8 项（魔数 7±2），满了就靠「置换」把旧的挤掉。编码靠 Conrad 的语音相似效应：发音像的字母互相干扰，说明这一单元是按「声音」存的，不是按意思。三个问题、三个实验、三个结论，一一对应。

4. The Gate Into Long-Term Memory

The short-term store is temporary, so the next question is: how does information get from the short-term store into the long-term store? The model's answer is rehearsal, and it distinguishes two kinds.

  • Maintenance rehearsal（维持性复述：机械重复，不加工含义）: repeating the sounds over and over, which strengthens the trace and keeps it alive.
  • Elaborative rehearsal（精细复述：思考信息的含义和意义）: thinking about the meaning and significance of the information.

This is the point where the model plants a seed it cannot water. Rehearsal is one route into long-term memory, but the model treats it as the central one — and the next section's evidence, plus the evaluation later, will show that is a weakness. For now, hold that question and see what the long-term store is actually like.

中文理解：从短时到长时，模型给出的门是复述，分两种：维持性复述是鹦鹉学舌式重复，精细复述是琢磨意思。先记这个区别（考试爱问 Smaragda 翻卡片六遍属于哪种）。但留个心眼：模型把复述当成通往长时的主路，后面评价会指出这不是唯一的路，甚至不是必需的路。这个伏笔现在先埋着。

5. Stop Three: The Long-Term Store

The final store is where information is meant to stay. The same three questions — encoding, duration, capacity — are answered here, and the answers are almost the opposite of the short-term store's.

Encoding: held by meaning

Baddeley (1966b) gave participants lists of words that were either close in meaning or unrelated, and found the semantically similar words were recalled less well. The reasoning mirrors Conrad and Hull: meanings can only confuse each other if the store is holding information by meaning, so the long-term store uses semantic encoding（语义编码：按意义储存）.

Duration: potentially a lifetime

Bahrick et al. (1975) tested whether memories survive decades by asking 400 people aged 17 to 74 to match names and faces from their school yearbooks. Recognition was 90% accurate within 15 years of leaving school, and still 70–80% after 48 years — so the long-term store can hold information for a lifetime.

Capacity: potentially infinite

Brady et al. (2008) showed participants 2,500 objects over 5.5 hours, then asked them to pick, from pairs, which object they had seen. Recognition was over 80%, suggesting the store does not fill up the way the short-term store does.

The three stores contrasted on encoding, capacity, duration and the key evidence for each.

Back to the number: the friend's voice reached the second store, and if you had done more than repeat it — if you had processed what the digits meant — it would have been encoded semantically and transferred to this store, where it could stay.

中文理解：长时记忆是三个单元的终点，三个答案几乎和短时相反：按「意思」存（Baddeley，语义相近的词互相干扰）、留一辈子（Bahrick，毕业 48 年还认得出 70–80% 的老同学）、几乎装不满（Brady，2500 个物体认得 80% 以上）。三张表放一起看，评价题的分数就在「两两对比」上：短时按声音、长时按意思；短时 20 秒、长时一辈子；短时 5–8 项、长时无限。

6. Are the Stores Really Separate?

So far the three stores are a useful description. But a description is not evidence — the next question is why believe short-term and long-term memory are two separate systems, rather than one continuous line? Two kinds of evidence answer it.

Evidence one: damage can hit one store and spare the other

Henry Molaison, following brain surgery for epilepsy, was left unable to form new long-term memories while his short-term memory stayed largely intact. Clive Wearing, after encephalitis（脑炎：脑部炎症，损伤脑结构）, suffered the same pattern: long-term memory lost, short-term memory preserved. The reasoning is what matters: if short-term and long-term memory were one system, damage would weaken both together. The fact that one can break while the other keeps working means they are separate systems, likely in different brain regions.

But notice what the same cases also show: Clive Wearing could not recall his own past, yet he could still play the piano and conduct an orchestra. That is the first crack in the model — long-term memory itself may not be a single store, a point the evaluation returns to.

Evidence two: one experiment, two different curves

Glanzer and Cunitz (1966) asked whether a word's position in a list changes how well it is recalled. Participants recalled the words at the beginning and the end well, and the middle worst. The model explains this serial position effect（系列位置效应：列表开头和结尾的回忆好于中间） in two steps. Words at the start had time to be rehearsed, so they transferred to the long-term store — that is the primacy effect（首因效应：开头记得好）. Words at the end were still sitting in the short-term store's slots when recall began — that is the recency effect（近因效应：结尾记得好）. The middle words had been displaced by later ones. One curve, two ends, two stores: the beginning reflects long-term memory, the end reflects short-term memory.

The serial position curve: its two raised ends are each held by a different store.

Back to the number: the model now has a body of evidence, not just a diagram — the stores can break separately, and one list produces a curve that splits cleanly into two. That is what makes the three-store claim scientific rather than just tidy.

中文理解：这一节回答「凭什么说它们是分开的」。证据一：HM 和 Clive Wearing 都是长时坏了、短时没坏。一个系统若是一体的，就该一起坏，能单独坏说明是两个独立系统。证据二：系列位置效应，记 20 个词，开头（有时间复述→进了长时）和结尾（还坐在短时槽里）记得好，中间（被后进来的挤掉）最差。一条 U 形曲线两头各自对应一个仓库。但别漏：Clive Wearing 能弹琴，这个细节已经埋下「长时记忆内部也分」的伏笔。

7. Where the Model Fails

The model earned its place, but the same evidence that built it also exposes its limits. Four criticisms follow, each one a claim about something the model cannot explain.

What the model gets right

  • Separate stores. HM and Clive Wearing show the long-term store can fail while the short-term store works.
  • Two ends, two stores. Glanzer and Cunitz's curve splits into a long-term (primacy) and a short-term (recency) part.
  • Different codes. Conrad and Hull (acoustic) and Baddeley (semantic) show the stores differ in kind, not just size.

What the model cannot explain

  • Dual-task performance. We struggle with two verbal tasks or two visual tasks at once, but manage one verbal plus one visual easily. A single short-term store with fixed capacity cannot explain why performance depends on the type of task, not just the amount. The working memory model (Baddeley and Hitch, 1974) was built to solve exactly this.
  • Rehearsal is not essential. People learn skills and facts without deliberately rehearsing them; imagery leaves a strong long-term trace with no rehearsal at all. The model overrates the rehearsal route.
  • Long-term memory is not one store. Tulving (1972) split it into episodic memory for personal events, semantic memory for facts, and procedural memory for skills — and the brain-damage cases support this, since Clive Wearing kept skills while losing his past.
  • Reductionism. Cutting memory into separate stores makes experiments easier, but it downplays how the systems actually work together.

Back to the number: the model explains why your number vanished, but it stumbles the moment you ask it about two tasks at once, or about learning something without repeating it. That is the honest limit of the framework.

中文理解：反对意见四条，每条都是「模型解释不了的某个现象」。① 双任务：两件同类事做不好、一嘴一眼做得好，单仓库解释不了，这是工作记忆模型的起点。② 复述不是必需的：不复述也能学会，用图像就能留下长期痕迹。③ 长时记忆不是一个仓库：Tulving 分出情节、语义、程序三类，Clive Wearing 会弹琴但记不得过去，正好支持。④ 还原论：切得太碎，忽略了系统之间的协作。前三条都有具体证据，考试能各写一条。

8. Evaluation

Supports the model

  • Brain-damage cases. HM and Clive Wearing lost long-term memory while short-term memory stayed intact: the stores can be damaged separately, so they are distinct systems.
  • Serial position effect. Glanzer and Cunitz (1966): the primacy end reflects the long-term store, the recency end the short-term store.
  • Store-specific encoding. Conrad and Hull (1964) show the short-term store holds sound; Baddeley (1966b) shows the long-term store holds meaning — a difference in kind, exactly as the model predicts.

Challenges the model

  • Dual-task performance. Two similar tasks clash, but one verbal plus one visual does not. A single fixed-capacity store cannot explain this; the working memory model can.
  • Rehearsal is not essential. Skills and facts are learned without conscious rehearsal; imagery leaves a long-term trace with none.
  • Long-term memory is not unitary. Tulving (1972) proposed episodic, semantic and procedural stores; HM and Clive Wearing support this, since episodic memory failed while skills and facts survived.
  • Reductionism. Splitting memory into stores simplifies experiments but underplays how the systems interconnect.

A fair verdict: as a first map of memory the model was enormously productive — it launched the research that later produced sharper theories. Its store structure survives in those theories; its two biggest simplifications, the single short-term store and the single long-term store, are exactly what the working memory model and Tulving corrected.

Exam tip: contrast, then link

Evaluation marks come from two moves. First, contrast the stores: saying Miller found five to eight items earns little on its own; say it against the long-term store's near-infinite capacity and it becomes a point. Second, link the evidence back to the model: state what the finding proves or fails to prove about the three stores, not just what the researcher did. A worked example: "Clive Wearing could not form new long-term memories but his short-term memory was intact — this supports the model because it shows the two stores can fail independently."

Close the loop: explain the lost number

The friend's voice entered the sensory register. You attended to it, so it moved into the short-term store, where your silent repetition was maintenance rehearsal keeping the trace alive. When the classmate called your name, your attention left the rehearsal; with nothing maintaining it, the trace decayed within about 20 seconds and the number was gone. That is the prediction confirmed: keep rehearsing and the trace survives; stop and it dies. To keep the number for good, maintenance would not be enough — you would need elaborative rehearsal, processing what the digits mean, so the semantic encoding of the long-term store has something to hold.

Reflect before the Exam Lab: the model says the number is lost because rehearsal stopped. Can you think of something you once learned without any rehearsal? Which criticism of the model does that point at?

中文理解：评价记住「三支持三反对」加一句裁决。支持：病人案例证明能单独坏（两个仓库分开）、系列位置效应一条曲线两头对应两仓库、编码差异（短时按声、长时按义）是质的区别。反对：双任务解释不了、复述不是必需、Tulving 说长时还要再分三层。裁决句：作为第一张记忆地图，它很能生发研究；它最大的两个简化（短时只有一个、长时只有一个）恰好是后来工作记忆模型和 Tulving 分别修正的地方。答题套路：先拿数字做对比，再把证据连回模型说「证明了什么」。

10. Exam Lab

Q1 · Application 4 marks

Bashir is struggling to learn the functions of organs for his biology test. He read his textbook in class and took notes, but cannot remember how the organs function. He has taken the textbook home to revise. Explain how the multi-store model could be used to help Bashir remember the function of organs.

Each step of the model maps to a concrete revision action:

  • Attention (sensory register → short-term store): Bashir should revise without distraction, so the organ functions are attended to rather than lost from the sensory register.
  • Maintenance rehearsal (within the short-term store): repeating the key facts keeps the trace alive past the 15–30-second window.
  • Elaborative rehearsal (short-term → long-term store): connecting each organ to what it does (the heart pumps blood) gives the semantic encoding of the long-term store something to hold.
  • Retrieval practice (long-term → short-term store): closing the book and recalling the functions exercises retrieval and strengthens the traces.

Marking guidance: one mark per accurate step applied to Bashir, up to 4. Naming a store or process without applying it earns little — the command is explain how, so every point must be tied to Bashir.

Q2a · Describe 2 marks

Smaragda plans to put her notes onto flashcards and go through each card six times, then write out the information later to see what she remembers. Describe, using the multi-store model, how Smaragda is using information processing.

Going through each flashcard six times is maintenance rehearsal: repeating the information to keep it alive in the short-term store and strengthen the trace. Writing out what she remembers afterwards is retrieval: pulling the information back from the long-term store into the short-term store.

Marking guidance: two processes, one mark each, both linked to the model.

Q2b · Explain 4 marks

Explain, using the multi-store model, two further ways Smaragda could revise.

Way 1: elaborative rehearsal. Instead of only repeating the cards, Smaragda could think about the meaning of each item and connect it to what she already knows. Meaningful processing matches the semantic encoding of the long-term store, so the information transfers more effectively.

Way 2: spaced rehearsal. She could spread the six repetitions over several days rather than one session. Each session re-attends to the material and re-rehearses it, giving repeated chances for the trace to transfer to the long-term store before it decays.

Marking guidance: two ways, up to 2 marks each: 1 for naming the process, 1 for explaining how it maps onto the stores.
