2.1.1 THE MULTI-STORE MODEL OF MEMORY
Atkinson & Shiffrin (1968) · Sensory register · STM · LTM · 多重记忆系统模型

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Content Spec 2.1.1 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.8 · pp.81–87

What this page is really asking

  1. Memory is not one box. Atkinson and Shiffrin split it into three stores that differ in how long they hold information (duration), how much they hold (capacity), and how they hold it (encoding).
  2. Information flows in one direction. Incoming information passes from the sensory register to short-term memory to long-term memory, and each step needs a process: attention, rehearsal, retrieval.
  3. Each store has its own numbers. Learn the numbers for each store, because evaluation questions ask you to contrast them.
  4. The model is a starting point, not the final word. Case studies and dual-task evidence support the split into stores but also reveal what the model leaves out.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Outline the multi-store model: the three stores, the processes that move information between them, and what happens to information that is not processed.
  • Define encoding, storage, retrieval, capacity and duration and use each one to describe a memory store.
  • Quote the key evidence for each store's characteristics: Peterson and Peterson (1959), Miller (1956), Conrad and Hull (1964), Bahrick et al. (1975), Brady et al. (2008), Baddeley (1966b).
  • Evaluate the model with brain-damage cases and the serial position effect as supporting evidence, and dual-task performance and Tulving's (1972) types of long-term memory as criticism.
  • Justify conclusions about a memory scenario by mapping each step of the scenario onto the stores and processes of the model.

1. Three Stores, One System

Atkinson and Shiffrin (1968) proposed a general framework for human memory that became known as the multi-store model. They distinguished two kinds of component. The structural features are the memory stores themselves: fixed, built-in, like the hardware of a computer. The control processes（控制过程：你自己决定怎么对待信息的操作）are the things we consciously do with information: how we encode it, rehearse it and retrieve it. Control processes are like programs a programmer writes: flexible, chosen, and under our control.

The model divides memory into three stores arranged in a sequence: the sensory register, the short-term store and the long-term store. Information entering the system either gets processed and moves on, or is lost. What separates the stores is not just their order, but three measurable properties:

  • Encoding（编码：信息以什么形式被储存）: the format in which a store holds information.
  • Capacity（容量：一个 store 能装多少）: how much information a store can hold.
  • Duration（保持时长：信息能留多久）: how long information lasts there.
The multi-store model, adapted from Atkinson and Shiffrin (1968). Green arrows are the control processes that move information forward; dashed orange arrows show loss.

中文理解：把记忆想成一条流水线，三个仓库串着排。感官登记处先接住五官进来的所有信息，但只留零点几秒；被注意到的信息才能进短时记忆（保质期约 20 秒），短时记忆里反复复述的信息才有机会进长时记忆（可能存一辈子）。每一级都有淘汰出口：没被注意的、没被复述的，直接丢失。三个仓库各记三个属性：编码方式、容量、保持时长，考试评价题考的就是用这些数字把仓库两两对比。

2. The Sensory Register

We experience the world through five senses, and the model proposes a sensory register for each modality: seeing, hearing, touching, tasting and smelling. The visual register is the most researched. In a typical experiment, a visual array of letters is flashed up by a tachistoscope（速示器：控制显示时间的仪器）for a moment and then participants are cued to recall the whole display or one specific row of it.

Sperling (1960, 1963) presented a twelve-letter array and found participants typically recalled on average 4.32 letters of the whole display. This tells us two things about the sensory register:

  • Capacity is small: only a limited amount of the display survives.
  • Duration is very short: a few hundred milliseconds at most, and then the trace is gone.

Whatever is attended to moves on to the short-term store for processing; everything unattended is lost immediately.

中文理解：感官登记处就像一个超短曝光的摄像头：把眼前所有画面先拍下来，但照片几百毫秒后就褪色。Sperling 让人看 12 个字母，普通人只能报出 4.32 个，说明既装不多、又留不久。想保住哪部分，就得把注意力放到那部分上。

3. The Short-Term Store

Information that is attended to enters the short-term store and is held temporarily for 15–30 seconds. Unless it is maintained, it is assumed to decay completely.

Duration: Peterson and Peterson (1959)

Lloyd Peterson and Margaret Peterson used an interference task to prevent rehearsal. Participants memorised a trigram（三连字母组：三个辅音组成的一组）of three consonants, then counted backwards in threes from a number (for example, 679, 676, 673) for intervals of 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 or 18 seconds before recall. Recall was good after short intervals but dropped rapidly after 15–18 seconds. Assuming the counting task blocked rehearsal, the conclusion is that decay occurs in the short-term store over roughly 20 seconds.

Capacity: Miller (1956)

The capacity of the short-term store is around five to nine items. George Miller (1956) refined this to the magical number seven, plus or minus two. Think of the store as between five and nine slots, each holding one item.

Forgetting by displacement

Because the slots are limited, forgetting in short-term memory happens mainly through displacement（置换：新信息把旧信息挤出槽位）: as new information enters, older information, or information with a weaker memory trace, is knocked out. Digit-span experiments demonstrate exactly this limit.

Encoding: acoustic (Conrad and Hull, 1964)

The memory trace in the short-term store is held in an auditory or verbal form: acoustic encoding. The evidence is the phonological similarity effect（语音相似效应：发音相近的字母更难记）: letters and words that sound alike are harder to recall than dissimilar-sounding ones, because similar sounds confuse the store. This confusion is only explainable if the store is holding information by sound.

Two kinds of rehearsal

  • Maintenance rehearsal（维持性复述：机械重复原样）: repeating the sounds over and over to strengthen the trace so it can transfer to long-term memory.
  • Elaborative rehearsal（精细复述：琢磨信息的含义）: thinking about the meaning and significance of the information, which also transfers it into long-term memory.

中文理解：短时记忆是一个 5–9 格的储物柜，保质期约 20 秒。柜子满了之后新东西进门，旧东西就被挤出去（displacement）。柜子里存的是「声音」：发音像的字母会互相干扰，所以记不准。想让东西进长时仓库有两条路：一种是鹦鹉学舌式重复（maintenance rehearsal），一种是琢磨它的意思（elaborative rehearsal）。

4. The Long-Term Store

Atkinson and Shiffrin explained that information in long-term memory is encoded semantically（语义编码：按意义储存）, according to its meaning.

Encoding: Baddeley (1966b)

Participants learning a sequence of semantically similar words recalled less well than participants learning semantically unrelated words. The similar meanings confused each other, which only makes sense if long-term memory stores information by meaning.

Duration: Bahrick et al. (1975)

The duration of long-term memory is potentially a lifetime. Harry Bahrick and colleagues tested Very Long-term Memory using the names and faces of classmates in high-school yearbooks. Four hundred participants aged 17 to 74 were tested: identification of names and faces was 90% accurate within 15 years of leaving school, and still 70–80% accurate 48 years after leaving.

Capacity: Brady et al. (2008)

The capacity of long-term memory is potentially infinite. Timothy Brady and colleagues showed participants 2,500 objects over 5.5 hours, then presented pairs of objects and asked which one had been seen before. Object recognition was over 80%.

The three stores contrasted on encoding, capacity, duration and the key evidence for each.

中文理解：长时仓库按「意思」存放：意思相近的词会互相干扰（Baddeley），所以找不到正确答案。它几乎装不满（Brady：2500 个物体认得 80% 以上），也几乎不过期（Bahrick：毕业 48 年还能认出 70–80% 的老同学）。三个仓库放一张表对比着背，评价题的分数就在「对比」两个字上。

5. Evidence for Separate Stores

Case studies of brain-damaged patients

Henry Molaison suffered amnesia following brain surgery for epilepsy: his long-term memory was severely impaired, but his short-term memory was largely intact. Clive Wearing suffered long-term memory impairment following encephalitis（脑炎）, while his short-term store remained unaffected. In both cases the patient could not transfer information from the short-term store to the long-term store. This dissociation supports the claim that the two stores are separate systems, perhaps located in different brain regions.

Two cautions come with this evidence. Case-study patients are unique and so are their injuries, so generalising from individual cases is risky. And the same cases cut the other way: Clive Wearing could not recall events from his past, yet could still play the piano and conduct an orchestra. That pattern suggests long-term memory is not one single store, but separate stores for practised skills and for factual, autobiographical information.

The serial position effect

Murray Glanzer and Anita Cunitz (1966) investigated whether a word's position in a list affects recall. Participants recalled more words from the beginning (the primacy effect, 首因效应) and the end (the recency effect, 近因效应) of the list, and fewest from the middle.

The multi-store explanation: words at the beginning had the chance to be rehearsed, so their traces were strengthened and transferred to the long-term store. While those words were being processed, middle words were filling the short-term slots. End words then displaced the older middle traces, leaving only the final words sitting in short-term memory. One curve, two stores: the beginning reflects long-term memory, the end reflects short-term memory.

The serial position curve (Glanzer and Cunitz, 1966): the two ends of the list are recalled well because they are held by two different stores.

中文理解：记 20 个词，开头和结尾记得牢、中间忘得多，这就是系列位置效应。解释要用两个仓库接力：开头的词有时间被复述，已经进了长时仓库（首因效应）；结尾的词还坐在短时记忆的槽位上（近因效应）；中间的词被后进来的词挤掉了。一条 U 形曲线同时指向两个仓库，这就是「仓库分开存在」最直观的证据。

6. Evaluation

Supports the model

  • Brain-damage cases. HM and Clive Wearing lost long-term memory while short-term memory stayed intact: the stores can be damaged separately, so they are distinct systems.
  • Serial position effect. Glanzer and Cunitz (1966): the primacy part of the curve reflects the long-term store, the recency part the short-term store.
  • Store-specific encoding. Conrad and Hull (1964) show the short-term store holds sound; Baddeley (1966b) shows the long-term store holds meaning: qualitative differences between stores, as the model predicts.

Challenges the model

  • Dual-task performance. We do poorly on two verbal or two visual tasks together, but well on one verbal plus one visual task. A single short-term store with fixed capacity cannot explain this; the working memory model (Baddeley and Hitch, 1974, page 2.1.2) can.
  • Rehearsal is not essential. We often learn skills and information without consciously rehearsing them; imagery is one strategy that leaves a strong long-term trace with no rehearsal at all.
  • Long-term memory is not unitary. Tulving (1972) proposed separate long-term stores: episodic memory for personal experiences, semantic memory for facts, procedural memory for skills. HM and Clive Wearing support this: their episodic memory was impaired while procedural and semantic memory survived.
  • Reductionism. Artificially splitting memory into stores makes experiments easier but underplays how the systems interconnect.

A fair verdict: despite these criticisms, the multi-store model has been a valuable framework. It stimulated a huge wealth of memory research, and the better, more precise theories that followed were built in response to it.

Exam tip: evaluate by contrasting the stores

When evaluating the multi-store model, use research evidence that highlights the qualitative and quantitative differences between the short-term and long-term stores. It is not enough to state that Miller (1956) found the short-term store holds five to nine items: contrast that with the long-term store's capacity. And always explain how the research supports or challenges the model. Simply reporting findings without linking back to the model is not effective evaluation.

Worked example:

Point: Clive Wearing suffered amnesia which meant he could not remember events from his past or store new memories. However, his short-term memory was not impaired.

Link: This supports the multi-store model because it shows his long-term store was damaged while his short-term store was unimpaired; the stores must be independent of one another.

中文理解：评价这张图记两条主线。支持线：病人案例（HM、Clive Wearing）证明两个仓库能单独坏掉，系列位置效应证明一条曲线两头对应两个仓库。反对线：双任务实验（一动嘴一动眼做得好、两件同类事做不好）单仓库解释不了；不复述也能学会东西；Tulving 说长时记忆里还分情节、语义、程序三个子仓库。最后一句万能评价：模型虽然简单化，但它推动了整个记忆研究，后来的精细理论都是在修正它的过程中长出来的。

8. Exam Lab

Q1 · Application 4 marks

Bashir needs to revise for his biology test. He is struggling to learn the functions of organs in the body, such as the heart and lungs. Bashir read his biology textbook in class and took notes but cannot remember all of the information about how organs function. He has taken his biology textbook home to learn and plans to use his textbook and notes to help revise for the test. Explain how the multi-store model (Atkinson and Shiffrin, 1968) could be used to help Bashir remember the function of organs in the body.

Because the model says information must be attended to, rehearsed and transferred between stores, Bashir should design his revision around each step:

  • Attention (sensory register → short-term store): read the notes actively and without distraction, so the information is attended to rather than lost from the sensory register.
  • Maintenance rehearsal (within the short-term store): repeat the key facts aloud or rewrite them, keeping the trace alive beyond the 15–30-second window.
  • Elaborative rehearsal (short-term → long-term store): connect each organ's function to its meaning, for example linking the heart to pumping blood, so the semantic encoding of the long-term store has something to hold.
  • Retrieval practice (long-term store → short-term store): close the book and recall the functions from memory, which exercises retrieval and strengthens the traces.

Marking guidance: one mark for each accurate step of the model applied to Bashir's revision, up to 4. Naming a store or process with no application earns little; the command word is explain how ... could be used, so every point must be tied to Bashir.

Q2a · Describe 2 marks

Smaragda is planning her revision for her end-of-year exams. She plans to put her class notes onto flashcards and go through each flashcard six times before writing out the information later to see what she remembers. Describe, using the multi-store model of memory (Atkinson and Shiffrin, 1968), how Smaragda is using information processing during her revision.

Going through each flashcard six times is maintenance rehearsal: she is repeating the information to keep it alive in the short-term store and strengthen the trace. Writing out what she remembers afterwards is retrieval: pulling the information back from the long-term store into the short-term store.

Marking guidance: two steps, one mark each. Both must be linked to the model's processes.

Q2b · Explain 4 marks

Explain, using the multi-store model of memory (Atkinson and Shiffrin, 1968), two further ways that Smaragda could revise for her end-of-year exam.

Way 1: elaborative rehearsal. Instead of only repeating the flashcards, Smaragda could think about the meaning of each item and connect it to what she already knows. In the model, meaningful processing matches the semantic encoding of the long-term store, so the information transfers more effectively from the short-term store.

Way 2: spaced rehearsal over time. She could spread the six repetitions across several days instead of one session. Each session re-attends to the material and re-rehearses it, giving repeated chances for the trace to be transferred into the long-term store before it decays from the short-term store.

Marking guidance: two ways, up to 2 marks each: 1 mark for naming the process, 1 mark for explaining how it maps onto the model's stores.

9. Key Terms

multi-store model
多重记忆系统模型：三个仓库串成流水线的记忆框架（Atkinson & Shiffrin, 1968）
structural features
结构特征：天生的记忆仓库本身，像电脑硬件，改不了
control processes
控制过程：自己决定的加工方式（编码、复述、提取），像可写的程序
sensory register
感官登记处：五官各一个，只留几百毫秒
tachistoscope
速示器：精确控制视觉材料显示时长的仪器
digit span
数字广度：能按顺序无错背出的数字个数
trigram
三连字母组：三个辅音组成的一组，测 STM 时距用
interference task
干扰任务：阻止复述的任务，如倒数三
displacement
置换：新信息把旧信息从有限的槽位中挤出去
acoustic encoding
听觉编码：短时记忆按声音形式储存
semantic encoding
语义编码：长时记忆按意义储存
phonological similarity effect
语音相似效应：发音相近的字母在 STM 里互相混淆、更难回忆
maintenance rehearsal
维持性复述：机械重复，保住短时记忆的痕迹
elaborative rehearsal
精细复述：加工含义，把信息送进长时记忆
serial position effect
系列位置效应：列表开头与结尾回忆好于中间
primacy / recency effect
首因效应／近因效应：开头进长时仓库，结尾还在短时槽位
encephalitis
脑炎：脑部炎症，损伤脑结构（Clive Wearing 的病因）
episodic / semantic / procedural memory
情节／语义／程序记忆：Tulving (1972) 的三个长时子仓库
