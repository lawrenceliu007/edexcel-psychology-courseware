1.2.7 NORMAL AND SKEWED DISTRIBUTIONS
Bell curve · 68% / 95% rule · Positive and negative skew · 正态与偏态分布

Topic A · Social psychology Methods Spec 1.2.7 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.5 · pp.52–53

What this chapter is really asking

  1. Averages summarise the centre; the distribution describes the whole shape. This page teaches you to read that shape and predict where people fall on it.
  2. Normal means predictable. A bell curve puts the three averages at the centre and fixes how many people fall within one or two standard deviations of it.
  3. Skewed means dragged. When scores bunch to one side, the tail drags the mean away from the peak while the median and mode hold their ground.
  4. Naming follows the tail. Tail on the high end = positive skew; tail on the low end = negative skew.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Define a distribution and explain why the overall shape carries information that a single average cannot.
  • Describe the normal distribution: symmetry, the three averages coinciding, tails never touching the axis.
  • Apply the 68% and 95% rules to estimate what range most of a population falls in, given the mean and standard deviation.
  • Recognise positive and negative skew, explain what causes each, and name which average is pulled by the tail.
  • Place the mean, median and mode on any distribution curve and justify their order.
  • Justify conclusions about a data set by referring to its distribution shape.

1. The Shape of Data

The previous page compressed a data set into a few numbers: a mean, a median, a mode, a standard deviation. Those numbers describe the centre and the spread, but they say nothing about the distribution（分布：整组分数的整体形状）, the pattern formed by the frequency of every value across the whole data set.

Two data sets can share the same mean and still look completely different. One can pile up neatly around the average; another can split into two crowds at the extremes with almost nobody in the middle. The shape is the information that averages throw away, and exam questions often ask you to read it back.

With a large sample you can plot how many times each value occurs and see the pattern directly. That pattern takes one of two headline forms on this page: the normal distribution, the tidy symmetric bell, and the skewed distribution, where the heap of scores leans to one side.

中文理解：为什么要看形状
平均数只告诉你「中间在哪」，分布形状告诉你「人是怎么站的」。同样平均分 70 分，可以是人挤人地堆在 70 附近（正态），也可以是一半人考 90 多、一半人考 50 多（两头高中间低，这就不正态了）。所以光报平均数不够，还得看曲线长什么样。本页两种形状：正态（对称钟形）和偏态（挤向一边）。

2. The Normal Distribution

When the frequency distribution of a whole population is plotted, many human measurements form a bell-shaped curve: a normal distribution（正态分布：中间高两头低的对称钟形）. Height is the classic example: most people gather around the average, and the further a score is from the average, the rarer it is.

Figure: a normal distribution curve, with the 68% and 95% bands marked

Three features define the normal distribution:

  • Symmetry around the mid-point. The mean, median and mode should all be aligned at the centre.
  • The tails never touch the horizontal axis. Extreme scores are always possible, just vanishingly rare.
  • Fixed proportions under the curve. It is a rule of normal distribution that 68% of the population falls between one standard deviation each side of the mid-point, and 95% falls between two standard deviations each side.
中文理解：钟形曲线三个记忆点
正态分布记住三件事：三点重合（均值、中数、众数都站在正中间）、尾巴不落地（极端分数永远存在只是极少）、比例固定（±1σ 装下约 68% 的人，±2σ 装下约 95%）。这把「标准差」从描述工具变成了预测工具：知道均值和标准差，就能断言大多数人落在哪个区间。

3. Working with σ

Standard deviation is given the symbol σ, so 1σ means one standard deviation. The proportions under the normal curve let you turn a mean and an SD into a statement about real people: if you know the centre and the spread, you know where most of the population stands.

Worked example: obedience scores

The standard deviation for a set of obedience scores was 2.8 and the mean, median and mode score was 7. Place 7 at the mid-point:

  • One standard deviation above the mid-point: 7 + 2.8 = 9.8. One below: 7 − 2.8 = 4.2. So 68% of the sample scored between 4.2 and 9.8.
  • Two standard deviations each side: 7 − 5.6 = 1.4 and 7 + 5.6 = 12.6. So 95% scored between 1.4 and 12.6.
  • To know exactly what values those intervals represent, the standard deviation must be calculated on the raw scores.

Notice what happened there. The mean and SD alone told us that only about 1 person in 20 scores outside 1.4–12.6, and that a score of 9.8 already sits at the edge of the range where 68% of people live. That is why the normal distribution is useful for comparing individuals: it converts "how unusual is this score?" into arithmetic.

中文理解：怎么用 68 和 95
口算流程只有一步：均值 ± 标准差，再均值 ± 两个标准差。均值 7、标准差 2.8：加减一个标准差得 4.2 到 9.8，这段装 68% 的人；加减两个标准差得 1.4 到 12.6，这段装 95% 的人。考试考的就是这一步减法加法，方向别搞反：数字越大越靠右（高分端），加减都是对均值做的。

4. Skewed Distributions

Some distributions are not symmetrical: they are skewed（偏态：峰不在中间，被挤向一边）. The cause is usually the difficulty of the test or the nature of the sample.

Figure: the tail always drags the mean; the median stays in the middle and the mode stays on the peak

How each skew arises

  • Negative skew: if a test is easy, or the aptitude of the sample is unusually high, most people score highly. Scores bunch on the right with a tail on the left. The mean is pulled left by the few low scores, so the mean is lower than the mode.
  • Positive skew: if a test is particularly difficult, or the aptitude of the sample is low, most people achieve low scores. Scores bunch on the left with a tail on the right. The mean is pulled right by the few high scores, so the mean is higher than the mode.
中文理解：命名跟尾巴走
偏态命名只看尾巴在哪端：尾巴拖向分数高的那端（右边）叫正偏态，拖向低分端（左边）叫负偏态。成因一句话：卷子太难（大多数人低分）出正偏态，卷子太简单（大多数人高分）出负偏态。均值是最容易被拽走的那个：它要平均每一个分数，少数离群值就能把它拉向尾巴；中数和众数只看位置和次数，尾巴碰不到它们。

5. Placing the Three Averages

When plotting the mean, median and mode on any distribution curve, three placement rules hold:

  • The median is always exactly in the middle of the x-axis, because that is its definition: half the scores below, half above.
  • The mode sits where the most values are, which is the peak of the curve.
  • The mean is dragged toward the tail: right on a positive skew, left on a negative skew. The mean is affected by extreme scores, by the lower scores on a negative skew and by the higher scores on a positive skew, so the mean will be lower than the mode in a negative skew and higher than the mode in a positive skew.

A memory trick

A common confusion is which skew is "positive". A positive skew has its tail at the positive (high) end of the horizontal axis, and a negative skew has its tail at the negative (low) end. Some students draw a face on the curve so it becomes a whale: a whale swimming towards the vertical axis is coming home, so it is positive; a whale swimming away is negative, because it is leaving.

中文理解：三个平均数的排队规律
任何曲线上的站位：中数永远站正中间（一半人左一半人右），众数站峰顶（分数最密集处），均值被尾巴拉走。所以负偏态（尾巴在左）排队是 mean、median、mode 从左到右，正偏态（尾巴在右）排队是 mode、median、mean。鲸鱼记法：把曲线想象成一条鲸鱼，往纵轴游是在「回家」（正偏态），远离纵轴是「离家」（负偏态）。

6. Checkpoint

Quick checks

Q1 In a normal distribution, where do the mean, median and mode sit?

Q2 A data set has a mean of 7 and a standard deviation of 2.8 and is normally distributed. Between which values do 68% of scores fall?

Q3 A test was very difficult: most students scored low and only a few scored high. The frequency graph shows scores bunched on the left with a tail on the right. What is this distribution?

Q4 On a negative skew, the mean is ______ the mode.

7. Exam Lab

Distribution questions usually arrive dressed as data interpretation: you are shown a description of a histogram or a set of summary statistics and asked to name the shape, place the averages, or justify which measure best represents the data. The three questions below work through one scenario.

Exam practice — the vocabulary test

A class of 200 students took a vocabulary test. The scores were bunched at the high end, with a long tail of a few low scores stretching to the left. The mode was 24, the median was 22 and the mean was 19.

Q1 (2 marks)

Identify the type of distribution shown by these scores and explain your choice.

A negative skew. The scores bunch at the high (right) end with a tail at the low (left) end, and the three averages confirm it: the mode (24) is greater than the median (22), which is greater than the mean (19). One mark for naming the negative skew, one mark for a linked justification (bunching at the high end, or the mode > median > mean ordering).

Q2 (2 marks)

Explain why the mean is lower than the mode in this distribution.

The mean is calculated from every score, so the few extremely low scores in the left tail drag it down; the mode only counts the most frequent score, which the tail does not touch. One mark for identifying that extreme/low scores pull the mean, one mark for the contrast with the mode (it ignores the tail because it is a frequency count, not an average of all values).

Q3 (2 marks)

Explain why the median might be a more appropriate measure of central tendency than the mean for these scores.

The median is a positional measure, so the extreme low scores in the tail do not affect it, and it better represents the typical student. The mean is distorted by outliers in a skewed distribution, so it understates where most students actually scored (the mode was 24 but the mean only 19). One mark for noting the mean is distorted by the extreme scores, one mark for stating the median is unaffected by them and therefore more representative of the skewed data.

Two habits that earn the marks:
  • Name, then justify. "Negative skew" alone is one mark; the second mark comes from evidence: where the scores bunch, where the tail is, or the mode > median > mean ordering.
  • Use all three averages as a diagnostic. If the question gives you mean, median and mode, their order tells you the shape before you look at any graph: mean lowest → negative skew; mean highest → positive skew; all equal → normal.

distribution
