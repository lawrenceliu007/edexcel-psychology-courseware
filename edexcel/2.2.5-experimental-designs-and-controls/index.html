2.2.5–2.2.8 EXPERIMENTAL DESIGNS & CONTROLS
Designs · Operationalisation · Extraneous variables · Counterbalancing · 实验设计与控制

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Methods Spec 2.2.5–2.2.8 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.11 · pp.101–111

What this page is really asking

  1. Participants must be allocated to conditions, and every allocation method has a flaw. Independent groups risk participant differences; repeated measures risk order effects; matched pairs try to buy the best of both at the cost of time.
  2. Every weakness has a named control. Random allocation, counterbalancing, standardisation, control groups and blind procedures are the toolkit: learn which control answers which problem.
  3. Vague variables ruin studies. Operationalisation means deciding exactly how the IV is changed and exactly how the DV is recorded, so the study can be replicated and checked.
  4. Anything you did not manipulate is a threat. Extraneous variables become confounding variables the moment they actually distort the DV; situational ones come from the setting, participant ones from the people.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Identify the experimental design (independent groups, repeated measures, matched pairs) used in a described study.
  • Evaluate each design with its named weakness and the control that fixes it: random allocation, counterbalancing, single/double-blind.
  • Operationalise IVs and DVs and judge whether a given operationalisation is good or poor.
  • Classify extraneous variables as situational or participant, and explain when one becomes confounding.
  • Justify control decisions (standardisation, control group, randomisation) for a given study scenario.

Start Here: The Memory Club Test

背景The psychology club wants to know whether a memory technique really works. The plan: give everyone a word-list test, teach the technique, then test again two weeks later with a different list.
角色You are the club member the others turn to when the results look strange.
任务Results are in: every single member improved on the second list. The president announces "the technique works!" — but you are not so sure. Something else could explain the improvement.
目标Name the hidden explanation before you read on. Commit below: the whole page is built to confirm or correct your answer.

先猜用同一批人前后测两次，结果全员进步，最大的隐患是什么？

1. Three Experimental Designs

Once the IV is operationalised and its levels chosen, every participant must be allocated to a condition. Three designs can do this, and each one trades a different weakness for a different gain.

Three ways to allocate participants to conditions (Figures 11.3 and 11.4, simplified): who is tested, and how often.
DesignStrengthsWeaknesses
Independent groups（独立组设计：每组人只参加一个条件）Participants only experience one level of the IV, so they are less likely to guess the aim: demand characteristics and expectancy effects are reduced. It also avoids order effects entirely, because nobody is tested twice.Recruiting twice as many participants. Participant variables (individual differences) between the groups may make the comparison unreliable; random allocation（随机分配：抽签式分组）makes an even distribution probable, though not certain.
Repeated measures（重复测量设计：同一批人完成所有条件）Individual differences are resolved: each participant is compared with themselves across conditions. Fewer participants are needed, so it is more economical.Demand characteristics are greatly increased, because participants see every condition and can guess the aim. Order effects appear: practice (better later) or fatigue (worse later). Controls: a single-blind technique, and counterbalancing.
Matched pairs（配对组设计：不同人各测一个条件，但按关键特征配对）Conditions can be compared fairly despite using different people, so any difference is more likely to come from the IV and causation can be established.Time-consuming: matching needs pre-testing and background research. Many participants are excluded for not meeting the criteria, and matching can never cover every relevant variable.

Match on characteristics central to the aim, not trivial ones: a study of driving ability needs matched driving experience, eyesight and reaction time, and gains nothing from matching hair colour.

中文理解：三种设计的取舍可以记成一句话：独立组怕「两组人本来就不一样」，解药是随机分配；重复测量怕「做第二遍不是第一次了」，解药是抵消平衡；配对组想两头讨好，代价是配不全、配得慢。答题时先点出设计，再说它的天生弱点，最后给对应的解药，三步就是满分结构。

2. Order Effects & Counterbalancing

Order effects（顺序效应：表现受测试先后顺序影响）are the problem with presentation order. A participant may learn the task in the first condition and perform better in the second (practice), or become tired and perform worse (fatigue). In the club scenario, the hidden explanation you may have guessed was exactly this: the second test came after everyone had already done the task once.

Counterbalancing（抵消平衡：一半人先 A 后 B，另一半先 B 后 A）does not remove order effects but spreads them equally: half the participants experience Condition A then Condition B, while the other half does B then A. Practice or fatigue now boosts (or drags down) both conditions equally, so they balance out of the comparison.

Randomisation helps too. Random allocation means participants are allocated to a condition at random, for example names drawn from a hat, so every participant has an equal chance of each condition and participant variables are spread evenly across the groups.

Back to the club: the design the president chose was repeated measures, and the improvement was contaminated by practice. Counterbalancing would have helped only partly, because the real difference was two weeks of training between the tests; what the club actually needed was a control group（控制组：走完全流程但不接受实验处理的一组）: a second group taught no technique, tested at the same two time points. Any improvement beyond the control group's would then be attributable to the technique. A control group gives the baseline from which the experimental group's change is judged.

中文理解：顺序效应有两种面孔：练熟了变好（练习），做累了变差（疲劳）。抵消平衡的逻辑是让它们对两个条件「一碗水端平」：AB 和 BA 各一半，效应互相抵消。但开头社团那个情景里，真正的干扰远不止顺序一项：中间还夹着两周的训练，所以还需要一个不学技巧的控制组当参照线。看到「全员进步」先问一句：跟谁比？

3. Operationalisation

Operationalisation（操作化：把变量定义得具体可测）means defining the variables specifically so they are directly tested: deciding exactly how the IV will be manipulated and exactly how the DV will be recorded. Operational definitions state what the variables are and how they are measured.

Why it matters: precise operationalisation means the study can be exactly replicated to check the conclusions are reliable; it increases objectivity, because the DV is measured the same way by all researchers and is not open to interpretation; and it lets other psychologists assess whether the research is valid.

Poor operationalisation: a researcher suspects children without a healthy breakfast struggle in morning lessons. She asks children what they had for breakfast, splits them into "healthy" and "unhealthy" groups, then watches them read a book and decides how well they read.

Nothing here is defined: what counts as a healthy breakfast is unclear, and "how well they read" is one researcher's judgement. A second researcher could not replicate the study, might judge the same child differently, and cannot check whether the variables measured what they claimed.

Good operationalisation: the same researcher asks children and parents to record breakfasts for a week; a nutritionist categorises them, with breakfasts exceeding recommended salt, fat and sugar allowances defined as unhealthy. Each child then reads the same story aloud during literacy hour, timed by the researcher, who also records reading errors.

Now the IV (breakfast type) and the DV (reading time and errors) are defined without ambiguity. The study is replicable, and another researcher can assess whether the definitions measured what was intended.

中文理解：判断操作化好坏只看一条：换一个研究者来，能不能照着定义原样重做一遍、并且量出一模一样的数。好操作化的两个标志：IV 的分组标准有明确界线（营养师定标准、有具体指标），DV 的测量有统一工具（同一个故事、计时、记错处）。「看一眼然后凭感觉判断」永远是不合格的 DV。

4. Extraneous Variables & the Control Toolkit

An experiment should control factors that might have an unwanted effect on the DV. An extraneous variable（无关变量：可能影响 DV 但不是 IV 的变量）may have affected the dependent variable even though it was not the independent variable. When an extraneous variable actually succeeds in influencing the DV, it makes the effect look as though it came from the IV: it is then a confounding variable（混淆变量：直接搅浑结果的变量，研究不再测量原本想测的东西）, and the study no longer measures what it intended.

Extraneous variables come in two families:

  • Situational variables（情境变量：来自研究环境的干扰）: lighting, noise, temperature, other people, disturbances, time of day. They are controlled by being held constant for all participants, or eliminated by removing the possibility altogether.
  • Participant variables（参与者变量：来自人本身的差异）: motivation, personality, intelligence, experience, age, skills. Age and gender are easy to control; motivation and experience take more thought.

Only variables that might genuinely affect the DV need controlling: room temperature matters little unless the study tests something temperature could influence. This is why the designs of section 1 matter so much: participant variables are exactly what independent groups designs risk and repeated measures designs solve.

The control toolkit for everything else:

ControlWhat it does
Standardisation（标准化：所有人经历完全相同的研究体验）Standardised instructions given to every participant remove the experimenter's chance to give verbal or non-verbal cues; standardised procedures (stages, timings, apparatus) treat everyone the same way. It also improves replicability.
Control group（控制组）Undertakes the procedure but not the experimental manipulation, giving a baseline: any difference between the groups shows the effect of the IV.
Single-blind（单盲：参与者不知道研究目的）Participants are unaware they are part of an experiment, or are deceived about its true nature, so their beliefs about the aim cannot shape their performance.
Double-blind（双盲：参与者与研究者都不知道目的）Independent researchers who do not know the aim conduct the study, so neither participant beliefs nor researcher cues can influence the outcome.

Close the loop: rebuild the club test properly

The club's design was repeated measures: the same members did both tests, so individual differences were not the problem. The contamination came from order: the second test followed practice on the first. Counterbalancing would spread that practice across both conditions if the study truly compared two conditions; but because the club compared "before training" with "after training", the honest fix is a control group that spends the same two weeks without the technique, tested at the same two time points. Standardised instructions and identical word-list difficulty keep the situational variables constant, and if the president counting the scores does not know who was in which group, the scoring is effectively blind. Every control on this page has a job in this one scenario.

Reflect before the Checkpoint: look back at your Start Here answer. If you spotted the practice effect, you already think like an experimental designer; if not, re-read section 2 with the club results in mind and check where your intuition went.

6. Exam Lab

Q2a · Describe 2 marks

Zara and Aiden developed a new memory technique to help students with their revision. Volunteer students were given a word list to learn and their recall was recorded. They were then asked to practise the new memory technique for two weeks. After the two weeks they were given a different word list to learn and their recall was measured. The difference in recall before and after learning the memory technique was compared. Describe one control that Zara and Aiden would have considered when designing the word lists for this study.

The two word lists must be matched for difficulty, so any change in recall cannot be explained by the second list simply being easier. For example, both lists should contain the same number of words, drawn from similar word types, with similar word lengths and familiarity. Holding list difficulty constant in this way is standardisation: it removes a situational variable that would otherwise confound the comparison.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for identifying the control (matching the lists for difficulty/word type/length), 1 mark for explaining that it stops list difficulty from explaining the change in recall.

Q2b · Explain 2 marks

Zara and Aiden randomised the word lists. Explain how they did this.

Randomisation means putting the words into a random order so that no systematic pattern decides which words appear in each position, for example by drawing the words from a container or using a random number generator to shuffle them. Each word has an equal chance of appearing anywhere on the list, so position effects (words at the start or end being recalled better) cannot explain the difference between the two lists.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for a random method (random number generator, drawing from a container), 1 mark for the consequence (equal chance per word, removing order/position effects as an explanation).

Q3a · Explain one strength 2 marks

Noorie investigated encoding in short-term memory using a volunteer sample of 20 participants. Each participant took part in Condition A and Condition B. Condition A involved learning ten acoustically similar sounding words, such as cat, hat, bat. Condition B involved the same participants learning ten acoustically different sounding words, such as pen, dog, mat. Noorie recorded how many words each participant correctly recalled. Explain one strength of Noorie using a repeated measures design in her experiment.

Because the same 20 participants learn both word lists, participant variables (memory ability, age, motivation) are held constant across conditions: each participant acts as their own baseline. Any difference in recall between acoustically similar and acoustically different words is therefore more likely to reflect the encoding effect being tested rather than pre-existing differences between two groups of people.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for naming the strength (participant variables controlled / each participant compared with themselves), 1 mark for applying it to Noorie's encoding comparison.

Q3b · Explain one weakness 2 marks

Explain one weakness of Noorie using a repeated measures design in her experiment into short-term memory encoding.

Order effects are the main risk: doing the first word-list task practises the skill of recalling lists, so performance in the second condition may improve through practice (or deteriorate through fatigue) rather than because of the acoustic similarity manipulation. Participants also see both conditions, which increases the chance they guess the aim and show demand characteristics. Counterbalancing the two lists across participants would be the standard fix for the order problem.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for naming the weakness (order effects/practice/fatigue or increased demand characteristics), 1 mark for explaining how it distorts this encoding comparison.

Q3c · Explain how 2 marks

Explain how Noorie could have used counterbalancing in her study of encoding in short-term memory.

Split the 20 participants into two halves of 10. Ten participants learn the acoustically similar list (Condition A) first and then the acoustically different list (Condition B); the other ten learn Condition B first and Condition A second. Practice or fatigue gained on the first list now falls equally on both conditions, so order effects no longer favour one condition over the other.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for describing the two orders (AB and BA with equal halves), 1 mark for the consequence (order effects distributed equally across conditions).

7. Key Terms

independent groups design
独立组设计：不同的人分进不同条件，每人只测一次
repeated measures design
重复测量设计：同一批人完成全部条件
matched pairs design
配对组设计：不同人各测一个条件，但按关键特征提前配对
random allocation
随机分配：抽签式分组，让个体差异均匀散到各组
order effects
顺序效应：练习让第二次变好、疲劳让第二次变差
counterbalancing
抵消平衡：一半人 AB、一半人 BA，让顺序效应两边抵消
operationalisation
操作化：把 IV 怎么改、DV 怎么量定义到可直接检验
operational definitions
操作定义：变量是什么、用什么方式测量
extraneous variable
无关变量：可能影响 DV 但不是 IV 的变量
confounding variable
混淆变量：真的搅进了 DV，研究不再测量原本想测的东西
situational / participant variables
情境变量来自环境（噪音、温度、时段）；参与者变量来自人（动机、智力、经验）
standardisation
标准化：指导语与流程人人相同，掐断主试线索
control group
控制组：走流程但不接受处理，提供基线参照
single-blind / double-blind
单盲：参与者不知目的；双盲：参与者和执行研究者都不知道
