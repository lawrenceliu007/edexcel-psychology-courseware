2.3.2 CONTEMPORARY STUDY: SCHMOLCK ET AL. (2002)
Semantic knowledge · HM · medial & lateral temporal lobes · 语义知识与脑损伤定位

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Contemporary study Spec 2.3.2 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.13 · pp.128–132

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Describe the aim of the study: relating semantic test scores to the site of temporal lobe damage, and deciding whether HM was unique.
  • Reproduce the design: six amnesia patients in three groups (HF, MTL+, HM) compared with eight matched controls, and the 48 line drawings behind the tests.
  • Interpret the results: hippocampus-only damage leaves semantic knowledge intact, while anterolateral temporal cortex damage impairs it, especially for living things.
  • Explain the HM special case: his grammatical errors, the nurture explanation and why it cannot be settled.
  • Evaluate the study on converging evidence, the 14 raters, small samples, retrospective causal limits, ecological validity and social sensitivity.

What this page is really asking

  1. Can a brain lesion tell us where a memory system sits? Patients with damage in different temporal lobe regions were given the same semantic tests; the pattern of who failed what acts like a map.
  2. The hippocampus is not the semantic store. The two HF patients, whose damage was largely restricted to the hippocampus, performed like the controls. The three MTL+ patients, with medial plus anterolateral damage, did not.
  3. HM was almost, but not quite, one of them. He resembled the MTL+ group on definitions yet stayed in the normal range on other tests, and made a striking number of grammatical errors that the researchers traced to his upbringing rather than his surgery.
  4. The method is powerful but retrospective. Rare case studies cannot prove cause, the line drawings may lack mundane realism, and 14 independent raters were needed to keep the qualitative scoring objective.

提示

带着这两层追问往下读：Schmolck 真正做的事情，是用「哪里坏了」给「语义知识存在哪」画一张定位图；而那位说得最多的病人，恰好是图上最不好标注的一个点。

1. The Aim: Where Does Semantic Knowledge Live?

You have just spotted the difference between naming things and knowing what they mean: semantic memory（语义记忆：关于词义、事实与概念的长期记忆，不依赖某次具体经历）can stay intact even when a person cannot form new memories of events. Case studies of brain-damaged patients are valuable precisely because they draw boundaries between memory systems that blur together in a healthy brain. Damage to the temporal lobe（颞叶：大脑两侧靠耳朵的区域）is widely associated with anterograde and retrograde amnesia, yet well-established long-term semantic knowledge often survives.

Schmolck, Kensinger, Corkin and Squire set out to investigate the effects of specific brain damage on semantic memory, using case studies of brain-damaged patients compared with a control group of participants without brain damage. Two questions drove the study: what is the relationship between semantic test scores and the site of temporal lobe damage, and whether Henry Molaison (HM) was unique in how his damage affected his memory compared with similar damage in others.

2. Sample and the Thirteen Tests

The tests were built around 48 line drawings: 24 animals and 24 objects, split into eight groups of eight — domestic land animals, non-native land animals, water animals, birds, electrical household items, non-electrical household items, vehicles and musical instruments. Nine tests were conducted over three to five sessions (seven from an existing semantic test battery, two written by the researchers); a further four were given to some patients and controls, and of these HM received only test 10.

TestsWhat participants did
1–4Pointed to or named a picture when given its name or a description
5Answered yes/no questions about the physical and associated features of an object
6–7Category fluency and sorting: generated or sorted as many examples as possible within a category or class (living/non-living) without a picture cue
8–9Defined the 24 less common objects from a name or a picture
10Pyramid and palm tree test: chose which of two test pictures went with a target (a saddle goes with a horse, not a goat)
11Object/non-object discrimination: decided whether the object shown was real
12Coloured 28 line drawings using appropriate colours from four coloured pencils
13Nouns and verbs: a fill-in-the-gaps exercise on regular and irregular verbs and tenses

All tests except 8 and 9 were scored as a percentage of correct responses. The definitions in tests 8 and 9 were recorded, transcribed, and given an accuracy rating from 0 to 4 by the researchers; the transcripts were also checked for errors in grammar, expression, confusion and word intrusions, and inter-rater reliability was established for the scoring.

Back to the swan: HM's answer was one of the definitions in tests 8 and 9. It was not scored right/wrong but transcribed and rated — which is why a long answer can still receive a low score.

中文理解：材料是 48 张线条画（24 只动物、24 件物品，分成八类每类八张）。核心九项测验分三到五次做完，另有四项只给部分人做，HM 只做了第 10 项。评分分两套：其余测验算正确率百分比；第 8、9 项的定义要录音转写，按 0 到 4 分评准确度，同时检查语法错误、表达混乱和词语侵入。记住这个分工：定义题是质性资料，才需要 14 名独立评分者（见 §5）。

Rich in associated detail, thin on the concept itself — and full of the grammatical errors that made HM a special case.

When participants were ranked by overall performance, their rank corresponded directly with the extent of brain damage: damage to the anterolateral temporal cortex seemed to cause the semantic impairment. On tests 10–13, two MTL+ participants performed well on the object decision task but one made eight errors; all MTL+ patients scored well on colouring; MTL+ patients and HM scored either below the required 90 per cent or below the controls on the pyramid and palm tree test; and while MTL+ patients produced regular plurals and verbs but struggled with irregular ones, HM performed well on both, suggesting the difficulty with irregular items is associated with anterolateral temporal cortex damage.

4. Conclusions and the Special Case of HM

The MTL+ data show that damage to the anterolateral temporal cortex is consistent with a loss of semantic knowledge: conceptual knowledge becomes 'blurred', so categories overlap and cause confusion. This knowledge is associated with the anterolateral region, not the medial temporal lobe — a conclusion consistent with patients with semantic dementia（语义性痴呆：语义记忆进行性丧失的神经退行性疾病）, whose impairment is restricted to the anterolateral temporal cortex while the medial temporal lobe is relatively unaffected. MRI scans also suggest that the more progressed the disease, the greater the anterolateral damage. Because HF patients performed like the controls, the researchers concluded that the hippocampus is not involved in semantic knowledge, and because HM was less affected than the MTL+ patients, that the anterolateral temporal cortex rather than the medial temporal lobe is involved.

HM – a special case. He resembled the MTL+ patients on definitions, suggesting a similar physiological basis for that impairment, but in many other respects his semantic knowledge was in the normal range. Only HM produced a large number of grammatical errors. The researchers suggest this language-production deficit was unlikely to be related to his temporal lobe damage, but to other factors in his childhood: a seizure at age 10, low socioeconomic status and interrupted schooling. This is a nurture rather than nature explanation — though whether such errors can really be attributed solely to educational disruption and economic circumstances is itself questionable. The debate can never be resolved in this case, because HM's language was not tested before the surgery.

Back to the swan: the rambling answer is now readable twice over — as weak definitional accuracy (1.8–2.0 versus 3.2 for controls) and as a language-production pattern the researchers would not pin on the lesion. The study separates the two rather than collapsing them into one 'amnesia' label.

中文理解：结论三步：① MTL+ 组概念边界变模糊，定位到前外侧颞叶皮层；② HF 组与对照组无异，说明海马不承担语义知识；③ 语义性痴呆病人和 MRI 结果从旁佐证。HM 特例单独记：定义像 MTL+，其他语义测验却在正常范围；语法错误数量独一份，研究者归因于童年（10 岁癫痫发作、家境、学业中断）而非手术损伤。这条 nature–nurture 之争无法收尾，因为术前没有语言基线数据。

5. Evaluation: A Map Drawn from Six Patients

Strengths

  • Converging evidence. Case studies of brain-damaged patients are rare and therefore small in number, but findings from semantic dementia, neuroimaging, brain stimulation and unilateral lobectomy all support the conclusion that the anterolateral — especially the lateral — temporal cortex is involved in semantic knowledge, strongly reinforcing this study.
  • Objective scoring of qualitative data. The researchers used 14 raters to judge the definitions and gave each a quality score from 0 to 4. Qualitative data is open to subjective analysis, but 14 independent raters made the scoring more objective.
  • A properly matched comparison. The eight controls were matched to the patients on age, sex and education, and the patient groups were defined by damage site with a biography compiled for each, so differences in test scores can be linked to the lesion rather than to obvious demographic differences.
  • HM's case advanced the field. His surgery, however controversial, established that memory is a distinct cognitive process independent of language and thought, and helped neuroscience locate memory in the brain; he became the most researched individual in the field.

Weaknesses

  • Small sample. Six amnesia patients and eight controls limit the generalisability of the conclusions; rare cases make larger samples almost impossible.
  • Retrospective research cannot establish cause. Individual differences may reflect variation in neurology, and the brain can adapt and compensate for injury: test performance may reflect that adaptation rather than the injury itself. Prospective research is not possible, as it would require predicting who will sustain such damage.
  • Low ecological validity. The line drawings common to many cognitive investigations lack mundane realism（日常真实感：实验任务与真实生活情境的相似程度）and may not tap semantic knowledge as it is used in everyday life, so findings may not generalise to everyday use of semantic memory.
  • The HM nurture explanation is untestable. Whether his grammatical errors came from childhood circumstances rather than the lesion cannot be settled because no pre-surgery language measure exists; the attribution itself is questionable.
  • Socially sensitive research. Amnesia affects a person's past, their future and their family's caring role; studying such patients is sensitive for the individual and their family, especially when the deficit appears late in life.

A fair verdict: the localisation claim is stronger than the sample size suggests, because independent lines of evidence converge on the same region — but the causal story about HM's language, and the generalisation to everyday semantic memory, both remain open.

Exam tip: a 2-mark 'explain one strength' answer needs the point plus a linked justification, not a label. 'Fourteen independent raters scored the definitions from 0 to 4' earns the first mark; 'because qualitative definitions are open to subjective interpretation, using many independent raters makes the scoring more objective and the findings more trustworthy' earns the second. Keep the same point-plus-because structure for weaknesses.

Close the loop: back to the three definitions of a swan

甲's clean definition matches the HF pattern: hippocampal damage alone left semantic knowledge essentially intact (definitions 3.1/3.2 against 3.2 for controls). 乙's failure to name or define fits the MTL+ pattern (78.1 per cent on naming, 2.0/1.8 on definitions), where damage has spread to the anterolateral temporal cortex and conceptual boundaries blur. 丙's long answer is HM: accurate on many other semantic tests, weakest on definitions (1.8/2.0), and marked by grammatical errors the researchers traced to his childhood rather than his surgery. The three answers are not three degrees of one deficit — they are three different lesion sites plus one unresolved nature–nurture question, which is exactly why the study needed matched groups, thirteen tests and fourteen raters to read them.

Reflect before the Exam Lab: if HM's language had been tested before his surgery, which single conclusion in this study would change the most, and why?

6. Exam Lab

Q1 · Describe one aim 2 marks

Describe one aim of the study by Schmolck et al. (2002).

One aim was to investigate the relationship between semantic test scores and the site of temporal lobe damage, using case studies of brain-damaged patients compared with a control group of participants without brain damage. Specifically, the researchers wanted to know whether HM was unique in the way his brain damage affected his memory compared with other patients with similar damage.

Marking guidance: 2 marks for a detailed description — 1 for the general aim (relating semantic memory performance to specific brain damage), 1 for the specific comparison or the HM question. A bare 'to study memory' is not enough.

Q2 · Explain one strength 2 marks

Explain one strength of the study by Schmolck et al. (2002).

One strength is the objectivity of the scoring for the qualitative definition data. The researchers used 14 independent raters to identify the definitions participants gave and to award a quality score from 0 to 4. Because qualitative data is open to subjective interpretation, using many independent raters reduces the influence of any one rater's judgement, making the definition scores more objective and the conclusions drawn from them more trustworthy.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for identifying the strength (14 independent raters / converging evidence from semantic dementia, neuroimaging, brain stimulation and unilateral lobectomy / matched controls), 1 for explaining why it improves the study. 'It used a control group' alone stays at 1 mark.

Q3 · Explain one weakness 2 marks

Explain one weakness of the study by Schmolck et al. (2002).

One weakness is that the study is retrospective, so it cannot establish a causal relationship between the injury sustained and the impairments measured. Only six amnesia patients were tested, and the brain is adaptable and can compensate for injury, so the test results may reflect the brain's adaptation rather than the damage itself. Prospective research would be needed to establish cause, but it is not possible because researchers cannot predict which individuals will sustain such brain damage. This limits the confidence with which the anterolateral temporal cortex can be claimed as the cause of the semantic deficits.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for a valid weakness (small sample; retrospective/causal limits; line drawings lacking mundane realism and ecological validity; untestable nurture explanation for HM; social sensitivity), 1 for explaining its effect on the validity or generalisability of the conclusions.
