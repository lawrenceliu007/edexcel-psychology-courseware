2 · The 7 Chapters — Quick Checklist

One chapter = one card. Each card lists what you must be able to write down, plus one self-check question you should answer out loud before ticking the box.

Use this section as your last-mile revision sheet（考前一遍过的清单）. Tick a card only when you can answer the self-check from memory.

Ch20

Definitions and Debates in Diagnosis（诊断的定义与辩论）

  • Four definitions of abnormality（异常的四种定义）: statistical infrequency（统计稀有性）, failure to function adequately（无法正常生活）, deviation from ideal mental health（偏离理想心理健康）, deviation from social norms（偏离社会规范）. Know one strength + one limitation of each.
  • Statistical infrequency: 2 standard deviations from the mean（离均值两个标准差）; limitation — some rare behaviours are desirable (high IQ).
  • Failure to function: Rosenhan & Seligman (1989) seven criteria of dysfunctional behaviour（功能失调行为的七条标准）.
  • Classification systems（分类系统）: ICD (WHO) vs DSM (APA). DSM-IV used a multi-axial system（多轴系统）; DSM-5 dropped the axes.
  • Culture-specific disorders（文化特定障碍）: some diagnostic categories do not travel across cultures.
  • Reliability vs validity of diagnosis: criterion validity（效标效度） and aetiological validity（病因效度）.
  • Medical model（医学模型） vs alternative approaches — be ready to argue both sides.
Name the four definitions of abnormality and give one limitation of statistical infrequency.
Ch21

Schizophrenia（精神分裂症）

  • Positive symptoms（阳性症状） = experiences added to normal life: delusions（妄想 — grandiose / persecutory / referential）, thought insertion / withdrawal / broadcasting（思维插入/被夺/广播）, hallucinations（幻觉 — auditory, visual）, disorganised thinking, speech, behaviour and catatonia（紧张症）.
  • Negative symptoms（阴性症状） = normal functions lost or reduced.
  • Features: lifetime prevalence 0.3–0.7% (APA, 2013); onset early adulthood to mid-thirties; men diagnosed earlier than women; earlier onset = worse prognosis.
  • Biological explanations: dopamine hypothesis（多巴胺假说） + genetics（遗传）. Be ready to evaluate both, including reductionism vs holism（还原论 vs 整体论）.
  • Drug therapy: typical（第一代） vs atypical（第二代） antipsychotics — mechanism + evaluation.
  • Family therapy（家庭治疗） as a psychological therapy.
  • Treatment–aetiology fallacy（治疗–病因谬误）: a treatment working does NOT prove the cause.
Distinguish positive from negative symptoms with one example of each, and state the dopamine hypothesis in one sentence.
Ch22

Unipolar Depression（单相抑郁）

  • DSM-5 symptoms: at least five, including low mood or loss of interest, for two weeks; must cause significant distress or impairment.
  • Biological explanation: monoamine hypothesis（单胺假说） — low serotonin / noradrenaline activity; supported by drug action, challenged by the delay in drug effect.
  • Drug therapy: antidepressants (SSRIs, MAOIs, tricyclics) + evaluation (side effects, placebo effects, treats symptoms not causes).
  • Cognitive explanation: Beck's cognitive triad（贝克的认知三联征） — negative views of the self, the world and the future.
  • CBT（认知行为疗法）: cognitive restructuring + behavioural activation; evaluation points include efficacy vs effectiveness and relapse prevention.
Draw Beck's cognitive triad and explain one strength and one weakness of the monoamine hypothesis.
Ch23

Anorexia Nervosa（神经性厌食症）

  • Symptoms & features: restricted food intake leading to significantly low body weight (BMI < 18.5 in adults / below the 5th percentile for age) + intense fear of gaining weight + distorted body image.
  • Two subtypes（两种亚型）: restricting（限制型） vs binge–purge（暴食–清除型）.
  • Onset most common in adolescence; relapse within 18 months is common; cultural differences in prevalence — a caveat for generalisability.
  • Biological explanation: role of genes（基因的作用） — twin studies, linkage studies, GWAS（全基因组关联研究） and polygenic findings.
  • Non-biological explanation: social learning theory（社会学习理论） — role models, vicarious reinforcement（替代强化）, media and social media.
  • Treatments: drug therapy + CBT-E（增强型认知行为疗法） — know one strength and one limitation of each.
Explain how social learning theory accounts for anorexia, and name one piece of biological evidence that challenges it.
Ch24

Studies in Clinical Psychology（临床心理学研究）

  • One classic study: Rosenhan (1973) "On Being Sane in Insane Places" — 8 pseudopatients（假病人）, 12 hospitals, average 19 days — diagnosis is unreliable.
  • Schizophrenia: Suzuki et al. (2014) — the contemporary study for this disorder.
  • Depression: choose ONE of Hans & Hiller (2013) or Ma, Quan & Liu (2014).
  • Anorexia: choose ONE of Becker et al. (2002) or Reichel et al. (2014).
  • For every study: Aim, Method, Sample, Procedure, Results, Conclusion — plus 3 evaluation points (validity, generalisability, ethics).
  • Concept: lived experience（亲历经验） — why first-person accounts matter in clinical psychology.
Which studies must you prepare for each disorder, and how many do you choose yourself?
Ch25

Methods in Clinical Psychology（临床心理学研究方法）

  • RCTs（随机对照试验）: randomisation, placebos（安慰剂）, double-blind（双盲） — the gold standard for testing treatments.
  • Neuroimaging（神经影像）: structural MRI vs functional fMRI; what each measures; evaluation of scanning research.
  • Conventions of published research（研究报告规范）: the five-section structure — abstract, introduction, method, results, discussion.
  • Peer review（同行评审）: the process and its weaknesses.
  • HCPC guidelines（临床从业者准则） for clinical practitioners.
  • Decision making and interpretation of data; reliability（信度）, validity（效度）, generalisability（泛化性）, credibility, objectivity and subjectivity（可信度、客观性与主观性）.
What makes an RCT the gold standard, and what is the difference between MRI and fMRI?
Ch26

Clinical Practical: Content Analysis（临床实操：内容分析）

  • What content analysis（内容分析） is, and why it suits attitudes-to-mental-health research.
  • Design decisions: research question, gathering sources, secondary data（二手数据） and source credibility.
  • Ethical considerations: pseudonyms（化名）, managing harmful or distressing material.
  • Manifest vs latent content（显性内容 vs 隐性内容） — the coding distinction.
  • Presenting results: nominal data（称名数据）, percentages, and inter-rater reliability（评分者间信度）.
  • Report writing: procedure, results, discussion + strengths, weaknesses and improvements of your investigation.
Distinguish manifest from latent content with one example from an online comment.

3 · Key Terms Glossary（核心术语小词典）

A condensed vocabulary list — every term you should be able to define without looking it up.

Abnormality 异常
Statistically rare, dysfunctional or norm-violating psychological state — definitions differ and each has weaknesses.
Aetiological validity 病因效度
A diagnosis is valid if all people with it share the same cause.
Anorexia nervosa 神经性厌食症
Restricted intake, significantly low weight, fear of gaining weight, distorted body image.
Atypical antipsychotics 非典型抗精神病药
Second-generation drugs (e.g. clozapine); act on dopamine and serotonin; fewer motor side effects.
Beck's cognitive triad 认知三联征
Negative views of self, world and future proposed to maintain depression.
CBT 认知行为疗法
Cognitive restructuring + behavioural activation; first-line psychological treatment for depression.
CBT-E 增强型 CBT
Enhanced CBT specially adapted for eating disorders.
Content analysis 内容分析
Systematic coding of qualitative material (e.g. comments) into categories for quantitative analysis.
Criterion validity 效标效度
A diagnosis is valid if people with it genuinely differ from those without on related measures.
Culture-specific disorder 文化特定障碍
A diagnostic category found mainly in one culture — evidence diagnosis is not universal.
Delusion 妄想
Fixed false belief — grandiose, persecutory or referential in schizophrenia.
Deviation from ideal mental health 偏离理想心理健康
Abnormality as falling short of positive mental-health criteria.
Deviation from social norms 偏离社会规范
Abnormality as violation of accepted standards of behaviour; culturally relative.
Dopamine hypothesis 多巴胺假说
Overactive dopamine transmission underlies positive symptoms of schizophrenia.
Double-blind 双盲
Neither participant nor researcher knows the condition — controls expectancy effects.
DSM 精神障碍诊断与统计手册
APA classification system; DSM-5 dropped the DSM-IV multi-axial system.
Efficacy 效力
How well a treatment works in ideal controlled conditions (RCTs).
Effectiveness 效果
How well a treatment works in routine clinical practice with real, complex patients.
Failure to function adequately 无法正常生活
Abnormality judged by Rosenhan & Seligman's seven criteria of dysfunctional behaviour.
Family therapy 家庭治疗
Works with the family system to reduce expressed emotion and relapse in schizophrenia.
fMRI 功能性磁共振
Functional brain scanning; BOLD signal as a proxy for neural activity.
GWAS 全基因组关联研究
Scans millions of genetic markers for associations with a disorder; supports polygenic view of anorexia.
Hallucination 幻觉
Perceptual experience without external stimulus; auditory is most common in schizophrenia.
HCPC 英国健康与照护专业委员会
Regulator publishing standards of conduct, performance and ethics for clinical practitioners.
ICD 国际疾病分类
WHO classification system covering all diseases, including mental disorders.
Inter-rater reliability 评分者间信度
Two coders reaching the same judgements; the quality test of content analysis.
Latent content 隐性内容
Underlying meaning of a message — what it implies, not what it literally says.
Lived experience 亲历经验
First-person accounts of mental disorder; challenges the outsider view of diagnosis.
Manifest content 显性内容
The surface, directly observable content of a message.
Medical model 医学模型
Views mental disorder as illness with symptoms, diagnosis and treatment; rival approaches stress social and personal factors.
Monoamine hypothesis 单胺假说
Depression reflects low monoamine (serotonin / noradrenaline) activity.
Negative symptoms 阴性症状
Loss or reduction of normal functions in schizophrenia.
Nominal data 称名数据
Category counts — the data type produced by content analysis.
Peer review 同行评审
Expert scrutiny of research before publication; guards quality but is fallible.
Placebo 安慰剂
Inert condition controlling for expectancy; central to RCT design.
Polygenic 多基因
Influence of many genes of small effect — the genetic architecture of anorexia.
Positive symptoms 阳性症状
Experiences added to normal functioning — delusions, hallucinations, disorganised thinking.
Prognosis 预后
Expected course of a disorder; earlier onset of schizophrenia predicts worse outcome.
RCT 随机对照试验
Random allocation + control condition; the gold-standard treatment design.
Reliability of diagnosis 诊断信度
Same clinician / different clinicians reaching the same diagnosis for the same person.
SSRI 选择性血清素再摄取抑制剂
Antidepressant blocking serotonin reuptake; the most prescribed class.
Statistical infrequency 统计稀有性
Abnormality as more than 2 SD from the mean; fails for desirable rare traits.
Treatment–aetiology fallacy 治疗–病因谬误
Assuming that because a drug reduces symptoms, the disorder was caused by that neurotransmitter system.
Typical antipsychotics 典型抗精神病药
First-generation dopamine blockers; effective but with motor side effects.
Vicarious reinforcement 替代强化
Learning from seeing others rewarded — key SLT mechanism in anorexia explanations.

4 · Must-Know Studies（必记研究）

One classic + five contemporary studies. Each card has the AMSPRC skeleton: Aim · Method · Sample · Procedure · Results · Conclusion.

Classic

Rosenhan (1973) — On Being Sane in Insane Places

Covert participant observation · 8 pseudopatients · 12 US hospitals · average 19 days

  • Aim: test whether psychiatrists can tell the sane from the insane in hospital settings.
  • Method: sane volunteers presented themselves complaining of hearing voices; once admitted, behaved normally and took notes.
  • Results: all but one admitted with schizophrenia; release took on average 19 days; notes were recorded as "patient engages in writing behaviour" (symptoms were "in remission", never "absent").
  • Conclusion: psychiatric diagnosis is unreliable and labels distort staff perception of normal behaviour.
  • Critique: only one symptom presented (voices) — psychiatrists may be warier with other disorders; 1970s USA — ethnocentric and dated; ethical issues (deception of hospitals).
Schizophrenia

Suzuki et al. (2014) — Underweight and Undernutrition in Japanese Inpatients with Schizophrenia

Cross-sectional study · Japanese inpatients vs healthy controls · BMI + blood biomarkers

  • Aim: assess the prevalence of underweight and undernutrition in schizophrenia inpatients.
  • Method: measured BMI and blood chemistry (nutritional markers) of inpatients with schizophrenia and controls.
  • Results: being underweight was significantly more common in patients than controls (p < 0.001) across all three BMI categories; blood markers showed undernutrition.
  • Conclusion: underweight and undernutrition are highly prevalent among Japanese inpatients with schizophrenia.
  • Critique: cross-sectional design — cannot say whether low weight is cause, consequence, or a side effect of antipsychotic medication; all patients were on medication (confound); inpatient sample limits generalisability.
Depression

Hans & Hiller (2013) — Effectiveness of Outpatient CBT for Adult Unipolar Depression

Meta-analysis · non-randomised effectiveness studies · outpatients

  • Aim: assess how well CBT works in routine outpatient practice, not just in RCTs.
  • Method: meta-analysis of non-randomised effectiveness studies of outpatient CBT for adult unipolar depression; also analysed drop-out（脱落率）.
  • Results: CBT showed substantial effectiveness in routine care; comparable to RCT efficacy benchmarks; drop-out was low to moderate.
  • Conclusion: CBT is not only efficacious in the lab but effective in the real clinic.
  • Critique: non-randomised designs — selection bias; heterogeneity between clinics and therapists; meta-analysis quality depends on the primary studies.
Depression

Ma, Quan & Liu (2014) — Core Self-Evaluation, Social Support and Depression in Chinese Students

Correlational study · N = 538 Chinese university students · online questionnaires

  • Aim: test how core self-evaluation and social support relate to depression, and whether support mediates the link.
  • Method: three scales — CSES (core self-evaluation 核心自我评价) + MSPSS (perceived social support 感知社会支持) + Zung SDS (depression 抑郁).
  • Results: core self-evaluation negatively correlated with depression (r = −0.636, strong negative); social support acted as a partial mediator（部分中介）, indirect effect −0.18.
  • Conclusion: positive self-evaluators tend to gain more social support, which in turn protects against depression.
  • Critique: correlational — no causal claims; self-report bias; students only — generalisability limited; single time point.
Anorexia

Becker et al. (2002) — Eating Behaviours and Attitudes after Prolonged TV Exposure, Fiji

Natural experiment · ethnic Fijian adolescent girls · before-and-after TV introduction

  • Aim: test whether prolonged exposure to western television changes eating attitudes and behaviours in a previously media-naive population.
  • Method: interviews and measures of eating attitudes with Fijian girls, comparing responses before and after television reached the area.
  • Results: after TV exposure, girls showed more disordered eating attitudes and behaviours, self-induced vomiting and body-image concern; effects strongest in girls who identified with TV role models.
  • Conclusion: media transmission of the thin ideal can change eating attitudes in a non-western culture — supports social learning explanations.
  • Critique: no control group in the classic sense; other cultural changes may coincide with TV arrival; self-report measures.
Anorexia

Reichel et al. (2014) — "Glass Fairies" and "Bone Children": Startle Reflex in Anorexia

Laboratory experiment · eye-blink startle reflex · EMG · emaciation vs neutral vs pleasant images

  • Aim: test whether patients with anorexia respond differently, at reflex level, to images of extreme thinness.
  • Method: measured the eye-blink startle reflex（惊跳反射） via EMG magnitude and latency while patients and non-patients viewed emaciation (thin body), neutral, and pleasant images; pre-set rules for rejecting trials (e.g. latency < 20 ms) removed researcher bias.
  • Results: startle was greater for unpleasant than neutral images and greater for neutral than pleasant — true for both groups; for patients, the startle response towards emaciation images was less extreme than that created by unpleasant non-body images.
  • Conclusion: patients with anorexia show a distinctive automatic affective response to thin-body images — consistent with a positive attitude to emaciation.
  • Critique: small samples (two non-responders excluded); lab setting — low ecological validity; reflex is an indirect measure of attitude.

The Chapter 20 core: how abnormality is defined and classified, in four tables you should be able to reproduce.

Four definitions of abnormality

DefinitionCore ideaLimitation to quote

ICD vs DSM

ICDDSM
PublisherWorld Health Organization (WHO)American Psychiatric Association (APA)

Is diagnosis any good?

Reliability of diagnosis（诊断信度） = would different clinicians reach the same label? Validity of diagnosis（诊断效度） = does the label carve nature at its joints?

Criterion validity: people with the diagnosis should differ from people without it on related measures. Aetiological validity: people with the diagnosis should share the same cause.

Culture-specific disorders show that diagnostic categories do not always travel across cultures — a standing challenge to validity.

The debate you must be able to run

Medical model（医学模型）: disorder = illness with symptoms, diagnosis and treatment — strengths: enables research, treatment, insurance. Alternatives: diagnosis ignores social and personal context, labels can stick (Rosenhan), and lived experience（亲历经验） matters.

6 · Explanations & Treatments Quick Card（解释与治疗速查）

Three disorders × biological vs psychological, with the matching treatments — the master grid of Chapters 21–23.

The master grid

DisorderBiological explanationPsychological explanationDrug treatmentPsychological treatment
SchizophreniaDopamine hypothesis; genetics—Typical vs atypical antipsychoticsFamily therapy
Unipolar depressionMonoamine hypothesisBeck's cognitive triadSSRIs / MAOIs / tricyclicsCBT
Anorexia nervosaRole of genes (twin / linkage / GWAS / polygenic)Social learning theoryDrug therapyCBT-E

Evaluation hooks — always pair treatment with explanation

  • Dopamine hypothesis: supported by antipsychotic action and amphetamine psychosis; challenged by the treatment–aetiology fallacy（治疗–病因谬误） — a drug reducing symptoms does not prove the cause.
  • Genetics of schizophrenia and anorexia: both run in families, but concordance is never 100% — genes set risk, not destiny (diathesis–stress thinking).
  • Monoamine hypothesis: drugs raise monoamine levels within hours but mood lifts weeks later — a serious timing problem.
  • Beck's triad vs monoamine: nature–nurture（天性–教养） and reductionism vs holism（还原论 vs 整体论） essays can be run on this contrast.
  • SLT for anorexia: Becker et al. (2002) Fiji evidence — media exposure changed attitudes in a previously media-naive culture.
  • Drug vs psychological therapy: drugs act on symptoms and are cheap to deliver; CBT / CBT-E target the mechanism and protect against relapse — but depend on therapist skill and patient engagement.

7 · Common Exam Traps（常见考点陷阱）

Six mistakes that trip up otherwise-prepared students. Read once, then never make them.

Efficacy ≠ effectiveness

Efficacy = ideal RCT conditions; effectiveness = routine practice. Hans & Hiller (2013) studied effectiveness.

Fix: use the two words precisely — they carry different marks.
Treatment–aetiology fallacy

Antipsychotics dampen dopamine and reduce symptoms — this does NOT prove schizophrenia is caused by dopamine.

Fix: name the fallacy explicitly when evaluating the dopamine hypothesis.
Correlation ≠ causation

Ma et al. (2014) found r = −0.636 — a strong negative correlation, not a causal pathway.

Fix: describe the mediation model as "consistent with", never "proves".
Positive vs negative symptoms

Positive = experiences ADDED (delusions, hallucinations). Negative = functions LOST (motivation, affect).

Fix: "positive" has nothing to do with "good" or "severe".
Statistical infrequency cuts both ways

2 SD below the mean is abnormal — but 2 SD ABOVE includes high IQ. The definition alone never suffices.

Fix: always pair the definition with its limitation.
ICD vs DSM — cite the right system

ICD (WHO) covers all diseases; DSM (APA) is mental-disorder only. DSM-5 dropped DSM-IV's multi-axial system.

Fix: don't write "DSM-11" or "ICD-5" — the two systems number differently.
