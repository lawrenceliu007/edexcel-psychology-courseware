2.2.1–2.2.4 DESIGNING EXPERIMENTS
Experiments · IV & DV · Hypotheses · 实验设计与假设

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Methods Spec 2.2.1–2.2.4 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.11 · pp.101–111

What this page is really asking

  1. An experiment is defined by three moves, not by white coats or labs: manipulate one variable, measure another, control everything else. Only this combination earns a causal claim.
  2. Where you run it decides what you trade. A laboratory buys control and reliability but loses realism; a field setting buys realism and natural behaviour but loses control and raises consent problems.
  3. The IV is what you change; the DV is what you count. Find them by asking "what did the researcher do?" and "what did the researcher record?"
  4. Hypotheses come in three strengths. Directional when evidence points one way, non-directional when it conflicts, and a null hypothesis that attributes any difference to chance.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Identify what makes a study an experiment by applying the manipulate–measure–control test to any research description.
  • Distinguish laboratory and field experiments and evaluate each with the control-versus-realism trade-off, including ethics in field settings.
  • Write an aim and identify the IV and DV for a given research scenario.
  • Construct directional, non-directional and null hypotheses for a scenario and justify which type the evidence supports.
  • Justify design choices by mapping a real argument (like the Start Here scenario) onto variables and conditions.

先猜要让班主任的质疑站不住脚，下面哪种做法最有说服力？

1. What Makes a Study an Experiment

Memory cannot be watched directly, so cognitive psychologists measure it through experiments（实验：研究者主动改动一件事、再测量结果的研究）. An experiment is an investigation in which the researcher manipulates or alters one variable and measures its effect on another, while maintaining control over everything else that might interfere. Three moves define it:

  • Manipulate one thing: deliberately change it, creating two or more conditions.
  • Measure another: record the outcome expected to change as a result.
  • Control the rest: hold every other factor still, so the only systematic difference between conditions is the thing you changed.

Only the researcher-controlled change licenses a causal（因果：改动是原因、变化是结果的确定关系）claim: "this caused that". Studies that merely measure things that already differ, however carefully, can only describe.

The experimental method (Figure 11.1): a theory generates a prediction, the prediction is tested by manipulating the IV and measuring the DV, and the outcome feeds back to support or refute the theory.

Every experiment starts from an aim（研究目的：说明研究什么领域的一句话）: a general statement of the area being researched, usually beginning with "To investigate…". It directs the overall ambition of the study and should be as clear and precise as a hypothesis.

中文理解：实验三步口诀「改一个、量一个、其余按住不动」。改的那个叫 IV，量的那个叫 DV，其他一切变量都要控制住。三步凑齐才敢说因果：如果没改任何东西，或者别的因素没按住，那么测到的变化就不能算在 IV 头上。开头情景里班主任的质疑，就是在攻击「其余按住不动」这一步。

2. Laboratory vs Field Experiments

The same three-move logic can run in two very different places, and the choice of place decides what you gain and what you pay.

Laboratory experiment（实验室实验：在受控环境里做）Field experiment（现场实验：在行为自然发生的环境里做）
SettingAn artificial, controlled environment built for the study.The setting where the behaviour would naturally occur: a classroom, a supermarket, a high street.
GainsTaking participants away from their natural environment removes extraneous variables; tight control over relevant variables achieves a scientific approach and ensures causality. High control also means the study can be repeated to check the findings, so it is highly reliable.Behaviour is natural and the environment realistic, so the study has greater ecological validity（生态效度：结果能否推广到真实日常情境）. If participants are unaware they are taking part, they will not show demand characteristics.
CostsArtificial setting and artificial tasks mean the findings may not apply to real life. Participants are often invited, so they know they are being studied and may change their behaviour; the researcher's presence can also influence performance.Less control: extraneous variables have a greater chance of affecting the DV. Ethical problems arise if participants are unaware they are taking part: they have not given consent and cannot withdraw. The experimenter may debrief them afterwards and offer the right to withdraw their data.

Notice that the two columns are the same trade seen from opposite ends: the control that makes laboratory findings causal is the very thing that makes them unlike everyday life, and the realism that makes field findings convincing is the very thing that makes them messy to control.

中文理解：实验室和现场是一笔交易的两端。实验室花钱买控制：环境、流程、变量全按住，因果说得硬，可重复性好；代价是任务不真实，学生知道自己在被研究。现场花钱买真实：行为自然、没人觉得自己在被观察；代价是杂七杂八的干扰因素按不住，而且不知情参加还有伦理麻烦：没有事先同意、不能随时退出，只能事后做 debrief（向参与者说明研究并允许撤回数据）来补救。答评价题时写交易，不要只贴标签。

3. Independent and Dependent Variables

Every experiment has two named variables. The independent variable（IV，自变量：研究者主动改动、制造不同条件的变量）is manipulated by the researcher to demonstrate a difference between the experimental conditions. The dependent variable（DV，因变量：被测量、随 IV 变化而变化的变量）is the measured outcome: it records any changes that occur because of the IV.

Worked example: "A researcher wished to investigate whether participants will recall more words from an organised list compared to a random list."

  • The researcher changes which list participants learn from: one set gets randomised words, one set gets organised words. The type of word list is the IV.
  • The researcher records how many words each participant recalls correctly. The number of words correctly recalled is the DV.

Back to the assembly scenario: the option you picked in Start Here was exactly this move. Group A has the videos removed for two weeks, Group B keeps watching: what the researcher changes is video exposure, so video exposure is the IV. What the researcher records is end-of-term test scores, so scores are the DV. Matching the groups on ability beforehand is the "control the rest" move that answers the head of year's objection.

Once the IV and DV are chosen, both must be made precise and specific so they are directly testable. How exactly that is done is the next page's job; for now, remember that a vague DV cannot be scored the same way by two different researchers.

中文理解：找 IV 的办法是问「研究者动了什么」；找 DV 的办法是问「研究者数的是什么」。两个词出自同一个句子：动「词表类型」，量「回忆词数」。IV 在前、DV 在后，顺序不能颠倒：因为变化的是 DV，它才要「依赖」IV；IV 自己不依赖任何人，所以叫自变量。

4. Hypotheses: Predicting the Result

An experiment begins with a prediction of what is likely to happen, based on previous knowledge, research or theory. This prediction is the experimental/alternative hypothesis（实验假设／备择假设：对研究结果清晰明确的预测）: a clear and precise statement predicting the results of the study.

How bold you can be depends on the evidence behind the prediction:

  • Directional (one-tailed)（方向性假设／单尾：预测结果朝哪个方向走）: used when the outcome can be predicted with confidence, because strong evidence or a robust theory points one way. Cats will purr for longer when they are fed tinned food compared to dry food.
  • Non-directional (two-tailed)（非方向性假设／双尾：预测有差异，但不预测方向）: used when theories conflict or relevant evidence is lacking, so a difference is predicted but not its direction. There will be a difference in the length of time a cat purrs when given tinned and dry food.
  • Null hypothesis（虚无假设：预测没有差异，任何差异都归因于偶然因素）: the default prediction that there is no difference, or that any difference found is due to chance factors. There will be no difference in the length of time cats spend purring when fed tinned or dry food. Any difference found will be due to chance factors.

The null hypothesis exists because results are never truly "proved". We almost always find some difference between conditions; the real question is whether the difference is big enough to be more than chance variation. If praise really affected how long children spend tidying their toys, we would still expect some small change from chance alone, so the null hypothesis says: any effect found is due to chance. Chapter 2.2.12 shows how inferential tests turn that sentence into a decision.

Close the loop: settle the assembly argument

The head of year's objection was "the weak students were weak first". A bigger dataset (option A) cannot remove that objection, because the groups still differ in ways nobody controlled. Option B is an experiment: it manipulates video exposure (the IV) across two matched groups, controls the rest by matching ability, and measures the outcome (test scores, the DV). Only this design can license the causal sentence "short videos lowered the marks". Its experimental hypothesis, if prior evidence pointed one way, would be directional: students who stop watching short videos for two weeks will score higher on their end-of-term tests than students who keep watching.

Reflect before the Checkpoint: pick any study you have already met in this topic, for example Peterson and Peterson's trigram recall. Say out loud what was manipulated, what was measured, and what was held still. If you can fill all three slots, you are reading experiments correctly.

6. Exam Lab

Q1a · Explain 2 marks

Zikri investigated working memory and set up an experiment to test the difference between participants performing a single cognitive task and a dual task. Zikri recruited ten friends to perform the single task, and ten classmates to perform the dual task, which involved the same cognitive task while also learning a list of words. Zikri timed how long it took each group to perform the tasks. Explain how one participant variable could have affected Zikri's investigation.

The two groups were different people: his friends in one condition and his classmates in the other. Any natural difference between them, other than the task, could have affected the timed performance. For example, the friends may have been more motivated, or more practised at that kind of cognitive task, so they completed the single task faster for reasons that had nothing to do with single versus dual processing. Because the groups were made of different people, Zikri cannot tell whether the timing difference came from the task or from who was in each group.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for identifying a participant variable (for example motivation, ability, practice), 1 mark for explaining how it distorted the comparison between the two groups.

Q1b · Describe 2 marks

Zikri wanted to increase the ecological validity of the study and decided to conduct a field experiment to test dual processing in memory. Describe a task that Zikri could use to test dual processing in memory in this field experiment.

The task must keep the IV manipulated but move it into a natural setting where memory is used in everyday life. For example, Zikri could have participants walk a real route while either holding a conversation (dual task) or not talking (single task), and afterwards measure how many landmarks from the route they recall. The setting is natural (a real street or campus), the IV is still set by the researcher, and recall of the route is the measured DV.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for a realistic everyday setting with a dual-task element, 1 mark for a measurable memory outcome. A task that stays in a laboratory earns neither mark.

Q1c · Explain two weaknesses 4 marks

Explain two weaknesses of Zikri using a field experiment to investigate short-term memory.

Weakness 1: control. In a natural setting the researcher cannot hold extraneous variables constant. Traffic, noise, weather, other pedestrians and time of day all vary between participants, so any difference in recall could come from these situational factors instead of the dual task, and the causal claim weakens.

Weakness 2: ethics. Passers-by do not know they are taking part, so they have not given consent and cannot exercise the right to withdraw during the study. Zikri would need to debrief participants afterwards and offer them the right to withdraw their data, but consent obtained after the fact is still weaker than consent obtained before.

Marking guidance: up to 2 marks per weakness: 1 for naming it (control / ethics), 1 for explaining the consequence for this study. Generic answers with no link to the field setting score little.

7. Key Terms

experiment
实验：改动一个变量、测量其效果、控制其余变量，三步齐备才能说因果
aim
研究目的：以 To investigate… 开头的领域说明，指明研究的方向
causality
因果：改动变量与测量结果之间确定的 cause-and-effect 关系
laboratory experiment
实验室实验：在受控环境中进行，控制强、生态效度弱
field experiment
现场实验：在行为自然发生的环境中进行，真实性强、控制弱
ecological validity
生态效度：结果能否推广到日常真实情境
independent variable (IV)
自变量：研究者主动改动、制造条件差异的变量
dependent variable (DV)
因变量：被测量的结果，记录 IV 引起的变化
experimental / alternative hypothesis
实验假设／备择假设：基于理论或已有研究做出的明确结果预测
directional (one-tailed)
方向性假设／单尾：证据充分时预测差异的方向
non-directional (two-tailed)
非方向性假设／双尾：证据冲突或不足时只预测有差异、不预测方向
null hypothesis
虚无假设：预测无差异，或任何差异都由偶然因素造成
