Eyewitness memory is honest but not always accurate — four factors damage it at two moments in time:
① post-event information 在事后改写记忆：讨论、提问、媒体报道混进原始记忆。
② stress and trauma、③ weapon focus、④ other-race effect 都在看的时候动手：压力太大、注意力被武器吸走、外族面孔编码不准——信息从一开始就没存好。

The one-sentence distinction 一句话分清
诚实 ≠ 准确。 A witness never lies; the memory itself gets damaged. This whole topic is about the four ways it gets damaged — not about lying witnesses.

Where post-event information comes from 三个来源
  1. Talking to other witnesses — discussions before the police interview.
  2. Being questioned by police or lawyers — leading questions, sometimes unintentional, sometimes used deliberately in court.
  3. Watching — especially if the case is high-profile.media coverage 媒体报道

It matters because witnesses are interviewed over a period of time and a case can take a long time before it reaches court — what fills that gap changes what the witness remembers.

The mechanism: reconstructive memory 机制：重构记忆
How memory actually works 记忆的真实工作方式
We do not replay memories like video. Reconstructive memory（重构记忆） means we the scene using the main points and our interpretation, influenced by our cultural norms and expectations.rebuild 重建 Because the memory is already a reconstruction, new details from after the event right in.slot 无缝插入
The name of the outcome 结果的名字
misinformation effect（错误信息效应） — memory becomes because of exposure to post-event information.less accurate 变得不准确 Pioneered by Elizabeth Loftus since the 1970s.
类比：记忆不像硬盘里的录像文件，更像一篇每次重讲都会改写的作文。朋友说"那人戴了帽子吧？"——下次回忆时"帽子"就真的出现在你的画面里了。不是撒谎，是作文被补写了一笔。

p.17 · Q1 — stress and reliability (2 marks)

Stress increases arousal; according to the Yerkes–Dodson Law, once arousal passes the optimum level, performance — including recall of an event — declines. Stress also narrows attention (Easterbrook, 1959): the witness encodes only central details and misses peripheral information, so testimony is incomplete even when it feels vivid.

p.17 · Q2 — weapon focus (1 mark)

Weapon focus is an eyewitness's concentration on a weapon to the exclusion of other details of a crime.

p.17 · Q3 — post-event information (1 mark)

Post-event information is any information after the witnessed event which may influence the original memory of the incident.

p.17 · Q4 — why weapon focus matters (2 marks)

The presence of a weapon narrows the witness's attention onto the weapon itself, so other important details — especially the perpetrator's face — are never encoded. Loftus et al. (1987) found only 11% of weapon-viewers identified the customer, versus 39% with a chequebook.

p.17 · Q5 — ethical issue (2 marks)

Research may involve exposing participants to a traumatic event or distressing film footage, risking psychological harm; questioning real witnesses forces them to relive the incident. Researchers must weigh the value of the findings against protection from harm.

p.18 · Q6/Q7 — evaluate the reliability of EWT (8 marks)

¶1 Importance. EWT is the most common form of evidence in court and juries find it convincing — yet incorrect eyewitness accounts are the leading cause of miscarriages of justice.
¶2 Unreliable. Memory is reconstructive, so post-event information alters it: Gabbert et al. (2003) — 71% of discussing witnesses recalled items not in their own video. Stress narrows encoding: Morgan III et al. (2007) — 1 in 3 misidentified their interrogator; Loftus et al. (1987) — 11% vs 39% identification. Meissner and Brigham (2001): own-race faces 1.40× more likely to be correctly identified.
¶3 Reliable. Real witnesses resist the lab predictions: Yuille and Cutshall (1986) — not misled; Thompson et al. (1997) — 74 of 86 statements accurate; Odinot (2008) — 84% accurate; Pickel et al. (2006) — training overcame weapon focus. Lab studies, lacking genuine stress, may overstate unreliability.
¶4 Conclusion. EWT is conditionally reliable: stress, weapons, post-event information and the other-race effect degrade it in controlled research, but real, close involvement often produces accurate memory. Courts should weigh the conditions — and treat confidence as separate from accuracy.
