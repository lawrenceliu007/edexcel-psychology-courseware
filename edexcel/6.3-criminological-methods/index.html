6.3 METHODS
Researching criminological psychology · 犯罪心理学的研究方法

Topic F · Criminological psychology Methods Spec 6.3.1–6.3.4

What this chapter is really asking

  1. Why the lab? Testing real witnesses is illegal and real jurors cannot be studied, so crime research has to be simulated: films for witnesses（目击者）, mock juries for jurors.
  2. Three methods, one trade-off. Lab experiments buy control and reliability at the cost of realism; field experiments buy realism at the cost of control; mock juries buy access to verdicts at the cost of a real juror experience.
  3. Evaluation has a fixed vocabulary: reliability, validity, objectivity, generalisability, credibility, ethics. Use these words in every AO3 answer.
  4. Ethics and risk management are examinable content: BPS Code (2009), protection, deception and consent, right to withdraw, debrief, and OASys-style risk assessment.

3. Laboratory Experiments and Eyewitness Memory

laboratory experiment（实验室实验） — conducted in an artificial environment constructed by the experimenter, in which the researcher manipulates the independent variable (IV) and measures the dependent variable (DV).

Laboratory experiments are common in this area, especially for eyewitness testimony（目击证词）. The reason is simple: it is illegal to test real witnesses, because it could bias their testimony. So researchers test participant witnesses in a simulated context, such as watching films in a laboratory.

The IV and DV in crime research

  • The IV is a factor such as weapon presence（武器在场） or leading question types（引导性问题）.
  • The DV is the testimony provided or the witness recall, often after watching a film.

Loftus' three-step laboratory procedure

The Loftus studies used laboratory experiments with a number of key steps:

StepWhat happensWhere the IV can be introduced
1. Gather participantsRecruit people to take part in the studyVary participant characteristics: age or gender
2. Show the incidentShow a film or photographs of an incident or a potential suspectShow different films, offences or suspects
3. Test memoryAsk them to recall what they sawPost-event information（事后信息）: delay recall, or change how they recall it
中文理解：三步法是答题骨架
任何「用实验室实验研究目击记忆」的题，都按这三步写：招人 → 看事件 → 测记忆。IV 可以在任意一步动手脚：第一步换被试（年龄、性别），第二步换刺激（不同影片、嫌疑人），第三步换回忆条件（延迟、事后信息）。写 8 分大题时按这个顺序铺开，结构分就稳了。

4. Evaluating Lab Experiments in Criminological Psychology

Strength 1 · Standardised and replicable

Laboratory experiments use a standardised procedure（标准化程序）, so they are easier to replicate than any method carried out in a real setting.

Strength 2 · Control gives cause and effect

Precise control of extraneous variables, and direct manipulation of events that could never be staged in a real witnessed event, allow a cause-and-effect relationship to be established: which factors really influence eyewitness accuracy.

Limitation 1 · Artificiality lowers validity

The setting may produce unnatural behaviour with low ecological validity（生态效度）. Participants expect to see something and pay attention, unlike a passer-by before an unexpected accident. Stress is also lower: no police interview, no discussion with others, so extra influencing variables are minimised away from real life.

Limitation 2 · Reductionism（还原论）

Laboratory experiments isolate single variables on a single DV: experimental reductionism. Ruva, McEvoy and Bryant (2007) isolated pre-trial publicity, but failed to research the many other variables that influence juries. Exploring single variables cannot capture mediating factors, so explanations can be incomplete.

Ethics note

Film clips of crimes would be unethical to experience in real life. Watching a clip is less distressing than a real accident, but may still upset a participant with previous experience of a similar accident. In that case they are given the right to withdraw（退出权）.

中文理解：控制换来的代价
实验室实验的两条优点和两条缺点是同一枚硬币的两面：正因为控制得死（标准化、单变量），才换来了因果结论；也正因为控制得死，行为才不真实、解释才不完整。评价题里把这对因果关系写出来，比各说各的强得多。

5. Field Experiments in Criminological Psychology

field experiment（现场实验） — like a laboratory experiment it manipulates an IV and measures a DV, but it is conducted in a more natural setting where the phenomenon would naturally occur. The experiment is still artificially constructed.

A field experiment follows the same three steps as a laboratory experiment, but at step 2 the participants witness a simulated crime or an actor playing an offender. A researcher might realistically recreate a car accident in a high street.

Worked example: Valentine and Mesout (2009)

The London Dungeon study used real visitors in the dungeons (the natural setting) to assess the effect of stress on memory. Visitors would not normally be asked to complete questionnaires or identify people they saw, but the setting itself was real.

Do not confuse field experiments with natural experiments: in a natural experiment（自然实验） the IV occurs naturally, so the researcher does not have to manipulate it. In a field experiment the researcher still stages and manipulates the IV.

6. Evaluating Field Experiments in Criminological Psychology

Strength 1 · Genuine witness experience

Participants essentially experience the conditions of a real witness: they may feel stress or anxiety, discuss what they saw with others, and are not forewarned to recall. This raises ecological validity and makes findings representative of real eyewitness situations.

Strength 2 · Demand characteristics minimised

Participants may be unaware they are in a study, so their responses are more likely to be genuine and indicative of real-life witnesses. Findings can be relied on more than where response bias is a concern.

Limitation 1 · Control is harder

Many situational variables occur in natural settings, such as distractions from other witnesses. Replicating exactly is unlikely, so results are inconsistent and reliability is low. Some field studies can still control variables, for example a staged bank robbery set up inside a bank.

Limitation 2 · Distress is an ethical problem

Participants believe they are witnessing a real incident, so they are more likely to experience distress than in a laboratory. The researcher must weigh the nature of the staged incident to make sure it does not create excessive distress.

中文理解：真实与失控是一对
现场实验和实验室实验正好反过来：越真实，越失控。真实带来了生态效度和真心反应（不被察觉），失控带来了重复困难和信度下降。这对比较是 6.3 最常考的评价角度，记住哪边换来哪边。

7. Mock Jury Studies

real jury — members of the public randomly selected to sit through a trial and reach a verdict. mock jury（模拟陪审团） — an experiment which tries to emulate aspects of a real juror experience.

Why mock juries exist

  • It is not permissible to study genuine jurors as they sit through a trial or deliberate.
  • Jurors are not permitted to talk about what happened in the deliberation room（合议室）after the trial is over.

What a mock jury study looks like

Mock jury studies are essentially laboratory experiments where participants are exposed to trial evidence through video recordings or transcripts. They are a simulation of a real juror's experience. Participants are then asked for their verdict, or other information such as the strength of the case or witness confidence.

The contemporary study Ruva, McEvoy and Bryant (2007) is a mock jury study: participants read negative pre-trial publicity or unrelated coverage, watched a video of a criminal trial, answered questions about both, and gave a guilty or not guilty verdict.

中文理解：一个记忆钩子
为什么不直接研究真陪审团？两条法律原因：审中不许研究，审后不许聊。所以只能造假陪审团。而模拟陪审团本质就是实验室实验，只不过 IV 变成了「审判证据怎么呈现」，DV 变成了「判罪与否」。你已经在 6.2.3 里完整学过一个例子。

8. Evaluating Mock Jury Studies

Strength · Control over evidence

Researchers have good control over what jurors are exposed to. Standardised procedures mean every juror sees the same video or transcript (except the manipulated variable), so cause-and-effect conclusions can be drawn about factors affecting jury decision-making.

Limitation 1 · Simulation fidelity varies

Reading court proceedings does not resemble sitting through a trial, so ecological validity is weak. A video showing opening statements, witness accounts, cross-examination and the judge's instructions is better. But many studies skip deliberation, arguably the most important aspect; even where it happens, time is restricted and the jury is smaller than 12. A real jury deliberates as long as necessary, elects a foreperson and reviews evidence per the judge's instructions.

Limitation 2 · No real consequences

Reaching a verdict or setting a sentence is unlike real juror experience because nothing happens to an actual defendant. Mock verdicts may be less considered or cautious, and jurors know they are role playing. Extra measures (witness confidence ratings, culpability, sentence length) are not part of a genuine juror's experience either.

Limitation 3 · Student samples

Students are convenient to access, so samples are unlikely to represent typical jurors drawn randomly from a geographical area: students are younger and educated, while real juries span a range of ages and educational levels. However, how much this matters is still debated.

The counter-argument · Mitchell et al. (2005)

Some researchers question whether mock jury studies represent real trials, meaning factors found in mock studies may not influence real juries. This is contested by psychologists such as Mitchell et al. (2005), whose meta-analysis suggested that race may influence real jurors in the same way as mock jurors.

中文理解：评价要成对出现
写模拟陪审团的评价时，别只堆缺点。每条缺点都能配一个让步：样本局限，但影响有争议；模拟不像真审，但 Mitchell 的元分析显示真陪审团反应类似。缺点 + 反驳这个组合是高分 AO3 的固定写法。

10. Reliability in Criminological Research

reliability（信度） — the consistency of the research findings. A design with many experimental controls results in high reliability and allows replication.
MethodWhy
Lab experimentsHigh reliability: controlled design prevents other factors affecting what is studied. The same film clip can be shown, or only a specific age group researched. High controls allow replication many times and provide consistency.
Field experimentsLower reliability: more chance of situational variables in a natural setting, so exact replication is difficult and findings may be inconsistent.
Case studiesHard to control participant and situational variables: it is difficult to find two identical people who experienced the exact same situation to compare findings.

11. Validity — and Three Studies That Test It

validity（效度） — how well a study measures what it is supposed to measure.

Doubt has been raised about the validity of laboratory experiments and mock jury studies for eyewitness testimony: participants may be more or less cautious than a real witness or juror because they know it is an experiment. Three studies from the textbook test this:

StudyWhat it didWhat it shows about lab findings
Wagstaff et al. (2003)Studied real witnessesLittle evidence that weapon focus, age or level of violence affected real testimony, despite laboratory (and field) experiments suggesting these variables are influential.
Yuille and Cutshall (1986)Studied real witnesses to a robberyLimited effect of leading questions on real witnesses, although the effect had been demonstrated in the laboratory.
Ihlebaek et al. (2003)Compared a live staged robbery (field) with film footage of the same robbery (lab)The film group recalled more details with greater accuracy; errors were equal in both conditions. Laboratory experiments overestimate witness recall, likely due to attention levels, so reliance on lab findings should be viewed with caution.

Field experiments are more valid: participants experience what a real witness would, the only exception being that they are unlikely to be interviewed by the police.

Demand characteristics（需求特征）

Demand characteristics also reduce validity in artificial experiments. A participant may guess the aim is to test how stress affects witness recall and recall less to meet the researcher's expectations. That behaviour does not reflect a real-life situation.

中文理解：三个研究一句话记
Wagstaff：真证人身上武器聚焦等效应不明显；Yuille & Cutshall：真证人不怕引导性问题；Ihlebaek：实验室组记得更多更准，说明实验室高估了记忆。三个研究合起来就是一个结论：实验室的发现拿到真实世界里会打折扣，所以效度要小心。考试里随便抽一个做 AO3 论据都够用。

12. Objectivity, Generalisability, Credibility

Objectivity and subjectivity（客观性与主观性）

Researchers should remain totally value free: gather facts that may prove or disprove the hypothesis, without bias.

  • Quantitative data (for example, the number of guilty or not guilty verdicts) requires no interpretation, minimising potential for bias. It is more objective and obtained in field and laboratory experiments.
  • Qualitative data (for example, free recall of a witnessed event) needs a coding method（编码方法） so the quality or quantity of memory can be analysed objectively.

Achieving objectivity makes the research more scientific.

Generalisability（可推广性）

Much criminological research uses students, often receiving course credit, because researchers have access to student lists. But anyone can be a witness, and most of us could be called to jury service, so real witnesses and jurors are far more diverse by age and educational level than a typical study sample. The risk: a body of knowledge about eyewitness memory and jury decision-making that does not actually apply to real cases.

Credibility（可信度）

Research should be taken seriously and influence the field. All methods achieve credibility by striving to be reliable, valid and objective. Criminal psychology research especially needs credibility, because its findings are applied by the courts and police.

13. Ethics and Risk Management

The rule books

  • The British Psychological Society (BPS) Code of Ethics and Conduct (2009) guides psychological research and forensic practice such as formulations and treatment interventions. Other countries have their own standards.
  • Practitioner psychologists（执业心理学家） (forensic, clinical, health, etc.) follow additional guidance from their legal governing body, such as the Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC) in the UK. Those who do not follow the guidelines can be disbarred and are no longer allowed to practise.

Protection from harm（保护被试）

It would be unethical to expose someone to a real crime for an experiment. Watching a film clip is less distressing than seeing it in reality, but it can remind a person of a past similar experience, for example if they were a victim of a similar crime. Participants should be allowed to withdraw whenever they feel distressed. Laboratory experiments protect participants more than field experiments or case studies because the event is artificial; with real situations, careful consideration is required to minimise distress.

Deception and consent（欺骗与知情同意）

  • Deception may be used to minimise demand characteristics, which increases the validity of findings. The researcher may not tell participants the true aim.
  • If deception is used there is a lack of informed consent（缺乏知情同意）: participants cannot fully understand the nature and consequences of the research.
  • Laboratory experiments are more likely to require consent (participants know they are in a study). Field experiments may need it less if the staged incident is one a person could experience in everyday life, though this possibility is limited in criminological psychology. Deception remains an issue in the field too: participants may not know they are about to witness a staged crime.
  • Where deception and consent are issues, guidance suggests a debrief（事后告知） once the research is complete.

Right to withdraw（退出权）

Less of an issue in field experiments, where participants would experience the situation in everyday life anyway. Laboratory experiments should always offer the right to withdraw to comply with protection guidance, and participants should be debriefed where withdrawal is an issue.

Practical applications of research（研究的应用）

Criminological research has gathered a large body of knowledge about causes of offending, treatment effectiveness, eyewitness reliability and jury decision-making. Governments use it to inform policy-making to tackle crime and reduce reoffending, and police use it to ensure robust testimony secures accurate convictions. One example is the cognitive interview, developed from Loftus' eyewitness research.

Risk management（风险管理）

All research should undertake a risk assessment to identify potential harm or hazards:

ContextRisks to assess
Staged field incident (actor fleeing a crime scene)Witness stress; a bystander pursuing the actor; likelihood of trips and falls
Laboratory video footagePsychological distress, particularly if the participant has been a victim of crime; stress or embarrassment when recalling the event
Working with offendersForensic psychologists undertake regular risk assessments, such as reoffending risk, using established tools like the Offender Assessment System (OASys), designed to predict the probability of an offence causing serious harm. Other tools predict likelihood of abuse, violence or specific offences.
中文理解：伦理题的答题套路
伦理题答题三件套：点出伦理问题 → 说明它在这个情境里怎么出现 → 给出处理办法。比如欺骗：用了欺骗就没法知情同意，但在犯罪研究里欺骗是为了压住需求特征，处理办法是事后 debrief。风险管理同理：辨认风险（谁可能受伤、多严重、多可能）→ 给出管理动作。注意 OASys 这个词，它是 6.3.2 里少见的必背缩写。

16. Exam Lab

Textbook exam practice AO1 + AO2 + AO3 8-mark discussion

Question 1 — Michaela's mock jury study (from the textbook)

Michaela used an opportunity sample of college students, split into two groups. Both watched the same video of a witness identifying the defendant, and decided guilty or not guilty. Group 1 were told the witness was 100% confident; Group 2 were told 80% confident. The higher the confidence, the more likely participants reached a guilty verdict.

✍️ 1a — Explain two strengths4 marks

Explain two strengths of Michaela using a mock jury research method to investigate jury decision-making. (4)

Strength 1 (control): the mock jury method gave Michaela good control over what jurors were exposed to, since both groups watched the same video and differed only in the stated witness confidence, so a cause-and-effect conclusion about confidence and verdicts can be drawn. Strength 2 (access): genuine jurors cannot be studied as they sit through a trial or deliberate, and are not permitted to discuss the deliberation room afterwards, so the mock jury method is the only way to investigate jury decision-making directly.

中文方法框：四分两个优点怎么拆
每个优点两分：一分点出优点，一分解释在这个研究里它怎么起作用。别背通用优点（信度、效度这种词干说一遍就完了），必须挂回 Michaela 的细节：同样的视频、只有自信度不同。这是爱德思评分的硬要求：贴题才给分。

✍️ 1b — Improve generalisability2 marks

Explain one way that Michaela could improve the generalisability of her mock jury study. (2)

Her opportunity sample of college students is younger and more educated than typical jurors. She could use a sampling method that draws participants from a wider range of ages and educational backgrounds, for example a random or stratified sample from the local community, so the sample better represents the population of potential jurors and findings can be generalised to real juries.

✍️ Question 2 — Ethics of eyewitness research2 marks

Explain one ethical issue that should be considered when researching factors that influence eyewitness memory. (2)

Protection from harm: participants watching a filmed crime may become distressed, particularly if they have been the victim of a similar crime themselves. The researcher should therefore allow participants to withdraw if they begin to feel distressed and debrief them once the research is complete.

中文方法框：两分伦理题公式
问题名词（1 分）+ 在目击研究情境里的具体表现和处理（1 分）。只写「要保护被试」没有情境是拿不满的；加上「看到犯罪影片可能唤起亲身受害经历，所以给退出权并做 debrief」就满了。

✍️ Question 3 — The 8-mark design question8 marks

Fezile decides to conduct a laboratory experiment to investigate the influence of defendant characteristics on jury decision-making. Discuss how Fezile would go about conducting this laboratory experiment. (8)

Frame your answer: sample → IV and DV → design and allocation → procedure and controls → how results are analysed → one evaluation point.

Fezile could recruit a sample of participants to act as mock jurors and randomly allocate them to conditions. The IV would be a defendant characteristic, for example defendant race or gender, and the DV would be the verdict (guilty or not guilty) or a guilt rating. All jurors would watch the same video recording of a criminal trial, identical except for the manipulated characteristic of the defendant, with the trial length, evidence and instructions standardised so extraneous variables are controlled. Participants would give their verdict individually, and the number of guilty verdicts in each condition would be compared using quantitative analysis, for example percentages or a bar chart, and an appropriate inferential test. A laboratory experiment gives good control, allowing cause-and-effect conclusions about defendant characteristics; however the artificial setting lowers ecological validity, so Fezile should be cautious about generalising the findings to real jurors, who deliberate in groups of twelve with real consequences for the defendant.

中文方法框：8 分设计题的骨架
这类题就是 §1 Getting Started 那张清单的展开版：样本 → IV/DV → 设计 → 程序和控制 → 数据分析 → 一句评价。每项一两句，六项正好铺满八分。最后一句评价是加分关键：点出实验室的因果优势，同时提醒生态效度限制，前后呼应。

17. Key Terms

laboratory experiment 实验室实验
An artificial environment constructed by the experimenter, where the IV is manipulated and the DV measured.
field experiment 现场实验
An experiment in a more natural setting; the IV is still manipulated but the phenomenon occurs where it naturally would.
natural experiment 自然实验
An experiment where the IV occurs naturally and the researcher does not have to manipulate it.
mock jury 模拟陪审团
An experiment emulating aspects of a real juror experience; real jurors cannot legally be studied during a trial.
experimental reductionism 实验还原论
Reducing complex behaviour to a single isolated variable for testing, as laboratory experiments do.
reliability 信度
The consistency of research findings; high control allows replication.
validity 效度
How well a study measures what it is supposed to measure.
objectivity 客观性
Remaining value free; quantitative data needs no interpretation and minimises bias.
subjectivity 主观性
Data open to interpretation by the researcher, as with uncoded qualitative recall.
generalisability 可推广性
The extent to which findings from a sample apply to the target population.
credibility 可信度
Research being taken seriously; achieved through reliability, validity and objectivity.
demand characteristics 需求特征
Participants altering behaviour to match what they guess the study expects.
debrief 事后告知
Explaining the true aim to participants after the study, required where deception or withdrawal issues exist.
BPS Code of Ethics and Conduct (2009)
The British Psychological Society's guidance for research and forensic practice.
HCPC
The Health and Care Professions Council, the UK legal governing body for practitioner psychologists.
OASys
The Offender Assessment System, used to predict the probability of an offence causing serious harm.
