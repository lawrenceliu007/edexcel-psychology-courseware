What this workbook covers 本册讲什么
Real witnesses cannot legally be tested and real jurors cannot be studied at all. So crime research is simulated: film clips in labs, staged incidents in the field, and mock juries watching trial videos. This booklet walks through all three methods, how to evaluate them with the fixed vocabulary (reliability, validity, objectivity, generalisability, credibility), the BPS ethics and risk management rules, and the textbook exam questions.

The whole chapter in one sentence 一句话总结
Because testing real witnesses is illegal and real jurors cannot be studied, criminological psychology relies on laboratory experiments (control, cause and effect, but artificial), field experiments (realistic, but unreliable) and mock jury studies (access to verdicts, but role play without consequences), all of which must be judged on reliability, validity, objectivity, generalisability, credibility and ethics under the BPS Code (2009) and proper risk management.

Laboratory Experiments 实验室实验6.3 METHODS · CRIMINAL PSYCHOLOGY
Key term 关键术语
laboratory experiment（实验室实验）
Conducted in an artificial environment constructed by the experimenter; the researcher manipulates the IV and measures the DV.
Why labs? Why not real witnesses? 为什么要用实验室

It is illegal to test real witnesses because it may bias their testimony. So participant witnesses are tested in a simulated context, such as watching films in a laboratory.

IV and DV in crime research
RoleIn eyewitness research
IV（自变量）A factor such as weapon presence or leading question types
DV（因变量）The testimony provided or the witness recall, often after watching a film
The Loftus three-step procedure 三步法（必背骨架）
StepWhat happensWhere the IV can be introduced
1 participants gatheringVary participant characteristics: age, gender
2Show them a or of an incident or potential suspect film; photographsShow different films, offences, suspects
3Ask them to what they saw recall information: delay recall or change how they recall post-event
为什么三步法是答题骨架（中文理解）
任何「用实验室实验研究目击记忆」的题都按这三步写：招人、看事件、测记忆。IV 可以在任意一步动手：第一步换被试特征，第二步换刺激材料，第三步换回忆条件。8 分设计题就按这个顺序铺开。

PointConsequence（必须写出后果）

Limitation 2Cannot findings to a real-life setting generaliseLab results may not match what research on a real accident would find
Wider issues and debates: reductionism 还原论
experimental reductionism（实验还原论）
Reducing complex behaviour to a for the purpose of testing single isolated variable. Laboratory experiments do this: they isolate specific variables to measure their impact on a single DV.

Example: Ruva, McEvoy and Bryant (2007) isolated to test its effect on verdicts pre-trial publicity, but did not research the many other variables influencing juries. Exploring single variables cannot capture mediating factors, so lab explanations are often incomplete.

Ethics note 伦理提醒

Film clips of crimes would be to experience in real life unethical. Watching a clip is less distressing than the real event, but may still upset a participant with of a similar accident previous experience. They are given the right to withdraw.

中文理解：控制换来的代价
两条优点和两条缺点是同一枚硬币的两面：正因为控制得死，才换来因果结论；也正因为控制得死，行为才不真实、解释才不完整。评价题把这对因果写出来，比各说各的强。

Field Experiments 现场实验6.3 METHODS · CRIMINAL PSYCHOLOGY
Key term 关键术语
field experiment（现场实验）
Like a lab experiment it manipulates an IV and measures a DV, but it is conducted in a where the phenomenon would naturally occur more natural setting. The experiment is still artificially constructed.
Same three steps, one change

A field experiment follows the same three steps as a lab experiment, but at step the participants witness a or playing the role of an offender 2; simulated crime/event; an actor.

Worked example: Valentine and Mesout (2009)

The London Dungeon study used in the dungeons, its natural setting, to assess the effect of on memory real visitors; stress. Visitors would not normally be asked to complete questionnaires or identify people they saw, but the setting itself was real.

Do not confuse 不要混淆
field experiment vs natural experiment（自然实验）
In a natural experiment the IV occurs , so the researcher does manipulate it naturally; not have to. In a field experiment the researcher still the IV stages and manipulates.

mock jury（模拟陪审团）
Real juries are members of the public selected to sit through a trial and reach a verdict randomly. Mock juries are experiments which try to aspects of a real juror experience emulate.
What a mock jury study looks like

Essentially experiments where participants are exposed to trial evidence through or laboratory; video recordings; transcripts. They are a of the real juror experience simulation. Participants give a or other information such as strength of the case or witness confidence verdict. Example: Ruva, McEvoy and Bryant (2007).

Evaluating Research 1 — Reliability and Validity 信度与效度6.3 METHODS · CRIMINAL PSYCHOLOGY
Reliability（信度）：consistency of findings
MethodWhy
Lab experiments reliability: controlled design prevents other factors affecting the study; same film clip, specific age group; replication possible many times high
Field experiments reliability: situational variables occur in natural settings; exact replication difficult; findings inconsistent lower
Case studiesHard to control participant and situational variables: difficult to find two identical people who experienced the exact same situation
Validity（效度）：measuring what it claims to measure

Doubt about lab experiments and mock jury studies: participants may be than a real witness or juror because they know it is an experiment more or less cautious.

Three studies that test lab validity（必背，AO3 论据）
StudyWhat it didWhat it shows
Wagstaff et al. () 2003Studied witnesses realLittle evidence that , age or level of violence affected real testimony weapon focus, despite lab (and field) experiments suggesting they are influential

demand characteristics（需求特征）
Participants alter behaviour in response to the situation. Example: a participant guesses the aim is to test how stress affects recall and to meet the researcher's expectations recalls less. This does not reflect a real-life situation and lowers validity.

Exam Practice 真题（Textbook Exam Practice）6.3 METHODS · CRIMINAL PSYCHOLOGY
Question 1 — Michaela's mock jury study（4 + 2 marks）

Michaela used an opportunity sample of students from her local college, split into two groups. Both watched the same video of a witness giving evidence identifying the defendant, and decided guilty or not guilty. Group 1 were told the witness was 100% confident; Group 2 were told 80% confident. The higher the confidence, the more likely participants reached a guilty verdict.

a) Explain two strengths of Michaela using a mock jury research method to investigate jury decision-making. (4)

Strength 1: control — same video, only confidence differs, so cause-and-effect. Strength 2: access — real jurors cannot be studied or discuss the deliberation room. 每个优点：一分点名词，一分挂 Michaela 的细节。

b) Explain one way that Michaela could improve the generalisability of her mock jury study. (2)
Question 2 — Ethics of eyewitness research（2 marks）
Explain one ethical issue that should be considered when researching factors that influence eyewitness memory. (2)

公式：问题名词（1 分）+ 情境表现和处理（1 分）。例如 protection from harm：影片可能唤起受害经历，给退出权并 debrief。

Question 3 — The 8-mark design question（8 marks）
Fezile decides to conduct a laboratory experiment to investigate the influence of defendant characteristics on jury decision-making. Discuss how Fezile would go about conducting this laboratory experiment. (8)

按六步骨架写：样本 → IV/DV → 程序 → 控制 → 分析 → 一句评价。

Model answer sketches 简要范文

Q1a: Strength 1: the mock jury method gave Michaela control over what jurors were exposed to (same video, only stated confidence differed), so a cause-and-effect conclusion about witness confidence and verdicts can be drawn. Strength 2: genuine jurors cannot be studied during a trial or deliberate, and cannot discuss the deliberation room afterwards, so mock juries are the only way to investigate jury decision-making directly.
Q1b: Use a sample drawn from a wider range of ages and educational backgrounds (e.g. random or stratified community sample), so it better represents potential jurors and findings generalise to real juries.
Q2: Protection from harm: watching a filmed crime may distress participants, especially past victims of similar crime; allow withdrawal and debrief.
Q3: See the six-step frame on p.10: sample with random allocation; IV = defendant characteristic; DV = verdict or guilt rating; standardised trial video except the manipulated characteristic; compare guilty verdicts with percentages/charts/inferential test; strength = control and cause-and-effect, limitation = artificial setting lowers ecological validity so generalise with caution.
