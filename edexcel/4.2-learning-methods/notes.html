Types of Observation 观察的类型4.2 METHODS · SPEC 4.2.2
Pair 1 · Naturalistic vs structured

Naturalistic（自然观察）: behaviour watched in a environment where it would normally occur natural. Higher validity because behaviour is natural and spontaneous ecological. But it raises issues of and privacy if participants are unaware consent, and has limited over extraneous variables control.

Structured（结构观察）: are manipulated so the behaviour is displayed variables. Staged, usually in an environment the researcher , sometimes behind a one-way screen controls.

Worked example: Ainsworth's Strange Situation
Ainsworth et al. (1978) studied parent–child attachment. The sequence (mother leaves, stranger enters, reunion) would occur naturally rarely, so she staged it: a observation in a controlled environment structured.
Pair 2 · Participant vs non-participant

Non-participant（非参与）: observing from a distance; risk of if the detached observer misinterprets behaviour observer bias. Gain: the observer can on recording, collecting data more objectively concentrate.

Participant（参与）: joining the group; stranger effect on participants' behaviour no, reveals data other methods miss. But hard to record while taking part, and the researcher may lose notes; objectivity.

Pair 3 · Overt vs covert

Overt（公开）: participants know; consent possible and withdrawal respected informed. Covert（隐蔽）: participants unaware, problematic ethically; but behaviour unchanged, so more valid.

Combinations 组合

Textbook examples: a , participant, observation (an anthropologist joining a tribal group) naturalistic; overt; or a , non-participant, observation (secret watching behind a one-way mirror) structured; covert.

中文理解：每对选择都在换东西
自然换真实（生态效度），结构换控制（因果和重复）；参与换洞察，非参与换客观；公开换伦理，隐蔽换真实。答题写成「选了 A 换来什么、付出什么」就自动成对。

Content analysis observes the of certain words, images or concepts within material incidence, typically in , books, films, newspapers advertisements. Example from the textbook: instances of in children's books gender stereotyping. It is therefore a form of observation indirect.

How to conduct one 怎么做

Animal Research: For and Against 动物研究正反方4.2 METHODS · SPEC 4.2.8
Background 背景

Learning theorists such as and built their methods on animal experimentation Pavlov; Skinner, manipulating an IV to see its effect on behaviour.

Arguments in favour 支持方（四条）
  1. Breeding speed: a rat's gestation period is typically days 22; large samples bred quickly; generational effects appear without long waits.
  2. Control: animals can be in ways humans cannot caged; variables isolated, e.g. the box dispensing food on a lever press Skinner.
  3. Fewer ethical limits: unnecessary pain must be avoided but and distress are permitted pain; research still heavily , with regular welfare regulated; inspections.
  4. Benefits and continuity: insight into drug treatments, transplants, surgery, cloning; humans share ancestry with other animals, especially primates common/evolutionary, so we can generalise to some extent. The argument: a small number of animals suffering is justified because it helps a significant number of people utilitarian; (1991) supports this when research furthers the cause Gray.
Arguments against 反对方（四条）
  1. Credibility: lab animal behaviour is from natural behaviour, so eco validity is low different.
  2. Too many differences: evolution, genes, cognition, emotion; human behaviour has more variables (culture, social norms, ) language; humans have a proportionally much larger cortex. A newborn mouse equals a seven-week-old foetus.
  3. : mistakenly attributing human qualities to animals Anthropomorphism; and the continuity contradiction: if animals are so similar, why not the same considerations? ethical.
  4. : Singer (1975) called giving human suffering priority a form of discrimination Speciesism; benefits are unknown until the study after; on this view regulation can justify animal research no amount of.

支持方靠连续性（共同祖先、相似神经系统）说结果能推广；反方用同一把刀反击：既然这么相似，凭什么不给同样的伦理保护？再一步就是 Singer 的物种歧视。把这条正反两用写出来比堆理由高级。

Animal Ethics: The Law 动物伦理立法4.2 METHODS · SPA 1986 · THREE Rs · THREE LICENCES
Legislation 立法
  • The Animals (Scientific Procedures) Act () covers all animal research in the UK 1986. It covers any scientific procedure that may cause , suffering, distress or lasting harm to a 'protected' animal pain.
  • Protected animals = all plus a single invertebrate species non-human vertebrates; the octopus.
  • Also follow the Animal Welfare Act () 2006; in Europe, governs national regulations Directive 2010/63/EU.

BPS: permission will not be granted unless the researcher can the costs to the animals in relation to the likely justify; benefits. Curiosity alone is not enough. Three Rs (Russell and Burch, ) 1959:

Model answer highlights 高分要点（4+8 分题）
Q1 玻璃走道（4 分）
Strength (ecological validity): real setting where the behaviour normally occurs, so path choice reflects genuine avoidance of heights. Weakness (lack of control): weather, crowd levels and individual differences could not be controlled, so fear of heights may not be the only cause and the observation cannot be repeated exactly.
Q4 内容分析（8 分骨架）
Two strengths: unobtrusive (existing sources, few ethical issues, historical material available) + reliability (same categories make replication easy). Two weaknesses: subjective (personal interpretation; internal validity if categories misfit) + descriptive only (what not why; limited by material availability; media ≠ reality; culture bias). Conclusion: valuable where live research is impossible; check across coders and cultures.
