4.1.5 SYSTEMATIC DESENSITISATION
Unlearning a phobia, one small step at a time · 系统脱敏法

Topic D · Learning theories and development Therapy: behavioural Spec 4.1.5
A phobia is a learned fear. If it was learned, it can be unlearned — and this therapy does it by pairing the feared object with the one state that makes fear impossible: relaxation.

What this section is really asking

  1. A phobia is a learned response, not a character flaw — which is why telling someone to calm down never works.
  2. You cannot be anxious and relaxed at the same time (reciprocal inhibition). That single fact is the engine of the whole therapy.
  3. The client climbs a ladder of fears, rung by rung, relaxing at each rung until the fear fades — that is counterconditioning.
  4. The evidence is good but narrow: Capafons et al. (1998) found 18 of 20 flying-phobia clients improved, yet the therapy only works when the feared object is clearly identifiable.

1. Getting Started

Imagine a friend who is terrified of spiders. A spider appears on the wall and your friend freezes, backs out of the room, and cannot sleep until the spider is gone. You say the obvious thing: “It's tiny. It can't hurt you. Just calm down.”

It never works. Not because your friend is stupid, but because the fear is not a belief you can argue with. It is a response — the body has learned to react before the thinking part of the brain gets a vote.

The question this chapter answers

If fear can be learned, can it also be unlearned? And if so, how do you teach a body to unlearn something it has practised for years?

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

A therapist wants to cure a spider phobia. Which of these is closest to what systematic desensitisation actually does?

The answer is the second one. Explaining things targets thoughts; this therapy targets the body's response. And avoidance makes fear worse, never better — every time you escape, you teach yourself that escape was necessary.

中文理解：为什么「别怕」没有用？
恐惧症（phobia）不是观点，是条件反射。就像你闻到坏掉的食物会皱眉，不需要先想「这有毒」——身体比思考快一步。
所以治疗也不能靠讲道理，而要重新训练这个反射：让「蜘蛛」这个信号，慢慢和「放松」而不是「恐惧」连在一起。

2. Two Kinds of Therapy

Before the method itself, two distinctions do a lot of work in this topic — and in your exam answers.

Psychological therapies

Work through the mind — talking, learning, thinking, behaving. Examples: psychoanalysis, behavioural therapy, cognitive therapy, family therapy, humanistic therapy.

Biological treatments

Work through the body — medication, psychosurgery. Example: antidepressants for depression.

directive therapy（指导性治疗）
The therapist instructs the client to do certain things or to think in a different way. The therapist leads.
non-directive therapy（非指导性治疗）
The therapy focuses on making sense of the client's experiences and how the client understands themselves. The client leads; the therapist follows and interprets.

Systematic desensitisation sits in the psychological, directive, behavioural corner: the therapist gives instructions, and those instructions are about behaviour, not about insight.

中文理解：指导性 vs 非指导性
Directive（指导性）＝教练型：教练告诉你下一步做什么，「先深呼吸，然后看这张图」。系统脱敏法就是这样。
Non-directive（非指导性）＝陪聊型：治疗师不指挥你，而是让你自由说，他负责理解与解释。下一节 4.1.6 的精神分析就是这种。
考试常考「这个疗法是指导性还是非指导性」，记住这条线就不会混。

3. What Systematic Desensitisation Is

systematic desensitisation（系统脱敏法）
A directive behavioural treatment for anxiety disorders, especially phobias, developed by Joseph Wolpe in the 1950s. It is based on classical conditioning, and the name says it all: systematic = following a planned order, desensitisation = becoming less sensitive to the feared object.

Two words inside the term are worth unpacking, because students often use them without knowing what they mean:

Word
Meaning
What it looks like in therapy
systematic
Gradually facing up to the phobic object via a hierarchy of exposure, from least fearful to most fearful.
Nobody starts by holding a spider. The client starts with a picture, then a video, then a spider in a jar across the room.
desensitisation
Brought about by relaxation techniques taught before facing the phobic object.
The client learns to relax first, so that relaxation is available the moment fear appears.

The aim is to overcome an irrational fear by exposing the person to it gradually and in a controlled way. The therapy rests on one idea from classical conditioning:

The logic in one sentence

Phobias are acquired through an unhelpful learned response to a stimulus — so just as you can learn to fear something, you can unlearn the fear by relaxing whenever you are in contact with the phobic object.

中文理解：名字就是方法
systematic（系统的）＝按「恐惧等级表」一级一级来，不许跳级；
desensitisation（脱敏）＝让你对这个东西越来越「不敏感」，像打疫苗一样，从小剂量开始。
一句话：先把放松练好，再按等级表一点点接近害怕的东西，直到害怕消失。

4. The One Rule That Makes It Work

Everything in this therapy depends on a single claim about the nervous system:

reciprocal inhibition（交互抑制）
You cannot be anxious and relaxed at the same time. If relaxation is present, anxiety is blocked — and the two responses cannot coexist.
Figure 4.1.5a · Reciprocal inhibition. The therapist does not try to argue the fear away — the therapist makes relaxation physically impossible to combine with fear.

That is why the order of the therapy matters so much. If the client meets the spider first and learns to relax afterwards, there is nothing to inhibit the fear. Relaxation has to be already available, like a fire extinguisher before the fire.

中文理解：为什么「同时」这么关键？
交感神经（紧张）和副交感神经（放松）不能同时全开。所以只要你能真正放松下来，恐惧反应就「进不来」。
类比：手上已经握着一杯水，就没办法同时握拳。治疗师要做的是先让你学会「握水杯」，再把你带到害怕的东西面前。

5. The Five Steps

The textbook lists five key processes. Learn them in this order, because the exam asks you to explain how the therapy is conducted — and a process answer earns marks for sequence.

Figure 4.1.5b · The five processes in the order the textbook lists them.

Step by step

1. Functional analysis. A conversation between therapist and client to identify the nature of the anxiety and its possible triggers. This is the assessment stage: what exactly is feared, when does it happen, what makes it worse.

2. Develop a hierarchy of fears. Client and therapist build a list from the least anxiety-provoking situation to the most. It is not a list of the therapist's ideas: the client must have input, because only the client knows which rung feels worse.

3. Relaxation training. The client is taught relaxation methods that suit them — deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation, or visualisation such as imagining a peaceful setting.

4. Gradual exposure. Working through the agreed hierarchy, the phobic object is introduced slowly, at the speed agreed by the client. Nobody is pushed up a rung.

5. Counterconditioning. Through repeated pairings of the feared stimulus with relaxation, the client learns to associate the previously feared stimulus with relaxation rather than anxiety.

中文理解：五步其实是「问清楚 → 排队 → 学放松 → 一级级上 → 换掉旧反应」
① functional analysis＝问诊，搞清楚到底怕什么；② hierarchy＝把害怕的情境排成 1–10 级；③ relaxation training＝先学会放松；④ gradual exposure＝按等级表一级一级接触；⑤ counterconditioning＝反复「怕的东西 + 放松」配对，最后旧的条件反射被新的取代。
考试写「如何实施」时，用 firstly / secondly / thirdly 把这五步串起来，就是满分结构。

6. Building the Hierarchy

The hierarchy is the heart of the method. It turns “I am scared of birds” into ten concrete situations that can be tackled in order.

Figure 4.1.5c · The textbook's own spider hierarchy. The client climbs from imagining the spider (least fearful) to holding it (most fearful).

Two rules keep the hierarchy safe and effective:

The client writes the rungs

Only the client knows which situation feels worse. If the therapist imposes the order, the client may be pushed up a rung too early and the fear is reinforced rather than reduced.

The client sets the speed

Progress depends on the strength of the phobia and the client's ability to relax — which is why the number of sessions varies so much between clients.

🎯 Quick check No notes

A client's hierarchy reads: (A) hold a spider, (B) look at a photo, (C) stand in the same room. Which item does the client meet first?

中文理解：等级表为什么必须由来访者自己排？
因为「怕」是主观的。治疗师觉得「看照片」很轻，但来访者可能连照片都不敢看。排错等级＝把人直接推到承受不了的那一级，恐惧反而被强化（这叫 re-traumatisation，二次创伤）。
所以等级表既是技术工具，也是伦理保护。

7. Real or Imagined

Exposure can happen in two forms, and the difference is examinable vocabulary:

Form
What the client meets
Strength / weakness
in vivo
The real object, in real life — an actual spider, an actual aeroplane cabin.
Stronger, more convincing learning; but harder to arrange and more frightening for the client.
in vitro
An imaginary exposure — picturing the object, or watching a film of it.
Safe and easy to control; but the learning may not transfer to the real situation.

Modern practice adds a third route: virtual reality. A headset can put a client in a plane cabin or a room full of spiders while the therapist controls exactly how much appears and when. It keeps the graded control of in vitro exposure but feels much closer to in vivo.

中文理解：in vivo / in vitro 别记反
in vivo＝在活体里＝真实情境（真的蜘蛛）；in vitro＝在玻璃器皿里＝想象或影像。
记忆法：vivo 有「活」的意思——活的、真的。

8. Relaxation Training

Relaxation is not “try to feel better”. It is a skill, taught and practised, until the client can produce it on demand. Three common methods:

Deep breathing

Slow, abdominal breathing lowers heart rate. Simple, portable, usable in the moment.

Progressive muscle relaxation

Tensing and then releasing each muscle group, teaching the body the difference between tension and relaxation.

Visualisation

Imagining a peaceful setting — a beach, a quiet room — until the image itself triggers calm.

The therapist chooses methods that suit the client: a client who cannot sit still may prefer breathing to a long body scan; a visually imaginative client may take to visualisation quickly. What matters is that the client can reach relaxation reliably before exposure begins.

Why practice, not just instruction

Under fear, the body reverts to whatever it has practised. A relaxation technique used for the first time in front of a spider will fail — the client needs it to be automatic, so that it can compete with the anxiety response.

9. Evidence: Capafons et al. (1998)

Does the therapy actually work? The textbook's key evidence is a Spanish study of fear of flying.

Feature
Detail
Why it matters
Sample
41 Spanish participants who suffered from a fear of flying (aerophobia).
A clinical sample — people with a real phobia, not students asked to imagine one.
Groups
20 treated with systematic desensitisation; 21 in a waiting control group (waiting for therapy, not yet treated).
The comparison shows what would have happened without treatment.
Result
18 of the treated participants reported lower levels of fear and were less anxious.
A large improvement in the treated group, against a group receiving nothing.

Read that result carefully, because the exam rewards precision: 18 out of 20, not “all of them”, and the outcome is self-reported fear, not a behavioural test of actually boarding a plane.

What this study supports

Systematic desensitisation can reduce reported fear in a genuine clinical phobia — and it does so better than receiving no treatment. That is exactly the claim the therapy needs, because it is a behavioural treatment: if the behaviour (avoidance) does not change, the therapy has not done its job.

What it does not prove

A waiting control group is not a placebo control, so some of the improvement could come from expectation and attention. The measure is self-report, so demand characteristics are possible. And there is no long follow-up reported, so we cannot tell whether the fear stayed away.

中文理解：这组数据怎么用？
41 人、20 人接受治疗、21 人排队等待、18 人报告恐惧下降——这是支持「有效」的证据（AO3 里的 strength）。
但同时要会挑毛病：等待对照组≠安慰剂组；结果是自评不是真的去坐飞机；样本只有 41 人；没有长期追踪。这样一段「先支持再限制」的写法，才是 8 分题的高分结构。

10. Why It Works

Describe the therapy in the language of classical conditioning and it becomes much easier to remember — and much easier to write about under exam pressure.

Conditioning term
In the phobia
In the therapy
CS
The spider (originally neutral, now a signal for danger).
Still the spider — but now paired with something else.
UCS / UCR
A frightening experience (being bitten, startled) producing fear.
Relaxation training produces calm, deliberately.
New pairing
—
CS (spider) + relaxation, repeated at each rung.
New CR
Fear at the sight of the spider.
Calm at the sight of the spider — a counter-conditioned response.

The technical name for this replacement is counterconditioning: a new response is conditioned to the old stimulus, and because the two responses inhibit each other, the old one cannot run.

Counterconditioning or extinction?

Extinction would mean presenting the spider repeatedly without anything unpleasant, until the fear fades. Systematic desensitisation does something more active: it attaches a competing response to the spider. That is why relaxation training is not optional — without it, there is nothing to compete with the fear.

中文理解：用条件反射的语言重讲一遍
原本：蜘蛛（CS）→ 害怕（CR）。
现在：每次出现「蜘蛛」，同时让你处于放松状态，重复多次后，蜘蛛（CS）→ 放松（新的 CR）。
这叫 counterconditioning（反向条件化）：不是把旧反应「擦掉」，而是让一个更强、互斥的新反应占住位置。

11. Evaluation · Strengths

Four strengths, each with the reason behind it — not just a list of adjectives.

1. It has proven effectiveness

Most studies report that people receiving systematic desensitisation improve more than a non-treatment group. Capafons et al. (1998) is the textbook example: 18 of 20 treated flying-phobia clients reported lower fear, against a waiting control group who received nothing. Unlike some talking therapies, the outcome here is behavioural and measurable — you can see whether the client now approaches the spider.

2. It takes less time and effort than other therapies

Compared with psychoanalysis, which can run for years, systematic desensitisation is brief and structured. The hierarchy gives both therapist and client a clear plan, so progress is visible session by session. That also makes it cheaper for health services.

3. The client can, in principle, do it alone

Because the method is a set of explicit steps, it can be delivered through computer simulations, apps and virtual reality software. That widens access for people who cannot travel, cannot afford a therapist, or feel too ashamed to seek one.

4. It raises few ethical problems

The techniques — a graded hierarchy and relaxation — mean the client is not exposed to any high-anxiety situation. There is no deception, no distressing procedure, and the client controls the pace. Compare this with flooding, which deliberately puts the client in the most frightening situation at once.

中文理解：为什么「伦理风险低」是加分点
同属行为疗法的 flooding（满灌疗法）是直接把人放进最害怕的情境，效果快但可能造成创伤。系统脱敏法的等级表相当于安全绳：每一级都在来访者能承受的范围内。
在 AO3 里写「ethical」时，一定要说清为什么（等级表 + 放松训练保证不进入高焦虑），否则只是空话。

12. Evaluation · Limitations

1. It only treats some anxiety disorders

The object or situation has to be clearly identifiable for a hierarchy to be built. That makes it inappropriate for someone with generalised anxiety disorder — a long-term condition where the person feels anxious across a range of situations rather than about one specific object. You cannot build a ladder for “everything”.

2. Some phobias resist it

It is less effective for phobias with an underlying survival component — fear of the dark, of dangerous animals, of heights. One explanation is evolutionary: these fears may have kept our ancestors alive, so they are deep-rooted and very difficult to remove. A fear of heights is a poor candidate for a hierarchy of photographs.

3. It treats the symptom, not the cause

Systematic desensitisation changes the response and says nothing about why the fear arose. If the phobia is a surface expression of an unresolved conflict or a traumatic memory, removing the surface behaviour may leave the underlying problem untouched — and the anxiety may reappear in another form. This is exactly the criticism psychoanalysis makes of behavioural therapy.

4. It depends on skills the client may not have

The client must be able to relax on cue and to work with an imagined or real hierarchy. Clients who cannot achieve relaxation, or who cannot tolerate any exposure, progress poorly — and self-help apps remove the therapist's judgement about when to stop.

🎯 Quick check No notes

Which client is the least suitable for systematic desensitisation?

中文理解：三个限制其实是一条逻辑
系统脱敏法要求「有一个具体、可分级、可接近的恐惧对象」。
→ 对象不明确（GAD）＝做不出等级表；
→ 对象是进化写进基因的（黑暗、高处、猛兽）＝等级表也压不住；
→ 恐惧只是更深问题的表面＝治好了表面，里面还在。
记住这条主线，三条限制就不需要死背。

13. Therapy in an App

The textbook asks you to weigh up clients using electronic applications — apps and virtual reality — to treat their own phobia, without visiting a therapist. Here is the balanced case.

Question
Case for the app
Case against the app
Access
No waiting list, no travel, available at 2 a.m. when the fear is worst. Reaches people who would never enter a clinic.
Assumes a smartphone, data and a private space — it excludes some of the people who need it most.
Cost
A fraction of weekly sessions; can be free. Health services can offer it at scale.
Cheap partly because nobody is assessing the client — savings may be paid for elsewhere.
Control
The client moves at their own pace, which fits the principle that the client sets the speed.
No therapist to judge when the client is climbing too fast, and no one to notice if the fear is worsening.
Safety
In vitro and VR exposure cannot put the client in real physical danger.
Drop-out is invisible; a client who abandons the programme at rung four gets no follow-up at all.

The judgement to reach

Apps are a reasonable first step or top-up for mild, specific phobias, where the client is motivated and the hierarchy is easy to build. They are a poor substitute for a therapist when the phobia is severe, when the client cannot relax, or when the fear is tangled up with other problems — the very cases where professional judgement matters most.

When asked to explain or discuss how a therapy is conducted, treat it like a recipe: the examiner wants the steps. Framing your answer with firstly, secondly, thirdly makes sure you write enough distinct points — and makes it obvious to the examiner that you know the sequence.

The 8-mark paragraph that usually scores

AO1 (about 4 marks). Systematic desensitisation is a directive behavioural therapy developed by Wolpe, based on classical conditioning. The client is first given a functional analysis, then builds a hierarchy of fears from least to most anxiety-provoking. They are taught relaxation techniques, then work through the hierarchy while relaxing, so that the feared stimulus becomes paired with calm through counterconditioning — possible because of reciprocal inhibition.

16. Exam Lab

Question · Judith and the cat (8 marks)

Judith has developed a phobia of cats following an incident where a cat unexpectedly jumped out at her and bit her hand. Discuss systematic desensitisation as a treatment for Judith's phobia of cats. (8 marks)

AO1 4 marks · AO3 4 marks. Use Judith's case in your answer — a generic essay scores lower.

Model answer

AO1. Systematic desensitisation is a directive behavioural therapy developed by Wolpe, based on classical conditioning. Judith's fear of cats is a learned response to a stimulus — the cat that bit her — so it can be unlearned by pairing cats with relaxation. The therapist would begin with a functional analysis, identifying that the trigger is a cat moving suddenly towards her. They would then build a hierarchy of fears together, from least to most frightening: seeing a photo of a cat, watching a video, being in the same room as a cat in a carrier, being near a cat that is free, and finally stroking a cat. Judith would be taught relaxation techniques such as deep breathing or visualisation, and would then work up the hierarchy at her own pace, staying relaxed at each rung. Through repeated pairing of cats with relaxation, counterconditioning occurs: because she cannot be anxious and relaxed at once (reciprocal inhibition), the fear response is replaced by calm.

AO3. One strength is that the therapy is well supported: Capafons et al. (1998) found 18 of 20 flying-phobia clients reported lower fear after treatment, suggesting Judith is likely to improve. The graded hierarchy also keeps her out of any high-anxiety situation, so it raises few ethical issues. However, the therapy only reduces the response and does not address why the phobia developed. If Judith's fear is linked to a broader anxiety problem, the fear may return or appear elsewhere — and if her fear were not tied to a clearly identifiable object, a hierarchy could not be built at all, which is why the therapy would not suit generalised anxiety disorder.

17. Key Terms

systematic desensitisation 系统脱敏法
A directive behavioural therapy for phobias, developed by Wolpe, that pairs gradual exposure to the feared object with relaxation.
systematic 系统的
Gradually facing the phobic object via a hierarchy of exposure, from least fearful to most fearful.
desensitisation 脱敏
Becoming less sensitive to the feared object, brought about by relaxation techniques taught before exposure.
reciprocal inhibition 交互抑制
The principle that anxiety and relaxation cannot occur at the same time, so relaxation blocks the fear response.
functional analysis 功能分析
The opening conversation between therapist and client that identifies the nature of the anxiety and its triggers.
hierarchy of fears 恐惧等级表
A ranked list of situations from least to most anxiety-provoking, built with the client, who also sets the pace.
relaxation training 放松训练
Teaching the client to relax on cue — deep breathing, progressive muscle relaxation or visualisation.
counterconditioning 反向条件化
Replacing a conditioned fear response with a new, incompatible response (relaxation) through repeated pairing.
in vivo 真实情境暴露
Exposure to the actual feared object in real life.
in vitro 想象暴露
Exposure through imagination or images rather than the real object.
directive therapy 指导性治疗
The therapist instructs the client what to do or how to think; systematic desensitisation is directive.
generalised anxiety disorder 广泛性焦虑障碍
A long-term condition of anxiety across many situations rather than one specific object — unsuitable for a hierarchy.
waiting control group 等待对照组
Participants waiting for treatment who receive none during the study, used by Capafons et al. as a comparison.
