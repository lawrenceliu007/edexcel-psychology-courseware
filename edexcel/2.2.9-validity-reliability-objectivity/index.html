2.2.9–2.2.10 VALIDITY, RELIABILITY & OBJECTIVITY
Reliability · Validity · Objectivity · Experimenter effects · 效度信度与客观性

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Methods Spec 2.2.9–2.2.10 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.11 · pp.108–110

What this page is really asking

  1. Reliability and validity are different diseases. A scale that always over-reads by 2 kg is reliable but invalid; one that wanders is unreliable and almost certainly invalid too. Learn to name which disease a study has.
  2. Validity comes in three flavours. Internal (did the IV really cause the result?), predictive (does the measure forecast future performance?), and ecological (would it hold in everyday life?).
  3. Objectivity is the foundation. A measurement everyone records the same way (a ruler, a word count, a scan) is objective, and that is what makes reliability and validity possible at all.
  4. People are the biggest threat. Experimenters nudge with nods and smiles; participants guess aims and perform the expected behaviour. Single-blind, double-blind and standardised instructions are the named controls.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Define reliability, validity and objectivity and distinguish them using the speedometer/scale analogy.
  • Identify test-retest and inter-rater reliability and say what each shows about a study.
  • Classify validity problems as internal, predictive or ecological for a described study.
  • Explain experimenter effects and demand characteristics with examples, and name the control for each.
  • Evaluate a described experiment by diagnosing which of the three qualities it has and which it lacks.

先猜你家那台永远多报 2 公斤的体重秤，问题出在哪？

1. Reliability: Consistency of Findings

Reliability（信度：研究结果的一致性）refers to the consistency of findings from research, and it is an important criterion for being scientific. If findings are consistent, we can trust that the finding will happen again and again. Achieving reliability requires research to be replicable, which demands tight control of extraneous variables: if those variables vary between repetitions, the findings change too.

Two versions matter for experiments:

  • Test-retest reliability（重测信度：同一测量重复进行，结果应稳定）: if a measuring procedure is repeated, consistent results show reliability.
  • Inter-rater reliability（评分者间信度：不同研究者的评分应一致）: the scoring of one researcher is compared with another researcher's scoring of the same performance. The scores are correlated to show the rate of agreement, which controls for subjectivity and differences of opinion between researchers.

中文理解：信度只回答一个问题：再测一次、再量一遍、换个人来评，数字会不会一样？会一样，就叫可靠。注意它和「对不对」完全无关：一台秤可以每次都稳定地错同一个数，高度可靠，照样是错的。这正是你先猜那题的陷阱所在。

2. Validity: Measuring What Was Intended

Validity（效度：研究测到的确实是想测的东西）asks whether the study is measuring the behaviour or construct it intends to measure. Think about a car's speedometer: it is designed to tell you exactly what speed you are going. If it reads 30 km/h when you are actually travelling 20 km/h, it gives an invalid measure. If it keeps over-reading by the same 10 km/h every day, it is invalid, but consistent: reliable. And if it reads 30 one day and 40 the next while you travel the same 20, it is unreliable, and very likely invalid as well.

Type of validityWhat it asks
Internal validity（内部效度：结果真是 IV 造成的）How well the procedure establishes a causal link between the manipulated IV and the measured DV, or whether the result has been confounded by uncontrolled extraneous variables. It can be ensured by standardised procedures, controlling order effects and individual differences, and avoiding demand characteristics.
Predictive validity（预测效度：测量能预测相关表现）The extent to which the performance being measured can predict future performance on a similar criterion. A test of intelligence that accurately predicts future academic success has predictive validity.
Ecological validity（生态效度：结果能否推广到日常情境）The extent to which the research can be generalised to everyday situations. Memory experiments using artificial tasks such as meaningless word lists or trigrams may tell us little about how memory is used day to day; laboratory participants who know they are being studied also behave unnaturally.

中文理解：效度回答「量的是不是那个东西」，信度回答「量得稳不稳」。两种病可以单独出现：稳定地错（可靠但无效，恒差 2 公斤的秤），也可以抖来抖去（不可靠，基本也会连累效度）。内部效度看「结果是不是 IV 干的」，生态效度看「实验室结论搬到日常生活还成立吗」，预测效度看「这个测验分数能不能预言以后的表现」。评价题先分清问的是哪一种，再开药方。

3. Objectivity: Removing the Researcher's Judgement

Being objective（客观性：测量不受研究者主观判断影响）means being impartial and judgement-free. Imagine guessing the length of a table: your guess rests on your own opinion and will differ from other people's guesses. Guesses are subjective, so they are unlikely to be reliable or valid. Measure the same table with a ruler, and everyone who uses the same ruler records exactly the same number: an objective measurement, and therefore both reliable and valid.

Cognitive psychology faces a special difficulty: memory and other mental processes cannot be directly observed or measured. Cognitive psychologists accept that mental processes themselves cannot be objectively measured, but the data they produce can. A short-term memory test recording a recall of five words is an objective measurement of the capacity of the short-term store; a PET scan objectively shows which brain regions are active during a memory task.

This is also why operationalisation (the previous page) matters so much: a vague DV such as "how well the child reads" hands the judgement to the researcher's opinion, while a timed reading with recorded errors turns it into something like a ruler.

中文理解：客观性的标尺就是「换谁来测，数都一样」。主观猜测各不相同，尺子人人同数。认知心理学测不了「记忆」本身，但测得了记忆留下的数据：回忆词数、脑区激活。这条链要背下来：操作化让测量客观，客观保证信度和效度，三者合起来才够格叫科学。

4. Experimenter Effects & Demand Characteristics

Experimenter effects（实验者效应：研究者的言行或在场影响结果）are the ways an experimenter may influence the outcome of an experiment by their actions or mere presence. Some are obvious: a female researcher asking a male participant about his attitudes towards gender equality, or a young researcher asking an older participant what they think about youth culture. Others are subtle: a nod or a smile that quietly affirms a particular behaviour.

Closely related are demand characteristics（需求特征：参与者猜到研究目的后改变行为）: participants behave differently from normal because clues within the research setting lead them to change their behaviour. They may detect clues about the aim of the study and then behave as they believe they are expected to. Either problem threatens the validity of the findings, because the behaviour recorded is no longer the behaviour that occurs naturally.

Both have named controls, and you have already met them on the previous page: single-blind procedures keep participants unaware of the aim, double-blind procedures also keep the conducting researcher ignorant of it, and standardised instructions remove the experimenter's chance to nod, smile or improvise. Laboratory studies are especially exposed to both problems, because participants are invited and the researcher is present; field studies avoid them partly by catching participants unaware.

Close the loop: diagnose the scale, diagnose the study

Your scale was reliably wrong: it over-reads by 2 kg consistently, so it is high on reliability and low on validity. Now apply the same diagnosis to research. A memory experiment that consistently finds the same serial position curve across replications is reliable; whether it is valid depends on which kind of validity you ask about. Its artificial word lists may limit ecological validity; a confounding variable would damage internal validity; and if participants guessed the aim and performed the way they thought the experimenter wanted, demand characteristics have quietly replaced real memory with polite cooperation. Reliable-but-questionably-valid is exactly the standard criticism of laboratory experiments, and it is the same 2 kg in a different costume.

Reflect before the Checkpoint: name one study from this topic that you suspect is reliable but weak on ecological validity, and say why in one sentence. If you can also name the control that would improve it, you are ready for the Exam Lab.

6. Exam Lab

Q1 · Explain one weakness 2 marks

Zikri set up a laboratory experiment in which participants performed a single cognitive task or a dual task (the same task while learning a list of words), and he timed how long each group took. Explain one weakness of Zikri's laboratory experiment in terms of validity.

The study may lack ecological validity: the task was performed in an artificial setting with an artificial task (a timed cognitive task plus word learning), so the findings may not generalise to how memory works in everyday situations. In addition, participants were invited into a laboratory and knew they were being studied, so they may have behaved unnaturally or tried to perform as they believed was expected, which further weakens validity.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for naming the validity problem (ecological validity / artificiality / awareness of being studied), 1 mark for explaining the consequence for what Zikri's timings can tell us about real-life memory.

Q2 · Explain 2 marks

Noorie's participants took part in both Condition A (acoustically similar words) and Condition B (acoustically different words), and the aim of the study was fairly easy for them to work out. Explain how demand characteristics could have affected Noorie's investigation, and how she could have reduced them.

Because each participant experienced both conditions, they saw the pattern of the study and could guess its aim (comparing similar- and different-sounding words). Once they guess, they may change their behaviour to perform as they believe is expected, for example concentrating harder in the second condition, so the measured recall no longer reflects normal memory and the findings lose validity. Noorie could have reduced this with a single-blind procedure: not telling participants the aim of the study, or disguising the purpose so clues are harder to read.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for explaining how guessing the aim changed behaviour in this study, 1 mark for a named control (single-blind / deception about the aim).

Q3 · Explain 4 marks

A researcher scores children's reading from video recordings. Concerned about her own bias, she asks a second researcher to score the same recordings independently, and the two sets of scores agree closely. Explain why this improves the quality of the research. Refer to objectivity and reliability in your answer.

Objectivity: scoring with another researcher, using agreed criteria rather than one person's opinion, removes the researcher's personal judgement from how the DV is recorded. The measurement is no longer open to one person's interpretation, like guessing a table's length instead of using a ruler.

Reliability: close agreement between two independent scorers is high inter-rater reliability: it shows the scores are consistent regardless of who does the scoring, so the data is not distorted by any single researcher's subjectivity, and another team could replicate the scoring and expect the same result.

Marking guidance: up to 2 marks each: objectivity (judgement removed from measurement) and reliability (agreement between raters shows consistency), both applied to this scoring scenario.

7. Key Terms

reliability
信度：研究结果的一致性，重测结果应稳定
test-retest reliability
重测信度：同一测量重复进行，结果保持一致
inter-rater reliability
评分者间信度：不同评分者对同一表现评分的一致程度
validity
效度：研究测到的确实是想测的行为或构念
internal validity
内部效度：结果确实由 IV 造成，未被混淆变量污染
predictive validity
预测效度：测量能预测同类标准上的未来表现
ecological validity
生态效度：结果能推广到真实日常情境
objectivity
客观性：测量不受研究者主观判断影响，换人同数
experimenter effects
实验者效应：主试的言行或在场（一个点头、一个微笑）影响结果
demand characteristics
需求特征：参与者猜到目的后按「被期待的方式」行动
