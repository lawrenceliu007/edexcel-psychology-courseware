1.3.1 CLASSIC STUDY · MOSCOVICI ET AL. (1969)
Influence of a consistent minority on the responses of a majority in a colour perception task

One classic study for Topic A: can a minority of two really pull a majority of four towards a wrong answer about a colour?
Topic A · Social psychology 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.6 · pp.59–70 Studies: classic Spec 1.3.1

What this study is really asking

  1. Two confederates insist, again and again, that a blue slide is green. Do the four real participants start saying green too? Yes, some do: 8.42% of answers in the experimental groups, against 0.25% in the control.
  2. Consistency is the engine. When the confederates wavered (green 24 times, blue 12), agreement collapsed to 1.25%.
  3. The influence goes deeper than words. In experiment two, people who had never publicly agreed still shifted their private colour judgements afterwards, so the minority had altered their perceptual code.
  4. Conclusion: a consistent minority can change how a majority actually sees things; majority influence tends to change only what people say.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Describe the procedure: colour judgments, consistent and inconsistent confederate conditions.
  • Recall the quantitative results for both confederate conditions.
  • Explain what the study demonstrates about minority influence.
  • Evaluate the study: sample, control, ecological validity.
  • Compare minority-influence research with Asch's majority research.

1. Getting Started

You are sitting in a row of six people, judging the colour of a slide on a screen. The slide is clearly, unmistakably blue. The person next to you says "green". The next one says "green" too.

It is your turn. What do you say?

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

Two people before you, out of four, have said "green" about a blue slide. What is most likely to happen across a whole experiment?

Option B is what Moscovici found. The pull of a minority is small, but it is real, and it depends on one thing above all: the minority never wavers.

中文理解：这一讲研究什么
上一讲（1.1.6）讲过少数派影响的理论。这一讲是支持它的经典实验：两个托儿面对四个真被试，把蓝色幻灯片一口咬定说成绿色，看真被试会不会跟着说绿。
要记的三个数字：8.42%（实验组随少数派的比例）、0.25%（控制组）、1.25%（托儿前后不一致时）。一个一致、一个不一致，对比全在这两个数之间。

2. Why This Is the Classic Study

Asch showed that a majority can bend an individual. Moscovici asked the reverse question: can a small minority bend the majority? Three reasons earn this study the label “classic”:

It tested the reverse direction

Asch had the many pressing the one. Here, two people press against four. Minority influence needed a laboratory test of its own.

It isolated the key behaviour style

Consistency was built into the design: some groups met a minority that never changed its answer, and one group met a minority that did. The comparison does the explaining.

It probed below the surface

A second experiment measured what happened inside perception, so the study could tell a changed answer apart from a changed way of seeing.

perceptual code（知觉码）: a way of perceiving or interpreting information. In the context of Moscovici's experiments, when a minority consistently presents a viewpoint, it can challenge the majority's perceptual code.
中文理解：perceptual code（知觉编码）
你看见蓝色，大脑把蓝色"翻译"给你，这一套翻译规则就是知觉编码。
少数派一直说"这是绿的"，等于在挑战你的翻译规则：你也许嘴上不改，但心里那套规则可能被掰动。实验二测的就是后者，它让人写下自己真正看到的颜色。

3. The Three Questions (Aims)

Serge Moscovici and colleagues ran a series of experiments in 1969. The study has three aims. Learn them as three questions:

#The question the study set out to answer
1Can social pressure from a minority influence a majority into giving an incorrect response, on a colour perception task in a group?
2Would a consistent minority exert a greater influence than an inconsistent minority?
3Does minority influence create only a change in verbal response, or also an actual change in perception?

How the three experiments map onto the aims

Question 1 and 2 are answered by the main procedure plus experiment three (the inconsistent version). Question 3 is answered by experiment two, where people judged colours in private. Keep this mapping in mind: exam questions often name an aim and ask which part of the study tested it.

4. Sample

FeatureDetail
WhoAll participants were female students of liberal arts, law or social science.
Group sizePlaced in groups of six.
Who was realTwo of each group were confederates of the study; four were naïve participants.
Why femaleThe researchers thought women would have a greater involvement in evaluating colour.
中文理解：为什么挑女生
研究者假设女生平时更在意颜色（穿衣服、化妆这类判断里颜色出现得多），所以对颜色的判断会更投入。
记住这个理由，评价题里"只用了女性，结论能不能推广"就顺着这条线答：设计上有理由，但样本单一仍是弱点。

5. The Colour Task

The apparatus was a screen onto which a series of blue coloured slides were projected using coloured filters. Each group sat in a row in front of it and was told the study was about colour perception.

FeatureDetail
The judgementsSay aloud the colour of the slide, and judge its light intensity on a scale of 0–5 (0 dimmest, 5 brightest). A brief explanation of light intensity was given first.
The slidesSix slides, each presented six times, for 15 seconds per slide.
TotalEach participant judged 36 slides in all.
Warm-upA practice trial came first; after that each slide was shown one at a time.
The minorityThe two confederates consistently reported the slides were green.
Figure 1 — The basic task: two confederates answer before the four naïve participants and keep saying "green".
中文理解：为什么题目要带一个亮度打分
光报颜色太简单，真被试一眼就能看出"这题是不是在骗人"。加一个 0–5 的亮度判断，整个任务看起来像一场正经的视知觉测量，托儿说错颜色才显得像"看法不同"，不像故意捣乱。
亮度判断还有另一个用处：结果分析时能看出光线越弱，少数派影响越大（见第 11 节）。

6. The Vision Check

Before any slide was shown, all group members were given a test to check for visual abnormalities.

This control did two jobs at once:

Job 1 · screen out poor vision

Anyone whose colour vision was off could be removed, so odd answers could not be blamed on the eyes.

Job 2 · convince the participants

Everyone saw the test happen, so each naïve participant knew the other five had normal vision. Nobody could dismiss the "green" answers as a visual problem.

Why this small detail earns marks

This was an important control: it removed the possibility that naïve participants would attribute different colour judgements to visual differences. If someone says "green" and you know their eyes are fine, the only remaining explanation is social. That is exactly the situation the study needed.

7. Variation One: Where the Confederates Sat

In the first variation of the procedure, the seating and answering positions of the two confederates were changed across groups:

GroupsWhat the confederates did
12 groupsSat next to each other and gave the first and second responses of the group.
20 groupsGave the first and fourth responses, with naïve participants answering in between. This was aimed at making the second confederate appear independent of the first.

The finding: there was no difference between the responses given under the two arrangements. Whether the two "green" voices sat together or were split apart, the amount of influence stayed the same.

中文理解：拆开坐是在测什么
两个托儿挨着坐，看起来像"一伙的"；一个答第 1 位、一个答第 4 位，中间隔着两个真被试，第二个托儿就显得像是自己独立做出的判断。
结果两种摆法影响一样大。起作用的始终是"又有人说绿了"这件事本身，托儿之间坐得远近、看起来像不像一伙，都排不上号。

8. Experiment Two: The Private Test

Aim 3 asked whether minority influence touches perception itself. Experiment two was built to answer it.

Ten groups who had completed experiment one were then asked to consent to taking part in another researcher's study on the effect of exercise on visual phenomena. The researcher gained agreement, left the room, and a second researcher entered and repeated the request.

FeatureDetail
SettingParticipants seated around a table, each isolated by cardboard screens.
The judgementPrivate: each person wrote the colour down on paper instead of saying it aloud.
The discs16 discs: three blue, three green, and ten ambiguous (between blue and green).
TimingEach disc shown one at a time, for five seconds, on a neutral background in the centre of the table.
Wrap-upThe first experimenter returned, ended the study and administered the post-experimental questionnaire.

What this experiment was designed to detect

Two possibilities. First, whether experiment one had a lasting effect on perception for those who yielded to the minority. Second, whether participants who did not publicly yield in experiment one, perhaps because they repressed their response during the social interaction, had still changed their perceptual code. Writing the answer down, alone behind a screen, strips away the social pressure. Whatever shift remains must live somewhere inside perception.

中文理解：换研究者这个戏演给谁看
新实验先让"另一个研究员"来接手，被试就会觉得这是两件不相干的事，不会联想到刚才那群说"绿"的托儿。
再加上纸板隔开、各自写答案：没有人在听，没有面子问题。这时候如果答案还偏向绿，就只能解释成脑子里的颜色标准真的动了。

9. Experiment Three: The Inconsistent Minority

Eleven groups were tested to see if the degree of consistency between the confederates had an impact on minority influence.

The one change

The two confederates said the slide was green 24 times and blue 12 times, demonstrating inconsistency in their judgement. Everything else stayed as before.

So the three experiments form a staircase:

Figure 2 — The three experiments, each answering a different aim, each with its own control group.

10. The Control Groups

A control group of 22 participants also made colour judgements in groups of six, with no confederates present. There was a control group for each of the experiments conducted.

What the control gives you

Without it, a few stray "green" answers could mean many things: a strange batch of slides, a projector drifting in colour, tired eyes. The control group saw the same slides with nobody pushing, and they said green for 0.25% of answers. That near-zero baseline is what lets Moscovici claim the green responses in the experimental groups came from minority influence.

11. Results: The Main Experiments

Moscovici et al. measured minority influence by the number of "green" responses given by the naïve participants.

ResultNumbers
Green responses, experimental groups (first two experiments, n = 128)8.42% of answers
Green responses, control group (22 participants)0.25% of answers
Naïve participants who conformed at least once (said a blue slide was green)32%
Within groups that yielded, naïve participants who matched the confederates57% (an average of two participants per group)
Figure 3 — Green responses under a consistent minority, no minority, and an inconsistent minority.

Three more findings complete the picture:

  • Seating made no difference: responses were the same whether the confederates answered first and second, or first and fourth.
  • Light intensity mattered: minority influence was greatest when the light intensity of the slide was weak — but even so, the green response was always higher in the experimental groups than in the controls.
  • Post-experimental interviews: those who tended to agree with the minority described the slides as a range of green-blue shades; those who did not agree claimed the slides were simply blue. This suggests members of the majority were genuinely making an effort to consider the minority's viewpoint, perhaps because the minority were exerting informational social influence.

12. Results: Experiments Two and Three

Experiment two · the private shift

Participants who did not publicly agree with the minority during experiment one were more likely to shift their judgement in phase two than those who did publicly agree. This reads as a modification in perceptual code, more than a change of verbal response.

Experiment three · the collapse

With an inconsistent minority (green 24, blue 12), only 1.25% of responses agreed with the minority. Consistency of the minority is therefore an important behaviour style.

中文理解：实验二的结果反着看才有味道
嘴上没跟着说"绿"的人，私下写答案时反而更偏向绿。这些人当着大家的面憋住了，可颜色标准已经被动过。
这就是少数派影响和从众（Asch 那种）最不一样的地方：多数派影响改的是"说什么"，少数派影响改的是"怎么想"。面积小，扎得深。

13. What the Results Mean (Conclusions)

Four sentences draw the threads together and are worth learning:

  1. Minorities can exert some, albeit small, influence in a laboratory situation using female participants, particularly if the minority are consistent in their viewpoint.
  2. A consistent minority can modify the perceptual code or norm of a majority.
  3. A consistent minority is more capable of modifying a majority's perceptive code than their social response.
  4. This contrasts with majority influence, which is more likely to influence a social response than affect a perceptual code.

The mirror-image idea (worth a sentence in any essay)

Majority influence and minority influence work on different layers. The majority tends to change what people say (social response); a consistent minority tends to change what people see (perceptual code). The direction of pressure and the depth of the change go hand in hand.

中文理解：结论的镜像结构
多数派改变「嘴上说什么」（社会反应），一致少数派改变「眼睛看到什么」（知觉码）。多数派靠人数压你表态，少数派靠坚持让你重新看世界；压力方向与改变层次互相配套。
记法：多数管「说」，少数管「看」。写论述题时这一句可以直接当论点。

14. Evaluation · Strengths

The headline verdict: the study was conducted using a highly standardised procedure with variables tightly controlled. Three controls do the work:

Strength 1 · The vision test（视力测试）

All group members took it, so anyone with visual problems could be removed, and participants knew everyone had good colour vision. They avoided treating the minority's judgements as incorrect because of visual issues. （先把看不清颜色的人筛掉，「说绿」才不会被当成看错。）

Strength 2 · Random allocation（随机分配）

Participants were randomly allocated to the experimental or control group, which removed the possible bias of certain types of personality being grouped together. （不同性格的人被随机打散，避免某种人格扎堆左右结果。）

Strength 3 · Controlled light intensities（控制光强）

Altering and analysing different light intensities of the slides and participants' responses controlled for the intensity of light affecting visual perception. （亮度这个额外变量被锁住，颜色判断的差异才说得准。）

The payoff of all this control: the study can be replicated to check whether minority influence is a consistent outcome. （控制得越严，别人越能照着重做一遍来验证。）

15. Evaluation · Limitations

Sample limitation（样本局限）

Only female liberal arts, law and social science students took part, so the findings may not represent the behaviour of males or non-students in minority influence situations. Conformity to a minority was tested on a female student population only.

Culture limitation（文化局限）

Only American students were used, and cross-cultural research shows considerable variation in social influence in different countries.

Ecological validity（生态效度）

A laboratory environment is artificial and may not reflect how minority groups exert influence over real-life situations and real issues. We cannot be sure minorities will exert the same influence over people in real situations.

The size question（规模问题·诚实读法）

The influence found was small: 8.42% of answers, from a minority of two against four. The verdict itself: “some, albeit small, influence”. A fair evaluation weighs the effect as well as its significance.

中文理解：三条弱点怎么记
一条管人（只有女学生，男女差异、非学生群体说不清），一条管地方（只有美国学生，各国社会影响差别大），一条管场景（实验室里的颜色判断太干净，现实里少数派改变多数靠的是真实议题）。
写答案时各配一句"所以结论只能推到哪"，就够两分。

16. Suggest an Improvement

Exam tip. You may be asked to suggest an improvement to research listed on the specification. You must suggest a suitable improvement that will actually improve the study. This can be identified by considering the weaknesses of a study. You must also justify why your suggested improvement will improve the study.

A worked example, built directly on the sample weakness:

Model answer

One improvement to Moscovici's research would be to use male and female participants, as Moscovici only used female students. This would be a more representative sample so that we can be sure the minority influence over a majority would extend to the wider population and increase generalisability.

Notice the structure: weakness first, then the fix, then the justification. Three moves, and the mark scheme has everything it wants.

17. Exam Focus

Classic vs contemporary. You have learned about two compulsory studies and one option study in this chapter. You can be asked for specific details of the classic study, but only general questions about your option study. When revising, separate each study into aim(s), sample, procedure, results and conclusion(s). If you are asked to describe the procedure, you will not get marks for describing the aim(s), sample, results or conclusion(s).
Likely questionWhat a full-mark answer needs
State two results of Moscovici et al.'s (1969) studyAny two of: 8.42% green responses in the experimental groups; 0.25% in the control; 32% conformed at least once; 57% of naïve participants in yielding groups (two per group on average); 1.25% with an inconsistent minority.
Suggest one improvement (2 marks)The fix (e.g. male and female participants) plus the justification (more representative, increases generalisability). One without the other is one mark.
Explain one weaknessName it (all-female student sample / American students only / artificial laboratory), then explain the consequence for what the conclusions can claim.
中文提示：复习时把研究拆成五格
目的（aim）、样本（sample）、过程（procedure）、结果（results）、结论（conclusion）一格一行分开背。题目问过程就只写过程，把目的或结果写进去都拿不到分。

19. Exam Lab

The two questions below are exam-style practice for this chapter.

中文点题：2 分题背两个带数字的结果：实验组 8.42% 绿色回答（对照组 0.25%），以及 32% 的人至少从众一次。

Q1 — State two results of Moscovici et al.'s (1969) study. (2 marks)

In social psychology, you will have learned about one classic study in detail: Moscovici et al. (1969) Influence of a consistent minority on the responses of a majority in a colour perception task.

Firstly, green responses were given for 8.42 per cent of answers by naïve participants in the experimental groups, compared with 0.25 per cent in the control group. Secondly, 32 per cent of naïve participants conformed to the minority colour judgement by saying that the blue slide was green on at least one occasion.

Two results, two marks: each one must carry a number or a clear comparison to count.

中文点题：改进和理由各占一分：招男女两种被试是「改进」，样本更有代表性、推广性更强是「理由」，只写一半只得一分。

Q2 — Suggest one improvement that could be made to Moscovici et al.'s (1969) study. (2 marks)

One improvement would be to use both male and female participants, since Moscovici only used female students. This would make the sample more representative, so the researcher could be sure that minority influence over a majority extends to the wider population, which would increase generalisability.

中文点题：先点名弱点（被试只有女学生），再讲后果（结论只能推到女学生群体，推广性受限），两句连起来才完整。

Q3 — Explain one weakness of Moscovici et al.'s (1969) study. (from the chapter checkpoint)

Only female liberal arts, law and social science students took part, so the findings may not represent the behaviour of males or non-students in minority influence situations. This limits the findings about conformity to a minority to a female student population only, which reduces the generalisability of the conclusions.

20. Key Terms

minority influence（少数派影响）
A small group changing the views or perceptions of a larger group; tested here with two confederates against four naïve participants.
perceptual code（知觉码）
A way of perceiving or interpreting information; a consistent minority can challenge the majority's perceptual code.
consistency（一致性）
The behaviour style of never changing your answer; the consistent minority said green every time, and this drove the influence.
inconsistent minority（不一致的少数派）
In experiment three, confederates said green 24 times and blue 12 times; agreement collapsed to 1.25%.
light intensity（光线强度）
The brightness of each slide, judged on a scale of 0 (dimmest) to 5 (brightest); influence was greatest on weak-intensity slides.
naïve participants（真被试（不知情参与者））
The four real members of each group, unaware that two respondents were confederates.
confederate（托儿（研究者助手））
Someone working for the researchers who pretends to be a participant; here they consistently reported the blue slides as green.
control group（控制组）
22 participants judging colours in groups of six with no confederates; their 0.25% baseline isolates the effect of minority influence.
informational social influence（信息性社会影响）
Agreeing with others because you believe they know something you do not; offered as the reason some participants genuinely weighed the minority's view.
post-experimental questionnaire（实验后问卷）
Given at the end of each experiment; interviews suggested agreeing participants saw green-blue shades rather than plain blue.
generalisability（可推广性）
The extent to which conclusions apply beyond the sample; weakened here by the all-female, all-student, all-American sample.
ecological validity（生态效度）
Whether findings hold in real life; the laboratory colour task may not reflect how minorities influence over real issues.
