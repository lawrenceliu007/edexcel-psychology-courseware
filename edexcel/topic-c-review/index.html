3.1 · Biological Bases（生物基础）

3.1.1

Neurons & Neural Communication（神经元 & 神经通讯）

  • Know the structure of a neuron: dendrite → axon → terminal button（树突 → 轴突 → 终扣）, plus the cell body, myelin sheath, Nodes of Ranvier（郎飞结）, synaptic vesicles.
  • Be able to label Figure 15.3 in your sleep — exam nearly always asks for a neuron diagram with 4–6 labels.
  • Action potential: resting potential（-70 mV）→ depolarisation → repolarisation → hyperpolarisation → return. Mention Na⁺ influx then K⁺ efflux.
  • Synaptic transmission is chemical, not electrical. Neurotransmitters cross the synaptic cleft and bind to receptors on the post-synaptic neuron.
In what order do Na⁺ and K⁺ channels open during an action potential, and what happens at the synapse afterwards?
3.1.2

The Brain（脑）— Structure & Localisation

  • Know each lobe's primary function: frontal（推理 + 运动 + Broca 语言）、parietal（体感）、occipital（视觉）、temporal（听觉 + Wernicke 语言）、cerebellum（平衡 + 精细动作）.
  • Aggression brain areas exam loves: prefrontal cortex（计划/冲动控制 — underactive in impulsive aggression）、amygdala（情绪/威胁 — overactive in reactive aggression）.
  • Methods of studying the brain: fMRI（活动）、EEG（电活动，毫秒精度）、PET scan（葡萄糖代谢）、autopsy（死后结构）、lesion studies（局部损伤→功能推断）.
  • Neuroplasticity: the brain re-wires itself with experience; relevant for both aggression (poor early environment → less PFC control) and rhythm disruption.
If a patient can't stop punching people but can name what they saw, which area is most likely impaired and which is overactive?
3.1.3

Biological Explanations of Aggression（攻击行为的生物学解释）

  • Neural: low PFC + high amygdala activity → impulsive / reactive aggression. Raine et al. (1997) showed reduced PFC glucose metabolism in murderers pleading NGRI.
  • Hormonal — testosterone: linked to dominance-seeking; serotonin (5-HT) low levels linked to impulsive aggression in animal + human studies.
  • Genetic: twin studies suggest ~50% heritability. MAOA is the most-examined gene — "warrior gene" — only predicts aggression when paired with childhood maltreatment (Caspi 2002, gene × environment).
  • Reductionism is the main critique: reducing complex behaviour to one hormone / gene ignores nurture.
Why did Caspi (2002) call MAOA a "warrior gene" but warn that the title is misleading without childhood data?

3.1 · Body Rhythms（生物节律）

3.1.4

Endogenous Pacemakers & Exogenous Zeitgebers（内源起搏器 & 外源授时因子）

  • Endogenous pacemaker（内源起搏器）: the body's internal clock — the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN) in the hypothalamus is the master clock for circadian rhythms.
  • Exogenous zeitgeber（外源授时因子）: external time cue that resets the clock — light is the dominant zeitgeber; others include social cues, temperature, melatonin.
  • Light → retina → SCN → pineal gland → melatonin release. Melatonin is high at night, low in day. SCN damage → loss of circadian rhythm (the rat studies).
A hamster with SCN lesions loses its rhythm. What is one piece of evidence that light alone can re-entrain it?
3.1.5

Circadian / Ultradian / Infradian Rhythms（昼夜 / 超昼夜 / 超昼夜长节律）

  • Circadian（昼夜节律）≈ 24 h: sleep-wake, cortisol, body temperature.
  • Ultradian（超昼夜节律）< 24 h: stages of sleep (REM cycles ~90 min), heart-rate variability, feeding bursts.
  • Infradian（超昼夜长节律）> 24 h: menstrual cycle (~28 d), seasonal migration, seasonal affective disorder.
Give one example each of an ultradian and an infradian rhythm, and the unit of measurement that proves which it is.
3.1.6

Disruption & Free-Running（节律失调 & 自由运转）

  • Free-running: a circadian rhythm drifts later each day (~25 h) when zeitgebers are removed — shown by Aschoff's bunker experiments and Siffre's cave.
  • Real-world disruption: jet lag (eastward harder than westward because the body must advance, not delay), shift work (chronic misalignment → sleep loss, metabolic syndrome, increased accident risk).
  • Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD): recurrent depressive episode tied to season — typically winter onset, remits in spring. Linked to over-production of melatonin as daylight shortens.
  • Treatments: bright-light therapy (≥10 000 lux at wake), melatonin supplementation, CBT-I for the sleep side.
A night-shift nurse is drowsy at 3 a.m. but wide awake at 9 a.m. just after the shift ends — explain using free-running and exogenous zeitgebers.

3.2 · Research Methods（研究方法）

3.2.1

Hypotheses（假设）

  • Null hypothesis (H₀) = "there is no difference / no relationship". Tested statistically — if p < critical value, reject H₀.
  • Alternative / experimental (H₁) = "there IS a difference / a relationship".
  • Directional = states the direction of the effect ("Group A will score higher than Group B"). Non-directional = just says there IS a difference.
  • Directional → one-tailed test; non-directional → two-tailed test. State them before collecting data.
You predict men score higher than women on a testosterone measure. What type of hypothesis and what type of test?
3.2.2

Experimental Design & Variables（实验设计 & 变量）

  • IV = independent variable（自变量，what you manipulate）; DV = dependent variable（因变量，what you measure）; EV = extraneous variable（额外变量，confounds you need to control）.
  • Control = a comparison condition held constant so any change in DV can be attributed to IV.
  • Repeated measures = same participants in all conditions (controls for participant variables but order effects); independent measures = different participants (no order effects but group differences); matched pairs = a hybrid.
  • In Topic C: most studies are quasi-experiments or correlational because you cannot ethically manipulate aggression genes / hormones.
In Raine (1997), what was the IV, the DV, and what did the control group do?
3.2.3

Sampling（抽样）

  • Random（随机抽样）: every member of the target population has equal chance → most generalisable.
  • Opportunity / convenience（便利抽样）: whoever is available → quick but biased.
  • Stratified（分层抽样）: split population by a known variable (e.g. age bands) then random-sample within each → representative on that variable.
  • Volunteer / self-selecting（自愿抽样）: people opt in → bias toward people with strong views.
  • Topic C examples: Raine used opportunity (death-row inmates); Caspi used a large birth cohort (Dunedin) → closer to random.
A study recruits only university students — what kind, and why is generalisability limited?
3.2.4

Data Analysis — Descriptive vs Inferential（描述 vs 推断统计）

  • Descriptive: mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, bar chart, histogram.
  • Inferential: tests of significance — Wilcoxon, Spearman, chi-square, related-samples t-test. These tell you whether the result is likely real or just chance.
  • Most Topic C questions ask you to calculate the test value and interpret the result against a critical value table.
You have correlated scores — which test do you use and why not Pearson's?
3.2.5

Inferential Tests — Pick The Right One（推断统计 · 选对题型）

  • Spearman's rho（斯皮尔曼等级相关）: two related ranked / ordinal variables → correlated data.
  • Wilcoxon signed-rank（威尔科克森符号秩）: repeated-measures data (same participants, two conditions) with ordinal scores.
  • Chi-square (χ²)（卡方）: frequencies / counts in categories (e.g. boys vs girls × aggressive vs non-aggressive).
  • Related-samples t-test: repeated-measures with interval data.
  • Rule: naming the test gets 1 mark; justifying why (level of data, design) gets the second.
Same participants ranked aggression before and after a hormone injection — which test, and why?
3.2.6

Significance & Errors（显著性 & 错误类型）

  • Significance level: usually p ≤ 0.05 (5% chance the result is due to sampling error).
  • Type I error: rejecting H₀ when it is actually true (false alarm).
  • Type II error: failing to reject H₀ when it is actually false (missed effect).
  • One-tailed vs two-tailed test changes the cut-off — for α = 0.05, two-tailed critical r ≈ 0.329 (df 28); one-tailed ≈ 0.281.
A study finds p = 0.06 and concludes "no effect". What type of error might they have made?
3.2.7

Validity, Reliability, Generalisability（信度、效度、可推广性）

  • Internal validity（内部效度）: did the IV actually cause the change in DV, or could confounds explain it? High in lab experiments.
  • External validity / generalisability: can the result be applied to other people / settings / times?
  • Reliability（信度）: would the same result be obtained again? Test-retest, internal consistency.
  • Ecological validity（生态效度）: does the study reflect real-life behaviour? Low in scanner studies, high in field studies.
  • Trade-off: lab = high internal validity, low ecological validity.
An fMRI scanner study on 20 murderers — discuss internal validity, ecological validity, and generalisability.

3.3 · Core Studies（核心研究）

3.3.1

Brain & Aggression — Raine et al. (1997)

  • Aim: compare brain activity in murderers pleading not guilty by reason of insanity (NGRI) vs non-murderers.
  • Method: PET scans while doing a continuous-performance task; 41 NGRI vs 41 controls matched on handedness, age, ethnicity, gender, drug use.
  • Finding: NGRI group showed reduced glucose metabolism in prefrontal cortex but no difference in occipital or temporal cortex.
  • Conclusion: PFC under-activity → reduced impulse control → reactive aggression.
  • Critiques: small N, no random assignment, ecological validity (scanner ≠ real life), reductionism.
State Raine's IV, DV, controls, sampling technique, and one ethical issue.
3.3.2

Hormones & Aggression — Testosterone & Serotonin

  • Testosterone: associated with dominance-seeking, not direct aggression. Levels rise after winning (Mazur's "biosocial model"). Animal studies: castration reduces aggression in many species.
  • Serotonin (5-HT): low levels → impulsive aggression. Reduced serotonin metabolite (5-HIAA) in CSF linked to aggression in violent offenders and suicide attempters.
  • Both are correlational: cannot prove causation. Testosterone may rise after winning, not before.
Why is a low-serotonin → aggression finding correlational, not causal, and what design would be needed to test causation?
3.3.3

Genetics of Aggression — Twin Studies & MAOA

  • Twin studies: monozygotic (MZ) concordance > dizygotic (DZ) for aggression → heritable component. Concordance ≈ 0.50 MZ vs 0.20 DZ in many studies.
  • Adoption studies（收养研究）: biological vs adoptive parents — biological parent's criminal history predicts child's criminality more than adoptive parent's.
  • Caspi et al. (2002) MAOA: gene × environment interaction. Males with low-MAOA + childhood maltreatment → significantly higher conviction rates; low-MAOA alone → no effect.
  • Brunner et al. (1993) Dutch family — single X-linked MAOA mutation → impulsive aggression across generations.
Why does a low-MAOA + maltreatment finding NOT support genetic determinism?
3.3.4

Body Rhythms — SAD & Shift Work

  • SAD study (e.g. the "Noah" 2025 paper): comparison of mood scores across seasons, plus a bright-light therapy intervention vs control.
  • Outcome: bright-light therapy in the morning reduces depressive symptoms more than evening light or dim control.
  • Shift work study (e.g. Czeisler-type or the "Liesel" 2026 paper): chronic misalignment → increased accident rate, metabolic issues, shortened sleep.
  • Both rely on endogenous pacemaker (SCN) being out of phase with light-dark cycle.
Why is morning light more effective than evening light for SAD, in terms of melatonin?

3.4 · Practical Investigation（实操研究）

3.4.1

Practical Investigation — Correlational Design（实操 · 相关性研究）

  • You design a study collecting two related variables (e.g. daily sleep duration vs daily mood score) across at least 10 participants.
  • Use Spearman's rho if both variables are ranked / ordinal, or related-samples t-test if comparing two conditions.
  • Write-up needs: aim, hypothesis (directional or non-directional), design, IV / DV, controls, sample, procedure, ethics, results, conclusion, evaluation.
  • Correlation ≠ causation is the central evaluation point.
If your Spearman r = +0.62 and critical r (df 18, α = 0.05 two-tailed) = 0.444 — significant?

4 · Must-Know Studies（必记研究）

Six studies that show up most often in Topic C exam questions. Each card has the AMSPRC skeleton: Aim · Method · Sample · Procedure · Results · Conclusion.

Brain

Raine, Buchsbaum & LaCasse (1997) — Brain Abnormalities in Murderers Pleading NGRI

PET scan · Quasi-experiment · N = 82 (41 NGRI + 41 controls) · USA

  • Aim: test whether murderers pleading NGRI show reduced PFC activity.
  • Method: PET scan during continuous-performance task. IV: group (NGRI / control). DV: glucose metabolism in PFC. Controls: handedness, age, ethnicity, gender, drug history.
  • Results: NGRI group showed reduced PFC activity especially on the right; no diff on occipital / parietal cortex.
  • Conclusion: PFC dysfunction may contribute to impulsive aggression.
  • Critique: small N; cannot establish causation (correlational); low ecological validity; reductionism.
Experimental

Brunner et al. (1993) — Dutch MAOA-L Family

Genetic case study · 5 generations · Netherlands

  • Aim: link a single-gene mutation to impulsive aggression.
  • Method: pedigree analysis of a Dutch kindred with a stop-codon mutation in MAOA; affected males showed impulsive aggression (arson, rape, attempted murder).
  • Results: all affected males had low MAOA activity; unaffected males had normal MAOA.
  • Critique: small N, single family → low generalisability; biological determinism — single gene cannot fully explain behaviour; ethical issues around genetic screening.
Longitudinal

Caspi et al. (2002) — MAOA × Maltreatment in the Dunedin Cohort

Gene × environment · N = 442 males · NZ · 26-year follow-up

  • Aim: test whether MAOA moderates the effect of childhood maltreatment on adult antisocial behaviour.
  • Method: DNA at age 3 + maltreatment records + self-report antisocial behaviour at 26.
  • Results: low-MAOA + high maltreatment → 4× conviction rate; low-MAOA alone → no effect; high-MAOA + maltreatment → protective.
  • Critique: cohort effect (1980s NZ may not generalise); self-report bias; gene × environment still does not rule out reverse causation or epistasis.
Contemp

SAD Treatment Study (e.g. May 2025 WPS02 Q5 "Noah")

Randomised controlled trial · morning bright light vs evening vs dim control

  • Aim: test whether morning bright light is more effective than evening light for SAD.
  • Method: random allocation to 10 000 lux morning / 10 000 lux evening / dim control; 30 min daily, 4 weeks; mood measured by SIGH-SAD.
  • Results: morning light produced significantly greater reduction in depression scores than evening or dim conditions; effect size d ≈ 0.8.
  • Critique: small N; expectancy effects (placebo light); demand characteristics; doesn't work for everyone.
Contemp

Shift-Work Study (e.g. May 2026 WPS02 Q5 "Liesel")

Cross-sectional correlational · night-shift nurses vs day-shift

  • Aim: examine the relationship between years of night-shift work and sleep quality / metabolic markers.
  • Method: questionnaire 67 wrist-actigraphy sleep measure; blood markers for HbA1c and cortisol.
  • Results: night-shift workers showed reduced sleep efficiency, elevated HbA1c, blunted cortisol rhythm.
  • Critique: correlational (cannot prove causation); opportunity sampling; self-report bias; ecological validity is high.
Contemp

Twin Studies of Aggression — Concordance Comparison

Meta-analysis · many twin registries

  • Concordance for aggressive / antisocial behaviour ≈ 0.50 MZ vs 0.20 DZ.
  • Heritability estimate ≈ 0.50: roughly half the variance is genetic, half environmental.
  • Critique: MZ twins share more than just genes (identical treatment assumption violated in some studies); modern estimates vary widely.

5 · Methods Quick Card（方法速查）

Decide-on-the-spot reference for design, sampling and statistical tests.

Hypothesis types

TypeWhat it saysTest implied
Null (H₀)"No difference / no relationship"Whatever test you compute
Alternative (H₁) — non-directional"There IS a difference / a relationship"Two-tailed test
Alternative (H₁) — directional"Group A scores HIGHER than Group B"One-tailed test

Pick the right inferential test

SituationTestData level
Two related conditions, ranked scoresWilcoxon signed-rankOrdinal, repeated measures
Two variables both ranked / correlatedSpearman's rhoOrdinal, correlational
Counts in categories (e.g. aggressive vs not, male vs female)Chi-square (χ²)Nominal
Same participants in two conditions, interval dataRelated-samples t-testInterval, repeated measures
Different participants in two conditions, interval dataIndependent-samples t-testInterval, independent groups

Validity types — know the difference

TypeQuestion it answersWhen it's high
Internal validityDid the IV actually cause the change?Lab experiments with good controls
External validity / generalisabilityCan it be applied to other people / settings?Random sampling, large samples, real-world settings
Ecological validityDoes it reflect everyday behaviour?Field studies, naturalistic tasks
Test validityDoes the measure test what it claims?Standardised, validated scales (e.g. SIGH-SAD)
ReliabilityWould the same result be obtained again?Test-retest, inter-rater, internal consistency

Errors

Type I error = false positive — H₀ rejected when it was true. Probability = α (usually 0.05).

Type II error = false negative — H₀ not rejected when it was false. Higher when N is small or effect size is small.

6 · Body Rhythms Quick Card（生物节律速查）

What the examiner expects you to know about circadian rhythms, SAD and shift work.

Three rhythm lengths

RhythmLengthExamples
Ultradian< 24 hREM cycle ≈ 90 min, heart-rate variability, feeding bursts
Circadian≈ 24 hSleep-wake, cortisol, body temperature, melatonin
Infradian> 24 hMenstrual cycle ≈ 28 d, seasonal mood, hibernation

The clockwork

Light → retina → SCN → pineal gland → melatonin

SCN = master clock; pineal gland turns melatonin on at night. Light is the dominant cue. Other zeitgebers: social contact, temperature, melatonin supplements.

SAD — quick reference

  • Diagnostic feature: recurrent major depressive episodes with seasonal pattern, usually winter onset, remitting in spring/summer.
  • Mechanism: shorter daylight → over-production of melatonin → delayed / disrupted circadian rhythm → depressive symptoms.
  • Treatments:
    • Bright-light therapy (≥ 10 000 lux, morning) — most evidence.
    • Melatonin supplementation in the evening — phase-shifts the rhythm.
    • SSRIs — pharmacological option for severe cases.
    • CBT-I for the sleep symptoms.

Shift work & jet lag

Shift work & jet lag are both forms of circadian desynchronisation: the internal clock (≈25 h free-running) is out of phase with the light-dark cycle.

  • Jet lag: harder travelling eastward (must advance, not delay).
  • Shift work: chronic — increases accident risk, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease.
  • Free-running: rhythm drifts later (~25 h) when zeitgebers are removed — Aschoff / Siffre bunker experiments.

7 · Common Exam Traps（常见考点陷阱）

Six mistakes that trip up otherwise-prepared students. Read once, then never make them.

Correlation ≠ causation

Low serotonin correlates with aggression. Doesn't mean low serotonin causes it.

Fix: always evaluate "what third variable could explain both?"
Free-running ≠ insomnia

Free-running rhythm (≈25 h drift) is a phase shift, not a sleep disorder.

Fix: a free-running rhythm still allows sleep — it's just out of sync with the clock.
One-tailed vs two-tailed

Directional hypothesis → one-tailed test, NOT just the same test.

Fix: state the direction first, then pick the correct critical value from the table.
MAOA is NOT destiny

Caspi (2002): low-MAOA alone has NO effect on adult antisocial behaviour.

Fix: the effect appears only with childhood maltreatment. Always mention the G×E interaction.
Raine's PFC under-activity ≠ "no brain"

PFC was underactive relative to controls, not absent. The NGRI group still had PFC — just less activity.

Fix: state findings with precision — "reduced glucose metabolism" not "no PFC".
Confusing MAD with mean deviation

When asked to "show your workings" for a calculation, MAD needs you to (a) compute deviations, (b) absolute-value them, (c) sum, (d) divide by N.

Fix: lay out the table — x | mean(x) | |x − mean| — then a single line equation.
