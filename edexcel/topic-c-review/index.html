1. Topic C at a Glance（一页看懂 Topic C）

Topic C is Biological psychology（生物心理学）. The specification introduces it like this:

📖 Topic overview (specification, p.22)

“Students must show an understanding that biological psychology is about the mechanisms within our body and how they affect our behaviour, focusing on aggression and body rhythms.”

So Topic C splits into two content strands, plus three skill strands that sit underneath them:

StrandWhat it is aboutSpecificationChapter
1. Aggression The body as an explanation of aggressive behaviour — neurons, brain areas, hormones, genes 3.1.1 – 3.1.3 Ch 15
2. Body rhythms Circadian and infradian rhythms — the sleep-wake cycle, the menstrual cycle, SAD and its therapies 3.1.4 – 3.1.6 Ch 16
3. Methods Correlational research, Spearman’s rank, brain scanning (CAT / PET / fMRI), twin studies 3.2.1 – 3.2.7 Ch 17
4. Studies One classic study (Raine) and three contemporary studies (Brendgen, McDermott, Hoefelmann) 3.3.1 – 3.3.4 Ch 18
5. Practical investigation Design and run your own correlational study, analyse it with Spearman’s rank, write it up 3.4.1 Ch 19
一句话逻辑：Topic C 的“内容”只有两条主线——攻击性（身体如何解释攻击行为）和人体节律（昼夜节律与亚昼夜节律）；但它们永远和“方法”（相关研究 + Spearman）、“研究”（4 篇指定研究）、“实践调查”绑在一起考。所以复习顺序应该是：先学攻击性与节律的内容，再用四篇研究把内容“落地”，最后用方法与实践调查把分数“兑现”。

🎯 How much of Paper 2 is Topic C?
  • Section A = 34 marks, always 100% Topic C.
  • Section C: the 12-mark question is usually Topic C (4 of our 5 papers), and the 16-mark question always combines Topic C with learning theories.
  • So a realistic Topic C share is 34 + 12 = 46 marks, or 34 + 8 = 42 marks when the 12-marker goes to learning theories instead.
关键理解：Section A 是“纯 Topic C”，34 分，题目位置五年没变过（见 §6）。Section C 是“Topic C 与学习理论（Topic D）的合体卷”——12 分题多数时候考 Topic C，16 分题永远是两边各取一头（通常 Topic C 一边是基因或疗法，学习理论一边是社会学习理论或弗洛伊德）。

💡 Note on 3.2.7

3.2.7 says “List A from Topic A” — that is the descriptive-statistics list (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, graphs, percentages). It is reused here rather than taught here. Chapter 17 applies it in practice: you collect data, choose a graph, and comment on percentages. Descriptive statistics also drive the early parts of Section A questions about data tables and scatter diagrams.

4. The Five Chapters（五章分工）

🧠 Chapter 15 — Biological Psychology: Brain Regions and Aggression 3.1.1 – 3.1.3
  • Nervous system overview, neuron structure, action potential, synaptic transmission
  • Brain lobes and brain areas; the pre-frontal cortex and the limbic system
  • Neurotransmitters (serotonin, dopamine, GABA) and aggression
  • Hormones — testosterone and cortisol; Genetics — MAOA, twin and adoption evidence, Caspi et al.
  • Wider issues and debates, plus a six-question Exam Practice set
🌙 Chapter 16 — Body Rhythms 3.1.4 – 3.1.6
  • Circadian, ultradian, infradian and circannual rhythms; the sleep-waking cycle
  • SCN, melatonin and the pineal gland; internal pacemakers versus exogenous zeitgebers
  • Evidence: Siffre, Aschoff & Wever, Folkard, DeCoursey, Ralph
  • The menstrual cycle and the HPG axis; PMS / PMDD; menstrual synchrony
  • Seasonal affective disorder; light therapy; SSRIs
  • Real-paper focus on May 2025 and May 2026 Section C questions with official mark schemes
🔗 Chapter 17 — Methods: Correlation, Spearman, Scanning and Twin Studies 3.2.1 – 3.2.7
  • Correlation and co-variables; positive, negative and zero correlations
  • Scatter diagrams — reading, describing and drawing them
  • Spearman’s rank test — step-by-step calculation, tied ranks, rs, critical values, significance
  • Evaluating correlations — cause and effect, third variables
  • CAT, PET and fMRI scanning — uses, strengths and weaknesses
  • Twin studies, concordance rates and the nature–nurture debate
🔬 Chapter 18 — Studies in Biological Psychology 3.3.1 – 3.3.4
  • Raine et al. (1997) — PET scans of 41 NGRI murderers versus 41 controls; pre-frontal cortex, amygdala, hippocampus, thalamus
  • Brendgen et al. (2005) — 234 six-year-old twin pairs; physical versus social aggression
  • McDermott et al. (2008) — MAOA-L versus MAOA-H, the power-to-take game and the hot-sauce paradigm
  • Hoefelmann et al. (2006) — Brazilian night-school students; sleep, lifestyle, cross-sectional and prospective analysis
  • Each study broken down with the AMSPRC six-step frame, plus evaluation and model answers
🛠️ Chapter 19 — Practical Investigation 3.4.1
  • A seven-step workflow: Aim → Design → Procedure → Data → Significance → Validity & Improvements → Report writing
  • Your own correlational study into aggression or body rhythms, analysed with Spearman’s rank
  • Diagnostic Hikaru cases at every step, so you practise spotting weaknesses in a design
  • Three exam-style model answers on procedure, significance and validity

🎯 Two stable habits in Section C
  • The 12-mark question is a single-topic evaluation. It went to Topic C in 4 of 5 papers — and within Topic C, body rhythms (circadian, SAD, internal pacemakers) is the recurring favourite.
  • The 16-mark question is always a two-strand synoptic essay: one biological strand + one learning-theory strand. Biology’s contribution is usually genes (3 of 5 papers) or a therapy. Also, every 16-marker carries the instruction “You must refer to the context in your answer.”

📌 What the slot table tells you

  • Q1 and Q3 rotate around the four named studies. Raine and Brendgen each appeared twice; the contemporary choice (McDermott or Hoefelmann) appeared in the Jun 2024 Q1 and the Jan 2025 12-marker. Learn all four — you cannot pick which one is asked.
  • Q2 is always a method question, and it always carries the heaviest method marks of the section: graph drawing, an operationalised hypothesis, a Spearman calculation, or sampling and level of measurement.
  • Q4 is the “equipment” slot — a therapy (light therapy), a scanning technique (PET) or a research design (twin studies).
  • Q5 opens with Assess / Discuss / Evaluate and needs AO3 balance. Hormones has taken this slot in three of five papers.
中文提示：Section A 的五道题不是随机排的。Q1 考“研究”，Q2 考“方法/统计”，Q3 考“解释”，Q4 考“技术或疗法”，Q5 是 8 分大论述。复习时按这个位置去准备，比按章节顺序背要有效得多——尤其 Q2，它几乎决定你 Section A 的一半方法分。

📌 Reading the table

  • Q1 and Q3 are AO1-heavy — they ask you to know the study or the explanation. Describe accurately and you score. Add a strength or weakness for the AO3 mark.
  • Q2 is AO2-dominated — this is the apply slot. Hypotheses, calculations, graph drawing, sampling decisions and ethics all live here.
  • Q5 is a 4 + 4 balance — four marks for knowledge, four for evaluation. An answer that only describes and never weighs up cannot go beyond half marks. The one exception is Jan 2025, where Q5 was applied to a scenario and the second four marks were AO2.
  • Jun 2026 is worth noting: Q3 and Q4 were both moved into AO2 by giving them a named scenario (Shaima, Huberta). Scenario framing pushes a question from “describe” into “apply”, even though the underlying content is unchanged.

🎯 What this means for how you write
  • The 16-marker always gives 6 marks for knowledge and 10–12 for evaluation. Knowledge alone, however accurate, is capped at about a third. You must build a case, weigh both sides and reach a judgement.
  • Where AO2 appears (4 marks), it is asking you to apply the material to the named context in the question. Use the named person’s behaviour as your evidence, not generic textbook lines.

🔍 An honest note on what this table is

These counts come from five exam sessions (January 2024 to June 2026). They show what has been asked, not what will be asked. Five papers is a small sample, and examiners deliberately rotate content — which is exactly why 3.1.1 has not yet appeared. Treat the ranking as a guide to where to spend your revision time, not as a list of what you can safely skip.
