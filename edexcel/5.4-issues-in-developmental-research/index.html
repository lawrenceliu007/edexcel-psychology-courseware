5.4 ISSUES IN DEVELOPMENTAL PSYCHOLOGY
scientific status · strengths and weaknesses · ethical issues · 发展心理学的研究议题（WPS03 synoptic 来源）

Topic E · Developmental psychology Issues Spec 5.4.1–5.4.3 📖 Textbook Book 2 · Ch.6 · pp.85–90

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Assess the scientific status of developmental research using falsifiability, replicability, objectivity and empirical evidence.
  • Evaluate theories (supporting versus conflicting evidence, applications, comparisons) and studies (generalisability, application, reliability, validity, ethics).
  • Assess ethical issues as an issue in developmental psychology, using the cases of Genie and Bowlby's 44 Thieves.
  • Answer the synoptic questions: justify a claim (2 marks), an ethical weakness (2 marks), a scenario (4 marks) and a full 8-mark discussion.

What this page is really asking

  1. Is developmental psychology scientific? It depends which test you apply. Theories must be falsifiable; studies must be replicable, objective and (arguably) empirical.
  2. Genie is the running example. One neglected girl tested critical periods, exposed researcher bias, and raised ethics questions no adult study ever could.
  3. Some of our best-known work fails the tests. Erikson's stage 7 cannot be falsified; Bowlby's 44 Thieves interviews cannot be replicated; Hazan and Shaver's love quiz ran on subjective memory.
  4. Ethics is an "issue" only where people disagree. Genie's researchers were also her foster carers — was that care, exploitation, or both at once?

Start Here: The Girl in the Basement

背景1970 年代，洛杉矶。一个 13 岁的女孩被发现锁在地下室里：十年与社会隔绝、没有玩具、没有人跟她说话，一天只被喂一次婴儿食品。她不会走路，只会发出两个词：「mama」和「drink」。研究者叫她 Genie。
角色你是研究团队的一员。你们意识到：Genie 已经错过了依恋和语言发展的关键期。如果她仍能形成依恋、学会说话，就是对两大理论的反驳；如果不能，就是支持。这个机会千载难逢。
任务研究她、照顾她、教她说话。但你同时也是她的抚养人。这中间的每一个身份，都会在后面被拿出来审问。
目标先猜下面这题。读完这一页，你能用四把科学性的尺子量完本主题所有大研究，也能说清 Genie 案例在伦理上为什么至今有争议。猜完往下追两层。

先猜Genie 后来学会了大量词汇，却始终说不出符合语法的句子。这最支持哪个结论？

追问猜完先别走：这两个问题没有选项，先自己想，再点开提示。

追问一：Genie 对一位研究者形成了依恋。这对 Bowlby 的敏感期假说是支持还是反驳？

提示

偏向反驳：如果过了关键期就绝对无法形成依恋，Genie 的依恋不应该出现。但注意，这只是削弱不是推翻，因为她的依恋对象恰恰是研究者本人，而研究者的解读可能不客观（他们太想看到进步了）。Bowlby 说的是 sensitive period（敏感期），不是铁闸门。

追问二：Genie 的 EEG 显示过类似智力障碍的脑波。这个证据是研究前就有的，还是事后追溯的？这对结论有什么影响？

提示

是追溯的（retrospective：用历史资料和既有记录做研究）。这留下一个无法闭合的漏洞：如果 Genie 本来就有学习障碍，她学不会语法可能跟关键期毫无关系。案例研究的历史没法重来一遍，这个替代解释永远排除不掉。

Genie 会陪着我们走完这一页：每讲一条科学性的标准，就回头看看这个案例过不过得了这一关。

1. What Makes Research Scientific

Four tests decide whether work counts as scientific. Two apply to theories, two apply to studies — keep that split clear, because exam questions punish mixing them up.

  • Falsifiability（可证伪性：必须有可能证明一个理论是错的；无法证伪的理论不科学） — a theory test. If every possible outcome fits the theory, it explains nothing.
  • Replicability（可复制性：程序详细、标准化、原始材料公开，别人能重做这项研究） — a study test.
  • Objectivity（客观性：描述看到的事实，而不是解释你认为它意味着什么） — a study test.
  • Empirical evidence（实证证据：通过感官直接获得的观察或实验证据，而非纯逻辑推理） — arguably required: the evidence should be directly observable.

Modern science adds a wrinkle: researchers increasingly use computer modelling of neural networks to test theories of language acquisition. The machines have no senses, so the data is not empirical in the classic way — but that may mean older definitions of science are lagging behind, not that the new work is unscientific.

中文理解：四把尺子分两组：理论要过「可证伪性」；研究要过「可复制性、客观性、实证性」。电脑建模那段的考点：新方法不算经典意义的实证，但更可能是「科学的定义落后于技术」，不是「新研究不科学」。falsifiability 属于理论层面的属性，replicability 属于研究层面的属性，两者不要混。

2. Testing Our Own Studies

Now apply the four tests to work you already know. Each one passes some tests and fails others.

  • Genie and falsifiability: the critical-period claim for language can be refuted by one child who missed the period yet learned grammar. Genie never did, so the case supports Lenneberg — but her past is retrospective（追溯的：用历史资料和既有记录做研究，无法重新验证）, so a pre-existing learning disability can never be ruled out.
  • Erikson fails falsifiability. Stage 7 says generativity brings wellbeing — but over-extension (too much giving) brings poor mental health. Helping helps; helping too much harms. Every result fits, so nothing can disconfirm it. This is why Popper would call it unfalsifiable.
  • Bowlby's 44 Thieves fails replicability. To check his diagnoses you would need the interview transcripts — but they were almost certainly never recorded, and his case notes may include only details that confirmed him. Selective recording is subjective and untestable. Modern studies using published interview schedules like the AAI are replicable instead.
  • Hazan and Shaver fail objectivity. Their questionnaire asked adults to recall childhood and parental relationships from memory — and siblings remember the same childhood differently.
  • Dual roles damage objectivity. Genie's researchers were also her therapists and foster carers: emotionally tied to her, and highly motivated to see progress others might not see. Mindfulness researchers teaching their own classes had the same problem.
  • Lorenz passes the empirical test: goslings really do imprint on the first moving thing they see — you can watch it happen. Evolutionary explanations are hard to test directly, but animal work makes them testable in part.

Back to Genie: her case weakens Bowlby's sensitive period, supports Lenneberg's critical period for grammar, and fails objectivity through its dual roles — three different verdicts from one case, which is exactly why the exam loves it.

中文理解：用四把尺子量本主题的研究，记四组配对。可证伪性：Genie 支持语言关键期（词汇能补语法不能补），Erikson 第七阶段不可证伪（帮助有益、帮助过度有害，怎么都对），retrospective（追溯性）让学习障碍这个替代解释永远排除不掉。可复制性：Bowlby 44 小偷访谈没录音、案例笔记可能只记了证实他的细节，复制不了；用公开 AAI 访谈提纲的现代研究可以复制。客观性：Hazan & Shaver 靠回忆问卷太主观；Genie 研究者身兼抚养人（dual role 双重角色）；正例是 Lorenz 的印刻看得见。实证性：电脑建模不是经典实证，但可能是科学定义落后了。

3. Evaluating Theories and Studies

Two checklists cover every evaluation question in this topic.

Theories: evaluate in three ways

  • Does supporting evidence outweigh conflicting evidence?
  • Do applications work? Has the theory produced measurable positive (or negative) effects in real life, and can it explain real situations?
  • How does it compare with rival theories in predicting outcomes and explaining events?

Studies: evaluate in five ways

  • Generalisability — does the sample represent the target population?
  • Application — do the findings help real life?
  • Reliability — can the findings be replicated?
  • Validity — was it biased, artificial or confounded?
  • Ethics — were participants protected?

Back to Genie: as evidence, the case both weakens the attachment sensitive period and strengthens Lenneberg — but as a study, it is retrospective, subjective and impossible to replicate, so neither conclusion should be treated as settled. The evidence needs company before either theory changes.

中文理解：理论评价三条：支持证据是否压过冲突证据；应用有没有产生可测量的实际效果；跟对立理论比谁的预测力强。研究评价五条，按顺序是可推广性与应用，再加信度、效度和伦理。Genie 一案两边都用得上：作为证据它同时削弱 Bowlby（依恋敏感期被夸大）和支持 Lenneberg（语法关键期），但作为研究它追溯、主观、无法复制，所以两个结论都不能当成定案。

4. Ethics: Genie and Bowlby

Remember the rule from 5.3: something is only an issue if people can disagree about it. Genie's case gives you disagreement in abundance.

The case against the Genie researchers

  • Consent and conflict of interest. Genie was a vulnerable minor in state care, unable to consent to endless testing. Some of the people testing her were also her foster carers — so who was acting for her?
  • Right to withdraw. Could she leave the study? Nobody could ask her meaningfully, and her caregivers were the researchers.
  • Confidentiality. A case this personal cannot be fully anonymised; people who knew her could identify every detail.
  • Protection from harm. Her mother later sued the research team, accusing them of "excessive and outrageous testing" (PBS, 1997).

The case for studying Genie

  • The knowledge mattered. Her case was a rare real-world test of two major theories — critical periods for attachment and language — that no experiment could ever create.
  • She was cared for, not just studied. Researchers taught her, and one family fostered her; some involvement was genuine care.
  • Balancing act. Ethics committees weigh protection against the cost of not doing research. Removing all risk would also remove all learning about how to help children like Genie.

Bowlby's 44 Thieves raises the same issues from a different era. When he published in 1944, Britain had no ethical guidelines at all — the BPS code came decades later. He likely had parental consent, but mothers may not have known their children would appear in the report by first name. Anonymisation used initials (Fred B), yet so much family detail was included that people who knew them could identify them. One mother is described as "cruel" — sensitive information that could cause real psychological harm. And families referred by courts and schools may never have understood they could withdraw.

中文理解：伦理议题用「正反两案」记。反方：Genie 无法给出同意，研究者和抚养人身份重叠造成利益冲突，退出权无法行使，隐私保不住；她母亲后来还起诉研究团队「过度残忍的测试」。正方：这个案例是两大关键期理论唯一的真实世界检验；研究者确实也在照顾和教她；伦理权衡本身就要算「不做研究的代价」。Bowlby 1944 年发表时英国没有任何伦理指南：可能有家长同意，但妈妈们未必知道孩子会以名字出现在报告里；用首字母匿名但细节多到能认人；一位母亲被描述为「cruel」（残忍）；被法院和学校转介来的家庭未必知道自己能退出。

5. Exam Lab

Q1 · Justify 2 marks

Justify the claim that developmental psychology is not scientific.

Some of its best-known work fails the scientific tests (1 mark). For example, Bowlby's 44 Thieves interviews were not recorded, so the study cannot be replicated and his case notes may only include details that confirmed his diagnoses; Erikson's stage 7 cannot be falsified, because both high and low involvement in helping are explained by the theory (1 mark).

Marking guidance: 1 mark for a named example, 1 mark for linking it to a scientific criterion (replicability or falsifiability).

Q2 · Explain 2 marks

Explain one ethical weakness of research in developmental psychology.

Weakness: researchers playing dual roles. Genie's researchers were also her therapists and foster carers (1 mark). This created a conflict of interest: she could not meaningfully consent or withdraw from testing done by her own caregivers, and the researchers' emotional ties meant they may have interpreted her progress more positively than an objective observer would (1 mark).

Marking guidance: 1 mark for the issue with a named example, 1 mark for explaining the consequence. Bowlby's 44 Thieves (no guidelines, recognisable anonymity, unclear right to withdraw) is an equally valid choice.

Q3 · Apply 4 marks

A newspaper reports a boy found after five years living in the jungle. He makes snarling sounds, screams like a monkey, and can say only "mama" and "drink". Local psychologists are excited to meet him. Explain two issues the researchers must consider to make sure their research is both ethical and scientific.

Ethical issue: consent and dual roles. Like Genie's team, the researchers would be caring for an extremely vulnerable child while studying him. They must ensure someone independent of the research protects his interests, that testing is minimised, and that his care comes first — the failure mode is a repeat of the "excessive testing" accusations in Genie's case (2 marks).

Scientific issue: objectivity and falsifiability. The researchers will badly want the boy to test the critical period hypothesis, so they may see progress that is not there. They should use independent observers, record everything, and check alternative explanations first — for example whether his language failure reflects the critical period or a pre-existing learning disability, which Genie's retrospective case could never rule out (2 marks).

Marking guidance: 2 marks per issue, one ethical and one scientific, each with an application to the scenario and a link to the Genie precedent.

Q4 · Discuss 8 marks

Gloria observes peer-mentoring sessions in a primary school: nine-year-olds help six-year-olds bake, measuring ingredients and following instructions. Gloria uses time sampling every 30 seconds for ten minutes, recording behaviour as positive (older child praises) or negative (older child takes over). She studies ten pairs. Discuss whether Gloria's study can be considered scientific.

Scientific features. Gloria uses a standardised procedure: every pair is observed for the same ten minutes, sampled at the same 30-second intervals, so the observation is replicable — another researcher could follow the same plan. The data is empirical: she records what she directly sees, rather than asking for opinions. Her behavioural categories (praise versus taking over) are defined in advance, which supports objectivity, and time sampling is a practical solution to the problem of deciding where one behaviour ends and the next begins.

Limits on the scientific claim. Her categories are coarse: a single 30-second slot may contain both praise and taking-over, and coding decisions may differ between observers — so inter-observer reliability has not been established. One observer also risks subjectivity. Generalisability is weak: ten pairs from one school cannot represent peer mentoring elsewhere. There is no manipulation of variables, so this is an observation, not an experiment — findings describe, they cannot explain causes.

Judgement. Gloria's study meets several scientific standards — standardised, empirical, replicable in principle — but falls short on objectivity checks and generalisability. It is scientific in design but preliminary: adding a second independent observer and more pairs would make it defensibly scientific.

Marking guidance: AO1 accurate description of Gloria's procedure; AO3 each point must link a feature of her study to a scientific criterion (replicability, objectivity, empirical, generalisability), ending with a reasoned judgement about extent.

7. Key Terms

falsifiable
可证伪：有可能证明一个理论是错的；无法证伪的理论不科学（这是理论的属性）
replicability
可复制性：程序详细、标准化、材料公开，别人能重做这项研究（研究的属性）
objectivity
客观性：描述看到的事实，而不是解释你认为它意味着什么
empirical
实证：通过感官直接获得的观察或实验证据，而非纯逻辑推理
retrospective
追溯：用历史资料和既有记录（二次数据）做研究，无法重新验证
dual role
双重角色：研究者同时是参加者的照料者或老师，威胁客观性与同意权
falsifiable example: critical period
可证伪的例子：关键期假说可以被「错过关键期却学会语法」的孩子推翻
unfalsifiable example: Erikson stage 7
不可证伪的例子：Erikson 第七阶段，帮助有益、过度帮助有害，怎么都解释得通
empirical example: Lorenz
实证的例子：Lorenz 的印刻现象直接可见，进化解释由此获得部分可测性
theory evaluation checklist
理论评价三查：支持vs冲突证据、应用效果、与对立理论比较
study evaluation checklist
研究评价五查，依次看可推广性与应用，再看信度、效度和伦理
