1. Getting Started

🎭 Five people, one request Textbook activity

Someone asks you to stay behind after school on Friday to stack 200 chairs in the hall. The words are exactly the same every time. Only the person asking changes. Pick one and see what your reason for obeying turns out to be.

Five different reasons, five different kinds of "because". French and Raven (1959) gave each of them a name, and together they form a second explanation of obedience — one that looks at the person giving the order instead of the person carrying it out.

这一节的重点：上一节我们说服从是因为当事人进入了 agentic state（代理状态）——看的是服从的人。这一节换个方向：看下命令的人手里有什么。同样一句话，从班主任嘴里说出来和从明星嘴里说出来，我们照做的原因是完全不同的两回事。这就是 social power theory（社会权力理论）的出发点。

2. Two Theories, One Behaviour

Agency theory and social power theory are trying to explain the same event: an ordinary person doing what an authority figure tells them to do. They simply start from different places.

Agency theorySocial power theory
Who is being explained?The person obeying — what state they are in.The authority figure — what power they have.
Key termsautonomous state, agentic state, agentic shift, moral strain, displacement of responsibility.coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent power.
One sentence"They obeyed because responsibility moved from them to the person in charge.""They obeyed because of the kind of power that person was seen to hold."

Two explanations, not two enemies

Both explanations can be true of the same moment: a person may shift into the agentic state because the authority figure looked legitimate. Being able to say both — and to say which one fits a given situation better — is what an evaluation question rewards.

3. French & Raven's Five Bases of Power

social power theory（社会权力理论） — John French and Bertram Raven (1959) identified five bases of power, which are said to motivate and influence behaviour. Someone can exert influence or control because of the source of power they possess.

Notice one word doing a lot of work: perceived. The theory does not require the power to be real — it requires the target to believe it is there. If an individual has a base of social power, or is perceived to have that power, they have potential influence over other people, which may explain obedience.

French and Raven's model: each base is a different reason why the same order gets obeyed.

The five bases, one at a time

Coercive power（强制权）

The ability to impose a punishment — for example, a teacher can issue a detention.

照做是因为：不听话会变糟。

Reward power（奖励权）

The ability to give a reward or incentive — for example, a parent can give pocket money.

照做是因为：照做有好处。

Legitimate power（法定权）

An official / recognised position — for example, a police officer or an elected official.

照做是因为：这个位置本来就该被服从。

Expert power（专家权）

Superior knowledge or expertise held by a credible influence — for example, a college professor.

照做是因为：对方比我懂。

Referent power（感召权）

Respect or admiration — for example, a celebrity or a charismatic leader.

照做是因为：我想成为、或想被接纳为那样的人。

What counts as expert and referent power changes with who you ask

Wangari Maathai founded the Green Belt Movement and was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004 for her work in environment conservation and sustainability. To someone who cares about the environment she holds strong referent power; to someone who has never heard of her, she holds none at all. Power lives in the relationship, not only in the person.

记五个词的捷径：不要背字母顺序，背箭头方向。coercive 是推（惩罚），reward 是拉（奖励），legitimate 是位置，expert 是知识，referent 是认同。考试给一个场景让你判断是哪种 power，先看场景里出现的具体动作：有人会被罚？有人会得到东西？有人戴着制服/有头衔？有人说的话别人听不懂？有人被崇拜？

4. Power Depends on Context

The five bases are contingent upon context（取决于情境）— the same person can hold power in one setting and none in the next. A football referee maintains legitimate power on the field during a game, but not off the field once the game is over. Reward power only works if the target actually wants the reward on offer.

⏱️ Same referee, two moments

The referee blows the whistle and tells a player to leave the pitch. Choose the moment and see the power disappear.

这一点是得分点也是失分点：很多同学写 "the manager has legitimate power"，但忘了说明在什么情境下这个权力成立。情境一变，权力就消失 —— 在评价题里，这正是 "social power theory fails to take account of context" 这条批评的由来（下一节）。

5. Evaluating Social Power Theory

Support

It explains how organisations create obedience

Social power theory can be applied to society: corporations and the military use hierarchies to build structures of obedience. Senior officers hold legitimate power over lower-ranking soldiers, and also reward and coercive power, because they can incentivise or sanction behaviour under their command. This may explain why malevolent authority（恶意权威）has been implicated in many war crimes.

Application the theory reaches outside the laboratory and into real institutions.

It can be turned into protection

Because the theory says where power comes from, it can also be used to train soldiers to resist malevolent authority, and to promote whistleblowing policies（举报制度）that allow anonymous reporting of destructive obedience.

Application a good theory is not only descriptive — it tells you where to intervene.

Milgram's own study fits it

Milgram found that ordinary participants would shock an innocent victim because of the perceived power of the experimenter giving the orders. The experiment was conducted at Yale University, a highly regarded university, so participants may have perceived legitimate power in the setting. They may also have perceived Mr Williams, dressed in a lab coat, as holding expert power as a researcher — he was in control and knew what the shocks would do.

Evidence the same data supports a second explanation, which is exactly what you want in an evaluation.

Two sources of power at once

The Yale setting (institution) and the lab coat (expertise) acted together. People rarely obey for one reason, and this theory can handle several bases operating at the same time.

Evidence it can explain a detail of the study without being invented for it.

Criticisms

It ignores the circumstances of the order

Social power theory fails to take account of the context in which the authority figure gives orders. Latané suggests that the proximity（距离近）, strength（强度/地位）and number（人数）of authority figures relative to the target are important situational factors that determine the impact of influence.

Situational factors Why it matters: the same person obeys the same order shouted across the room and refuses it given over the telephone, so the setting itself has to be part of the explanation.

It ignores individual differences

The theory fails to explain differences in obedience and dissent（异议）. Obedience and dissent can occur for reasons of personality type, gender and cultural factors. Focusing on the power held by the authority figure ignores the individual differences of the target.

Individual differences Why it matters: two people facing the same authority with the same power make opposite choices, so something inside the person must be doing work too.

It is therefore reductionist

Because it traces obedience back to one thing — the perceived power of the authority figure — it can be argued to be a reductionist（还原论的）explanation.

Reductionism Why it matters: a single-cause explanation always fits some cases badly, and those cases are precisely the ones exam questions ask about.

Wider issue: social control

Social control（社会控制）considers how psychological knowledge may be used to manage or control other people's behaviour. Social power theory gives knowledge that can be used to manage individuals in contexts such as the workplace, raising ethical questions about the use of power in society and organisations, particularly if coercive measures are used to command obedience, as in an oppressive regime. If social power is used responsibly, however, it can maintain order and safety.

Ethics the same knowledge that protects can also be used to oppress.

评价的写作套路：每一条都由两块拼起来 —— 理论漏掉了什么（ignore the context / ignore individual differences）+ 为什么这是个问题（因为同一个人换个情境就反抗了 / 因为两个人面对同一个权威做出相反选择）。只写前半句是描述，两块一起才是评价。另注意：两条 weakness 本身正好预告了这一章后半段 —— 情境因素和个体差异，就是我们要接着填的两个坑。

6. Four Families of Factors

Milgram's participants were not identical machines: some went to the end of the shock scale and some refused. That is where the second half of the chapter starts. Obedience and dissent / resistance（异议与抵抗）depend on four families of factors.

The map for the rest of the chapter: every study you meet belongs in one of these boxes.

Keep the four apart

Individual differences means personality and gender. It is not the same as situation and it is not the same as culture. In an exam answer, naming the wrong family costs the mark even when the study you describe is correct.

四个盒子怎么记：问自己一句 "差异来自哪里" —— 来自人本身（性格、性别）→ individual differences；来自现场布置（权威离得远不远、有没有制服、几个人在催）→ situational；来自长大的环境（国家/文化对权威的看法）→ cultural。考试常见陷阱：把 "在两个不同国家做" 写成 situational —— 那是 cultural。

7. Individual Differences: The Authoritarian Personality

personality（人格） — a set of relatively stable psychological traits which can influence how we think and act.
authoritarian personality（权威主义人格） — a person who is typically submissive to authority, because they may have been raised in a strict household and punished for non-compliance.

How it is measured: the F-Scale

Theodor Adorno et al. (1950) devised the F-Scale (Fascism Scale)（F 量表）, a questionnaire used to detect the authoritarian personality.

fascism（法西斯主义） — a very nationalist and authoritarian position, intolerant of others' views, based on an extreme right-wing political position.

The authoritarian personality has been associated with higher levels of obedience, because people with this type tend to maintain social rules and order.

The evidence

StudyWhat was doneWhat was found
Milgram & Elms (1966) Compared the F-Scale scores of 20 obedient and 20 dissenting participants from Milgram's experiments. Obedient participants had a higher F-Scale score, indicating an authoritarian personality type, compared with the dissenters.
Dambrun & Vatiné (2010) A computer simulation / virtual environment replication of Milgram's procedure. Authoritarianism was linked to obedience: people with high authoritarian scores were less likely to withdraw from the study.

Why this evidence is weaker than it looks

  • The F-Scale is a self-reported measure（自我报告）— people describe themselves, and those answers can be shaped by how they want to be seen.
  • The link between authoritarianism and obedience is a correlation（相关）, so we cannot be sure that one causes the other. A third variable may be responsible.
  • Dambrun and Vatiné offered two readings of the same result: those participants were submissive to the authority of the experimenter, or they showed a desire to punish the failing learner — the design cannot tell these apart.
这一节最值钱的一句话："F-Scale is self-reported and the link is a correlation, so we cannot be sure it causes obedience." 只要是 questionnaire + 得出两个变量一起变，就套这句。它同时踩中两个常考的评价点（自我报告不可靠、相关不等于因果），比再写一段研究过程更有分。

8. Individual Differences: Locus of Control

locus of control（控制点） — Rotter's (1966) personality theory, which outlines two types:
  • internal locus of control — people believe they are responsible for their own actions and are less influenced by others.
  • external locus of control — people believe their behaviour is largely beyond their control and due to external factors, so they are more influenced by the people around them.

Milgram's responsibility disc

Milgram followed up 118 participants from Experiments 1–4, both obedient and disobedient, and asked them to judge who was responsible for the shocks. Three hands were moved around a round disc to show the proportionate responsibility of the experimenter, the teacher (themselves) and the learner.

Who was blamedDissenting participantsObedient participants
Themselves48%lower
The experimenter39%lower
The learner12%25%

Dissenters took more of the blame themselves; obedient people were more likely to displace blame onto someone else. Obedient participants also fit the pattern of an external locus of control: not only were they more influenced by the authority figure, they believed they were not responsible for what happened.

Blass (1991): the interaction

Albert Blass investigated the relationship between locus of control and how participants behaved in Milgram's research. He found that people with an external locus of control were more likely to obey the instruction to shock the learner, and were more likely to blame the experimenter. But he also pointed out that the situation had an important role to play: obedience should be considered as an interaction between situation and personality — some individuals with an external locus of control may be more susceptible to obedience in particular situations.

Why Blass matters more than the others

Every other study in this chapter tries to show that one factor wins. Blass refuses to pick a winner. That "both matter, and they combine" position is usually the strongest way to end an evaluation: it uses all the evidence instead of throwing half of it away.

9. Individual Differences: Gender

Most of Milgram's participants were male, but he ran one variation (Experiment 8) with 40 women. Earlier research suggested women were more compliant, yet traditional beliefs picture women as more empathetic and less aggressive, which would predict the opposite — so the two predictions cancel out.

StudyFinding about gender
Milgram (Experiment 8)Women were virtually identical to men in obedience (65%), with 27.5% breaking off at the 300-volt level. Obedient women showed much higher rated anxiety than obedient men — a pattern also seen in Burger's (2009) replication.
Sheridan & King (1972)A live puppy received genuine shocks. All 13 women delivered the maximum shock, compared with only about half of the men.
Blass (1999) reviewAcross a review of ten obedience experiments, obedience levels for men and women were consistent in nine of them.
Kilham & Mann (1974)A partial replication in Australia: women were far less obedient (16%) than men (40%) — the one exception in Blass's set.

What the textbook concludes

Obedience research has used different procedures and produced inconsistent outcomes. Rather than proving a difference, the pattern suggests there are few gender differences in obedience, despite traditional beliefs that women would be more compliant to authority.

In an answer, the gender evidence is strongest when you write it as two sides — a study that found a difference (Sheridan & King) and reviews that found none (Blass) — and then say why the results are hard to compare.

10. Situational Factors

social impact theory（社会影响理论） — Latané (1981): three factors determine the impact of an authority figure upon a target — strength, immediacy and number. These are features of the situation, and they predict the level of obedience observed.
FactorWhat it meansWhat it predicts
StrengthThe authority figure's legitimate status or power.The stronger they are seen to be, the more authority they exert over the target.
ImmediacyHow proximal / close they are to the target.Influence is greater when they are close than when there is distance.
NumberHow many authority figures there are relative to targets.More sources of influence relative to targets means greater influence.

Milgram's own test of these three

  • Distance acts as a buffer. Obedience rose and fell depending on whether the authority figure was close to or far from the target — most clearly in the telephonic instruction condition, where the experimenter gave orders by telephone from another room.
  • Legitimacy matters. Milgram claimed obedience could only be established when the authority figure was perceived to be legitimate. Obedience was found at Yale University, and it fell when the experiment moved to Bridgeport or was conducted by an ordinary man instead of a scientist.

Support from a partial replication

Wim Meeus and Quinten Raaijmakers (1986) tested whether participants would follow orders to be psychologically cruel to someone they believed was applying for a job. The experimenter instructed the participant to make the applicant nervous and disturb the test; because of this, the applicant failed and was not offered the job.

  • Experimenter close to the participant: more than 90% were cruel to the applicant.
  • Experimenter gave the orders and left the room: this fell to around 36%.

Proximity（距离近）is therefore an important situational factor in determining the level of obedience achieved — and the effect appears outside Milgram's laboratory too.

情境因素一句话定理：权威越近、越强、越多，服从越高。Meeus 与 Raaijmakers 的 90% → 36% 是最好用的数字：同一个实验者、同一句话，只是走了出房间，服从率直接砍三分之二。

11. Cultural Factors

Obedience varies across cultures because culture affects the way we perceive authority. Smith and Bond (1998) describe two broad types:

Individualistic cultures（个人主义文化）

People tend to behave more independently and tolerate dissent, which suggests they resist conformity or obedience.

Collectivistic cultures（集体主义文化）

People behave as a collective group based on interdependence and cooperation, meaning compliance is important for the stability of the group.

The obvious prediction is that collectivistic cultures will be more obedient and less tolerant of dissent. Thomas Blass (1999) reviewed obedience research carried out 35 years after Milgram's first studies, using procedures similar enough to compare.

Obedience research reviewed by Blass (1999): percentage of participants giving the maximum shock. The two blue rows used procedures that differed from Milgram's.

Why the table is harder to read than it looks

Procedure differences masquerading as culture differences

  • Ancona and Pareyson (1968), Italy: the maximum shock level was only 330 volts compared with Milgram's 450, and only student participants were used — a group Milgram actively avoided because of their compliant and competitive nature. 330 volts may simply have seemed less dangerous.
  • Burley and McGuiness (1977), UK: just 20 students and a maximum of 225 volts.
  • Milgram's own proximity studies found 73%, which is closer to Italy's 85% than his famous 65% is — so the comparison depends heavily on which condition you compare.

A modern, naturally occurring comparison

Carl-Benedikt Frey (2020) analysed the contact tracing data collected during lockdown in the COVID-19 pandemic. Collectivistic countries recorded less in-country mobility than individualistic countries, even though the countries compared had similarly stringent lockdown policies. Individualistic countries appear to have been more defiant of the rules — or at least less compliant.

Where this leaves us

The impact of culture on obedience is complex and unclear, and understanding it is hindered by a lack of research in certain cultures and by a lack of studies that can genuinely be compared country to country. This is why "culture affects obedience, so collectivistic cultures obey more" is a weak conclusion, while "the research does not let us isolate culture from procedure" is a strong one.

本节最有价值的句子："看起来是文化差异，其实可能是程序差异。" 意大利 85% 用的最高电压只有 330V（比 Milgram 少 120V）而且只招学生 —— 电压更低自然更容易按到底。写文化因素的答案时，先说理论预测（collectivistic 更服从），再说为什么数据支持不了（程序不一致），最后给结论（complex and unclear），比背书式地列九个国家好得多。

13. Exam Lab

🧭 The shape of almost every answer in this chapter

  • What: name the piece of psychology accurately (the type of power, the factor family, the study finding).
  • Why: say why it counts — what evidence supports it, or what it fails to explain.
  • Context: in any scenario question, name the people in the scenario and say what they actually did.

A sentence that only says what earns one mark; the same sentence plus why earns two — and most of this chapter's questions are built exactly that way.

✍️ Practice 1 — 2 marks

Two definitions of types of social power are given below. Name the type that each one describes.

  • The authority figure has an ability (perceived or real) to punish a person for disobedience.
  • The authority figure has superior knowledge and / or skills (perceived or real).

Coercive power for the first, and expert power for the second.

How to tell them apart: punishment points to coercive, knowledge points to expert. One word in the question decides which of the five it is.

✍️ Practice 2 — 6 marks

(a) Explain one strength of social power theory as an explanation of obedience.
(b) Explain two weaknesses of social power theory as an explanation of obedience.

Model structure

  • Strength: social power theory can be applied in training military personnel in whistleblowing policies if they believe a superior is abusing a position of legitimate power — which could reduce the atrocities committed by soldiers carrying out the orders of officers in appointed positions of authority without question.
  • Weakness 1: French and Raven claim obedience is a direct result of the type of power the authority figure is perceived to possess, which ignores individual differences of personality — for example, individuals with an internal locus of control may be more likely to dissent because they take personal responsibility for their actions.
  • Weakness 2: the theory fails to take account of the circumstances in which the order is given. Latané's social impact theory shows that proximity, strength and number change obedience, so the same authority producing the same order gets different results in different settings.

Why this scores: every point names an issue and gives the reason it is a strength or a weakness. A list of three issues with no reasons would lose half the marks.

✍️ Practice 3 — 8 marks, applied

Jorge has recently bought a car. He has noticed an unusual noise if he drives fast. His friends hear the noise but tell him that it is nothing to worry about and he should still drive fast. Jorge is very concerned, so he speaks to the car specialist at his local garage. He has known the car specialist for a long time and trusts their advice. The car specialist tells him to bring the car into the garage the next day and only drive at a slow speed.

Discuss, using social power theory, why Jorge obeys the car specialist and not his friends. You must make reference to the context in your answer.

Model answer — four developed points

  • Expert power. The car specialist has superior knowledge and expertise: he has qualifications and Jorge believes he knows whether the noise is dangerous, so Jorge slows down on his advice and ignores his friends, who have no such knowledge.
  • Referent power. Jorge has known the specialist for a long time and admires his work, so he identifies with him and wants his approval — that relationship is why the advice is taken seriously, while the friends are people he does not identify with in this way.
  • Legitimate power. The specialist's qualifications place him in an acknowledged role about whether a car is roadworthy, so his instruction carries the weight of the position as well as of the person.
  • A comparison that answers the question. The friends give the same advice in the opposite direction, but they hold none of these three bases of power — no expertise, no recognised position, no admired status. Perceived power is what separates two identical messages.

Why this scores: half the marks come from describing the theory and half from applying it to the details of the scenario. The last point uses the friends, a detail most answers throw away, to answer the "and not his friends" part of the question directly.

✍️ Practice 4 — 8 marks (planning only)

Evaluate how well social power theory can account for obedience.

Half the marks are for describing the theory and the evidence accurately, and half are for weighing it up. The conclusion is what turns a description into an evaluation.

ParagraphWhat goes in it
1Outline the theory: French and Raven's five bases — coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent — and the point that perceived power gives influence over the target.
2Support with application: hierarchies in the military and corporations; reward power at work (Randolph and Kemery found a positive relationship between supervisors using reward power and the empowerment of employees); legitimate power and the Holocaust.
3Criticisms: it ignores individual differences (personality, gender, culture) and it ignores the circumstances of the order (Latané's strength, immediacy and number).
4A conclusion that answers the question asked: how well does it account for obedience? Weigh the support against the two criticisms and say whether an interactionist position — as Blass suggested — explains the evidence better than the theory alone.

An answer that never reaches a conclusion stays in the middle levels no matter how much accurate detail it contains.

🔑 Exam key points — what a strong answer contains ›
  1. The five bases are coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent — and the theory cares about power that is perceived, not necessarily real.
  2. Power is contingent on context: the referee has it during the match and loses it afterwards.
  3. The two criticisms to memorise: it ignores the context of the order, and it ignores individual differences — which makes it reductionist.
  4. Every issue in this chapter needs a why it matters sentence, or it earns half marks.
  5. Factor families: individual differences = personality and gender; that is not the same as situation, and it is not the same as culture.
  6. The two limitations that fit any self-report study: it is self-reported, and the link found is a correlation, so cause cannot be claimed.
  7. Cross-cultural differences may be procedure differences in disguise — different maximum voltages and student-only samples.

locus of control控制点
Rotter's (1966) personality dimension: whether people see the causes of their behaviour as internal or external.
internal locus of control内控
Believing you are responsible for your own actions, so you are less influenced by others.
external locus of control外控
Believing your behaviour is beyond your control and due to external factors, so you are more influenced by others.
dissent / resistance异议 / 抵抗
Refusing to obey; dissenters take more personal responsibility for their actions.
social impact theory社会影响理论
Latané (1981): strength, immediacy and number determine the impact of an authority figure on a target.
proximity接近性
How physically or psychologically close the authority figure is to the target; greater distance acts as a buffer to obedience.
number数量
How many authority figures there are relative to targets; more sources means greater influence.
individualistic culture个人主义文化
A culture whose members behave more independently and tolerate dissent (Smith and Bond, 1998).
collectivistic culture集体主义文化
A culture whose members behave as an interdependent group, where compliance supports the stability of the group.
reductionist还原论的
Explaining a complex behaviour by a single cause, which ignores other important factors.
