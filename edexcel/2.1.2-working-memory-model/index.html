2.1.2 THE WORKING MEMORY MODEL
Baddeley & Hitch (1974) · Central executive · Phonological loop · VSSP · Episodic buffer · 工作记忆模型

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Content Spec 2.1.2 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.9 · pp.88–95

What this page is really asking

  1. Short-term memory is not one passive shelf. Baddeley and Hitch rebuilt it as an active system that holds information and works on it: mental arithmetic, following sentences, tracking a moving light.
  2. One boss, several workers. The central executive directs attention and hands material to two slave systems: the phonological loop for sound, the visuospatial sketchpad for sight and space.
  3. Each claim needs its evidence. Dual-task performance, brain-damaged patients and brain scans all back the split into subsystems.
  4. The 2000 update matters. The episodic buffer was added to explain how the parts combine with each other and with long-term memory.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Outline all four components: the central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad and episodic buffer, with the role and capacity of each.
  • Distinguish the two parts of the phonological loop and use the phonological similarity effect and the word length effect as evidence for them.
  • Explain dual-task performance: why two similar tasks interfere and two different tasks do not.
  • Evaluate the model with clinical cases (KF, HM, Williams syndrome, Alzheimer's patients), neuroimaging (Paulesu et al., 1993) and the criticism that the central executive is vague and untestable.
  • Apply the model to everyday scenarios, explaining which component is overloaded and why.

1. Why a New Model Was Needed

Baddeley and Hitch proposed working memory in 1974 as a three-component short-term memory system. The label was not new: Atkinson and Shiffrin had already used working memory for the short-term store in their multi-store model. What was new was the diagnosis. Baddeley and Hitch argued that the multi-store version of short-term memory was overly simplistic and leaned too hard on rehearsal as the critical process for learning. They set out to understand short-term memory as a complex, active system.

Working memory temporarily stores and manipulates the information currently in use. We rely on it for remembering phone numbers and lists, comprehending sentences, mental calculation and reasoning. It is also fragile: easily disturbed by distraction (someone talking while you hold a number in mind), overload (a long list) and overwork (complicated calculations).

The original model had three parts: a central executive running the whole system, and two slave systems（从属系统：听命于总裁的分工部门）handling verbal and visual information. In 2000 a fourth component, the episodic buffer, was added.

The working memory model (Baddeley and Hitch, 1974; episodic buffer added 2000). The central executive allocates incoming data to the slave systems; the episodic buffer binds their output together with long-term memory.

中文理解：把工作记忆想成一个小团队。总裁（central executive）不存货，只管分配注意力：决定先看什么、先听什么，把材料分给两个干活的部门。语音回路管「声音」，视空间画板管「图像和位置」，两个部门各自容量都很小。2000 年新加的情节缓冲器是个连接器：把两个部门的产出和长时记忆里的旧知识绑在一起，解释了为什么我们能记住整句长句子。这页的考点是「每个部件干什么、证据是什么」，评价题专挑总裁「说不清、测不了」这一点打。

2. The Central Executive

The central executive was originally described as a limited-capacity component in charge of general processing, supervising and coordinating the two slave systems. It is modality free（通道自由：什么形式的信息都能处理）: it can deal with acoustic, visual and other types of sensory information. Its best-understood role is as an attentional controller with the capacity to focus, divide and switch attention. It receives information from the senses and allocates verbal and visual material to the appropriate slave system.

Keep this criticism ready

The central executive is largely a theoretical concept with limited experimental support. Because it is abstract, it is not directly testable: empirical data cannot easily be gathered, so critics argue it does not fully meet the criterion of being scientific. Its precise function is undefined and its location in the brain has not been found.

中文理解：总裁的本事就三个词：focus（专注一件事）、divide（分心做两件）、switch（来回切换）。它不按声音或图像分类收货，什么都能管。但它也是最被诟病的部件：功能说不清、脑区找不到、实验难做，评价题写「central executive 概念模糊、缺乏直接证据」就是满分方向。

3. The Phonological Loop

The phonological loop is the slave system for verbal information. It has two parts:

  • The phonological store: holds a limited amount of verbal information for a few seconds. The hold can be extended by subvocalising, that is, refreshing the trace.
  • The articulatory rehearsal system（发音复述系统：心里默念的复读机）: the inner voice that repeats the sounds to keep them alive.

Two effects that prove the design

The phonological similarity effect. Similar-sounding items (man, cad, mat, cap, can) are harder to remember than different-sounding ones (pen, sup, car, day, hot). Crucially, the effect disappears for semantically similar words (huge, long, wide, tall, large) versus unrelated ones (thin, wet, old, late, strong). Only sound confusion matters, which demonstrates the store relies on acoustic encoding (Baddeley, 1966a).

The word length effect. Short monosyllabic words (cat, rug, hat) are recalled better than long polysyllabic words (intelligence, alligator, hippopotamus). Longer words fill up the limited capacity of the articulatory rehearsal system, so words earlier in the list decay. The longer the word, the more capacity it eats, and the more likely forgetting becomes. The same logic explains articulatory suppression（发音抑制：复述被占线）: rehearsal collapses when you must repeat a filler such as the while learning a list, and recall deteriorates.

Why the loop exists: language acquisition

The Italian patient VP, with an acquired phonological impairment, could not retain any vocabulary from a new language, suggesting the loop evolved for language learning (Baddeley et al., 1988). Children with Specific Language Impairment（特定型语言障碍）struggle to repeat non-words (slimp, dar, gep), and this correlates with vocabulary size: deficits in the loop make learning novel language hard (Gathercole and Baddeley, 1996). Non-word repetition is now a standard indicator test for SLI.

Back to the mall: your friend's spoken directions and the shop signs you read both end up in the phonological loop, because you translate each sign into inner speech before you can think about it. One rehearsal channel, two loads: that is why the earlier corners slipped away.

中文理解：语音回路 = 仓库 + 复读机。两个经典效应都在证明这条设计：发音像的字母互相干扰（证明存的是声音），长单词占容量多、忘得快（证明复读机带宽有限）。进化意义也考过：学外语靠它（病人 VP 与 SLI 儿童的证据），所以非词复述测试成了语言障碍的筛查工具。

4. The Visuospatial Sketchpad

The visuospatial sketchpad (VSSP) temporarily holds and manipulates visual and spatial information: shape, colour, and the location and movement of objects. It uses a visual code and has a limited capacity.

Measuring spatial span: the Corsi blocks

Spatial span is tested with the Corsi block tapping task（科西积木敲击任务：记方块亮起的顺序）: blocks on a screen light up in a sequence the participant must reproduce. Errors increase as the sequence grows, suggesting a limited capacity for spatial memory.

Are visual and spatial one system or two?

Klauer and Zhao (2004) found visual memory tasks were disrupted more by visual interference and spatial tasks more by spatial interference: evidence for separate components inside the VSSP. Darling et al. (2007) pushed this further with 72 participants memorising a letter p on a screen of 30 white squares, recalling either its appearance or its location. A spatial interference task (tapping keys in a figure of eight) disrupted memory for location but not appearance; a visual disruption (flickering black and white dots) affected memory for appearance, to a lesser extent, but not location. One caution: real visual memory usually juggles a whole scene, not one letter, so the task is not an everyday slice of visual processing. This study is examined in detail on page 2.3.3.

A boundary case: congenital blindness

David Lieberman (1980) criticised the VSSP concept using people with congenital blindness（先天失明）. Lacking the visual experience on which the sketchpad is built, they appear to run tasks that would rely on the VSSP through other sense modalities instead. The model may therefore need a more inclusive account of its components.

中文理解：视空间画板管「看到什么」和「东西在哪」。科西积木测空间容量：方块亮几块、按原顺序敲回来。视觉和空间是不是一个系统？两组干扰实验给出了分离证据：动态敲键盘破坏位置记忆、黑白闪烁破坏外观记忆，各管各的。边界案例：先天失明的人没有视觉经验却照样能做这类任务，说明画板理论需要修正。

5. The Episodic Buffer

The episodic buffer was added in 2000. It is a temporary store of limited capacity whose role is to integrate information from different sources: the central executive, the phonological loop, the visuospatial sketchpad and long-term memory. It processes auditory, spatial and visual forms alike.

Two puzzles forced the addition:

  • The sentence-span puzzle. The phonological loop holds only a few word sequences, yet we can store far longer sentence sequences, up to 15 to 20 sentence units. Words in sentences can be bound together by meaning and grammar, and that binding reaches into long-term memory. The original model had no place for this.
  • The combination puzzle. Verbal-span experiments showed verbal and visual encoding can be combined, but the original model could not explain how its subcomponents interface with each other or with long-term memory. The episodic buffer is the answer.

中文理解：情节缓冲器是 2000 年的补丁，补的是旧模型的两个漏洞。第一，语音回路明明只能装几个词，为什么整句话能记住十几句？因为句子靠意义和语法打包，打包动作要连到长时记忆。第二，语音信息和视觉信息能拼在一起用，旧模型没写部件之间怎么对接。缓冲器就是那个「把所有来源的信息捆到一起」的连接舱。

6. Evaluation

Supports the model

  • Dual-task experiments (Baddeley and Hitch, 1976). Participants tracked a moving light with a pointer while imagining the letter F and judging the angles at its top and bottom. Each task was easy alone, but together they failed: two visual tasks compete for the VSSP. Add a verbal task instead and performance holds, because the phonological loop takes the load. Separate slave systems explain the pattern.
  • Clinical cases. KF (Shallice and Warrington, 1974) damaged his parietal lobe in a motorbike accident: digit span of one to two items, a gross phonological-store impairment, but intact visual memory. HM shows the mirror image: gross spatial-memory impairment with verbal short-term memory relatively unaffected. Two subsystems, independently damageable.
  • Williams syndrome. Normal language ability but impaired visual and spatial ability: the same word-length and similarity factors operate, yet Corsi performance is poor. Clinical evidence for separate subsystems (Phillips et al., 2004, also found problems with spatial prepositions such as behind, underneath, against).
  • Neuroimaging (Paulesu et al., 1993). PET scans showed different regions at work: Broca's area during subvocal rehearsal, the supramarginal gyrus when the phonological store was used. Physical evidence for the loop and its two subparts.
  • The word length effect across development (Sebastián and Hernández-Gil, 2012). Spanish digit span grows with age from around age 7, when subvocalising starts, and is shorter than the Anglo-Saxon figure, plausibly because Spanish digit names take longer to pronounce.
  • Alzheimer's disease (Baddeley et al., 1991). Alzheimer's patients handled single verbal or visual tasks as well as young and elderly controls, but failed badly when doing both together: the slave systems were intact, the coordination (central executive) was not.

Challenges the model

  • The central executive is underspecified. Largely theoretical, limited experimental support, not directly testable: an abstract concept that resists empirical data. Its exact function and brain location remain unknown.
  • Case studies generalise poorly. KF and HM are unique individuals with unique injuries; care is needed before extending their dissociations to everyone.
  • The VSSP may not be universal. Lieberman (1980): people with congenital blindness handle such tasks through other modalities, so the sketchpad as described does not cover them.
  • Laboratory tasks are narrow. Much of the evidence uses designed verbal and visual learning tasks; everyday working memory juggles a rich array of stimuli, so ecological validity is a fair question.

Exam tip: describe then explain

Describing the model means a factual recount of the original model and each subcomponent in terms of capacity, function and coding, with detail scaled to the marks. You are not required to evaluate in a describe question, but examples help elaborate points.

For explain questions, make a point then justify it, anchored in the scenario. Worked example: Why is it difficult to process two conversations simultaneously, such as talking on the phone while a friend tells you something?

Point: Both conversations are verbal, so both utilise the phonological store.

Expansion: The articulatory rehearsal system is overloaded by two people talking at once, so we cannot recall all the information.

中文理解：支持线抓三类证据。行为证据：双任务（同通道抢资源、异通道不打架）。临床证据：KF 坏了语音回路但视觉记忆完好，HM 反过来，正好证明两个子系统独立。脑成像证据：PET 扫出复述用布洛卡区、仓库用缘上回，部件在脑子里真的分了工。反对线三把刀：总裁说不清测不了、个案病人不好推广、实验室任务太窄。背证据时每个都带上人名年份，评价题的分数就落在「证据指到哪个部件」上。

Close the loop: the mall route, explained

Your friend's spoken directions entered the phonological store, and silently repeating them ran the articulatory rehearsal system. The shop signs were visual at first, but thinking about a sign means converting it into inner speech, which feeds the same loop. Two loads on one limited channel: the earliest corners decayed while you rehearsed the newer ones. Music and jogging never collide like this, because familiar lyrics need no rehearsal and running is controlled by well-practised motor programmes outside working memory. The collision is not about how many tasks you do, but about which component they both load.

Reflect before the Exam Lab: think of a moment when you failed at doing two things at once. Which two components collided? Answer in your own words first, then check it against the model above.

8. Exam Lab

Q1a · Describe 2 marks

Nico is a library assistant working at a busy library. A visitor asks Nico about the location of a few particular books. At the same time, Nico overhears a conversation his library manager is having about a different book with another visitor. As Nico begins to search, he has forgotten the names of some of the books he is looking for. Describe, using the phonological loop, why Nico could not remember all of the books the visitor wanted.

The book titles and the manager's conversation are both verbal information, so both enter the phonological loop. The loop has a limited capacity: the phonological store holds verbal information for only a few seconds, and the articulatory rehearsal system can refresh only so much at once. With two verbal streams competing for the same limited store, the trace of some book titles decays or is displaced before Nico can act on them.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for identifying that both sources compete for the limited phonological loop, 1 mark for linking the overload to forgetting the titles.

Q1b · Explain one strength 2 marks

Explain one strength of using the working memory model (Baddeley and Hitch, 1974) to explain why Nico could not remember the books that the visitor wanted to read.

Strength: The model is supported by strong empirical evidence, including dual-task experiments showing that two verbal tasks interfere while a verbal and a visual task do not.

Why it helps here: This evidence base makes the model a credible tool for Nico's scenario, because his forgetting fits the same mechanism the dual-task research demonstrates: two simultaneous verbal streams overload the phonological loop. A model with converging evidence from experiments, brain imaging and clinical cases can be applied to everyday situations with confidence.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for stating a strength (e.g. supported by dual-task / clinical / imaging evidence), 1 mark for explaining why that strength makes the model useful for explaining Nico's forgetting.
