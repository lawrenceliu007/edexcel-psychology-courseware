4.4 PRACTICAL INVESTIGATION
Observing learned behaviour · Chi-squared · Thematic analysis · 观察法实操

Topic D · Learning theories and development Practical investigation Spec 4.4.1

What this chapter is really asking

  1. You design and conduct observations on an aspect of learned behaviour: two observations, or one that gathers both qualitative and quantitative data.
  2. Every design decision is examinable: behavioural categories, coding sheets, tallying, control, hypothesis construction, ethics, observer bias and effects.
  3. Analysis is two-track: a chi-squared test on the tally frequencies plus a thematic analysis (Braun and Clarke, 2006) on the qualitative notes.
  4. The worked example (thanking the bus driver) is your template: reproduce its logic with your own behaviour and you have every 4-mark and 8-mark practical question covered.

1. Getting Started — Observation Is Systematic

An observation is more than simply seeing: it is the systematic collection of pre-defined behaviours, with careful consideration of what behaviours would be expected for a given aim. The textbook gives three example aims; for each, think what pre-defined behaviours you would expect:

  • Investigate non-verbal communication patterns during job interviews.
  • Investigate classroom behaviour when teachers use different behaviour management strategies.
  • Investigate parent–child interactions in a preschool setting.
中文理解：先定行为，再开观察
实操题的第一个失分点是把「观察」当成随便看。考纲要求 pre-defined behaviours：动手之前就要把要记的行为列清楚、定义清楚。4.4 的所有题都围绕「你怎么设计」展开，而这的第一步永远是：这个研究问题里，哪些行为值得记？怎么算发生了一次？

3. The Worked Example: Prosocial Behaviour on Public Transport

The textbook works through one complete practical: gender differences in prosocial or polite behaviour on a bus. Prosocial behaviour（亲社会行为） is defined as any behaviour that is intended to benefit another.

Link to learning theories

Chapter 15 of this book covered Bandura: children observing an aggressive role model later model that antisocial behaviour. By observing prosocial behaviour you can test whether social learning theory also explains the modelling of prosocial behaviours in society.

  • Social learning theory suggests we learn gender-appropriate behaviour from parents, teachers, siblings, peers and the media, with rewards and punishments reinforcing it.
  • Eagly (2009): women and men both engage in prosocial behaviour, but women tend towards more communal and relational prosocial behaviours while men display more agentic ones, engaging in prosocial acts to gain status or show strength.
  • Leslie, Snyder and Glomb (2013): examining workplace donations to charity suggested women donated more than men.
中文理解：实操也要有理论背景
4.4 的考察形式是「描述你的实操」，但高分答案里必须出现为什么研究这个行为的理论依据：SLT 说性别化行为是被榜样和强化教出来的，所以礼貌行为是合适的「习得行为」素材。Eagly 和 Leslie 那两条研究告诉你背景文献是混合的，这正是下一步选非定向假设的理由。因果链一路通到底。

4. Hypotheses — Directional or Non-directional?

  • Use a non-directional (two-tailed) hypothesis when researchers are unsure of the effect.
  • Use a directional (one-tailed) hypothesis when previous studies suggest which way the research will go.
  • Whatever you choose, it must be clearly testable.

The worked example's decision

Because the background literature on gender and prosocial behaviour is mixed (Eagly says both genders help in different ways; Leslie et al. suggest women donate more), the practical puts forward a non-directional (two-tailed) hypothesis:

"There will be a difference in the number of women and men who display prosocial behaviour by thanking the bus driver before alighting."

中文理解：定向与非定向有理有据
选哪种假设不能拍脑袋：文献一致 → 定向；文献矛盾或没有文献 → 非定向。考试描述你的实操时，写完假设要补一句理由（"因为背景文献结果混合"），这句就是两分题里常考的设计决策。假设句式照抄课本：有差异（difference）的写法，别写成相关。

5. Planning the Observation and the Coding Sheet

Design decisions

  • Type: a naturalistic observation of people on a bus, in a natural setting. The sampling technique for participants is opportunity sampling.
  • When and where: which bus route, day of week, time of day and journey duration. All influence how many passengers there are and what type.
  • Statistical test in mind: plan the quantitative side knowing you will use chi-squared later; participants fall into unrelated categories (male or female), so the data is unrelated.

Behavioural categories and the coding sheet

Decide the behaviours to observe: the behavioural categories（行为类目）. For example, measure polite behaviour by whether passengers thank the driver before alighting; other categories could include assisting other passengers, giving up a seat, or making way on a crowded bus. Then draw a chart to tally each occurrence:

Behavioural categoryMaleFemale
Thanking the bus driverIIII
Giving up a seat on the busII
Helping a passenger with a pushchairIII

Counting instances on the coding sheet gives quantitative data that can be tested for significance. The observation also gathers qualitative data by writing down what passengers actually said, annotating details such as the driver's gender, the participants' ages, and whether there was eye contact. The qualitative notes then go through thematic analysis.

中文理解：编码表是量化数据的源头
编码表（coding sheet）把行为变成类目、把类目变成正字计数：先分类目，再计数，最后进列联表。考「描述你怎么收集定量数据」，答案就是这三步；考「你怎么同时收集定性数据」，答案是在计数之外随手记下原话和细节（说了什么、司机性别、有没有眼神接触）。一份观察两条数据线，正好满足 4.4.1 的「一次观察收两种数据」。

6. Pilot Study, Controls and Ethics

Pilot study（预实验）

  • Run the observation once to address potential problems and test the criteria: where is the best position to observe? (Sit closer to the front to catch driver interactions.) How long does the journey need to be to gather enough data? At rush hour, can you see everyone?
  • A pilot study is not used to test validity or reliability: it only checks that apparatus, timings and so on work correctly.
  • It is also a chance to ask participants whether they felt uncomfortable, suspected anything, or felt their behaviour was influenced.

Controls

  • Consider recruiting a second observer, for example one observing male passengers and one observing female passengers.
  • You would need inter-rater reliability（评分者间信度）: the degree of agreement between two or more observers, ensuring consistency and controlling for observer bias.
  • The observation is likely covert, but check whether passengers notice your note taking and become suspicious: suspicion is a confounding variable（混淆变量）.

Ethics

  • Public places are considered acceptable to observe because people expect to be observed in day-to-day interactions: buses and trains included.
  • Collect no names or personal data, only gender and the observed behaviour, so ethical guidelines are not contravened.
  • You might gain consent after the observation is complete, though this may not be practical.

Common confusion

The pilot study question trips people up: it does not check validity or reliability. It checks practicalities (apparatus, timings, position, whether you can see everyone) and whether participants were uncomfortable or suspicious.

中文理解：预实验答三件事
考「为什么要做 pilot study」就写三件：先测流程行不行（设备、时长、观察位置），再确认能不能看到所有人，最后问被试有没有起疑或不舒服。再补一句「它不检验效度和信度」当保险。伦理段背两个关键词：公共场所（public place）默认可观察、不收个人信息。

7. Analysing Quantitative Data

The worked example collected data on an hour's bus journey between 3.00 p.m. and 4.00 p.m. on a Thursday: 51 passengers observed, 24 women and 27 men. The tallies go into a contingency table ready for the chi-squared test:

MenWomenTotal
Thanked the bus driver11 (Cell A)18 (Cell B)29
Did not thank the bus driver16 (Cell C)6 (Cell D)22
Total272451

This is a two-by-two contingency table suitable for nominal data. Reading it: 11 men and 18 women thanked the driver; 16 men and 6 women did not. There is a fairly equal frequency of male and female passengers overall, and a visible difference in who thanked the driver.

Maths tip (from the textbook): nominal data calls for a bar graph as the graphical representation. Match the graph type to the level of measurement. The best descriptive statistic to summarise this quantitative data is frequency.
中文理解：先读表再算卡方
拿到列联表先做两件事：检查行列总数是否对得上（27+24=51），再把四个格子的数和行列总数抄清楚。考试描述数据时用一句话概括模式：「女性感谢司机的比例更高」。称名数据配频次和条形图，这两个搭配是 checkpoint 原题。

8. The Chi-Squared Test, Worked Through

Chi-squared is the appropriate test because the study predicts a difference, uses unrelated data / independent groups, and has nominal level data.

Step 1: Expected values

Expected value (E) = row total × column total ÷ overall total. These are the frequencies expected if there were no difference between males and females:

CellE = row × column / total
A29 × 27 / 51 = 15.35
B29 × 24 / 51 = 13.65
C22 × 27 / 51 = 11.65
D22 × 24 / 51 = 10.35

Step 2: The chi-squared calculation

For each cell: subtract E from O, square the result, divide by E; then add all four:

Cell(O − E)² ÷ E
A(11 − 15.35)² / 15.35 = 1.23
B(18 − 13.65)² / 13.65 = 1.39
C(16 − 11.65)² / 11.65 = 1.62
D(6 − 10.35)² / 10.35 = 1.83
Total= 6.07

Step 3: Degrees of freedom and the critical value

  • df = (rows − 1) × (columns − 1) = (2 − 1) × (2 − 1) = 1.
  • For a non-directional/two-tailed hypothesis with df = 1 at p < 0.05, the critical value is 3.84.
  • The observed value of 6.07 is greater than 3.84, so the result is significant and the null hypothesis can be rejected: there is less than a five per cent probability that the difference is due to chance.
  • The direction of the difference comes from examining the cells: females on the bus were more likely to thank the driver, males were not.
  • The observed/calculated value must equal or exceed the critical value to be significant at the level shown.
Maths tip (from the textbook): chi-squared has no 'n' column on the critical values table because nominal data gives no individual scores; that is why we calculate degrees of freedom instead. The df column replaces the n column.
中文理解：跟 4.2 的九步是同一套
这里只有三步，因为课本把九步压缩成了实操版：算期望 → 算卡方值 → 查表比大小。注意本例选的是非定向假设，查表用 two-tailed 那列的 3.84；如果当初定了向假设就要用 one-tailed 列。写结论时三个要件齐全：观测值 6.07 > 临界值 3.84 → 显著 → 拒绝虚无假设，再补一句方向（女性更爱感谢司机）。

9. Thematic Analysis（主题分析）

Qualitative data cannot be summarised in a chart or graph. Its analysis involves recognising repeated themes. Braun and Clarke (2006) outline a five-phase approach, shown here with the bus example from the textbook:

PhaseWhat you doBus example
1. Familiarisation（熟悉数据）Read through your notes and notice patternsMales and females thank the driver in different manners: males say 'Thanks' or 'Thank you, driver' with small talk ('What time does your shift finish?'); females say thank you with a departure greeting ('Goodbye') and more eye contact
2. Generating initial codes（生成初始编码）Label specific categories identified'thank you, with familiarity'; 'thank you, with no familiarity'; 'departure greeting'; 'small talk'
3. Searching for themes（寻找主题）Seek themes on the basis of the codes'implied familiarity': passengers make small talk with the driver
4. Reviewing themes（复核主题）Try the categories out, possibly with another set of data; if new observations fit, the theme is worth further investigationCollect more data on another journey and check the themes still hold
5. Defining and naming themes（定义命名主题）Clearly define the themes, then produce a report telling the story of the emerging themesFinal report relates the categories with specific examples and quotes

Qualitative data provides much richer detail on the complexities of human interaction, but it is harder to select patterns and draw firm conclusions, and it is more likely to be subjective, reflecting the personal viewpoints and background of the researcher.

中文理解：五阶段考试就考顺序和例子
主题分析五步背顺序：熟悉 → 编码 → 找主题 → 复核 → 定义命名。每步配一个公交车例句，答题时「阶段名 + 做什么 + 公交例」三件套展开。优缺点各记一句：细节丰富但主观。考点通常问 phase 内容或让你说出 themes 怎么来的，五步顺序乱了就全错。

10. Writing Up: Conclusions

  • Quantitative conclusion: the statistical test informs the overall conclusion. Here chi-squared shows the result is significant, so the null hypothesis is rejected and the experimental hypothesis accepted: there is a difference in polite behaviour between females and males alighting the bus.
  • Qualitative conclusion: look at the themes and illustrate them with specific examples: more small talk with drivers by male passengers, backed up with specific quotes or observations.
  • The report needs procedure, results and discussion sections: what you did, what you found (table, graph, test), and what it means (linked back to learning theory).

11. Evaluating Your Practical Investigation

Validity · naturalistic strength

A naturalistic observation in real life suggests a degree of validity. 'Thank you' is a relatively objective behavioural category because it involves no judgement; qualitative notes such as 'small talk' involve more interpretation.

Validity · two improvement tools

Respondent validation（受试者验证）: gain feedback from participants on your interpretation, for example interviewing regular bus users about the findings; if they broadly agree, the interpretation is affirmed. Triangulation（三角验证）: use more than one approach, for example a questionnaire on politeness on public transport to check it corresponds with the observed behaviour.

Reliability

Repeat on another bus, town or time of day and compare. Qualitative data is harder to assess for reliability because it is subjective, but inter-rater reliability (agreement between observers) and the test–retest method (repeat a day, week or month later on the same route) both work. Lack of consensus means the behavioural categories may need revision.

Generalisability and credibility

Time of day affects the sample (rush hour commuters may be too hurried for polite interactions); an equal distribution of ages minimises bias; more retired passengers may raise polite interactions because they are less time-pressured. Credibility depends on meeting scientific principles such as replicability and measurable phenomena; weigh the strengths (valid naturalistic measurement) against weaknesses (single time of day limits generalisation) and repeat the observation to assess overall effectiveness.

中文理解：四个评价词对应四套工具
这是实操评价题的检索表：效度（自然情境 + 类目客观，改进用 respondent validation 和 triangulation）、信度（inter-rater + test-retest）、推广性（时段和年龄构成）、可信度（可重复、可测量）。考试问哪条答哪条，别把四套工具混在一起写。

12. Improving Your Practical Investigation

The textbook's six improvement questions. For each, name the problem and the fix:

Question to askWhat a fix looks like
Was the sample representative?Observe at different times and days so different passenger types are included
Was the situation natural?Keep the observation covert and unobtrusive so behaviour is unaffected
Were there issues with lack of control?Add a second observer; standardise the route and time window
Did the behavioural categories measure what was intended?Operationalise the categories more effectively, for example defining 'polite interaction' in observable terms
Was data collection or analysis subjective?Use inter-rater reliability; predefine coding rules for qualitative notes
Were there unforeseen ethical issues?Check whether passengers noticed and felt uneasy; add retrospective consent where practical

14. Exam Lab

Textbook exam practice AO1 + AO2 Your own practical

Textbook exam practice (from the textbook)

In your studies of learning theories and development, you will have conducted a practical investigation.

  • (a) Describe the procedure of your learning theories and development practical investigation. (4)
  • (b) Explain one strength of your practical investigation in terms of validity. (2)
  • (c) Explain one improvement you could have made in terms of reliability. (2)

✍️ (a) Describe the procedure4 marks

The observation was a naturalistic, covert observation of passengers on a bus, chosen because social learning theory suggests polite behaviour is modelled and reinforced differently for males and females. The behavioural categories were thanking the driver before alighting, giving up a seat, and helping with a pushchair, recorded on a coding sheet with tallies for male and female passengers separately. Detailed qualitative notes (what passengers said, eye contact, approximate ages) were written alongside the tallies, so the single observation gathered both quantitative and qualitative data. The observation ran for one hour (3.00–4.00 p.m. on a Thursday), covering 51 passengers; a non-directional hypothesis was used because the background research was mixed, and a pilot run checked the best seating position and whether the journey length collected enough data.

中文方法框：描述程序四分就四句
每句一个设计决策：①观察类型和场景（自然、隐蔽、公交车）→ ②行为类目和编码表 → ③双线数据记录（计数 + 详细笔记）→ ④时长、样本量、假设类型和预实验。全部换成你自己的实操内容，别背公交车的数字（除非你做的就是它）。

✍️ (b) One strength in terms of validity2 marks

Validity strength: the observation was naturalistic and covert, carried out in a real setting where the behaviour would normally occur, so passengers were unaware of being watched and behaved as they genuinely would; this raises ecological validity because the politeness measured reflects real everyday behaviour rather than behaviour changed by knowing about the observation.

✍️ (c) One improvement in terms of reliability2 marks

Reliability improvement: use the test–retest method: repeat the observation on the same bus route a week later and compare the tally frequencies; if similar results are achieved the observation is reliable. Alternatively, recruit a second observer and check inter-rater reliability: if both observers record similar tallies for the same behaviours, the behavioural categories are being applied consistently; any disagreement would show the categories need tighter operational definitions.

中文方法框：信度改进二选一
这题的标准答案池就两个：test-retest（隔一周重测同一线路，比对频次）和 inter-rater（第二名观察员，比对一致性，不一致就修类目定义）。写法都是「方法 + 怎么判断可靠」。选一个写透即可，别两个都只写半句。

15. Key Terms

behavioural categories 行为类目
The pre-defined behaviours chosen for observation, with clear operational definitions.
coding sheet 编码表
A record sheet used to tally each occurrence of a behavioural category.
tallying 计数记录
Writing down when and how many times behaviours occur.
prosocial behaviour 亲社会行为
Any behaviour that is intended to benefit another.
directional / non-directional hypothesis 定向 / 非定向假设
One-tailed when previous research suggests a direction; two-tailed when unsure; must be testable.
opportunity sampling 机会抽样
Recruiting whoever is available in the situation, as with bus passengers.
pilot study 预实验
A trial run checking apparatus, timings and criteria work; it does not test validity or reliability.
inter-rater reliability 评分者间信度
The degree of agreement between two or more observers.
test–retest 重测法
Repeating the observation later on the same route and comparing results.
confounding variable 混淆变量
Something unintended that affects results, such as passengers becoming suspicious of note taking.
contingency table 列联表
A table of frequencies by category, two-by-two here, suitable for nominal data.
degrees of freedom 自由度
(rows − 1) × (columns − 1); replaces the n column on the chi-squared critical values table.
critical value 临界值
The table value the observed chi-squared must equal or exceed to be significant (3.84 for df = 1, two-tailed, p < 0.05).
thematic analysis 主题分析
Analysing qualitative data by recognising repeated themes; Braun and Clarke (2006) five phases.
respondent validation 受试者验证
Checking your interpretation by gaining feedback from participants on the findings.
triangulation 三角验证
Using more than one approach to check results, increasing confidence in findings.
observer bias 观察者偏差
Subjective interpretation of behaviour by the observer; controlled with inter-rater checks.
bar graph 条形图
The appropriate graph for nominal data such as tally frequencies.
