The one-line map of the whole chapter 全章地图
Freud asks: why do we do things we cannot explain? His answer has three steps:
① psy 看不见 unconscious (mind has a hidden layer that drives behaviour) → ② psy 压不下去 repression (the unwanted wishes get pushed away but leak out) → ③ mid 三我 structures (the mind is split into id / ego / superego — three voices negotiating every choice).
Getting started — guess the unconscious motive 猜一猜无意识动机

For each situation below, write the hidden wish or fear Freud would say is driving it. The point is not to be right — the point is to think in unconscious terms. 每行写下你猜的「无意识里想要/害怕的」——不用追求答案对，重要的是学会这种思考方式。

The one rule to follow when guessing 猜动机的一条规则
Freud always assumes behaviour has a — even when the person can't name it.cause 原因 / 缘由
The trick is to ask: "what would this behaviour if the person knew they were doing it?"avoid / hide 躲避 / 隐藏 If even the person can't see the reason, the cause must live somewhere below awareness.

§3 · psy Eros & Thanatos · 生与死的本能ANSWERS → 课件 §5
Two energies drive the unconscious 两种本能
Freud believed the unconscious is fuelled by two opposing drives. They push behaviour in opposite directions — and most of life sits somewhere between them.
Compare the two drives 对比填空
Drive 本能Greek name 希腊名It drives us to ...Daily example 生活例
Eros
生之本能		 love / life 爱 / 生命 create, bond, , survive.reproduce 繁衍 caring for a child 养育孩子
Thanatos
死之本能		 death 死亡 destroy, , return to a state of calm.aggress / hurt 攻击 road rage · outburst 愤怒冲动
Why Freud needed TWO drives 为什么是两种
One drive (eros) explained love, sex, creativity. But it could not explain why humans are also drawn to , war, and .aggression 攻击 · self-destruction 自毁 So Freud posited a second, opposing instinct. This is the only chapter where two drives sit side by side — most behaviour is a mix.

Why the ego needs defence 为什么自我需要防御
The ego is caught between two pressures: the id pushing for satisfaction, the superego disapproving. When the conflict is too strong, the ego uses defence mechanisms（防御机制） — unconscious tricks to the anxiety.lower / push down 降低
The two main defences — fill in the definitions 定义填空
Mechanism 机制What it does 怎么办Everyday example 生活例
Displacement

Denial

The key feature 共同特点
Both defences are unconscious — the person does not know they are doing it. If you shout at your dog and then choose to be nicer to your boss to compensate, that's not displacement. If you decide the bad news is fake, that's not denial. Both must be .below awareness / automatic

§8 · Thinking Like a Psychologist · 心理学家怎么想ANSWERS → 课件 §11
Why Freud divides psychology 为什么弗洛伊德让心理学界分裂
Modern psychology loves testable claims. Freud's claims about the unconscious, repressed wishes, and Oedipal conflicts cannot be directly.observed / measured 直接观察 A theory that explains everything risks explaining nothing — this is the falsifiability debate.
Three boxes to fill in — hypothesis, alternative, test 三框练习
Freud's hypothesis
弗洛伊德的解释
 Alternative explanation
另一种解释
 How would I test it?
如何验证

Pick any ONE behaviour — e.g. someone biting their nails during exams. Apply the three-box test above.

The two questions that always decide marks 决定分数的两个问题
  1. Can the claim be disproved? If not, it's a story — not a scientific theory. 可证伪性
  2. Is there a simpler explanation that fits the data? Freud's theory is so elaborate it can explain the same observation in many ways — that is a weakness, not a strength. 奥卡姆剃刀
Short answer · 简答练习
A critic writes: "Freud's theory is unfalsifiable — you can always say the unconscious caused it." Write 4–5 lines defending OR attacking this view. Use one specific concept (repression / defence / Oedipus) and one real-life behaviour to make your argument.

p.12 — Exam practice (Part A: structure on scenario)

Q1 — Noore (id): Noore acts on the pleasure principle — she takes the cake immediately to satisfy her hunger with no regard for the rule. "Couldn't help herself" shows the id's inability to delay gratification.

Q2 — Suresh (superego): His superego enforces a moral rule against rides from unknown adults — "didn't feel right" is the inner moral voice judging the impulse, regardless of practical comfort (rain).

Q3 — Lucy (ego): The ego operates on the reality principle — she recognises the id's wish (videos) but delays it 90 min. The timer is the ego's compromise: neither killing the wish nor surrendering to it.

Q4 — Outline (id vs superego): Id = instant gratification of instinct with no regard for consequence. Superego = enforces moral standards and judges the self as good or bad regardless of wish. Contrast: pleasure vs morality.

p.12 — Exam practice (Part B: 8-mark essay)

Q5 — PEEL plan (4 blocks, 8 marks):

P1 strength — first systematic account: Freud gives one of the first systematic accounts of how unconscious conflicts shape behaviour. Little Hans showed a single phobia reflecting an Oedipal conflict, providing rich qualitative detail not available in lab studies.

P2 strength — therapeutic legacy: Talking therapy / psychoanalysis remains a foundation for many modern therapies — CBT, psychodynamic therapy, and the "therapeutic alliance" concept all trace back to Freud's talking-cure method.

P3 limitation — unfalsifiability: Every behaviour can be retro-fitted to an unconscious wish; hard to design a study that could disprove the theory. This is a scientific status problem, not just a measurement problem.

P4 limitation — Little Hans evidence: The case relied on the father's reports, creating researcher bias and limiting generalisability from a single case. Findings cannot be replicated, and Hans's age (5) means the "Oedipal interpretation" may be projection rather than evidence.
