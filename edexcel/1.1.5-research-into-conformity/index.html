1.1.5 RESEARCH INTO CONFORMITY
从众的研究 · Asch (1951, 1952, 1956)

Social Psychology · 线段判断实验：基线 + 每一个变式 + 三年研究的对照与评价
🧠 Core idea: wrong, but unanimous 📏 32% of answers were wrong on purpose 📖 Textbook: Chapter 2

1. Getting Started

📏 You are the seventh person to answer Look carefully

The experimenter shows the room a standard line and asks a simple question: which comparison line — X, Y or Z — is the same length as the standard?

The Asch line task. You can see the correct answer — but six people before you have just said something else.

It is your turn. The answer you can see is Y. Everyone before you said X. What do you say?

中文理解
这个任务的答案明显得不能再明显 —— 没有任何模糊空间。Sherif 的光点实验（1.1.4）里人们从众，可以说“因为我真的不知道答案”；但在这里，答案就摆在眼前。
如果你还跟着大家说错话，那就只剩一种解释：不是信息问题，是压力问题。这正是 Asch 设计这个实验的原因。

2. Why Asch Studied Conformity

Solomon Asch conducted a range of laboratory experiments to test majority influence on conformity, and to investigate the various factors which affect conformity levels. While procedures vary slightly between his variation studies, typically one naive participant was placed in a group of confederates to see whether they would conform to the group norm.

The clever design choice

Asch used a perception task with an obvious answer. In 1.1.4 you saw Sherif use an ambiguous task — nobody knew the answer, so conformity could be informational. Asch did the opposite: the answer is unmistakable, so any conformity must be normative social influence — yielding to pressure, not seeking information.

Key term

confederate（同谋者） — an actor working for the researcher. In Asch's studies the confederates gave wrong answers on purpose, on the researcher's instructions.

control group（控制组） — a group that does not experience the experimental situation but acts as a baseline against which to judge any change.

中文理解：三个年份不要背混
考纲要求三个研究：1951（基线 + 破裂多数 + 多数规模）、1952（熟人群体 + 异议者）、1956（123 人的经典版本 + 圆盘实验）。
它们用的是同一个基本程序，每次改一样东西 —— 和 Milgram 的三个变式（1.1.2）思路完全一样：改情境，看行为跟着怎么变。

3. Asch (1951) — How It Worked

Fifty male college students were put into groups of eight and asked to match the length of a standard line with one of three comparison lines of unequal length — one of which matched the standard line.

The procedure, step by step

  • Each group member judged which line matched, publicly, one after another.
  • Unknown to one participant, the other seven were confederates who responded with a wrong answer.
  • The naive participant was therefore confronted with a group that unanimously selected the incorrect line.
  • There were 12 critical trials in which the group answered wrongly.
  • The frequency of incorrect responses made by the participant was calculated as the measure of conformity.
  • Afterwards, the participant was immediately interviewed and debriefed about the true nature of the study.
中文理解：四个必须写准的细节
① 50 名男大学生；② 每组 8 人，其中 7 个是同谋者、只有 1 个真被试；③ 答案公开说出（这是压力来源）；④ 12 次“关键试验”里全员故意说错。
从众的测量方式 = 错误回答的次数/比例 —— 这个“客观测量”是 1.1.5 评价里的一条优点。

4. Asch (1951) — What Happened

The headline numbers

Over the 12 critical trials, 32 per cent of estimates made were errors in line with the majority's incorrect response. Despite the majority pressure, 68 per cent of estimates were correct.

The finding hidden inside the average

There were strong individual differences: some participants conformed in almost every trial, and some never conformed. Observations suggested those who kept their independence did so because they were confident, maintained individuality, or kept task focus. Those who conformed were thought to lack confidence in their perception or to want to fit into the group.

Asch's conclusion: conformity can only be explained by a combination of situational pressure and the individual's response in that situation. To investigate further, he designed a set of experiments to test different situations.

Exam trap 考试陷阱： the result is “32% of responses given were errors” — not 32% of participants and not 32% of trials. The textbook checkpoint question tests exactly this distinction.
中文理解：怎么读 32% 这个数字
正着读：三分之一的回答跟着多数人错 —— 压力真实存在。
反着读：68% 的回答是对的 —— 大多数时候人们顶住了。两个方向都要会写，评价题常考“结果其实显示人们大部分时间没从众”。

5. Asch (1951) — Broken Majorities

Asch then tested what happens when the majority is not unanimous — when it “breaks”.

中文理解：只要一个人站在你这边
七个人说错、你一个人说对 → 32% 从众。但有一个人跟你一起说对 → 从众掉到 5.5%。
一个盟友就能抵掉大半压力 —— 这就是 social support（社会支持）的力量。考试问“how could Asch's findings be applied”，这是最好的应用点。

6. Asch (1951) — Majority Size

In a final series, Asch placed participants into groups with a unanimous majority of 1, 2, 3, 4, 8 or 16 confederates — all giving the same wrong answer.

Figure 1 — the force of Number from social impact theory, seen in Asch's data.

What the numbers say

  • 1 confederate: did not really influence the naive participant's decision.
  • 2 confederates: participants selected the wrong line 12.8% of the time.
  • 3 confederates: the greatest error rate.
  • 4, 8, 16: larger majorities produced no greater effect than a majority of three.
中文理解：灯泡定律的实验版
这正是 1.1.4 里 Latané 灯泡类比的数据版：从 1 到 3 增长最快，3 之后几乎不涨。考试把 1.1.4 的理论和 1.1.5 的数据连起来写，是 AO3 的高级操作。

7. Asch (1952) — The Dissenter Study

What was different

Male college students were placed in groups of seven to nine — and this time the participants were acquaintances, chosen as such by the confederate majority. The majority gave the correct answer for the first two trials. On the third trial, an individual in the group claims the majority's answer is wrong. From time to time he repeats his dissent — and sometimes he agrees with the incorrect response.

Results and conclusion

  • 33.2% of the naive participants' responses were incorrect and consistent with the majority — a similar rate to 1951.
  • The control group wrote their answers in private: only 7.4% of responses were in error.
  • The dissenter had questioned the majority's judgement — so participants could not be conforming because they believed the majority was right. They were yielding to social pressure.
中文理解：这组对照为什么值钱
公开回答：33.2% 错；私下写答案：7.4% 错。同一批人、同一套题，唯一区别是要不要当众说 —— 差距就是 NSI 的直接证据。
再加一层：这次明明有人公开质疑过多数人的判断（说明答案可疑），大家还是照样从众 —— “我不知道”这条借口被彻底排除了。

Asch's conclusion (1952)

We are still likely to yield to majority influence when it is questioned and when it is obviously wrong.

中文理解：四个变式一句话收
错得离谱照样跟（27.2%）；一个人不算多数（归零）；有一个坚定盟友减半（13%）；反过来一个人对十六个真被试，只会被嘲笑 —— 力量全在“多数”这两个字上。
最后一个变式留个钩子：一个人怎么才能反过来影响多数？那是 1.1.6 Moscovici 少数派影响的问题。

9. Asch (1956) — The Classic Version

Procedure

Asch ran the basic procedure on 123 college students in groups of seven to nine. The single naive participant sat in the second-to-last chair. Across 12 critical trials where the majority answered wrongly, the wrong comparison line was either moderately or extremely different from the standard. Public answers were compared against a control group who wrote their judgements privately.

Results

  • The control group of 37 private responders gave only three incorrect judgements in total — versus about one third wrong in the experimental group.
  • When the majority chose a moderately different line, naive participants also made a moderate selection.
  • When the majority chose an extreme line, four-fifths modelled the judgement and one-fifth compromised — picking the moderately different line instead.
  • Again: considerable individual variation — some maintained absolute independence, some yielded completely.
中文理解：“妥协”这个细节最值钱
多数人指着一个极端错的答案，五分之一的人不是照抄、而是选了个折中的错误 —— 说明压力之下人真的会扭曲自己的判断，不是简单复制别人的答案。写 evaluation 或 application 时这是很鲜活的细节。

10. Discs and Privacy (1956 continued)

Beyond lines: the coloured discs

To test whether conformity would extend to other perceived stimuli, Asch asked participants to judge whether a comparison coloured disc was brighter or darker than a standard disc. Over 12 trials where the group deliberately judged wrongly, 39% of the naive participants' responses were incorrect.

The privacy variation

When the group gave public judgements but the naive participant answered in private, conformity dropped to just 12.5% incorrect estimates.

Conclusions (1956): the experiments demonstrate the power of the majority — participants were complying because of normative social influence. Situational factors (social support, size of the majority) influence conformity rates, but individual differences also affect how people respond.

Figure 2 — the same task produces wildly different error rates depending on the social situation.
中文理解：一张图记住 1952 的四个数字
33.2（公开·全体说错）→ 27.2（错得离谱）→ 13（有一个盟友）→ 7.4（私下写）。
这四个柱子就是 Asch 研究的骨架 —— 任何 describe / evaluate 题都从这里取材。

12. What the Three Studies Show Together

Three conclusions that build on each other

  • People yield to pressure, not information. In 1952 the dissenter openly questioned the majority's judgement — yet 33.2% still conformed. The private control groups (7.4%; 3 errors out of 37) show the true error rate is almost zero.
  • The situation can be tuned. Unanimity, ally support, majority size, obviousness, public vs private — each factor moves the error rate in a predictable direction.
  • The individual still matters. Every study found some participants who never conformed and some who conformed almost always — conformity is not automatic.

13. Evaluating Asch's Studies

▶ Strengths — why the research is still trusted
  • STANDARDISED 高度标准化：All participants were given the same line-judgement tasks in exactly the same way, allowing researchers to precisely measure conformity using the objective measure of error rate — so the study can be retested to check reliability.
  • VALIDITY 内部效度：The highly standardised, controlled nature also increases internal validity — the error rates were affected only by the majority, and not by any other variable.
▶ Limitations — and the evidence that sharpens them
  • ECOLOGICAL 生态效度：We cannot be sure the findings extend beyond a laboratory. Conformity to a trivial line judgement may occur in the lab, but real-life decisions carry greater importance — the pressure may not exist, or may be stronger. This is a problem of ecological validity.
  • ETHICS 伦理：Participants were not fully informed — they were deceived about the other group members and put under considerable stress when giving public judgements against the majority. However, these breaches were a necessary part of the procedure: had participants known the true nature, they would have acted very differently.
  • STRANGERS 真实群体是熟人：Conformity usually exists within groups of familiar people — family and friends — not among strangers. Mori and Arai (2010) partially replicated Asch using participants who knew the confederates: males (but not females) were less likely to be swayed by the incorrect answers. This offers more useful insight into everyday conformity.
  • STABILITY 跨时代文化不稳定：Asch tested American male university students in the 1950s. Research with different populations in different decades has not produced the same conformity rates — suggesting conformity may not be a stable human characteristic.
How to get top marks. Do not list — judge. “Because Asch tested only American male students in the 1950s, and later studies with other populations found different rates, we cannot treat 32% as a fixed human constant — the figure is time- and culture-bound.”

14. Improving the Study — the exam's favourite question

The exam tip, turned into a method

For any named study you may be asked how it could be improved. Take a weakness → turn it into an improvement → justify why it is an improvement.

Improvement 改进Justification 理由（课本原文句式）
Use a more representative sample that also includes female participants instead of just the 50 male students.This would make his findings about conformity to a majority group more generalisable to a wider target population.
Use a more realistic task, such as providing photographs of real objects, to test conformity.This would increase the validity of Asch's findings about conformity in real-life situations.
Use familiar groups (friends) rather than strangers — as Mori and Arai (2010) did.This would better reflect how conformity operates in everyday group settings.

弱点 → 改进 → 理由。弱点必须是“真弱点”（不能是伦理——伦理难以既保留程序又消除），改进要可操作，理由落到 generalisability / validity 这类评价词上。背熟课本给的两个例句，考场上换内容即可。

16. Exam Lab

How to read the question

Describe — who, where, what they did, and the key numbers.
Explain — why, or what it means. One point plus one reason per mark.
Evaluate — strengths and limitations, each with a consequence, plus a judgement.

Q1 · Describe Asch's (1951) study into conformity. (4 marks)

Asch placed 50 male college students in groups of eight and asked them to publicly judge which of three comparison lines matched the length of a standard line. Unknown to one naive participant, the other seven group members were confederates who deliberately gave the same wrong answer, so the participant faced a unanimous incorrect majority across 12 critical trials. The frequency of the participant's incorrect responses was used as the measure of conformity: 32% of estimates followed the majority's error, while 68% were correct. Participants were interviewed and debriefed immediately afterwards.

Q2 · Explain one conclusion that can be drawn from Asch's (1952) control group. (2 marks)

The control group wrote their answers in private and made errors on only 7.4% of responses, compared with 33.2% when answering publicly against a unanimous wrong majority. This difference shows that the errors in the experimental group were caused by social pressure rather than the difficulty of the task — the task itself was almost never misjudged when no majority was watching.

Q3 · Explain how one variation of Asch's research demonstrates the power of social support. (2 marks)

When one confederate consistently gave the correct answer alongside the naive participant, conformity fell from around 32% to 13% of estimates. Because the only change was the presence of one ally, the drop shows that a partner acts as social support against majority pressure — breaking the unanimity of the group removes most of its power.

Q4 · Evaluate Asch's (1951) research into conformity. (8 marks)

Plan four paragraphs first:

¶Job of that paragraph
1Method: standardised procedure, objective error measure — strength (reliability, internal validity).
2Ecological validity: trivial task, laboratory — what that does to the conclusion.
3Ethics: deception and stress — and why they were necessary.
4Sample and stability: American males, 1950s; Mori and Arai (2010); balanced conclusion.

¶1. A strength of Asch's research is that the procedure was highly standardised and controlled: every participant faced the same line judgements in exactly the same way, and conformity was measured objectively through the error rate. This means the study is replicable, so reliability can be checked, and the high control increases internal validity — the error rates were affected only by the majority's answers and no other variable.

¶2. However, the findings may not extend beyond the laboratory. Conformity to a trivial line judgement may occur under laboratory pressure, but real-life conformity involves decisions of greater importance, where the pressure may not exist or may be even stronger. This limits the ecological validity of the research, so we cannot be confident that 32% reflects everyday conformity.

¶3. The research also raises ethical problems: participants were deceived about the true nature of the study and the identity of the group members, and experienced stress when giving public answers against a unanimous majority. Asch defended this because the deception was a necessary part of the procedure — participants who knew the truth would have acted very differently — but it still means participants could not give informed consent.

¶4. Finally, the sample — American male university students in the 1950s — limits generalisation, and research with different populations in later decades has not produced the same conformity rates, suggesting conformity is not a stable human characteristic. Mori and Arai (2010) used familiar groups and found males, but not females, were less likely to be swayed, further showing the effect depends on who the group is. On balance, Asch's research remains a convincing demonstration of normative influence, but its figures should be treated as specific to its time, task and sample.

Exam tip from the textbook. Review the weaknesses of any named study — they are the areas for improvement. Identify an improvement, then justify why it improves on the original (generalisability, validity…).

17. Key Terms

confederate 同谋者
An actor working for the researcher — in Asch's studies, the group members giving wrong answers on purpose.
naive participant 真被试
The one real participant in each group, unaware the others are confederates.
critical trials 关键试验
The 12 trials in which the confederate majority unanimously gave the wrong answer.
unanimous 一致的
When the whole majority gives the same (wrong) answer — the condition that produced most conformity.
broken majority 破裂多数
A majority whose unanimity is destroyed by a dissenting or correct-answering member.
social support 社会支持
The presence of an ally answering correctly, which acts as a buffer against majority pressure.
control group 控制组
A group not experiencing the experimental situation — a baseline against which change is judged (e.g. private written answers).
error rate 错误率
The objective measure of conformity: the percentage of responses matching the majority's deliberate error.
dissent / dissenter 异议 / 异议者
Disagreeing with the majority; the group member who questions the majority's judgement.
compromise response 妥协反应
Selecting a moderately wrong line under pressure from an extremely wrong majority — one-fifth did this in 1956.
ecological validity 生态效度
Whether laboratory findings apply to real-life settings — a key criticism of the trivial line task.
internal validity 内部效度
Confidence that only the majority influenced the error rate — a strength of the standardised design.
deception 欺骗
Misleading participants about the study's nature — the main ethical issue in Asch's research.
normative social influence 规范性社会影响
Conforming to be accepted and avoid rejection — the explanation Asch's studies demonstrate.
majority influence 多数派影响
The power of a numerical majority to shape an individual's judgements and behaviour.
individual differences 个体差异
Stable personal characteristics — Asch found some participants never conformed, others did so in nearly every trial.
