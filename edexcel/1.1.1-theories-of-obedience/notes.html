Psychology explains the same behaviour in several ways; each way is called an approach（研究取向）. This topic uses the social approach: behaviour is explained by .the real or imagined presence / pressure of others 他人在场或想象中的压力

§2 · term Obedience — the behaviour we are explainingANSWERS → 课件 §6
The definition — learn it word for word 定义，一字不差地背
obedience（服从）: .complying to the orders of an authority figure 服从权威人物的命令
Two terms that explain why obeying feels normal 为什么服从像本能
hierarchical
等级制的		A system of social organisation where there are and those who follow.leaders 领导者
socialisation
社会化		The process by which we learn the of society through socialising agents such as parents and teachers.rules and norms 规则与规范
The mechanism in three steps 机制三步
  1. We grow up in a hierarchical society — some people give instructions, others follow them.
  2. Socialisation rewards us for obeying and corrects us for refusing.
  3. By adulthood obeying is close to a .habit 习惯
Why this is worth studying 为什么值得学
The same training that makes society work — stopping at red lights, queueing, handing in homework — also explains the darkest events of the twentieth century, where ordinary soldiers, clerks and railway workers took part in systematic murder and repeated the same defence: "I was only following orders."
The question that started it all 一切的起点
How far will an ordinary person go when someone in authority tells them to hurt another person?

Milgram's question was not "are these people evil?" — it was a question about the situation. He ran a series of experiments and then proposed agency theory to explain what he saw.实验本身的细节在另一个专题页完整学习；这里只把它当作理论的证据来源

Think-write · 想一想（3–4 行）
"I was only following orders" — is this an excuse or an explanation? Write what psychology can say about it that a court cannot.
Exam tip 考点提示
A definition question is the cheapest mark on the paper — and the one most often thrown away. "Doing what you are told" scores nothing; "complying to the orders of an authority figure" scores full marks. The word authority is what carries the mark.

The two states side by side 两态对照
Autonomous state 自主态Agentic state 代理态
Whose will? 谁的决定your own free will 自己的Acting for someone else 别人

The name of the move 这个动作叫什么
agentic shift（代理转移） — the move from the autonomous state into the agentic state.
displacement of responsibility（责任转移） — .passing responsibility for your own actions to the person who gave the order 把责任交给下令的人
人不是突然变坏，而是悄悄把"负责的人"换掉了。这是 agentic state 最核心的一句。
Why the shift is so easy 为什么这个转移很容易发生
Because socialisation has rehearsed it long before anyone gives us a harmful order: teacher says sit down, coach says run, manager says sign. The agentic state is not a rare state of mind — for most of us it is a one.well-rehearsed 反复演练过的

What happened, in four lines 实验四句话
  1. Participants believed they were in a study about learning and memory; each one was given the role of .teacher 老师
  2. They were told to give an electric shock to a every time a mistake was made, increasing the shock each time.learner 学习者
  3. The learner was in another room and was a confederate（同谋者）— were given, but the participant believed they were.no real shocks 没有真的电击

Two weaknesses that point forward 两条批评预告了下一节
"It ignores the situation" and "it ignores individual differences" are the two gaps that the factors affecting obedience topic fills: who the person is and where the order is given. Every criticism of social power theory (pp. 9–10) is built from the same two gaps.
Write it out · 写一条完整的批评（3–4 行）

The reductionist paragraph — ready to use 还原论那一段（可直接用）
Agency theory can be argued to be reductionist, because it explains a complex behaviour — obedience — using one single mechanism, the agentic shift, and leaves out culture, personality, mood and the situation. This matters because a single-cause explanation always fits some cases badly, and those cases are the ones exam questions ask about.

§7 · theory Social Power Theory — five basesANSWERS → 课件 §11–§12
Two theories, one behaviour 两个理论，同一个行为
Agency theorySocial power theory
Who is explained? 解释谁the person obeying 服从的人the authority figure 下命令的人
One sentence 一句话"They obeyed because responsibility moved from them to the person in charge.""They obeyed because of the that person was seen to hold."kind of power 哪种权力

两个解释可以同时成立：一个人之所以进入代理态，正因为那位权威看起来很正当。评价题的高分答案会说出两者，并说明在这个情境里哪个更合适。

The theory 理论原文
social power theory — French and Raven (1959) identified bases of power, which are said to motivate and influence behaviour.five 五种
The key word is perceived: the power does not have to be — it has to be believed to be there.real 真实的
The five bases 五种权力（填空）
BaseSource of the power 权力来源照做是因为…（一句话）Example 例子

Reward

Expert
专家权		 held by a credible influence.superior knowledge / expertise 专业知识对方比我懂A college professor; a car specialist.

感召权		.respect or admiration 尊敬或崇拜我想成为/被那样的人接纳A celebrity; a charismatic leader.
How to identify a base in a scenario 场景里怎么判断是哪种 power
找场景里的具体动作，不要找形容词：有人会被罚 → coercive；有人会得到东西 → reward；有人戴制服/有头衔 → legitimate；有人说的话别人听不懂 → expert；有人被崇拜 → referent。

§10 · exam Exam Skills — how answers are markedANSWERS → 课件 §16
IDEA — 回答带情境的题目
  • I — Identify: decide which piece of psychology the scenario is about (agency theory, or one of the five bases).
  • E — Explain: state that piece of psychology accurately, in its own terms.
  • A — Apply: link it to the details — name the people, say who holds the power, say what the person actually did.
只复述理论、完全不碰情境，写得再标准也是 0 分。说是什么得 1 分；同一句加上为什么重要得 2 分。
Command words 指令词
Command wordWhat it asks for 要求你做什么
Outline / DescribeSay what the theory claims — accurate content, using its own terms. No judgement needed.
ExplainSay what it claims and why it works that way — one step further than describe.
Evaluate / Assess / DiscussTwo halves: describe the theory accurately, then judge it with evidence — strengths, weaknesses, and a conclusion.
"using…" / "with reference to the context"The scenario is compulsory. Every point must touch a named detail of it.
How the big question splits 大题的分怎么分
  • 6-mark (one strength + two weaknesses): three developed points. Each = what + why it matters.
  • 8-mark (evaluate / assess / discuss): half the marks for outlining the theory accurately, half for judging it. Four paragraphs: outline → support → criticisms → conclusion.
  • A generic answer scores nothing. An answer that could be written without reading the question — or a conclusion that never answers the question asked — stays in the middle levels.
Sentence templates · 考试句式（填空后就能用）
  • Theory + application: "In the scenario, is an authority figure, so shifted into the agentic state and displaced responsibility onto them."
  • Power + context: " is perceived to hold power because — and in this context that perception is what produces obedience."
  • Strength: "A strength is ; for example . This supports the theory because ."
  • Weakness: "A limitation is ; in this study . This weakens the theory because ."
  • Conclusion: "Overall, , because ."

§11 · exam Two 8-mark essay plans · 八分题提纲Fill in, then write in full on p.14
Plan A — "Assess how well agency theory can explain obedience"
¶What goes in it 内容My notes 我的要点
1Outline: socialisation in a hierarchical society → autonomous state → agentic shift → agentic state → displacement of responsibility → moral strain.
2Support: 65% reached the highest shock level; visible moral strain; participants blamed the experimenter; My Lai and "following orders".
3Criticisms: 35% refused and Brandt resisted (individual differences); Latané's strength / immediacy / number (situational factors); reductionist.
4Conclusion that answers the question asked: how well? Weigh support against criticism and say which side is stronger.
Plan B — "Evaluate how well social power theory can account for obedience"
¶What goes in it 内容My notes 我的要点
1Outline: French and Raven (1959) — coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent; perceived power gives influence over the target.
2Support: hierarchies in the military and corporations; reward power at work; legitimate power and the Holocaust.
3Criticisms: ignores individual differences (personality, gender, culture); ignores the circumstances of the order (Latané: strength, immediacy, number).
4Conclusion: how well does it account for obedience? Weigh it up — and say whether an interactionist position (person × situation) explains the evidence better.
What separates a middle-band answer from a top-band one 中档与高档的分界
  • Middle: accurate description, then a list of "a weakness is…" with no reason attached.
  • Top: every point carries its why it matters, the scenario details are used rather than ignored, and the conclusion actually answers the question word (how well?).

p.2 — Foundations

Social approach: the real or imagined presence / pressure of others.
theory = explain behaviour · study = test a theory · methodology = collect data.
Conformity: majority → you (majority). Minority influence: minority → majority (minority). Obedience: authority figure → you (complying to the orders of an authority figure).
Allport: experimental methods.

p.3 — Obedience

obedience = complying to the orders of an authority figure.
hierarchical = leaders and those who follow. socialisation = rules and norms of society (parents, teachers).
Step 3: obeying is close to a habit.

p.4 — Agency theory

autonomy = own free will · agency = an agent for another · moral strain = anxiety … against your moral judgement.
Autonomous: your own will / you are responsible. Agentic: for someone else / the authority figure is responsible / moral strain.
displacement of responsibility = passing responsibility for your own actions to the person who gave the order. "Well-rehearsed" state.

p.5 — The evidence

teacher / learner; the learner was a confederate; no real shocks were given.
100% reached 300 V; 65% reached 450 V; 35% refused.
Signs: nervous laughter; sweating, trembling, stuttering; three seizures; blamed the experimenter.
My Lai: Lieutenant William Calley.

p.6 — Agency theory: support

65% continued to the highest level. A theory is stronger when what it predicts is what happens.
Moral strain: a detail it was not built to explain.
Debrief: the experimenter was responsible — the mechanism was reported by participants.
My Lai: following orders; explains obedience outside the laboratory.
Application: whistleblowing.

p.7 — Agency theory: criticisms

35% refused; Brandt (grew up in Nazi Germany) resisted.
Latané: strength, immediacy, number; the same person refuses in another setting.
reductionist = explaining a complex behaviour by isolated factors / a single cause.
Application: understanding malevolent authority helps institutions limit the damage.

p.8 — Five bases

French and Raven (1959) — five bases; power need not be real, only perceived.
Coercive = punish (不听话会变糟). Reward = reward / incentive (照做有好处). Legitimate = official / recognised position (这个位置本就该被服从). Expert = superior knowledge or expertise (对方比我懂). Referent = respect or admiration (我想成为 / 被那样的人接纳).
Bridge table: agency = the person obeying; social power = the authority figure / the kind of power.

p.9 — Context & support

Power is contingent upon context: none in the next setting; referee holds legitimate power on the field; reward power works only if the target wants the reward.
Hierarchies: legitimate + coercive power. Protection: train soldiers to resist. Milgram: Yale (legitimate) + lab coat / Mr Williams (expert). Handles several bases at once.

p.10 — Social power: criticisms

Ignores the context / circumstances of the order; Latané: proximity, strength, number.
Ignores individual differences: dissent, personality type, gender, culture — two people make opposite choices.
Social control: manage or control others' behaviour; the same knowledge can oppress.

p.13 · Q1 — Brandon (2 marks)

Brandon loaded the boxes because he was in the agentic state in the presence of his manager — an authority figure who could give him an instruction — and he displaced responsibility for the task onto the manager. When the instruction arrived by email instead, the manager was no longer immediate: without the authority figure present, Brandon slipped back towards the autonomous state and did not finish unpacking on time. Each point names the state and ties it to a detail (present / away).

p.13 · Q2 — Katrina (2 marks)

The doctor is an authority figure on the ward, so Katrina shifted into the agentic state and acted as the doctor's agent rather than on her own judgement. She experienced moral strain — she was worried about the dose, which shows the order went against her own moral judgement — but displaced responsibility onto the doctor and carried out the order anyway.

p.13 · Q3 — naming the bases (2 marks)

(a) Coercive power (punishment). (b) Expert power (superior knowledge). One word in the question decides which of the five it is.

p.13 · Q4 — social power theory, 1 strength + 2 weaknesses (6 marks)

Strength: it can be applied in training military personnel in whistleblowing policies if they believe a superior is abusing a position of legitimate power — which could reduce atrocities committed by soldiers carrying out orders without question. Why: a theory that tells you where to intervene is worth more than one that only describes.
Weakness 1: French and Raven claim obedience is a direct result of the power the authority figure is perceived to possess, which ignores individual differences of personality — for example, individuals with an internal locus of control may be more likely to dissent because they take personal responsibility for their actions. Why: two people facing the same authority make opposite choices.
Weakness 2: the theory fails to take account of the circumstances in which the order is given — Latané shows that proximity, strength and number change obedience, so the same authority giving the same order gets different results in different settings. Why: the setting itself must be part of any explanation.

p.14 · Q5 — Jorge (8 marks)

Expert power. The specialist has superior knowledge and expertise; Jorge believes he knows whether the noise is dangerous, so he slows down on his advice and ignores his friends, who have no such knowledge.
Referent power. Jorge has known the specialist a long time and admires his work, so he identifies with him and wants his approval — the relationship is why the advice is taken seriously.
Legitimate power. The specialist's qualifications place him in an acknowledged role about whether a car is roadworthy, so his instruction carries the weight of the position as well as of the person.
The comparison that answers the question. The friends give the same advice in the opposite direction, but hold none of these bases — no expertise, no recognised position, no admired status. Perceived power is what separates two identical messages.
