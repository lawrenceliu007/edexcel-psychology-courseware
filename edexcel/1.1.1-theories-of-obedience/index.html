2. What Is Psychology?

🌳 One garden, several ways of looking

Imagine a garden. A biologist studies the plants, insects and soil. An artist studies the colours, shapes and light. A child sees somewhere to play. A poet sees beauty. The garden has not changed — what changes is the way of looking at it.

Psychology works the same way. One person, one behaviour, and several different explanations. Each way of explaining behaviour is called an approach（研究取向）. Psychology studies behaviour from four main approaches.

ApproachHow it explains behaviourMethods it typically uses
Biological
生物取向		Behaviour is explained by biological structures and processes: the brain, the nervous system, hormones and genes.Experiments, brain scans, twin studies
Cognitive
认知取向		Behaviour is explained by internal mental processes: memory, perception, attention, thinking and language.Experiments, case studies
Learning
学习取向		Behaviour is explained by experience: what we associate, what we are rewarded or punished for, and what we observe others do.Experiments, observations
Social
社会取向		Behaviour is explained by the presence of others — or by their real or imagined pressure.Experiments, observations, questionnaires, interviews, correlations
我们现在站在哪个角？Social approach。它不问"你脑子里发生了什么"，而问"谁在你身边、谁在对你说话"。图书馆里的那把椅子，用生物取向解释会很荒谬，用社会取向解释就很自然 —— 这就是取向的意义：先选镜头，再拍照片。

3. Theory, Study, Methodology

Every topic in this course is built from the same three pieces. Learn these three words once and the rest of the course becomes much easier to organise.

theory（理论） — a set of principles that attempt to explain behaviour.

study（研究） — a piece of research carried out to test a theory, or to explore behaviour.

methodology（方法学） — the method used to collect data, and how the study is designed.

Figure 1 · How the three pieces fit together

📌 What you are asked to do with each one

  • Evaluate it — judge it with evidence: what supports it, what it cannot explain.
  • Use it — apply it to a situation you have never seen before.

4. Where Social Psychology Began

Social psychology did not start in a laboratory. It started with a crowd.

  • Gustave Le Bon (1895) watched how people behave in crowds and described how individuals seem to lose themselves in a group. His work was observational and descriptive rather than scientific.
  • Floyd Allport (1924) argued that social psychology should use experimental methods — that claims about how people influence each other should be tested, not just described.

That argument was won. Social psychology today studies how our thoughts, feelings and behaviour are influenced by the real or imagined presence of others, and it does so with experiments, observations and self-reports.

5. Three Forms of Social Influence

social influence（社会影响） — how our opinions, beliefs and behaviour are influenced by the real or imagined pressure of an individual or a group.

Social influence comes in three forms. They differ in one thing only: who influences whom.

FormWho influences whomDefinitionEveryday example
Conformity
从众		a majority → youA form of social influence where our opinions, beliefs and/or behaviour are influenced by a majority.Everyone in your class wears the same trainers, so you buy the same pair.
Minority influence
少数派影响		a minority → the majorityA form of social influence where the opinions, beliefs and/or behaviour of the majority are influenced by a minority (an individual or a small group).One student starts a recycling habit; slowly the whole form follows.
Obedience
服从		an authority figure → youComplying to the orders of an authority figure.A lifeguard blows the whistle and you get out of the pool.
三者的分界线：看"压力来自谁"。来自大多数人是 conformity；来自少数人却改变了多数人是 minority influence；来自有权力的人是 obedience。考试给情境时，先找"那个人凭什么让你做" —— 凭人多还是凭职位 —— 答案就分出来了。

6. Obedience

obedience（服从） — complying to the orders of an authority figure.

hierarchical（等级制的） — a system of social organisation where there are leaders and those who follow.

socialisation（社会化） — the process by which we learn the rules and norms of society through socialising agents, such as parents and teachers.

We are trained to obey

Most of us live in a hierarchical society: there are people who give instructions and people who follow them. From early childhood, socialisation teaches us the rule — parents, teachers and police officers give instructions, and we are rewarded for obeying and corrected for refusing. By adulthood, obeying is close to a habit.

为什么这件事值得学？训练出来的服从让社会能运转 —— 红灯停、排队、交作业，都靠它。但同一套机制也解释了最黑暗的事：当发命令的人要你伤害别人时，你还会照做吗？

The dark side

In the twentieth century, ordinary people in hierarchical societies — soldiers, clerks, railway workers — took part in the systematic murder of millions. When the perpetrators were put on trial, the same defence was repeated again and again: "I was only following orders."

Stanley Milgram, a psychologist in the United States, wanted to know whether this defence means anything. His question was not "are these people evil?" but:

❓ Milgram's question

How far will an ordinary person go when someone in authority tells them to hurt another person?

To answer it, Milgram ran a series of experiments — and then proposed agency theory to explain what he saw.

7. Agency Theory: Two States of Mind

Milgram's agency theory says that we can be in one of two psychological states, and that obedience happens when we move from the first into the second.

autonomy（自主） — acting on one's own free will, exercising self-determination.

agency（代理） — when we act as an agent for another, for example when we obey orders.

moral strain（道德紧张） — experiencing anxiety because you are asked to do something that goes against your moral judgement.

Figure 2 · Agency theory: the agentic shift and moral strain
两态怎么理解：自主态是"我自己决定，出了事我负责"；代理态是"我只是替他执行，出事他负责"。这个把责任交给别人的动作叫 displacement of responsibility（责任转移），是代理态的核心 —— 人不是突然变坏，而是悄悄把"负责的人"换掉了。

🔁 Why the shift is so easy

Because socialisation has already prepared us for it. A hierarchical society trains the move long before anyone gives us a harmful order: teacher says sit down, coach says run, manager says sign. The agentic state is not a rare state of mind — for most of us it is a well-rehearsed one.

8. The Evidence

confederate（同谋者） — a researcher or actor who pretends to be a participant in a study.

In Milgram's first experiment, participants believed they were taking part in a study about learning and memory. Each participant was given the role of teacher and was told to give an electric shock to a learner every time the learner made a mistake, increasing the shock each time. The learner was in another room — and the learner was a confederate. No real shocks were given, but the participant believed they were.

Figure 3 · How far participants went in Milgram's first experiment

Signs of moral strain

The theory predicts that people caught between "I must obey" and "this is wrong" will show distress — and that is what was observed:

  • nervous laughter while giving the shocks
  • sweating, trembling and stuttering
  • three participants had seizures
  • in the debrief afterwards, participants rated the shocks they believed they had given as extremely painful — and said the experimenter, not themselves, was responsible
这条证据为什么关键：如果人真的完全进入了代理态、内心毫无波澜，我们不应该看到笑、抖、抽搐这些反应。正因为看到了，Milgram 才说：人不是"变成了坏人"，而是一边照做一边承受冲突 —— 这就是 moral strain。

9. Evaluating Agency Theory

Support

Research support: the numbers

In Milgram's first experiment, 65% of participants continued to the highest shock level. Most people did make the agentic shift.

Evidence a theory is stronger when the thing it predicts is what actually happens.

Moral strain was visible

Nervous laughter, sweating, trembling and seizures were observed. Agency theory predicts exactly this inner conflict.

Evidence the theory explains a detail it was not built to explain.

Displacement of responsibility

In the debrief, participants said the experimenter was responsible for what happened, not themselves — the defining feature of the agentic state.

Evidence the mechanism itself was reported by the participants.

Real-life support

At the My Lai massacre, Lieutenant William Calley and his men killed unarmed civilians; his defence was that he was following orders.

Application the theory explains obedience outside the laboratory too.

Criticisms

It cannot explain individual differences

35% of Milgram's participants refused to continue. If the agentic shift were automatic, this should not happen. Some people resist: one participant, known as Brandt, had grown up in Nazi Germany and refused to obey.

Individual differences Why it matters: personality and upbringing must be part of the explanation, not just the state you are in.

It ignores situational factors

Latané (1981) argued that obedience depends on the strength, immediacy and number of the sources of influence. Whether the authority is close by, important to you, and how many people are pressing you matters more than a shift of state.

Situational factors Why it matters: the same person obeys in one setting and refuses in another, so the setting has to be explained.

It is reductionist

reductionist（还原论的） — a theory that explains a complex behaviour by isolated factors, or a single cause, which ignores other important factors; such a theory can be argued to be simplistic. Agency theory explains obedience with one mechanism and leaves out culture, personality, mood and the situation.

Reductionism Why it matters: a single-cause explanation will always fit some cases badly.

Useful application

If obedience comes from passing responsibility upwards, then organisations can be designed to hand responsibility back: clear rules that a harmful order can be refused, and protection for people who report one — whistleblowing — instead of treating refusal as disobedience.

Application understanding malevolent authority — someone who uses their authority for harmful purposes — helps institutions limit the damage it does.

评价的写法套路：每一条批评都由两块组成 —— 说什么不对（理论解释不了个体差异）+ 为什么这是个问题（因为 35% 的人拒绝了，个体差异必须被解释）。只写第一块只能算说了一半；两块都写完整，才是评价题要的答案。

10. Whose Voice Is It?

🎭 Five people, one request Textbook activity

Someone asks you to stay behind after school on Friday to stack 200 chairs in the hall. The words are exactly the same every time. Only the person asking changes. Pick one and see what your reason for obeying turns out to be.

Five different reasons, five different kinds of "because". French and Raven (1959) gave each of them a name, and together they form a second explanation of obedience — one that looks at the person giving the order instead of the person carrying it out.

这一节的重点：上一节我们说服从是因为当事人进入了 agentic state（代理状态）——看的是服从的人。这一节换个方向：看下命令的人手里有什么。同样一句话，从班主任嘴里说出来和从明星嘴里说出来，我们照做的原因是完全不同的两回事。这就是 social power theory（社会权力理论）的出发点。

11. Two Theories, One Behaviour

Agency theory and social power theory are trying to explain the same event: an ordinary person doing what an authority figure tells them to do. They simply start from different places.

Agency theorySocial power theory
Who is being explained?The person obeying — what state they are in.The authority figure — what power they have.
Key termsautonomous state, agentic state, agentic shift, moral strain, displacement of responsibility.coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent power.
One sentence"They obeyed because responsibility moved from them to the person in charge.""They obeyed because of the kind of power that person was seen to hold."

Two explanations, not two enemies

Both explanations can be true of the same moment: a person may shift into the agentic state because the authority figure looked legitimate. Being able to say both — and to say which one fits a given situation better — is what an evaluation question rewards.

12. French & Raven's Five Bases of Power

social power theory（社会权力理论） — John French and Bertram Raven (1959) identified five bases of power, which are said to motivate and influence behaviour. Someone can exert influence or control because of the source of power they possess.

Notice one word doing a lot of work: perceived. The theory does not require the power to be real — it requires the target to believe it is there. If an individual has a base of social power, or is perceived to have that power, they have potential influence over other people, which may explain obedience.

French and Raven's model: each base is a different reason why the same order gets obeyed.

The five bases, one at a time

Coercive power（强制权）

The ability to impose a punishment — for example, a teacher can issue a detention.

照做是因为：不听话会变糟。

Reward power（奖励权）

The ability to give a reward or incentive — for example, a parent can give pocket money.

照做是因为：照做有好处。

Legitimate power（法定权）

An official / recognised position — for example, a police officer or an elected official.

照做是因为：这个位置本来就该被服从。

Expert power（专家权）

Superior knowledge or expertise held by a credible influence — for example, a college professor.

照做是因为：对方比我懂。

Referent power（感召权）

Respect or admiration — for example, a celebrity or a charismatic leader.

照做是因为：我想成为、或想被接纳为那样的人。

What counts as expert and referent power changes with who you ask

Wangari Maathai founded the Green Belt Movement and was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 2004 for her work in environment conservation and sustainability. To someone who cares about the environment she holds strong referent power; to someone who has never heard of her, she holds none at all. Power lives in the relationship, not only in the person.

记五个词的捷径：不要背字母顺序，背箭头方向。coercive 是推（惩罚），reward 是拉（奖励），legitimate 是位置，expert 是知识，referent 是认同。考试给一个场景让你判断是哪种 power，先看场景里出现的具体动作：有人会被罚？有人会得到东西？有人戴着制服/有头衔？有人说的话别人听不懂？有人被崇拜？

13. Power Depends on Context

The five bases are contingent upon context（取决于情境）— the same person can hold power in one setting and none in the next. A football referee maintains legitimate power on the field during a game, but not off the field once the game is over. Reward power only works if the target actually wants the reward on offer.

⏱️ Same referee, two moments

The referee blows the whistle and tells a player to leave the pitch. Choose the moment and see the power disappear.

这一点是得分点也是失分点：很多同学写 "the manager has legitimate power"，但忘了说明在什么情境下这个权力成立。情境一变，权力就消失 —— 在评价题里，这正是 "social power theory fails to take account of context" 这条批评的由来（下一节）。

14. Evaluating Social Power Theory

Support

It explains how organisations create obedience

Social power theory can be applied to society: corporations and the military use hierarchies to build structures of obedience. Senior officers hold legitimate power over lower-ranking soldiers, and also reward and coercive power, because they can incentivise or sanction behaviour under their command. This may explain why malevolent authority（恶意权威）has been implicated in many war crimes.

Application the theory reaches outside the laboratory and into real institutions.

It can be turned into protection

Because the theory says where power comes from, it can also be used to train soldiers to resist malevolent authority, and to promote whistleblowing policies（举报制度）that allow anonymous reporting of destructive obedience.

Application a good theory is not only descriptive — it tells you where to intervene.

Milgram's own study fits it

Milgram found that ordinary participants would shock an innocent victim because of the perceived power of the experimenter giving the orders. The experiment was conducted at Yale University, a highly regarded university, so participants may have perceived legitimate power in the setting. They may also have perceived Mr Williams, dressed in a lab coat, as holding expert power as a researcher — he was in control and knew what the shocks would do.

Evidence the same data supports a second explanation, which is exactly what you want in an evaluation.

Two sources of power at once

The Yale setting (institution) and the lab coat (expertise) acted together. People rarely obey for one reason, and this theory can handle several bases operating at the same time.

Evidence it can explain a detail of the study without being invented for it.

Criticisms

It ignores the circumstances of the order

Social power theory fails to take account of the context in which the authority figure gives orders. Latané suggests that the proximity（距离近）, strength（强度/地位）and number（人数）of authority figures relative to the target are important situational factors that determine the impact of influence.

Situational factors Why it matters: the same person obeys the same order shouted across the room and refuses it given over the telephone, so the setting itself has to be part of the explanation.

It ignores individual differences

The theory fails to explain differences in obedience and dissent（异议）. Obedience and dissent can occur for reasons of personality type, gender and cultural factors. Focusing on the power held by the authority figure ignores the individual differences of the target.

Individual differences Why it matters: two people facing the same authority with the same power make opposite choices, so something inside the person must be doing work too.

It is therefore reductionist

Because it traces obedience back to one thing — the perceived power of the authority figure — it can be argued to be a reductionist（还原论的）explanation.

Reductionism Why it matters: a single-cause explanation always fits some cases badly, and those cases are precisely the ones exam questions ask about.

Wider issue: social control

Social control（社会控制）considers how psychological knowledge may be used to manage or control other people's behaviour. Social power theory gives knowledge that can be used to manage individuals in contexts such as the workplace, raising ethical questions about the use of power in society and organisations, particularly if coercive measures are used to command obedience, as in an oppressive regime. If social power is used responsibly, however, it can maintain order and safety.

Ethics the same knowledge that protects can also be used to oppress.

评价的写作套路：每一条都由两块拼起来 —— 理论漏掉了什么（ignore the context / ignore individual differences）+ 为什么这是个问题（因为同一个人换个情境就反抗了 / 因为两个人面对同一个权威做出相反选择）。只写前半句是描述，两块一起才是评价。另注意：两条 weakness 本身正好预告了 1.1.3 —— 情境因素和个体差异，就是下一节要填的两个坑。

16. Exam Lab

🧭 IDEA — how to answer a question with a scenario

  • I — Identify: decide which piece of psychology the scenario is about (agency theory, or one of the five bases of power).
  • E — Explain: state that piece of psychology accurately, using its own terms.
  • A — Apply: link it to the details of the scenario — name the people, say who holds the power, say what the person actually did.

An answer that only repeats the theory and never touches the scenario earns nothing, even if the theory is described perfectly. A sentence that says what earns one mark; the same sentence plus why it matters earns two.

✍️ Practice 1 — 2 marks

Brandon works in a warehouse. The manager came to speak to him and instructed him to load 10 boxes of goods onto a collection van by 11:00 am. Brandon followed the instructions and completed the task. The manager had to leave the warehouse for a meeting at another site. He emailed Brandon to ask him to unpack five boxes by 3:00 pm. Brandon did not complete this task on time, finishing the unpacking at 4:00 pm.

Describe, using agency theory, why Brandon may have behaved in this way.

Model answer

  • Brandon loaded the boxes because he was in the agentic state in the presence of his manager, an authority figure who could give him an instruction, and he passed responsibility for the task to the manager.
  • When the instruction arrived by email instead, the manager was no longer immediate: without the authority figure present, Brandon slipped back towards the autonomous state and did not finish unpacking on time.

Why this scores: each point names the state and ties it to a detail of the scenario (the manager present / the manager away).

✍️ Practice 2 — 2 marks

Katrina is a nurse. One of the doctors on the hospital ward tells Katrina to give a patient a higher dose of their medication than they would normally have. Katrina is worried about the increase in the medication, but she gives the higher dose of medication to the patient anyway.

Describe, using agency theory, why Katrina gives the higher dose of medication to the patient.

Model answer

  • The doctor is an authority figure on the ward, so Katrina shifted from the autonomous state into the agentic state and acted as the doctor's agent rather than on her own judgement.
  • She experienced moral strain — she was worried about the dose, which shows the order went against her own moral judgement — but she displaced responsibility onto the doctor and carried out the order anyway.

Why this scores: the worry in the scenario is evidence of moral strain, and it is used instead of ignored.

✍️ Practice 3 — 2 marks

Two definitions of types of social power are given below. Name the type that each one describes.

  • The authority figure has an ability (perceived or real) to punish a person for disobedience.
  • The authority figure has superior knowledge and / or skills (perceived or real).

Coercive power for the first, and expert power for the second.

How to tell them apart: punishment points to coercive, knowledge points to expert. One word in the question decides which of the five it is.

✍️ Practice 4 — 6 marks

(a) Explain one strength of social power theory as an explanation of obedience.
(b) Explain two weaknesses of social power theory as an explanation of obedience.

Model structure

  • Strength: social power theory can be applied in training military personnel in whistleblowing policies if they believe a superior is abusing a position of legitimate power — which could reduce the atrocities committed by soldiers carrying out the orders of officers in appointed positions of authority without question.
  • Weakness 1: French and Raven claim obedience is a direct result of the type of power the authority figure is perceived to possess, which ignores individual differences of personality — for example, individuals with an internal locus of control may be more likely to dissent because they take personal responsibility for their actions.
  • Weakness 2: the theory fails to take account of the circumstances in which the order is given. Latané's social impact theory shows that proximity, strength and number change obedience, so the same authority producing the same order gets different results in different settings.

Why this scores: every point names an issue and gives the reason it is a strength or a weakness. A list of three issues with no reasons would lose half the marks.

✍️ Practice 5 — 8 marks, applied

Jorge has recently bought a car. He has noticed an unusual noise if he drives fast. His friends hear the noise but tell him that it is nothing to worry about and he should still drive fast. Jorge is very concerned, so he speaks to the car specialist at his local garage. He has known the car specialist for a long time and trusts their advice. The car specialist tells him to bring the car into the garage the next day and only drive at a slow speed.

Discuss, using social power theory, why Jorge obeys the car specialist and not his friends. You must make reference to the context in your answer.

Model answer — four developed points

  • Expert power. The car specialist has superior knowledge and expertise: he has qualifications and Jorge believes he knows whether the noise is dangerous, so Jorge slows down on his advice and ignores his friends, who have no such knowledge.
  • Referent power. Jorge has known the specialist for a long time and admires his work, so he identifies with him and wants his approval — that relationship is why the advice is taken seriously, while the friends are people he does not identify with in this way.
  • Legitimate power. The specialist's qualifications place him in an acknowledged role about whether a car is roadworthy, so his instruction carries the weight of the position as well as of the person.
  • A comparison that answers the question. The friends give the same advice in the opposite direction, but they hold none of these three bases of power — no expertise, no recognised position, no admired status. Perceived power is what separates two identical messages.

Why this scores: half the marks come from describing the theory and half from applying it to the details of the scenario. The last point uses the friends, a detail most answers throw away, to answer the "and not his friends" part of the question directly.

✍️ Practice 6 — 8 marks (planning only)

The same question shape can name either theory. Practise the plan for both:

  • Assess how well agency theory can explain obedience in society.
  • Evaluate how well social power theory can account for obedience.

Half the marks come from outlining the theory accurately and half from judging it with evidence. Plan four paragraphs.

If the question names agency theory:

ParagraphWhat goes in it

If the question names social power theory: half the marks are for describing the theory and the evidence accurately, and half are for weighing it up.

ParagraphWhat goes in it

🔑 Exam key points — what a strong answer contains ›
  1. Obedience is complying to the orders of an authority figure — that is what separates it from conformity.
  2. Agency theory: autonomous state (own free will, own responsibility) versus agentic state (acting for another, responsibility displaced).
  3. Moral strain is the evidence that the shift is uncomfortable: laughter, sweating, trembling.
  4. The five bases are coercive, reward, legitimate, expert, referent — and the theory cares about power that is perceived, not necessarily real.
  5. Power is contingent on context: the referee has it during the match and loses it afterwards.
  6. Both theories draw the same two criticisms: they ignore the context of the order and ignore individual differences, which makes them reductionist.
  7. Every criticism needs a why it matters sentence, not just a statement that the theory is limited.
  8. In any scenario answer, name the people — who holds the power, and what each person did.

17. Key Terms

social influence社会影响
How our opinions, beliefs and behaviour are influenced by the real or imagined pressure of an individual or group.
conformity从众
A form of social influence where our opinions, beliefs and/or behaviour are influenced by a majority.
minority influence少数派影响
A form of social influence where the opinions, beliefs and/or behaviour of the majority are influenced by a minority (individual or small group).
obedience服从
Complying to the orders of an authority figure.
hierarchical等级制的
A system of social organisation where there are leaders and those who follow.
socialisation社会化
The process by which we learn the rules and norms of society through socialising agents, such as parents and teachers.
autonomy自主
Acting on one's own free will, exercising self-determination.
agency代理
When we act as an agent for another, for example when we obey orders.
moral strain道德紧张
Experiencing anxiety because you are asked to do something that goes against your moral judgement.
confederate同谋者
A researcher or actor who pretends to be a participant in a study.
displacement of responsibility责任转移
Passing responsibility for your own actions to the person who gave the order.
individual differences个体差异
Natural variation in human characteristics — here, the fact that some people obey and some refuse.
malevolent authority恶意权威
Someone who uses their authority for harmful purposes.
reductionist还原论的
Explaining a complex behaviour by isolated factors or a single cause, which ignores other important factors; such a theory can be argued to be simplistic.
theory理论
A set of principles that attempt to explain behaviour.
study研究
A piece of research carried out to test a theory, or to explore behaviour.
methodology方法学
The method used to collect data, and how the study is designed.
social power theory社会权力理论
French and Raven's (1959) explanation of obedience: five bases of power which can motivate and influence behaviour.
coercive power强制权
The ability to impose a punishment, e.g. a teacher can issue a detention.
reward power奖励权
The ability to give a reward or incentive, e.g. a parent can give pocket money.
legitimate power法定权
An official / recognised position, e.g. a police officer or elected official.
expert power专家权
Superior knowledge or expertise held by a credible influence, e.g. a college professor.
referent power感召权
Respect or admiration, e.g. a celebrity or charismatic leader.
whistleblowing举报
Anonymous reporting of destructive obedience, used to protect people from malevolent authority.
social control社会控制
How psychological knowledge may be used to manage or control the behaviour of others — which can be useful or unjust.
