How a jury works 陪审团怎么运作
  1. Typically jurors, selected from an electoral roll of local people.12 · randomly 12 人 · 随机
  2. Strict rules: jurors must not with anyone outside the jury, and must avoid of the case.discuss the case · media coverage 讨论案件 · 媒体报道

The mechanism 机制

During a trial jurors can see and hear the defendant. Characteristics such as can trigger that jurors already hold.race, gender, attractiveness, accent · stereotypes 种族、性别、外表、口音 · 刻板印象

How a stereotype becomes a verdict 刻板印象怎么变成判决
看到某一类人 → 脑子自动归类（categorise）→ 自动带上"这类人更可能犯罪"的预期 → 影响对证据的解读。陪审员不是故意的——这就是为什么它危险。
Why researchers take race seriously 为什么认真对待
  • Human Rights Watch (2000) reported that minorities are imprisoned in America — a larger share of the prison population than of the general population.disproportionately 不成比例地
  • Juries are randomly selected, but problems arise when the defendant's race .differs from the majority of jurors 与陪审员多数不同
  • In voir dire（陪审员遴选）, lawyers may question potential jurors. Selecting by race directly is , but lawyers can ask about racial bias.not permitted · indirectly 不被允许 · 间接地
The common finding from mock trials 模拟审判的常见发现

White jurors in mock trials show bias to Black defendants: giving them sentences, and finding Black defendants guilty than white defendants.negative · harsher · more 负面 · 更重 · 更多

Vocabulary to use in answers 答案用词
bias 偏向（不是 prejudice 也可以）· leniency 宽大（判得轻）· lenient（形容词）· guilty verdict 有罪判决。写评价时用 "showed leniency to defendants of their own race" 比 "was nicer" 专业得多。

p.19 — Q1a/Q1b model (4 + 4 marks)

Strengths. ① Control: ZsaZsi keeps the trial identical and varies only witness confidence, so confidence is the likely cause of any verdict difference. ② Generalisability of mock juries: Bornstein (1999) found little difference between student and non-student mock juries, and Penrod and Cutler (1989) found students judged the same as jury-eligible jurors — so common-room students may still represent jurors.

Weaknesses. ① Low ecological validity: participants know no one will really go to prison, and there is no deliberation, yet Ruva et al. (2007) showed deliberation changes the effect of bias. ② Sample: students from one common room are unrepresentative; Bornstein notes that where differences appear, students tend to be more lenient, which could underestimate guilty verdicts.

p.19 — Q2 model (3 marks)

① Pre-trial publicity is information about a case that reaches potential jurors through the media — internet, television, newspapers, social media — before the trial begins. ② Factual publicity contains incriminating information and can be corrected as the trial presents evidence, whereas emotional publicity arouses negative emotion (e.g. about the defendant's past) and is longer-lasting. ③ It works through schemas and through source monitoring error: jurors mistake media reports for evidence heard in court.

p.19 — Q3 model (2 marks)

① Mitchell et al. (2005) meta-analysed 46 data samples from 34 studies and found a small but significant racial bias — more guilty verdicts when the defendant was a different race from the juror. ② Bradbury and Williams (2013) found Black defendants were more likely to be convicted by juries with a higher proportion of white and Hispanic jurors.

p.20 — Q4 model (8 marks) — key numbers to have used

¶1 Study One: 128 experiments, 16,950 participants — lab findings hold in the field, so expert testimony has grounds. ¶2 Study Two: 321 + 129; ten factors, 64 versions; only witness confidence mattered — jurors naïve. ¶3 Study Three: 538 students; sensitivity improved; less weight on confidence; descriptive better than descriptive-and-quantitative; no scepticism effect. ¶4 Evaluation: builds logically, realistic video, two samples — but still mock with no consequences, students only, and contradicted by Jones et al. (2017), who found expert testimony did not help jurors distinguish good from bad evidence.
