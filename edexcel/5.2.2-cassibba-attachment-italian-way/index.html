5.2.2 CONTEMPORARY STUDY: CASSIBBA ET AL. (2013)
Attachment the Italian way · meta-analysis of 67 studies · 依恋的意大利方式

Topic E · Developmental psychology Contemporary study Spec 5.2.2 📖 Textbook Book 2 · Ch.4 · pp.59–63

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Describe the aim: whether Italian attachment patterns match the global distribution, and whether religiosity explains fewer unresolved attachments.
  • Describe the method and sample: a meta-analysis of 67 studies (1990–2009), 627 children and 2258 adults.
  • State the key findings for babies (Type A is higher than the US) and adults (fewer unresolved), with the table figures.
  • Evaluate the study on reliability, experimenter bias, publication bias, temporal validity and social sensitivity.
  • Answer an application question about how the findings support families at risk.

What this page is really asking

  1. Does attachment look the same in Italy? The global picture is "secure is most common". Cassibba's team asked whether a country with strong family values and deep Catholic faith fits that picture.
  2. The answer is mostly yes, with two twists. Secure is still the biggest group, but Italian babies show more avoidant (Type A) attachment than the US, and Italian adults show fewer unresolved attachments.
  3. The "why" is where it gets careful. The team suggested Catholic faith explains the low unresolved rate, but they never actually measured anyone's religiosity.
  4. It is a meta-analysis, so its strengths and flaws are the meta-analysis ones. Big samples, but also experimenter bias, publication bias and a time window that may now be out of date.

Start Here: One Country, Two Italies

背景意大利不是一种文化：北方更像北欧，看重个人独立；南方更看重家庭纽带，天主教信仰也很深。这样一个国家，孩子的依恋模式会跟全球平均一样吗？
角色你是 Cassibba 研究组的一员。你们手里有几十个在意大利做的依恋研究，但没有经费去重新观察任何一对母婴。
任务把这些已有的研究合并起来，回答两件事：意大利婴儿和成人的依恋分布跟全球像不像？宗教虔诚到底有没有让意大利人的「未解决依恋」更少？
目标先猜下面这题。读完这一页，你能说出意大利和美国的两个具体差异，也能说清楚「宗教性」这个结论哪里站不住。猜完往下追两层。

先猜把意大利几十个依恋研究合并起来，最可能看到什么？

追问猜完先别走：这两个问题没有选项，先自己想，再点开提示。

追问一：研究组说「意大利未解决依恋少，是因为天主教信仰」，但他们没有测量任何一个人的宗教虔诚程度。这个结论站得住吗？

提示

站不住，除非他们真的测了宗教性。他们只看到了「未解决依恋少」和「意大利天主教文化浓」这两件事同时存在，就把它俩连了起来。这是相关不等于因果，也是社会敏感：用「天主教」解释一群人的心理，可能被拿来支持某些政治立场。评价一节会专门讲这一点。

追问二：一个 meta-analysis 如果只挑「发表了的、结果显著」的研究，会漏掉什么？

提示

会漏掉那些「没发现差异」的研究。期刊不爱发表阴性结果，所以被雪藏的研究装满了抽屉，这叫 file drawer effect（文件柜效应）。这会让已发表的文献看起来比实际更支持某个结论。Cassibba 特意收录了未发表的博士论文和会议论文，就是为了对抗这个偏差。但未发表的东西没经过同行评审，又会带来新问题。

两个追问先放在心里。往下读，你会看到这个研究怎么把几十项研究拼成一张意大利的依恋地图，以及这张地图哪里可信、哪里要打问号。

1. Background and Aim

Italy mixes two things that usually pull in opposite directions. In some regions, people hold the individualist values of the West. In others, family loyalty comes first — a more collectivist trait. Previous research had shown that this individualist–collectivist balance predicts the ratio of Type A to Type C attachment.

Rosalinda Cassibba and her colleagues also had a second idea to test. They thought that a high level of religiosity（宗教性：一个人认同某个宗教、并让信仰影响生活的程度） might shape adult attachment types — specifically the unresolved（未解决：与过去创伤、丧失相关的成人依恋类型，带着未处理的负面情绪） category.

So the aim had four parts. Does the Italian distribution of child and adult attachment: match the global pattern (secure as the most common type), differ for people with and without psychological disorders, and show a gender difference? And do Italians have fewer unresolved attachments than people in other nations because of their strong Catholic faith?

Back to the briefing: you are not testing one study, but a whole country. The tool for that is the one you met in 5.2.1 — a meta-analysis.

中文理解：背景一句话：意大利同时有个人主义（北方）和重家庭的集体主义（南方）两股力量，这个平衡预测 A 型和 C 型的比例。研究还有第二个假设：宗教虔诚可能影响成人的「未解决」依恋。所以研究目标分四个问题：分布像不像全球、有无心理障碍的人是否不同、有无性别差异、未解决依恋是否因天主教而更少。工具是 5.2.1 用过的那把，即 meta-analysis。

2. Method and Sample

This is a meta-analysis（元分析：把很多项已经做好的研究合并起来，重新分析它们的原始数据）. It is a way of asking one big question — "what does attachment look like in Italy?" — without running a single new observation. Instead, you gather the studies that already exist and re-analyse their numbers together.

The team examined 67 studies published between 1990 and 2009. These split into two groups:

  • 17 Italian replications of the Strange Situation Procedure, used to classify babies and young children.
  • 50 studies using the Adult Attachment Interview or a similar interview, used to classify adults.

The sample was 627 Italian infants and children, plus 2258 adults. To compare Italy against the rest of the world, the team matched their numbers against 21 non-clinical US samples for babies, and against published US adult samples for adults. For the adults, they only included interview studies whose coders agreed closely — an inter-rater reliability of 0.75 or better.

Back to the briefing: 67 studies, two age groups, no new participants. The hard part is not gathering the studies — it is deciding which ones count, and that choice is where the evaluation begins.

中文理解：方法记三个数：67 项研究（1990–2009）、17 项意大利 SSP 复制、50 项成人依恋访谈。样本两组：627 名婴幼儿、2258 名成人。对比基准是美国的非临床样本。成人研究只收「编码者一致性 ≥ 0.75」的，这是为了控制分类的主观性。注意：这是二次数据研究，没有观察任何新参与者，和 5.2.1 一样。

3. Results: Babies and Adults

The results split cleanly into two parts — what the Strange Situation studies found for babies, and what the interviews found for adults.

Babies: secure is still first, but avoidant is high

Most non-clinical Italian children were secure, just like the global picture. But the exact numbers differed from the US. The baseline (21% A / 67% B / 12% C) sat next to a different Italian non-clinical pattern: 33% Type A, 53% Type B, 14% Type C. In other words, Italian children showed more avoidant attachment than American children. Where researchers also coded Type D（混乱/迷失型：面对照料者时行为矛盾、无组织的第四种依恋）, Italy showed 22 per cent compared with the US's 15 per cent.

The clinical sample told a starker story. Only 32 per cent of these infants were securely attached, and the most common insecure type was Type A at 40 per cent. The least likely to be secure were infants whose mothers had a psychological disorder or were considered "at risk" — their secure rate was only 28 per cent.

Adults: fewer unresolved

The adult data looked a lot like the American baseline. Non-clinical Italian mothers were 22% dismissive, 59% secure, 19% preoccupied — very close to the US. Fathers were more often dismissive（疏离型：把关系的重要性看得很轻、回避亲密、强调自立） than preoccupied, but statistical tests found no significant gender difference. On the unresolved category, though, only 10 per cent of non-clinical Italian mothers were unresolved, against 18 per cent of American mothers.

In the clinical group, only 38 per cent were secure. Where the unresolved rating was used, it accounted for 33 per cent of the sample. Parents of children with psychological disorders were especially over-represented in the unresolved category, at 46 per cent. Parents in families exposed to violence were similar, at 42 per cent.

Back to the briefing: the two twists you predicted are both here — babies lean avoidant, adults lean away from unresolved. The next question is what those numbers actually mean.

中文理解：结果分两半。婴儿：非临床意大利分布是 A 33% / B 53% / C 14%，对比美国基线 A 21% / B 67% / C 12%。意大利回避型明显偏高、安全型偏低；Type D 意大利 22% 对美国 15%。临床样本更极端：只有 32% 安全，回避型 40% 成主力，母亲有心理障碍的婴儿安全率最低（28%）。成人：意大利非临床母 22/59/19 跟美国几乎一样，但未解决依恋只有 10%（美国 18%）；临床组只有 38% 安全，父母有心理障碍的 46% 是未解决型。

4. Conclusions

Like the United States, the majority of Italian infants and adults are securely attached, and the cross-cultural differences are modest. That supports the idea that attachment behaviour is universal: babies everywhere tend to form secure bonds. But the higher Type A rate in Italy is evidence for nurture — the differences come from how children are raised, not from anything built in.

Why the extra avoidant attachment? The researchers pointed to differing beliefs about child development. American mothers tend to feel responsible for how their children turn out. Italian mothers believe parenting has less overall effect, and expect infants to show early social maturity. They encourage independence and joining in family life, rather than dependence on the mother. These beliefs may change how mothers interact with infants.

The low unresolved rate, they argued, comes from the strong Catholic faith common in Italy, which may help adults process trauma and loss. That is the conclusion the evaluation will press hardest on.

中文理解：结论三句话：一，多数意大利婴儿和成人安全，跨国差异「温和」，支持依恋的普遍性；二，意大利 A 型偏高是教养（nurture）的证据，因为意大利母亲认为养育影响没那么大、期待孩子早熟独立、鼓励参与家庭而非依赖母亲；三，未解决依恋少，研究者归因于天主教信仰帮助处理创伤和丧失。第三点就是评价要重点敲打的地方。

5. Evaluation

Strengths

  • Reliable classification. The adult interview studies were only included if their coders agreed closely, at an inter-rater reliability of 0.75 or better. So the adult attachment types are not one person's subjective judgement.
  • It avoids the file drawer. The team used unpublished doctoral work and conference papers, not just published journal studies. That dodges publication bias（发表偏差：期刊不爱发表没发现显著差异的研究） and the file drawer effect（文件柜效应：阴性结果被雪藏，让已发表文献显得更支持某个结论）, giving a more balanced view of attachment in Italy.
  • No new ethical cost. The study re-used existing data, so no additional infants or adults were put through any procedure.
  • A real application. The clinical findings point to where support should go: families with psychological disorders, especially those with violence, should be prioritised for parenting interventions to build secure attachment.

Weaknesses

  • Experimenter bias in selection. The studies were found with specific search terms, and the team may have included studies that fit their hypotheses and left out ones that did not. That subjectivity weakens the scientific status of the findings.
  • Unpublished studies are unchecked. Doctoral work and conference papers have not been peer-reviewed, so flaws in how the SSP or interview data were coded may have gone unnoticed.
  • Temporal validity. The data only runs 1990–2009. Italian culture has moved since — people may be less religious and more influenced by globalisation and social media after 2012, so today's distribution could differ.
  • Socially sensitive, and the key claim is unsupported. Grouping people as "Italians", "at-risk mothers" or "Catholics" can change how they are viewed. The claim that Catholic faith explains the low unresolved rate is especially shaky: the study never measured religiosity, yet that claim could be used to support political objectives.

A fair verdict: the "what" is solid — Italy looks mostly like the global pattern, with more avoidant babies and fewer unresolved adults. The "why" is weaker, because the religiosity claim rests on an association, not a measurement.

Exam tip: for a 2-mark application question, give one concrete use and say who it helps. Example: "the finding that infants of mothers with psychological disorders are least likely to be secure means these families should be prioritised for parenting intervention programmes, to help them build secure attachments."

Close the loop

The two questions from the start now have answers. Italy mostly fits the global pattern — secure is still the biggest group — but the avoidant type runs higher in babies and the unresolved type runs lower in adults. And the Catholic explanation for that second difference is exactly the kind of claim a meta-analysis can suggest but not prove, because it was never measured.

Reflect before the Exam Lab: if a study reports that two things happen together in one country, what would you need to see before you accepted that one caused the other?

中文理解：评价「四强四弱」。强：成人研究只要一致性 ≥ 0.75 的（分类可靠）、收录未发表研究躲开发表偏差、二次数据零伦理成本、临床发现能指导家庭支持。弱：检索词选择带实验者偏差、未发表研究没同行评审、只覆盖 1990–2009（时间效度）、把「天主教」扣在未解决依恋上却没测宗教性（社会敏感且站不住）。裁决：what 可靠（意大利大体像全球，回避略高、未解决略低），why 站不住（宗教性是关联不是测量）。

6. Exam Lab

Q1 · Application 2 marks

Explain one practical application of the findings from Cassibba et al. (2013).

The finding that infants whose mothers have a psychological disorder are the least likely to be securely attached (only 28 per cent) suggests these families should be prioritised for additional support (1 mark). Parenting intervention programmes could be offered to help them develop secure attachments, especially in families where there are also cases of violence (1 mark).

Marking guidance: 1 mark for naming a specific finding, 1 mark for explaining how it leads to a concrete use that helps a named group.

Q2 · Describe 2 marks

Describe the method used by Cassibba et al. (2013).

It was a meta-analysis of 67 studies published between 1990 and 2009 (1 mark): 17 Italian replications of the Strange Situation Procedure and 50 studies using the Adult Attachment Interview or a similar schedule, comparing the Italian results against US samples (1 mark).

Marking guidance: 1 mark for meta-analysis (or secondary data), 1 mark for the number/type of studies or the comparison against the US.

Q3 · Evaluate 6 marks

Evaluate Cassibba et al.'s (2013) study.

AO1 — what they did and found. Cassibba et al. meta-analysed 67 studies (1990–2009): 17 Italian Strange Situation replications and 50 adult interview studies, with 627 children and 2258 adults. They compared Italian attachment distributions against US samples. Non-clinical Italian infants showed 33% Type A, 53% Type B, 14% Type C, versus a US baseline of 21/67/12 — so more avoidant, fewer secure. Adults were close to the US, but only 10% of non-clinical Italian mothers were unresolved against 18% of American mothers.

AO3 — strength: reliability and coverage. Adult studies were only included with inter-rater reliability of 0.75 or better, reducing subjectivity in the classifications. Including unpublished doctoral and conference work avoided the file drawer effect, so the picture is more balanced than published-only meta-analyses would give.

AO3 — weakness: bias and validity. Study selection used specific search terms, so experimenter bias may have shaped which studies were included. The unpublished work was not peer-reviewed, so coding flaws may have passed unnoticed. The 1990–2009 window also limits temporal validity, since Italian culture and religiosity have changed with social media.

AO3 — weakness: an unsupported causal claim. The conclusion that Catholic faith explains the low unresolved rate is socially sensitive and unsupported, because religiosity was never actually measured — the link is an association, not evidence of cause.

Marking guidance: AO1 accurate method and findings; AO3 points each tied to a named issue (reliability, publication bias, experimenter bias, temporal validity, social sensitivity) with a consequence for the study's credibility.

8. Key Terms

meta-analysis
元分析：把很多项已做好的研究合并起来，重新分析它们的原始数据
religiosity
宗教性：一个人认同某个宗教、并让信仰影响生活的程度
secure / insecure attachment
安全／不安全依恋：安全型是多数人的类型；不安全包括回避、反抗、混乱等
Type D (disorganised)
混乱/迷失型：面对照料者时行为矛盾、无组织的第四种依恋（Main & Solomon, 1986）
dismissive
疏离型：成人依恋类型，把关系看得轻，回避亲密，强调自立，淡化早年依恋
preoccupied
专注型：成人依恋类型，高度依赖伴侣、极度需要联系和安抚
unresolved
未解决：与过去创伤、丧失相关的成人依恋类型，带着未处理的负面情绪
interview schedule
访谈提纲：访谈中使用的预设问题清单
publication bias
发表偏差：期刊不爱发表没有显著结果的研究
file drawer effect
文件柜效应：阴性结果被雪藏，让已发表文献显得更支持某个结论
temporal validity
时间效度：结论能否推广到其他时代；弱时叫 era-bound（受时代局限）
alpha bias
α 偏误：夸大男女之间的差异，忽视相似之处
