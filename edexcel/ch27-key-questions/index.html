1. Learning Objectives & How Section D is Examined（学习目标与考试形式）

⚡ Quick Summary — 本节要学什么

  1. Unit 4 的 Section D 是一道 8 分的 extended response，专门考查 key question（社会关键议题） 的分析能力。
  2. 题目会给你一个真实社会场景（如职场霸凌），然后让你用它背后的心理学概念、理论或研究来讨论这个问题。
  3. 核心技能是 AO2 = 把知识"套"到给定的情境上——不套情境，写得再多也拿不到分。

🎯 By the end of this chapter you should be able to:

  • Explain what a key question in society is and how it differs from a research aim
  • Decode a Section D question: identify the key question and the given context
  • Apply concepts, theories and research from one or more topics A–H to the context
  • Balance AO1 (knowledge, 4 marks) and AO2 (application, 4 marks) equally
  • Write a Level 4 (7–8) answer with a balanced, context-linked discussion
  • Answer the official WPS04 Q10 workplace-bullying question at top-band standard

Where Section D sits in the paper

Key rule of Section D: the mark scheme states "Candidates must demonstrate an equal emphasis between knowledge and understanding vs application in their answer." This is the only place on the paper where the two halves are weighted exactly 50:50. Half your answer must be knowledge; half must be that knowledge applied to the given context.
中文理解：Section D 最难的地方不是"知道什么"，而是"会不会用"。8 分里有 4 分（AO2）专门奖励把知识扣到题目的情境上。很多同学把"社会支配倾向""规范影响"这些概念背得滚瓜烂熟，通篇却一次都没提到题目给的职场/霸凌场景——这样最多只能拿 4 分。记住口诀：每讲一个概念，就接一句"所以在题目描述的这个情境里……"。

2. What is a "Key Question in Society"?（什么是社会关键议题）

Spec 9.2.1 defines the requirement in a single line:

Spec 9.2.1: "Key questions for society using concepts, theories or research from one or more of topics A to H (except topics F and G)."

A key question in society（社会关键议题） is a real-world, socially important question that psychology can help to answer. It is not a laboratory hypothesis — it is the kind of question a government, employer, school or health service might actually ask.

TopicExample key question
A · SocialHow can destructive obedience be prevented?
B · CognitiveCan we trust eyewitness testimony?
C · BiologicalAre criminals born or made?
D · LearningShould conditioning be used to change behaviour?
E / H · ClinicalCan the media cause eating disorders? Can stigma against mental illness be reduced?
Organisational (WPS04)Can workplace bullying be reduced through improved working relationships?

Key question ≠ research aim

A research aim asks "what will this study find?" (e.g. "to see whether masculinity correlates with aggression"). A key question asks "what should society do / believe?" and demands you bring multiple areas of psychology to bear on it.

中文理解："Key question" 直译是"关键问题"，但它不是随便一个问题。它有两个特征：(1) 面向社会、有现实意义（不是实验室里的假设）；(2) 能用课程里多个主题的知识来回答。比如"职场霸凌能不能靠改善人际关系来减少？"——这既是老板/HR 真实关心的，又能调用社会心理学的权威、规范、社会支配倾向等概念。所以 Section D 本质是在考："你能不能用心理学的工具箱，去回应一个真实世界的问题？"

3. The KQ Answer Framework — Four Moves（KQ 答题四步法）

Every Section D answer can be built with the same four moves. Because the AO1 : AO2 split is 50:50, your plan should be roughly half knowledge, half application.

🧩 The four moves

  1. State the position (AO1/AO2). Briefly say what your answer will be — e.g. "Improved working relationships can reduce bullying, but only alongside structural change."
  2. Bring in concept #1 and apply it (AO1 + AO2). Introduce a concept/theory, then immediately apply it to the context in the question.
  3. Bring in concept #2 and apply it (AO1 + AO2). Choose a second idea — ideally from a different angle (e.g. individual vs organisational).
  4. Balance and conclude (AO2). Acknowledge the limitation or competing view, then give a reasoned judgement.

The application sentence — your AO2 engine

AO2 marks are earned by a simple habit: after every concept, write a sentence of the form "In the context of [the scenario], this means…". Examples:

Knowledge (AO1)Application (AO2)
Normative influence is conformity to a group's beliefs to gain acceptance."In a workplace, an employee may stay silent about bullying because they want to be accepted by colleagues who treat it as normal."
Social dominance orientation predicts egocentric behaviour."A manager high in social dominance orientation may bully to defend their position in the workplace hierarchy."

❌ All AO1 (max ~4/8)

Accurate theory, accurate studies — but the workplace context is never mentioned. The examiner cannot award AO2.

✅ Balanced AO1 + AO2 (7–8/8)

Accurate theory and every point is tied back to the scenario, with a balanced judgement at the end.

4. Worked Example — Workplace Bullying (WPS04 Q10)（真题拆解）

📝 The question (WPS04/01, Section D, Q10 — 8 marks)

Context given in the paper

One key question for society is whether bullying in the workplace can be reduced through improved working relationships.

Fast and Chen (2009) claimed that workplace aggression appeared to be driven by ego defensiveness, when someone in power felt a sense of incompetence in their role. They claimed this aggressiveness was eliminated when the individual's sense of self-worth was boosted.

It could be suggested that the nature of the relationships between people in the workplace is important in reducing experiences of bullying. Findings from a survey by O'Moore and Lynch (2007) showed that 72% of non-bullied respondents reported their work environment as friendly, but only 47% of bullied respondents did.

"Discuss the key question of whether bullying in the workplace can be reduced through improved working relationships. You should use concepts, theories and/or research studied in your psychology course. You must make reference to the context in your answer." (8)

Step 1 — Decode the question

  • Key question: can workplace bullying be reduced by improving working relationships?
  • Context: Fast & Chen's ego-defensiveness idea + O'Moore & Lynch's friendly-environment survey.
  • Command word: "Discuss" → give both sides, then judge.
  • Topic base: social/organisational psychology (concepts like social dominance, normative influence) plus developmental theory (Erikson).

Step 2 — The official indicative content (AO1 + AO2)

AOPoint the examiner is looking for
AO1Someone with a social dominance orientation is more likely to engage in egocentric behaviours that advance their personal interest.
AO1Organisations that enhance hierarchies may instil ideologies that favour social dominance of one group above others.
AO1Erikson suggested that without positive feedback at Stage 7 people may experience stagnation and become frustrated that they cannot succeed at work.
AO1Normative influence is when people are motivated to be accepted by other group members, so conform to the normative beliefs of a group.
AO2Bullying may increase when someone in power felt incompetent in their role and needed to re-establish their position in the workplace hierarchy.
AO2An organisation could change the nature of hierarchical relationships to encourage more positive experiences between employees and reduce bullying.
AO2As Fast and Chen (2009) claimed, aggressiveness dissipated when the individual's self-worth was boosted, so positive feedback may help prevent bullying.
AO2Where work environments were friendly, O'Moore and Lynch (2007) found less bullying, so establishing a belief that friendliness matters may encourage others to conform to friendly behaviour.
中文理解：注意这张表——AO1 和 AO2 各占 4 条、完全对半。这就是 Section D 的评分逻辑：考官先看你知道多少概念（social dominance orientation、Erikson Stage 7、normative influence），再看你能不能把它们"翻译"回题目的职场场景（重新确立层级位置、提升自我价值感、营造友善氛围）。答题时最好一条概念配一条应用，成对出现。

5. Model Answer — Workplace Bullying（范文）

Level 4 model answer (7–8 marks)

Improved working relationships are likely to reduce workplace bullying, but only when the underlying power structure also changes.

One explanation for bullying draws on social dominance orientation — the tendency to favour hierarchy and to advance one's own group above others. A manager high in social dominance orientation may engage in egocentric behaviour to protect their position, so in this workplace context, bullying can be understood as a strategy for defending status rather than simple cruelty. However, this means improving relationships alone may be insufficient: if the organisation still rewards hierarchy, the pressure to defend status remains.

A second explanation comes from Erikson's Stage 7. Erikson argued that without positive feedback at work people experience stagnation and become frustrated at their inability to succeed — and, as Fast and Chen (2009) claimed, aggression dissipated once the individual's self-worth was boosted. Applied to the scenario, this suggests that giving employees genuine positive feedback could remove the ego threat that drives bullying, which is exactly the "improved working relationship" the question describes.

The important qualification is normative influence — people conform to the beliefs of their group to be accepted. In a workplace where bullying is treated as normal, individuals may conform to it even if relationships are superficially friendly. Yet O'Moore and Lynch (2007) found that 72% of non-bullied workers described their environment as friendly, against only 47% of bullied workers — evidence that friendliness is genuinely protective.

On balance, improving working relationships can reduce bullying, but it works best when it changes the group norms and power dynamics, not just individual interactions.

Why this earns the top band

  • AO1Accurate concepts: social dominance orientation, Erikson Stage 7, normative influence, and both named studies.
  • AO2Every concept is applied back to the workplace context in an italicised "in this context…" sentence.
  • BALA competing argument (structure vs relationships) and a reasoned, balanced judgement.
LevelMarksWhat the examiner wants
11–2Isolated knowledge; little or no reference to the context.
23–4Mostly accurate knowledge; discussion imbalanced or superficial, occasionally applied.
35–6Accurate knowledge; coherent chains of reasoning; grasp of competing arguments but imbalanced.
47–8Accurate and thorough knowledge; well-developed logical balanced discussion with sustained application to the context.

6. Worked Example 2 — Media and Eating Disorders（第二个范例）

Key questions are not limited to the workplace. This example draws on the Clinical and Learning topics you have studied in Chapters 23 and 24.

📝 Practice key question (8 marks)

Context

One key question for society is whether the media can cause eating disorders. Becker et al. (2002) studied adolescent girls in Fiji before and after the introduction of television, and found a marked increase in disordered eating attitudes after TV arrived. Yet Hoek et al. (2005) found that anorexia nervosa was almost non-existent among the indigenous population of Curaçao, even though they were exposed to US and Dutch television.

"Discuss the key question of whether the media can cause eating disorders. You should use concepts, theories and/or research studied in your psychology course. You must make reference to the context in your answer." (8)

Indicative content (AO1 + AO2)

AOPoint
AO1Social learning theory: people learn behaviour by observing and imitating role models, especially when they are rewarded (vicarious reinforcement).
AO1Sociocultural explanation: Western "thin ideals" promoted by media can create body dissatisfaction, a risk factor for anorexia.
AO1Anorexia is multifactorial — biological, psychological and sociocultural risk factors interact (Dane & Bhatia, 2023).
AO1Becker et al. (2002) supported a media effect; Hoek et al. (2005) (Curaçao) contradicted it.
AO2Many girls who watch thin-ideal media do not develop eating disorders, so media is at most one contributing factor, not a sufficient cause.
AO2Fiji was undergoing rapid globalisation, so the rise in disordered eating cannot be attributed to television alone.
AO2The Curaçao finding — where being overweight was culturally desirable — shows that the same media exposure has different effects in different cultural contexts, so "the media causes eating disorders" is too strong.
AO2A more defensible conclusion is that media increases risk in individuals who are already vulnerable, rather than causing the disorder directly.

Model answer sketch (Level 4)

Social learning theory suggests that people imitate the behaviour of attractive media role models, particularly when that behaviour appears rewarded — so images of extreme thinness being praised online could model disordered eating. However, this does not establish cause: the vast majority of people exposed to such content do not develop an eating disorder. Becker et al. (2002) supports a media effect in Fiji, yet Hoek et al. (2005) found anorexia almost absent in Curaçao despite media exposure, which shows the effect depends on the surrounding culture. On balance, the media is best understood as one risk factor that interacts with biological and psychological vulnerability, rather than a direct cause.

Planning scaffold (use for any KQ)

  1. Position — one sentence stating your answer.

  4. Balance + judgement — acknowledge the competing view, reach a reasoned conclusion.
