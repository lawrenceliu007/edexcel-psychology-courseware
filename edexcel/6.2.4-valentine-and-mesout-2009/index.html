6.2.4 VALENTINE AND MESOUT (2009)
Eyewitness identification under stress · 压力下的目击者指认

Topic F · Criminological psychology Studies: contemporary Spec 6.2.4

What this section is really asking

  1. A real scare, not a lab slide show. Visitors to the London Dungeon met a robed actor blocking their path; heart rate confirmed they were genuinely anxious.
  2. High vs low anxiety was defined by the median split: 86.9 bpm versus 74.7 bpm on entering the Dungeon.
  3. The gap is huge: 17% of high-anxiety visitors (5 of 30) identified the actor correctly, compared with 75% of low-anxiety visitors (21 of 28).
  4. Conclusion: high physiological arousal disrupts identification — the person who frightened you is often the one you remember least accurately.

1. Getting Started

Imagine you are walking through a dark maze. Scary sounds play around you. Suddenly an actor in a dark robe — pale face, wounds and scars — steps out and blocks your way.

Twenty minutes later a researcher shows you nine photographs. “The person you saw may or may not be here. Can you point them out?”

🎯 Your gut answerNo wrong answer

While your heart was pounding — how well do you think you could describe that face?

中文理解：为什么这个实验特别
实验室研究只能「假装吓人」，被试知道自己在做实验。这个研究把人放进真的会吓到的地方（伦敦地牢鬼屋）—— 他们不知道自己之后要指认「吓人的演员」，就像真实案件的目击者一样。紧张是真的，记忆才是真的。

2. Why the Dungeon

Tim Valentine had researched witness identifications for years. With Jan Mesout (2009), he wanted to fix a weakness of laboratory research on stress: lab participants know they are in a study. So the researchers went to the London Dungeon — a tourist attraction where visitors walk through rooms showing London's history, with actors acting out each theme.

Two things this setting gives you

  • Genuine stress — real physiological arousal, not a staged threat.
  • Unaware witnesses — visitors did not know their testimony would be tested, exactly like a real eyewitness.
catastrophe model of memory performance（突变模型）
The prediction that high physiological arousal / anxiety can cause a sudden, marked impairment of eyewitness recall and identification. This study was designed to test it.
中文理解：为什么叫「突变」模型
不是「越紧张越差一点」的线性关系，而是紧张超过某个程度后表现断崖式下跌——像走路走稳了突然跌倒。本章 6.1.4 里 Christianson & Hübiner 的武器聚焦研究说「焦虑聚焦在武器上、周围变模糊」；这里的模型进一步说：高焦虑直接让指认崩盘。数据支持了它：低焦虑组 75% 指认成功，高焦虑组只有 17%。

3. The Aim

To test the hypothesis that high arousal (high levels of stress) can reduce the ability of an eyewitness to recall information and identify a perpetrator.

Specifically: could visitors to the London Dungeon describe and identify the “scary person” they met in the Horror Labyrinth?

The study has two parts:

PartPurpose
1. ValidationProve that visiting the Dungeon really raises anxiety — so the questionnaire is a valid measure of stress
2. Eyewitness studyTest recall and identification of the “scary person” against measured anxiety

4. Part One — Validating the Anxiety Measure

Before testing any witnesses, the researchers had to show the Dungeon is genuinely stressful.

  • 20 employees of a London department store walked the Dungeon wearing a wireless heart-rate monitor; baseline was recorded first, then monitored around the visit.
  • Afterwards they completed a State and Trait Anxiety Inventory.
  • Data from 18 used (2 outliers). Baseline 74.7 bpm vs 86.9 bpm in the Horror Labyrinth — reliably higher.
  • Mean state anxiety 43.2, which correlated with the heart-rate change → the inventory validly measures stress.

5. State vs Trait Anxiety

state anxiety（状态焦虑）
Anxiety right now, caused by the situation you are in — here: how stressed you felt inside the Dungeon.
trait anxiety（特质焦虑）
A personality trait: a tendency to worry about yourself and others regardless of circumstances. A stable, dispositional factor — not caused by the situation.
State anxietyTrait anxiety
SourceThe situation (situational)The person (dispositional)
Changes?Yes — moment to momentNo — consistent over time
Role in this studyThe IV-like measure: high vs low groupsA baseline of individual differences

1. “I felt terrified while walking through the dark maze.” — Which anxiety?

2. “I tend to worry about things, whatever I'm doing.” — Which anxiety?

中文理解：一句话区分
State = 此刻（在鬼屋里吓到腿软），Trait = 平时（天生爱操心的人）。本研究比的是当下的紧张程度，但记录平时的焦虑倾向作为个人差异的基线。

招募发生在参观之后 —— 所以参观时他们完全不知道要做实验。这保住了「不知情目击者」的核心设计；但愿意停下来填问卷的人，可能是自选样本（评价时会用到这一点）。

7. Into the Labyrinth

  • Each participant walked the Horror Labyrinth with around 30 other visitors, for about 7 minutes.
  • The labyrinth is a maze designed to disorientate: dark, with scary sounds played on a recording.
  • Among the “scares”: an actor in a dark robe, make-up to appear very pale, with wounds or scars on the face — the “scary person”.
  • The scary person would step out in front of the participant and block their way from passing.
  • All participants went around in the same direction.

After the tour — consent first, then the tests

  • Purpose explained, informed consent obtained, chance to withdraw at any stage.
  • State anxiety questionnaire (how they felt in the dungeon).
  • Trait anxiety questionnaire (general emotion tendencies).
  • Free recall: describe the scary person in your own words.
  • Cued recall: specific details — gender, age, height, hair colour. Told not to guess.
中文理解：自由回忆 vs 线索回忆
Free recall = 「随便说说你看到什么」——考量。
Cued recall = 「他多高？什么发型？」——考细节准确度。
两组问题分开计分，才能看出焦虑到底伤的是「记得多」还是「记得准」。

8. The Line-up

Finally, each participant saw a nine-person photograph line-up:

  • The scary person's photo sat in a randomly selected position, alongside eight similar-looking actors.
  • Instruction: “The person you saw in the labyrinth may or may not be in the line-up.” Guided to say if they could not identify anyone.
  • They rated their confidence from 0–100%.
中文理解：为什么 lineup 里要说「可能不在」
防止人们硬选一个。如果不说这句话，被试会觉得「九张里必有一张」，猜也会猜一张 —— 数据就被污染了。这句指令让「没认出来」成为合法答案，指认成功才真实。

10. Results — The Identification Cliff

The headline result. The sample was split at the median state anxiety score (52):

Read it like an examiner

  • Only 17% of high-anxiety witnesses correctly identified the scary person from the culprit-present line-up — versus 75% of low-anxiety witnesses.
  • Those who identified correctly also showed higher confidence — accurate witnesses knew they were right.
  • Males made more correct identifications than females — and the gender gap remained even when state anxiety was accounted for separately.

12. Conclusions

  • Females show higher levels of anxiety in stressful situations.
  • Being highly anxious reduces the accuracy of eyewitnesses in identifying perpetrators.
  • Experts assessing eyewitness accuracy should take into account the emotional state of the witness at the time of the event.
  • The sex-difference pattern suggests females may be particularly vulnerable to the effects of stress when witnessing or being the victim of a crime.
  • Overall, the failure of high-anxiety witnesses to describe and identify the perpetrator supports the catastrophe model of memory performance under anxiety.
中文理解：一句话版
紧张真的会砸了目击证词 —— 而且不是慢慢变差，是断崖式崩盘（75% → 17%）。司法系统必须问目击者：案发时你有多害怕？

13. Evaluating · Strengths

Strength 1 — A genuine field experiment

Natural setting and unaware witnesses make the findings more representative of real eyewitnesses than laboratory studies, where participants may guess the aims and respond to please the researcher. A genuine witness also would not know they would later have to identify the perpetrator — this reinforces the ecological validity.

Strength 2 — Excellent controls → replicability

Same actor for every set of visitors, acting the same manner, on the same tour. Every tour group experiences the same exhibits, same order, same time. This gives the study a high level of replicability — it could be re-tested whether anxiety impairs recall.

Strength 3 — Validated questionnaires

The extra validation study (office workers, heart-rate monitors) showed the State Anxiety Inventory reliably measures anxiety — not some other emotion. Assures the validity of the whole study.

Strength 4 — Individual differences built in

Measuring trait anxiety gave a baseline of each person's general tendency, against which the state anxiety of the Dungeon could be compared — rather than just comparing a stress group with a control group.

14. Evaluating · Limitations

Limitation 1 — Self-selected, scare-loving sample

Participants had all chosen to visit a scary attraction. They may prefer scary entertainment and react differently to scary events than people who avoid such places — limiting the generalisability of the findings to the wider population.

Limitation 2 — Ethics of genuine fear

Visitors experienced real stress before consent was possible (consent came only after the tour). The design deliberately kept them unaware — protection came from debriefing and the right to withdraw, but the fear itself could not be consented to in advance.

Limitation 3 — Not a real crime

The “scary person” is an actor, the encounter lasts seconds inside a 7-minute maze with ~30 other visitors. A real crime may involve longer exposure, a weapon, or consequences for the witness — different stress profile.

中文理解：缺点抓一条主线
生态效度的来源（真实紧张）同时也是它的代价：无法事先同意 + 来的人本来就爱找刺激。优缺点一体两面，这是本题最漂亮的评价。

15. Wider Issues — Real-life Implications

  • During trials of real crimes, be cautious about trusting accounts from witnesses who underwent significant anxiety or trauma during the crime.
  • Police should be informed that anxiety can impair recall and identification — a consideration when assessing the admissibility of eyewitness testimony.
  • It should also be considered when police use line-up identification techniques.
  • Important nuance: Valentine and Mesout told participants the person may or may not be present — yet anxious witnesses still failed. So this instruction alone is not enough to stop witnesses choosing the wrong suspect.
中文理解：这条应用为什么高分
它不止说「警察要小心紧张证人」，还顺着研究设计往前多走一步：就算用「可能不在 lineup 里」的标准指令，高焦虑证人照样会错 —— 指令挡不住焦虑对记忆的破坏。

17. Exam Lab

AO1 (4) + AO3 (4) = 8 marks Generic answers score 0

✍️ Question 1 — DescribeAO1

Describe the procedure of Valentine and Mesout (2009). (4)

Frame your answer: validation part → sample → labyrinth → tests → line-up.

First, the State Anxiety Inventory was validated: 20 employees wore heart-rate monitors around the London Dungeon, and their heart rate rose from a baseline of 74.7 to 86.9 bpm inside the Horror Labyrinth, with mean state anxiety (43.2) correlating with the heart-rate change. In the main study, 56 visitors (29 female, 27 male, mean age 31.0), recruited afterwards for a reduced admission price, walked the dark Horror Labyrinth for about seven minutes with around 30 other visitors, where an actor — the “scary person” — stepped out and blocked their path. Afterwards they completed state and trait anxiety questionnaires, gave free recall and cued recall of the actor's appearance without guessing, and attempted identification from a nine-person culprit-present photo line-up, rating their confidence from 0–100%.

✍️ Question 2 — EvaluateAO3

Evaluate the study by Valentine and Mesout (2009). (4)

One strength is the field setting with unaware witnesses: visitors did not know their testimony would be tested, just like a real eyewitness, which gives the findings strong ecological validity compared with laboratory research. It was also highly replicable because the same actor acted in the same manner on identical tours, and the questionnaire was validated against heart-rate data. However, the sample was self-selected — all participants had chosen to visit a scary attraction, so they may enjoy and respond differently to fear, limiting generalisability to people who avoid such places. The fear was also genuinely caused before consent could be given, raising ethical issues that had to be managed through debriefing and the right to withdraw.

✍️ Question 3 — Results and conclusionAO2

Describe the results and conclusions of Valentine and Mesout (2009). (4)

Only 17% of witnesses scoring above the median state anxiety (52) correctly identified the scary person from the culprit-present line-up, compared with 75% of those below the median; low-anxiety witnesses also recalled more correct information, and males made more correct identifications than females (19 vs 7 accurate). Females reported higher state anxiety (52.8 vs 45.3) with no sex difference in trait anxiety. The researchers concluded that high anxiety reduces eyewitness identification accuracy — supporting the catastrophe model — that experts should consider the witness's emotional state at the time of the event, and that females may be particularly vulnerable to stress when witnessing crime.

18. Key Terms

state anxiety
Anxiety caused by the current situation — how stressed you feel right now.
trait anxiety
A stable personality tendency to worry, regardless of circumstances.
catastrophe model
High physiological arousal can cause a sudden, marked impairment of recall and identification.
field experiment
An experiment in a natural setting — here, a working tourist attraction.
Horror Labyrinth
The dark, disorientating maze in the London Dungeon where the encounter occurred.
scary person
The robed, pale-faced actor with wounds/scars who blocked visitors' path.
culprit-present line-up
A photo line-up that actually contains the target (9 photos, random position).
free vs cued recall
Open description of the person versus specific prompted details (age, height, hair).
