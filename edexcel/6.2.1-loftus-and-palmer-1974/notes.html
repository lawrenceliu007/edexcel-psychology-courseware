The whole study in one sentence 一句话总结
Change one verb in the question — smashed, collided, bumped, hit, contacted — and witnesses estimate speeds from 40.8 down to 31.8 mph; and a week later, 32% of “smashed” witnesses remembered broken glass that was never there (vs 14% and 12%), proving that post-event information merges into memory itself — the reconstructive hypothesis.

§8 · The reconstructive hypothesisANSWERS → p.19
Define it in your own words first
Textbook definition 课本定义
Reconstructive hypothesis: memory for an event when the information supplied after the event merges with the information obtained from witnessing the event.
The chain — fill it in

Witness the event → receive information afterwards → the two sources as time elapses → we cannot tell the of some details → one memory of the event.

post-event / merge / source / overall

中文理解：一份“合成的”记忆
生活类比：手机相册里自己拍的照片和朋友发来的截图混在一起。过一阵子再看， 分不清哪张是自己拍的——但你会坚信自己当时在场。
记忆只存一份合成版本，不保留“这条来自哪里”的标签。所以目击者未必在说谎， 但记忆本身可能已经失真。

p.16 A1 IV = the verb used in the critical question (smashed, collided, bumped, hit, contacted); DV = the estimated speed of the cars in mph. A2 150 students in three groups of 50 watched a film of a multiple car accident lasting under a minute (the accident four seconds). One group was asked about cars that “smashed”, one about cars that “hit”, and the control group was not asked about speed. A week later they answered ten questions without seeing the film again, including “Did you see any broken glass?” A3 Speed estimates alone cannot separate the two explanations. The broken glass was never in the film, so only a changed memory — not a response bias — could make people report it. A4 Strength: high control, so replicable and reliable. Limitation: students with limited experience of car speeds may be more influenced by the verb, so findings may not generalise.

p.17 B — model judgement “On balance the study can be applied to real eyewitness memory: the effect was strong and lasted a week, the real speeds were controlled for, and its applications — the cognitive interview and the Devlin Committee's warning against single-eyewitness convictions — show it changed practice. However, students with limited driving experience and the absence of real emotional strain mean the size of the effect in real life may differ, and the study is reductionist in considering only one variable.”
