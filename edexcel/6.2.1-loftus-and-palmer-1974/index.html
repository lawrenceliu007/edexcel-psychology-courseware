6.2.1 LOFTUS AND PALMER (1974)
Reconstruction of automobile destruction · 汽车损毁的“重建”

Topic F · Criminological psychology Studies: classic Spec 6.2.1
The study that showed one word in a question can change what a witness remembers — and changed how police question witnesses.

What this section is really asking

  1. Change one verb in a question — smashed, collided, bumped, hit, contacted — and the speed estimate changes by up to 9 mph.
  2. Experiment two asks the real question: is that a changed memory, or just a different way of answering?
  3. The answer: it is a changed memory. A week later, “smashed” witnesses reported broken glass that was never there.
  4. Conclusion: memory is reconstructed — what we saw merges with what we are told later.

1. Getting Started

Imagine you are standing at a junction. Two cars crash in front of you. A police officer arrives and asks you one question:

Two versions of the same question

“About how fast were the cars going when they smashed into each other?”

“About how fast were the cars going when they contacted each other?”

Same crash. Same witness. Only one word is different.

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

You saw the same crash either way. So do the two groups give the same number, or do they differ?

Most people say “a few mph” — and that is right. But the interesting part is not that the number changed. It is why it changed, and what that means for anyone who has ever been asked to remember a crime.

中文理解：这一节要讲的“一个词”
两个目击者看的是同一场车祸，唯一区别是警察用了哪个动词：smashed（撞碎）听起来比 contacted（接触）严重得多。
如果这个动词真的改变了目击者说出的速度，那法庭上“问话用词”就不再是小事——它可能直接改掉了证词。

2. One Word, Two Answers

Elizabeth Loftus and John Palmer ran this in 1974. Their paper is called Reconstruction of automobile destruction: An example of the interaction between language and memory.

Three things make this study the classic study of this topic:

It is about a real problem

Courts rely on eyewitnesses. If a question can change a memory, convictions can rest on something false.

It changed practice

It contributed to the cognitive interview and to police guidance to avoid leading questions.

It is two experiments, not one

The first one raises a doubt; the second one settles it. You must learn both.

The textbook is explicit. Loftus and Palmer's research involves both experiments, so you must be able to describe experiment one and experiment two in detail. An answer that only covers one will lose marks.

3. The Two Experiments

Read the two experiments as one argument. Experiment one finds an effect; experiment two explains it.

Figure 1 — The two experiments as one argument: the first finds the effect, the second explains it.
中文理解：为什么要有第二个实验
实验一只能证明“说的数字变了”，但看不出心里发生了什么。可能只是嘴上换了个说法（想配合提问者的暗示），也可能记忆真的被改写了。
实验二就是来分辨这两种可能的：它不问速度，而是问一个片子里根本没出现的东西。

4. Experiment One · The Aim

Aim of experiment one： to investigate whether leading questions would influence the estimates of the speed of a vehicle among eyewitnesses.
中文理解：这一问在问什么
leading question（引导性问题） = 提问方式本身暗示了答案。问“车撞碎时多快”，其实已经在告诉你“撞得很严重”。
实验一要测的是：这种暗示能不能把目击者的回答往暗示的方向推。

5. Experiment One · The Procedure

Learn these numbers as a set — they are the detail the exam rewards.

FeatureWhat they did
Participants45 students, split into five groups of nine.
MaterialsSeven short film clips of traffic accidents, each lasting 5 to 30 seconds.
TaskAfter each clip they gave an account of the accident, then answered a questionnaire.
The one differenceEvery participant got the same questionnaire except one critical question — the verb was changed.
The five verbsOne group (nine) was asked about cars that hit each other. The other four groups got the same question with smashed, collided, bumped or contacted.
ControlsEach of the five groups saw the clips in a different order.
The checkLoftus and Palmer knew the actual speeds in four of the clips: one at 20 mph, one at 30 mph, two at 40 mph.

Why the last row matters

Because they knew the true speeds, they could check whether the estimates were being dragged around by the real speed of the car instead of by the verb. It let them say with confidence: the verb did it, not the film.

7. Experiment One · Two Possible Reasons

A change of word significantly affected the answer. But a changed number can mean two different things.

Reason 1 · response bias

The participant was uncertain of the speed, and the verb in the leading question created a bias which influenced their decision.

记忆没错，只是回答被暗示推了一把。

Reason 2 · the memory itself changed

The wording of the question causes a change in memory, so they recall the accident as more severe than it actually was.

记忆真的被改写了，事故在脑子里变严重了。

中文理解：两个理由的差别在哪里
回答偏差：心里知道是 30，但听到问“撞碎”就顺口报了个 40 —— 只是嘴上迎合。
记忆改变：脑子里的画面真的变成了“一场猛烈撞击”，于是回忆时自然觉得很快。
单看速度数字，这两种根本分不开。所以才需要实验二。

8. Experiment Two · Why It Had to Happen

So far, all we know is that the groups gave different numbers. That cannot tell us why. To find out, you need a clever trick.

The trick: ask about something that was never there

If the verb only nudges the answer, witnesses will keep their memory intact and simply report the crash as it was.
But if the verb rewrites the memory, then witnesses who heard “smashed” should now remember a more violent crash — including details of violence that were never filmed.

Violent crashes produce broken glass. The film contained none.

中文理解：碎玻璃这个设计巧在哪
片段里没有碎玻璃。所以任何人回答“看到了”，都不可能是真的看到了——只能来自被改写的记忆：既然记成了猛烈撞击，就顺理成章“看见”了碎玻璃。
回答偏差解释不了这一点（单纯迎合提问不会让人凭空多出一段画面），所以这个设计能把两种解释分开。

9. Experiment Two · The Aim

Aim of experiment two： the second experiment was designed to test whether the verb used in the question created a bias in the participant's response, or whether it created an actual change in memory of the incident.
Learn the two aims as a pair. Experiment one = “do leading questions influence speed estimates?” Experiment two = “is that a response bias or a memory change?” If an exam question gives you one aim and asks for the other, this is the sentence you need.

10. Experiment Two · The Procedure

FeatureWhat they did
Participants150 students, divided into three groups of 50.
The filmA film of a multiple car accident, lasting less than one minute — the accident itself lasted four seconds.
Group 1Asked “About how fast were the cars going when they smashed into each other?”
Group 2Asked “About how fast were the cars going when they hit each other?”
Group 3 (control)Not asked about the speed of the cars at all — no critical question.

smashed 组：听到最暴力的动词。
hit 组：听到中性动词，用来对比。
control 组：根本没被问速度 —— 用来证明“光是参加实验、看片子”本身不会让人多想出碎玻璃。
一周后的延迟是关键：说明这个影响不是当天随口应付，而是留在记忆里了。

The statistical test

A chi-square test was carried out and it was found that there was a statistically significant difference between the groups. A significantly higher number of participants in the “smashed” group reported seeing glass than in the “hit” group.

中文理解：怎么读这三个数字
不要只看“32% 的人说看到”——注意每组都有一半以上的人答对了（说没看到）。
关键是组间差距：32% 比 14% 高出一大截，而没被问速度的那组只有 12%。
学了统计的话，这个卡方检验说明差距不是巧合，而是动词造成的。

12. Experiment Two · The Conclusion

What the findings showed

The verb in the question acted as post-event information which influenced the participants' recall of the accident. It changed their memory of the event rather than causing a response bias.

The participants did not see any broken glass, and the findings suggest the verb changed their memory of the accident, even a week after seeing the film clip.

Loftus and Palmer concluded： reconstructive memory exists, and leading questions do influence eyewitness testimony.

Why this settles the doubt from experiment one

A response bias would only move the number people say out loud. It would not make them report a piece of the scene that was never filmed. Reporting broken glass a week later is evidence that the memory itself had been rebuilt.

13. The Reconstructive Hypothesis

Loftus and Palmer argue that two kinds of information go into a person's memory of a complex event.

1 · What you witnessed

The information obtained from witnessing the event itself.

2 · What you were told afterwards

The other information supplied after the event — post-event information.

As time elapses, the two sources merge, making it difficult to know the source of some details. This creates one overall memory of the event.

reconstructive hypothesis（重构假设）： memory for an event when the information supplied after the event merges with the information obtained from witnessing the event.
中文理解：大脑把“看到的”和“听说的”合成了一份
生活类比：你手机相册里自己拍的照片，和朋友后来发来的截图混在一起。过一阵子再看，你分不清哪张是自己拍的——但你会坚信自己当时就在那儿。
记忆也是这样：只存一份合成版本，不保留“这条来自哪里”的标签。所以目击者说谎的概率未必高，但记忆本身可能已经失真。

15. Evaluating · Strengths

Each strength below is one the textbook gives. Notice that most of them are about how carefully the study was controlled.

▶ 1. High control — it is a laboratory experiment
  • CONTROL Both experiments were conducted in a laboratory and therefore had a high level of control.
  • The questions asked were consistent for the groups (except the verb change), and so were the film clips used.
  • RELIABILITY This allows the study to be replicated to determine the reliability of the finding that recall is influenced by post-event information.
▶ 2. Construct validity — they measured exactly what they meant to
  • By changing just the verb associated with speed, Loftus and Palmer can be more certain that the IV of the verb was what impacted the speed estimates.
  • This increases construct validity — that they tested the effect of language on recall.

construct validity（结构效度）： the extent to which the researcher has actually measured the intended variable or underlying construct.

▶ 3. Demand characteristics were guarded against
  • The critical question was randomly included among other questions within the questionnaire.
  • This prevented participants from guessing the aim and displaying demand characteristics, which would have produced flawed results.
  • It minimises the chance that responses were due to the research methodology rather than a genuine response to the question.
▶ 4. They checked the real speeds, and used quantitative data
  • The experimenters knew the actual speeds for most films. They found it did not affect the estimates — so they could say with certainty that it was the verb, not the actual speed, that had the effect.
  • The yes/no responses and the speed estimates produced quantitative data, so there was no subjective interpretation — the findings are more objective and less likely to be the result of researcher bias.
中文理解：四条strength 的共同点
都在说同一件事：把别的解释一个个堵住了。
控制变量一致 → 能复制；只改动词 → 确定是动词的功劳；问题混在问卷里 → 参与者猜不到目的；知道真实车速 → 排除了“片子本身开得快”。
写评价题时，把“我们因此能确定是 X 造成的”这句写上，比只说“控制得好”得分高。

16. Evaluating · Limitations

1. The sample may have made the effect easier to find

It may be difficult to accurately distinguish the speed of cars from watching film clips, particularly when the participants were students who are likely to have limited experience of car speeds.

Limited experience in estimating speeds may have resulted in students being more influenced by the verb than would have been the case with a population with more experience of driving. The findings therefore may only represent the views of this specific population, rather than generalising to the wider population.

2. Watching a film is not witnessing an accident

Participants are unlikely to have been under the same emotional strain as an eyewitness of a real accident, as they only watched film clips in a laboratory. This makes the validity questionable — they may have responded differently as a result.

It cannot be said with certainty that a real eyewitness would respond in the same way as those in an artificial situation.

3. It is reductionist

As well as the type of question asked, many other factors could influence memory of a real-life event, including emotions and the environment in which it occurred. These are not considered. The study is therefore somewhat reductionist, considering only one potential variable that may influence human memory.

中文理解：三条 limitation 的共同点
都在问同一个问题：实验室里的学生，能代表真实目击者吗？
· 学生开车经验少 → 更容易被动词牵着走，效应可能被放大了。
· 看片子没有真事故的紧张和恐惧 → 真人目击时情绪会介入，反应可能不同。
· 只测了“用词”一个因素 → 还原论：真实记忆还受情绪、环境等影响。
写 8 分题时，把“学生经验少”和“缺情绪紧张”分开写，各配一句后果，比笼统说“样本不好”强。

17. Real Life · What Changed Because of It

This is the part examiners like, because it connects the study to the wider issue of the use of psychological knowledge within society.

Devlin Committee (1976)

It was set up to investigate the use of eyewitness testimony in court. It found that many people have been convicted of serious crimes by eyewitness testimony alone, and recommended against basing a conviction on a single eyewitness.

What Loftus and Palmer's work led to

  • Their research into post-event information contributed to the development of the cognitive interview — a technique police interviewers use when questioning eyewitnesses.
  • Police and legal professionals are guided to minimise the use of leading questions in order to ensure an accurate account is obtained.
  • This constitutes a marked change in policing techniques in the 20th century, and the cognitive interview is still used today.
中文理解：这个研究怎么改变了现实
逻辑链条：问话用词会改写记忆 → 那么警察问话时就必须尽量少用引导性问题 → 于是有了认知访谈（cognitive interview）这套不引导、让证人自己回忆的问法。
同时，Devlin 委员会据此建议：不能只凭一个目击者的证词就定罪。
这一节是整章最“有用”的一段——写 8 分题的现实应用时直接用。

20. Exam Lab

The specification names this as the classic study, so it can be examined closely. Two question types come up again and again.

Question type 1 · identifying variables. A short question such as “Identify the independent variable” and “Identify the dependent variable” in Loftus and Palmer's reconstruction of automobile destruction experiment.

🎯 Try the short one first2 + 1 marks

a) Identify the independent variable in experiment one.

The verb used in the critical question — smashed, collided, bumped, hit or contacted. (In experiment two it is the verb condition: “smashed”, “hit”, or no speed question.)

b) Identify the dependent variable in experiment one.

The estimated speed of the cars in miles per hour given by the participant.

Question type 2 · the eight-mark application question. “Assess whether this study can be applied to eyewitness memory in real-life situations.” (8 marks)

🎯 Plan it, then compare8 marks

Before reading the model answer, write down three reasons for applying the findings and three reasons against. Use the strengths and limitations from sections 15 and 16.

For — it does applyAgainst — be careful
The effect was strong and statistically significant (32% vs 14% vs 12%, chi-square).The sample was students with limited experience of judging car speeds.
It lasted a week, not just a moment — memory really had changed.Participants watched film clips in a laboratory, not a real accident.

AO1 (describe). In experiment one, 45 students in five groups of nine watched seven short film clips of traffic accidents and answered a questionnaire in which one critical question changed only the verb — smashed, collided, bumped, hit or contacted. Those given “smashed” estimated 40.8 mph and those given “contacted” estimated 31.8 mph, a difference of almost 9 mph. In experiment two, 150 students in three groups of 50 watched a multiple car accident; one group was asked about cars that “smashed”, one about cars that “hit”, and the control group was not asked about speed at all. A week later, of the participants asked “Did you see any broken glass?”, 32% of the “smashed” group said yes, compared with 14% of the “hit” group and 12% of the control group. There was no broken glass in the film.

AO3 (assess). A strength is that the study was conducted in a laboratory with a high level of control: the clips and the questionnaire were identical apart from the verb, and the critical question was randomly placed among other questions so participants could not guess the aim. Because the experimenters also knew the actual speeds of the cars and found it did not affect the estimates, we can be more certain that the verb caused the difference, which increases construct validity. The yes/no and mph responses gave quantitative data with no subjective interpretation, so the findings are less likely to reflect researcher bias, and the design can be replicated to test reliability.

However, it is questionable whether the findings apply to real eyewitnesses. Participants were students with limited experience of estimating car speeds, so they may have been more influenced by the verb than a population with more driving experience would be — the findings may represent this specific sample rather than the wider population. They also watched film clips in a laboratory and so were unlikely to be under the emotional strain of a real eyewitness, which makes the ecological validity questionable; a real witness might respond differently.

A further problem is that the study is reductionist: as well as the wording of a question, many other factors influence memory in real life, including emotions and the environment, and these were not considered. On the other hand, the research does have real-world value: it contributed to the cognitive interview and to guidance that police should minimise leading questions, and the Devlin Committee (1976) recommended against convicting on the evidence of a single eyewitness.

Conclusion. On balance the study can be applied to real eyewitness memory, because the underlying process — post-event information merging with what was witnessed — has been supported in practice and has already changed policing. But the size of the effect in a real, emotionally arousing event may be different from the laboratory figure, so the findings should be applied with caution rather than treated as an exact measure of how misleading a real witness is.

What earns the marks

  • AO1: describe both experiments with numbers. 45 and 150, five verbs and three groups, 40.8 to 31.8, and 32% / 14% / 12%.
  • AO3: state the strength, then say what it lets us conclude — “so we can be more certain the verb caused it” beats “it was well controlled”.
  • Distinguish the two limitations: students' inexperience with speed, and the absence of emotional strain. They are different points and are worth separate sentences.
  • End with a balanced conclusion that mentions the real-world applications.
中文理解：这道 8 分题怎么组织
结构固定：描述两个实验（带数字） → 优点 2 条，每条跟一句“所以我们能确定什么” → 缺点 2 条，一条讲样本经验、一条讲缺真实情绪 → 补一条还原论 → 收尾提到认知访谈和 Devlin，再给出“有保留地可以应用”的结论。
最忌讳：只说一个实验，或者写满缺点却没有一句后果。

21. Key Terms

leading question 引导性问题
A question whose wording suggests the answer — “how fast were the cars going when they smashed into each other?” already implies a violent crash.
post-event information 事后信息
Information supplied after an event which merges into the memory of it — in this study, the verb in the question.
reconstructive hypothesis 重构假设
Memory for an event when information supplied after the event merges with the information obtained from witnessing it, producing one overall memory.
response bias 回答偏差
The explanation that the participant's memory was intact and only their answer shifted — the alternative the second experiment ruled out.
construct validity 结构效度
The extent to which the researcher has actually measured the intended variable or underlying construct.
demand characteristics 需求特征
Cues that let participants guess the aim and change their behaviour; avoided here by hiding the critical question among others.
ecological validity 生态效度
Whether findings apply to real settings — questioned here because participants watched clips in a laboratory.
reductionist 还原论的
Explaining something using too few factors; the study considers only the wording of the question and not emotions or environment.
chi-square test 卡方检验
The statistical test used in experiment two; it showed a significant difference between the three groups.
quantitative data 定量数据
Numerical data such as mph estimates and yes/no counts — not open to subjective interpretation.
cognitive interview 认知访谈
A police interviewing technique for eyewitnesses developed with the help of Loftus and Palmer's findings; still used today.
Devlin Committee (1976) 德夫林委员会
Investigated eyewitness testimony; found many convictions rested on it alone and recommended against convicting on a single eyewitness.
