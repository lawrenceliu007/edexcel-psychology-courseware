2.3.1 CLASSIC STUDY: BARTLETT (1932)
War of the Ghosts · Serial reproduction · Reconstructive memory · War of the Ghosts 与记忆重构

Topic B · Cognitive psychology Classic study Spec 2.3.1 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.13 · pp.125–127

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Describe the aim, sample and procedure of Bartlett's War of the Ghosts study, including serial reproduction.
  • Recall the key findings: which details changed, which survived, and the numbers (330 → 180 words; title changes).
  • Explain the conclusion that memory is reconstructive, shaped by cultural expectations and schemas.
  • Evaluate the study on standardisation, replication (Roediger et al.), the unfamiliarity problem (Wynne & Logie 1998), controls and generalisability.
  • Answer exam questions on the sample (1 mark), one improvement (2 marks) and a full 8-mark evaluation.

What this page is really asking

  1. Can a story survive retelling? Bartlett gave students a Native American folk tale full of unfamiliar names and objects, asked them to recall it over minutes, days, weeks and years — and watched the story quietly turn into something the students found more familiar.
  2. Order survives, content transforms. The sequence of events stayed roughly intact, but the story shrank from 330 words to about 180, unfamiliar objects were swapped for familiar ones (canoe → boat), and the ghosts were dropped or explained away.
  3. The conclusion changed psychology. Memory is not a playback; it is rebuilt on every recall using what you already know — which is exactly why an unfamiliar story distorts fastest.
  4. The method was loose and the claim needs care. The distortion pattern itself has replicated (Roediger et al.), but the leap to "memory is unreliable in everyday life" is challenged by Wynne and Logie (1998), because everyday material is rarely this unfamiliar.

Start Here: The Story That Would Not Survive

背景心理课课间，老师组织了一场传话游戏。他私下把一段约三百字的故事拿给第一位同学看：故事来自另一个文化，人名、地名、物品全是大家没见过的。第一位同学凭记忆讲给第二位，一路传下去，直到第八位。
角色你是围观的同学。下课铃响时，老师把原版故事和第八位同学写下的版本并排贴在黑板上。
任务对比两个版本：事件的先后顺序基本没乱，但故事短了一大截；「独木舟」变成了「小船」，「猎海豹」变成了「钓鱼」，「鬼魂」那段情节被改成了「他们大概是敌人」。最流畅的地方，恰恰是改动最大的地方。
目标先猜下面这题：哪类内容最先被改掉？读完这一页，你不只能回答它，还能看懂一个更大的东西：心理学里「记忆是重构的」这个模型，是怎么从一个小小的传话实验里长出来的。猜完别急着走，再往下追问两层，先自己想，再点开提示。

先猜故事传到第八个人时，最先被改掉的是哪类内容？

追问猜完先别走：这两个问题没有选项，先自己想，再点开提示。

追问一：第八版又短又通顺，读起来完全是「我们这边的故事」，这算记性好，还是记性差？

提示

更通顺恰恰是问题所在。他能讲顺，是因为他顺手用自己熟悉的概念替换了陌生概念：替换得越顺滑，说明重构发生得越多。「讲得好」和「记得准」是两回事。

追问二：老师说「传话游戏证明人类的记忆不可靠」。这句话能直接推广到日常生活吗？

提示

不能直接推。传话游戏挑的材料刻意求陌生；日常生活里我们记的大多是熟悉的事件。Wynne 和 Logie (1998) 让大学生回忆入学初期的真实事件，反复回忆几个月，几乎没发现什么重构。材料越陌生，错误越多，这是 Bartlett 的设计选择，也是评价他时最关键的一句。

带着这两个追问往下读：Bartlett 用一个「最陌生的故事」逼出了记忆的重构本性，也顺手埋下了「记忆真有这么不可靠吗」的质疑。

1. The Story, the Aim and the Method

You have just watched reconstruction happen in miniature: every retelling in the game was one cycle of what Bartlett called serial reproduction（系列再现：同一名参加者在越来越长的时间间隔后反复回忆同一材料）. In his study, Bartlett turned that classroom game into an experiment. His aim was to investigate whether memory for an unfamiliar story would be affected by previously stored knowledge — in other words, whether memory is reconstructive（重构式的：回忆时用已有知识和预期把记忆重新搭出来，而不是原样播放）, and whether recall would be shaped by cultural expectations and schemas（图式：头脑中已有的知识框架，用来组织和解释新信息）.

The story itself: 'War of the Ghosts'

One night two young men from Egulac went down to the river to hunt seals, and whilst they were there it became foggy and calm. Then they heard war-cries, and they thought 'Maybe this is a war party'. They escaped to the shore, and hid behind a log. Now canoes came up, and they heard the noise of paddles, and saw one canoe coming up to them.

There were five men in the canoe, and they said, 'What do you think? We wish to take you along. We are going up the river to make war on the people.'

One of the young men said, 'I have no arrows.' 'Arrows are in the canoe,' they said. 'I will not go along. I might be killed. My relatives do not know where I have gone. But you,' he said, turning to the other, 'may go with them.'

So one of the young men went, but the other returned home. And the warriors went up the river to a town on the other side of Kalama. The people came down to the water, and they began to fight, and many were killed. But presently the young man heard one of the warriors say, 'Quick, let us go home: that Indian has been hit.' Now he thought: 'Oh, they are ghosts.' He did not feel sick, but they said he had been shot.

So the canoes went back to Egulac, and the young man went ashore to his house and made a fire. And he told everybody and said: 'Behold I accompanied the ghosts, and we went to fight. Many of our fellows were killed, and many of those who attacked us were killed. They said I was hit, and I did not feel sick.'

He told it all, and then he became quiet. When the sun rose he fell down. Something black came out of his mouth. His face became contorted. The people jumped up and cried.

He was dead.

  • Sample: 20 British undergraduate students at the University of Cambridge — 7 male, 13 female. They were not told the aim of the study, only that they would be tested on the accuracy of their recall. A Western sample recalling a culturally unfamiliar story was exactly the contrast Bartlett wanted.
  • Procedure: participants read the story to themselves twice, then recalled it at different intervals — once after 15 minutes, then at intervals of several hours, days, weeks and even years. Each recall is one reproduction, and the changes across reproductions are the data.

Back to the game: in the classroom version, eight people formed the chain and the story degraded as it went. Bartlett's participants did the same job alone, with time doing the work that the chain of classmates did.

中文理解：流程记四个数：20 名剑桥本科生（7 男 13 女）、故事读两遍、15 分钟后第一次回忆，之后间隔逐级拉长，从几小时一直到几周、几年。三个关键点：① 参加者不知道实验目的，只知道「要测回忆的准确性」，所以不会故意配合；② 材料是北美原住民故事，人名（Egulac、Kalama）和内容对英国学生全部陌生，而这种陌生正是刻意设计的；③ 「系列再现」指的是同一个人隔越来越久回忆同一材料，跟传话游戏（不同的人接力）不一样，考试别混。

2. Evidence: What the Retellings Changed

The reproductions changed from the very first recall at 15 minutes, and kept changing across the later ones. Two things happened at once: the order of events was relatively preserved, while the content was steadily transformed to align with the students' cultural schemas.

  • The story got shorter. From 330 words in the original to around 180 words after repeated reproductions, because the first recall already omitted or simplified details and each later version consolidated those cuts.
  • The title did not survive either. Seven participants omitted the title altogether, and ten transformed it, for example into 'War-Ghost story'.
  • Unfamiliar objects became familiar ones. 'Canoe' was changed to 'boat'; 'hunting seals' became 'fishing'.
  • The ghosts were the biggest casualty. Many participants did not grasp the role of the ghosts in the story, so they simply left them out or rationalised their presence in some way.

中文理解：发现记「三保三换」。保住的：事件顺序大体不变。换掉的分三类：① 物品熟悉化：独木舟变小船、猎海豹变钓鱼；② 情节合理化：鬼魂要么被删掉，要么被解释成说得通的东西；③ 整体压缩：330 词缩到约 180 词，标题 7 人直接删、10 人改名。最关键的时间点：15 分钟后的第一次回忆就已经出错，而且这些错误会在后面的回忆里被固定下来，不是越忘越少，是错误越滚越结实。

3. Conclusion: Memory Is Reconstructive

Bartlett concluded that memory is reconstructed each time it is recalled. It is rarely accurate, and it is prone to distortion, rationalisation, transformation and simplification. Even recall after a few minutes produced errors — and those errors were not corrected later but consolidated into subsequent reproductions. Remembering, in his account, is a constructive process guided by the cultural inferences the rememberer brings to the material.

This is the evidence behind the theory you met in 2.1.3: when Bartlett's students hit something their schemas could not absorb — a ghost, a seal hunt, a name like Kalama — they did not store it and forget it; they rebuilt the story around it until it made sense to them. The unfamiliar details were not simply lost. They were replaced.

Back to the game: the eighth student's version was smoother and shorter because reconstruction had eight cycles to work with. Bartlett's participants produced the same kind of drift alone — smoothness, familiarity, shrinking length — which is why the game makes a good opening but a poor substitute for his method.

中文理解：结论一句话：每次回忆都是一次重新搭建。四个关键词按考试频率排：distortion（歪曲）、rationalisation（合理化：把讲不通的情节说成说得通）、transformation（转换）、simplification（简化）。两条易错点：① 错误出现在 15 分钟后，且会被后续回忆固化，不是「刚记完最准」；② 记忆不是「存了再丢」，是「用熟悉的东西替换陌生的」，这正是 2.1.3 重构记忆理论的实验证据。

4. Evaluation: How Far Can the Ghosts Take Us?

Strengths

  • Pioneering. The study challenged the assumption that memory is an accurate, faithful record of events, and this new approach led to many advancements in memory research.
  • Depth through qualitative analysis. Describing how the stories changed gave a depth of understanding that a correct/incorrect score could never offer.
  • Real-world application. It is applied in legal settings: if eyewitnesses simplify and rationalise what they saw, the police can be cautious about treating testimony as evidence.
  • Standardised material, and it replicated. Every participant recalled the same story, so the study can be retested. Roediger et al. attempted a replication with 30 Washington University undergraduates and found similar forgetting and distortion after six months, supporting Bartlett's findings.

Weaknesses

  • The unfamiliarity problem. The story was deliberately unfamiliar — that was the point — but it can mislead us into assuming memory is generally unreliable. Everyday material is familiar, and Wynne and Logie (1998) found that students recalling real events from their first year at university showed very limited reconstructive memory. In real life, memory may not be as reconstructive as Bartlett claimed.
  • No controls between reading and recall. We cannot be sure participants did not re-read the story or discuss it with others across the hours, days and weeks, which reduces the reliability of the findings.
  • Small, culturally narrow sample. Only 20 British undergraduates. That made sense for testing cultural schema against an unfamiliar story, but we cannot know whether other cultures would transform the story the same way, because no other cultures were tested.
  • Lacks scientific rigour. Participants were reasonably free to deliberate over the stories; in one instance Bartlett simply bumped into a previous participant after six-and-a-half years and suggested he recall the story. The findings were qualitative and descriptive, open to subjective interpretation.

A fair verdict: the pattern of change is trustworthy — standardised material and the Roediger replication support it — but the pessimistic extrapolation to everyday memory is not, because everyday material is rarely as unfamiliar as the Ghosts.

Exam tip: the 8-mark evaluation is marked AO1 4 + AO3 4. Spend the AO1 marks describing the procedure and findings accurately (20 undergraduates, serial reproduction, 330 → 180 words, canoe → boat), then spend the AO3 marks making linked judgements — for example: standardisation + Roediger replication supports reliability, but no controls between readings + the unfamiliarity problem (Wynne & Logie) limits what the conclusions can claim.

中文理解：评价记「四绿四红」和一句裁决。绿记四条：开创性（推翻「记忆是精确录像」）；质性分析把「变化怎么发生」交代得细；应用于目击证词；材料标准化且被 Roediger 用 30 名华盛顿大学学生重复出类似结果。红：材料刻意陌生导致悲观结论（Wynne & Logie 1998 反例）、读与忆之间零控制、样本仅 20 名英国本科生、流程随意（六年后路上偶遇就让人回忆）加质性资料主观性强。裁决句：变化的「模式」可信，推广到日常记忆的「悲观结论」不可信。

5. Exam Lab

Q1 · Describe 1 mark

Describe the sample that Bartlett used in his study.

Bartlett's sample was 20 British undergraduate students attending the University of Cambridge, of whom 7 were male and 13 were female.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for an accurate description (number + who they were). One detailed sentence is enough for a one-mark question — but it must name both the number and the population.

Q2 · Explain one improvement 2 marks

Explain one improvement to Bartlett's War of the Ghosts study.

Improvement: control what participants did between readings. Bartlett applied no controls between reading the story and recalling it hours, days, weeks or years later, so participants might have re-read the story or discussed it with others, and we cannot be sure their recall reflected memory alone. The study could be improved by requiring participants to confirm at each recall that they had not encountered the story in the meantime — for example by signing a statement to that effect. This would isolate the effect of time and schemas on recall, so any transformations found could be attributed to reconstructive memory rather than to extra exposure, increasing the internal validity of the conclusions.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for identifying a valid improvement (e.g. controls between readings; a larger and more culturally varied sample; a familiar story as a comparison condition), 1 mark for explaining how it improves the study (validity, reliability or generalisability).

Q3 · Evaluate 8 marks

Evaluate Bartlett's War of the Ghosts study.

AO1 — what Bartlett did and found. Bartlett investigated whether memory for an unfamiliar story is affected by previously stored knowledge. Twenty British undergraduates (7 male, 13 female) at the University of Cambridge read the Native American tale 'War of the Ghosts' twice and recalled it after 15 minutes and at intervals of hours, days, weeks and years (serial reproduction). The order of events was relatively preserved, but the stories shrank from 330 to around 180 words, seven participants omitted the title and ten changed it, unfamiliar objects became familiar ('canoe' → 'boat', 'hunting seals' → 'fishing'), and the ghosts were omitted or rationalised. He concluded that memory is reconstructed at each recall, guided by cultural schemas.

AO3 — strength: standardisation and replication. Every participant recalled the same story, so the material was standardised and the study can be retested. Roediger et al.'s replication with 30 Washington University undergraduates found similar forgetting and distortion after six months, which supports the reliability of the reconstructive pattern and its status as a foundation for memory research.

AO3 — strength: application. The findings are applied in legal contexts: because eyewitnesses tend to simplify and rationalise criminal events, the police can treat testimony with appropriate caution rather than as a faithful record.

AO3 — weakness: the unfamiliarity problem. The story was deliberately unfamiliar, which inflates distortion and risks an overly pessimistic view of memory. Wynne and Logie (1998) asked students to recall real events from the start of their first university year over several months and found very limited reconstruction, suggesting everyday memory — which deals in familiar material — is less reconstructive than Bartlett claimed.

AO3 — weakness: controls and rigour. Nothing controlled what participants did between readings, so re-reading or discussion could explain part of the changes. The procedures were loosely standardised — one participant was simply bumped into after six-and-a-half years — and the qualitative analysis is open to subjective interpretation, weakening the scientific status of the conclusions. The sample of 20 British undergraduates also limits generalisability to other cultures, which were never tested.

Overall judgement. The reconstructive pattern itself is well supported, but the study's casual procedures mean the strength of the claim should be limited to unfamiliar material rather than memory in general.

Marking guidance: AO1 (4) accurate description of aim, sample, procedure and findings; AO3 (4) linked evaluation with evidence (Roediger replication; Wynne & Logie; controls; sample; scientific rigour). A judgement is needed for the top marks — not four unconnected strengths followed by four weaknesses.

7. Key Terms

serial reproduction
系列再现：同一名参加者在越来越长的时间间隔后反复回忆同一材料（从 15 分钟一直到几周、几年）
reconstructive memory
重构式记忆：回忆时用已有知识和预期把记忆重新搭出来，而不是原样播放录像
schema
图式：头脑中已有的知识框架，用来组织和解释新信息；材料不合图式时最容易被改写
rationalisation
合理化：把回忆里讲不通的情节改成能自圆其说的版本（例如给鬼魂的情节找一个「说得过去」的解释）
distortion
歪曲：回忆内容偏离原始材料的总称，包括替换、压缩与添加
qualitative data
质性资料：描述「变化是怎么发生的」的文字资料；信息量大，但分析带有主观解释空间
standardisation
标准化：所有参加者使用完全相同的故事材料，使研究可被重复检验
replication
重复验证：其他研究者在别处重做同一研究（Roediger et al. 用 30 名华盛顿大学学生得出类似结果）
generalisability
可推广性：结论能否推广到样本之外的人群与文化；20 名英国本科生限制了跨文化推广
