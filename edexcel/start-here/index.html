What this psychology course covers, what the exams look like, and how marks are won or lost
📄 Two written papers ⏱ 1h 30m + 2h 🎯 160 marks in total

1. Three Questions First

Answer these three questions honestly before you read anything else. Each one has been tested in a real experiment, and in each case most people predict the wrong answer — including people who later took part in the study.

TRY IT Your predictions

Pick the answer you believe. Nothing is scored as right or wrong yet — the point is to see what you assume before you know anything.

Question 1 · A group and a wrong answer
You are in a room with six people. They are shown three lines and asked which matches a fourth line. The answer is obvious. But all six confidently give the same wrong answer. You are asked last. What do you do?
Question 2 · A person in a coat
A researcher in a lab coat tells you to give a stranger an electric shock every time they get a question wrong. The shocks get stronger, and the stranger starts screaming and then goes silent. You want to stop. He says, “Please continue.” How far do you go?
Question 3 · A story you retell
You read a strange folk tale once. Weeks later you are asked to retell it to someone else. What happens to your version?

What these three have in common

In all three, people’s confident predictions about themselves did not match what they actually did. That gap between what we think we would do and what we do is exactly what psychology measures. Every topic in this course is an attempt to close that gap with evidence instead of intuition.

中文理解：这三道题都对应课程里真实的研究（从众、服从、记忆重构）。绝大多数人在"预测自己会怎样"和"实际会怎样"之间差距很大。心理学这门课不是教你"人性常识"，而是用实验数据替换掉你的直觉。The gap between what you think you would do and what you actually do is what this whole course measures.

2. What Psychology Studies

Your course is built from four topics. Two of them are examined in one paper, two in the other. Knowing which topic a fact belongs to tells you which half of which paper it can appear in.

The four topics of your course, and which written paper each one is examined in.
TopicThe question it asksThe kind of evidence it uses
Topic A · Social psychology How do other people — real, imagined or implied — change what an individual does? Laboratory experiments with groups, questionnaires, cross-cultural comparisons
Topic B · Cognitive psychology How is information taken in, stored, retrieved and sometimes distorted? Laboratory experiments, case studies of brain-damaged patients, memory tests
Topic C · Biological psychology How do the brain, nerves, hormones and genes shape behaviour? Brain scanning, hormone levels, twin and family studies, case studies
Topic D · Learning theories and development How do consequences, observation and maturation shape behaviour? Animal and human experiments, longitudinal studies, case studies
怎么记：四个 topic 是按"行为从哪来解释"分的 —— Social 说"是别人"，Cognitive 说"是你的信息加工"，Biological 说"是你的身体"，Learning 说"是过去的经验"。同一件事会被这四个角度给出四套不同答案，这套 parallele 的思维方式会在后面每一次"评价某理论"时反复用到。

4. Three Kinds of Thinking: AO1, AO2, AO3

Every mark on every paper belongs to one of three assessment objectives. This matters more than anything else on this page, because it tells you what kind of sentence earns the next mark.

Assessment objective（评分目标） — one of three categories of skill that exam marks are awarded for, printed on every long question in the mark scheme.
ObjectiveWhat it rewardsThe sentence that earns itShare of your total
AO1 Knowledge and understanding You know the theory, study, term or procedure accurately “Agency theory proposes that people obey when they enter an agentic state.” 35–40%
AO2 Application You can use that knowledge in a new situation, or handle and present data “In the scenario Adjoa agrees because she wants to stay in the group, which is compliance.” 30–35%
AO3 Analysis and evaluation You judge it: strengths, weaknesses, comparisons, conclusions “This is limited because it ignores differences between individuals, so it cannot explain defiance.” 30–35%

The practical version

A 2-mark “describe” question may be pure AO1. A 2-mark “explain one strength” question is deliberately split: one AO1 mark for naming the strength, one AO3 mark for saying why it is a strength. An 8-mark question is half AO1 and half AO3. An AO2-only question gives you a scenario you have never seen and asks you to bring the theory to it.

TRY IT Which objective is being asked for?

Read each question and decide which objective is being rewarded. This is the skill that stops people writing the right fact in the wrong place.

1. “State what is meant by the term ‘range’ as a measure of dispersion.”
2. “Explain one strength of using standard deviation to analyse quantitative data.”
3. “A new restaurant had recently opened in town... Describe, using conformity, why Adjoa agreed to go and eat at the restaurant.”
4. “Evaluate how well social power theory can account for obedience.” (8 marks)
关键判断技巧：看到 State / Define / Describe → 大概率 AO1（给事实）；看到 Explain one strength / Evaluate / Assess / Discuss → 一定有 AO3（给判断）；看到 一个你没见过的小故事 + "using X, why did..." → AO2（把知识套到新情境）。答非所问是这个考试丢分最常见的原因，不是记不住，而是没看清楚题目要哪一类思维。

5. Command Words

Every question begins with an instruction verb. It tells you precisely how much development your answer needs. Two students can know exactly the same psychology and get very different marks because one of them answered the wrong verb.

State / Name / Give
Recall one or more pieces of information. One clear sentence is usually enough. Do not add explanation — it costs time and earns nothing.
Define
Provide a definition. For anything in this course the examiner expects the technical definition, not a loose everyday description.
Describe
Give an account of something. Your statements should be developed and linked, but no justification or reason is required.
Explain
Requires justification or reasoning — the answer must contain some element of reasoning, not just facts. This is where most students leave marks behind.
Suggest
Make a proposal or propose an idea in written form. Used when there is no single right answer but it must be psychologically sensible.
Complete
Fill in or write all the details asked for. Often a table or a calculation grid.
Calculate
Obtain a numerical answer showing relevant working. If the answer has a unit, include it.
Identify
Select key information from a stimulus or resource you have been given.
Compare
Look for similarities and differences. You must include at least one of each, and you must address both things named.
Discuss
Explore the issue, articulating different or contrasting viewpoints. Both sides, then where you land.
Evaluate
Review information then bring it together to form a conclusion, drawing on strengths, weaknesses, alternative explanations or relevant data, and come to a supported judgement.
Assess / To what extent
Consider all the factors and identify which are the most important, then reach a conclusion. “To what extent” requires a balanced and reasoned argument before the judgement.

One verb per question

Each item uses a single command word. Old habits like “describe and evaluate” are no longer used, so if a question says evaluate, do not spend the first half of your answer describing — that half scores nothing beyond what the evaluation itself contains.

记住这条：动词决定了你要写多深。State 一句话；Describe 连成一段；Explain 必须出现"因为/所以"；Evaluate / Assess / Discuss 必须出现两方观点加你自己的结论。把动词翻译错，写再多都是零分的方向。

6. How Big Questions Are Marked

Short questions are marked point by point — the examiner ticks off each accurate statement. Long questions (8, 12 and 16 marks) are marked with levels: your whole answer is placed in a band, then given a mark within it.

Levels-based marking（等级评分） — the examiner reads your whole answer, decides which level of quality it belongs to, then awards a mark inside that level. It rewards the overall quality of your argument, not the number of separate points you listed.

Here are the real levels used for an 8-mark question. Notice what separates each level from the one below.

LevelMarksWhat your answer looks like
Level 47–8Accurate and thorough knowledge. Well-developed logical evaluation throughout, awareness of competing arguments, and a balanced conclusion.
Level 35–6Accurate knowledge. Arguments developed in mostly coherent chains of reasoning, a conclusion presented, awareness of competing arguments but evaluation may be imbalanced.
Level 23–4Mostly accurate knowledge. Statements with some development, leading to a superficial conclusion.
Level 11–2Isolated elements of knowledge. A conclusion may be presented but it is generic, supporting evidence is limited, and there is limited attempt to address the question.
Level 00No rewardable material.

The rule printed at the bottom of real mark schemes

“Generic answers score 0 marks.” A generic answer is one that could be written about any theory or any study — it never names this one’s specific machinery. It is not the same as a wrong answer; a wrong answer often still contains rewardable material.

TRY IT Be the examiner

Below is a real 2-mark question, and two real-style answers. Decide which one earns the marks before revealing the mark scheme.

The question (2 marks)
A new restaurant had recently opened in town and Adjoa’s group of friends were excited as it had a reputation for tasty, spicy food. They decided to go to the restaurant for a meal. Adjoa does not like spicy food so did not want to eat at the restaurant. However, she agreed to go and eat with them.
Describe, using conformity, why Adjoa agreed to go and eat at the restaurant.
Candidate A
“Adjoa agreed to go because she did not want to argue with her friends and she probably wanted to be polite. People often do things they do not really want to do when they are in a group, and she may have felt a bit of pressure from the others. Also the restaurant sounded popular so maybe she was curious about it and wanted to try something new anyway.”
Candidate B
“Adjoa decided to go with her friends to the restaurant as she wanted to be part of the group and not be isolated. Adjoa changed her behaviour to fit into the group although she did not change her belief that she would not enjoy the food.”

What Candidate B did that A did not

  • Used the scenario. Every sentence is about Adjoa, not about people in general.
  • Used the concept structurally. It separates outward behaviour (she went) from private belief (she still dislikes spicy food), which is exactly what the theory is built to explain.
  • Stayed inside the question. Nothing about curiosity, politeness or arguing — those could be pasted into a thousand answers, which is what makes them generic.
答题公式（8 分题适用）：先花一两句把理论/研究说准（AO1），再用同样的篇幅做评价（AO3）—— 各占一半分。AO3 的每一条都要写成"观点 + 为什么这是个 Strengths/弱点"。最危险的不是说错，而是说得太笼统 —— 因为 mark scheme 底部印的那句话是 "Generic answers score 0 marks"。

7. Planning Your Time

Almost nobody runs out of psychology knowledge in an exam. People run out of minutes, then give a five-line answer to a twelve-mark question. Use this rule so that never happens to you.

TRY IT What is this question worth in minutes?

Choose the question and see how long you should spend on it. Compare it with how long you usually spend.

Unit 1 paper (90 min)Unit 2 paper (120 min)
Reading and checking at the end8 minutes10 minutes
Short questionsabout 1.3 minutes per markabout 1 minute per mark
Each 8-mark question10 minutes12 minutes
The 12-mark question15 minutes16 minutes
The 16-mark question—20 minutes

Where the extra time comes from

A 10-minute limit on an 8-mark question feels brutal at first. It works if you plan before writing: one or two minutes deciding which two or three points you will make, and which one you will conclude with. Writing without planning is what turns an 8-mark question into a 15-minute one, taken from the 12-mark question at the end.

换算口诀：Unit 1 是 1 分 ≈ 1.3 分钟，Unit 2 是 1 分 ≈ 1 分钟；但大题不能按这个比例算 —— 8 分题（Unit 1）给 10 分钟，12 分题给 15 分钟，Unit 2 的 16 分题给 20 分钟。最后一大题一定要留够时间，因为它是 levels-based，写不完直接掉一个 level，等于一次丢 3–4 分。

8. How Each Page Works

Every topic page in this course is built in the same order, so once you learn the pattern you can move through any of them without guessing what comes next.

1 · Getting Started

A short situation to react to. Answer before you read the theory — comparing your instinct with the finding is what makes the idea stick.

2 · Key terms in the margin

Every technical term appears with its definition the first time it is used, before any later idea depends on it.

3 · The explanation itself

Figures and tables carry the structure; the text carries the reasoning. Each abstract idea has a plain-language box in Chinese underneath it.

4 · Evaluation

Supporting points and criticisms, each tagged with the reason it matters. This is AO3 material, and it is what long questions are built from.

5 · Checkpoint

Classification and matching tasks with instant feedback. Use them to find out which part you cannot yet explain to someone else.

6 · Exam Lab

Real past questions for that same topic, with the marking points and a model answer you can compare your own writing against.

The order that works when revising

  1. Cover the page and write what you remember — a page of headings from memory beats reading the whole page twice.
  2. Check the Key Terms list at the bottom. Any term you cannot define accurately without looking is the one to study next.
  3. Attempt the Exam Lab question before reading its model answer. Comparing afterwards teaches you more than reading does.
  4. Return to the Checkpoint for anything you got wrong, then redo that one item a day later.
给基础薄弱同学的建议：不要从头到尾通读。先只看每张页最后的 Key Terms，把中文遮住能说出意思的划掉，剩下的才是你这页真正要学的；然后再回去读那几段。Key Terms 是你的地图，不是附录。

10. Key Terms

These are the terms used on this page. Each one is also re-taught inside the topic page where it first becomes useful.

Assessment objective评分目标
One of three skill categories that exam marks are awarded for: AO1 knowledge and understanding, AO2 application, AO3 analysis and evaluation.
AO1知识与理解
Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of scientific ideas, processes, techniques and procedures.
AO2应用
Apply knowledge and understanding in a theoretical context, in a practical context, and when handling qualitative and quantitative data.
AO3分析与评价
Analyse, interpret and evaluate scientific information, ideas and evidence to make judgements, reach conclusions, and develop practical design and procedures.
Command word指令词
The verb at the start of a question that tells you exactly how much development your answer needs.
Levels-based marking等级评分
Long-question marking in which the whole answer is placed in a quality band, then given a mark within that band.
Generic answer笼统答案
An answer that could be written about any theory or study. Real mark schemes state this scores zero.
Extended open-response question开放式长答题
The 8-, 12- and 16-mark questions at the end of each topic section, marked using levels.
Research methods研究方法
Experiments, self-report methods, sampling, data analysis and ethics. Assessed in both papers, not tied to one topic.
Qualitative data定性数据
Data expressed in words rather than numbers, often analysed using thematic analysis.
Quantitative data定量数据
Data expressed as numbers, analysed using measures of central tendency, dispersion and graphs.
Mark scheme评分方案
The examiner’s document listing the acceptable answers, the objectives each question targets, and any restrictions such as generic answers scoring zero.
▸ Exam key points
  • Unit 1 paper: 1h 30m, 64 marks, Topics A and B. Section A 26 marks, Section B 26 marks, Section C one 12-mark question.
  • Unit 2 paper: 2h, 96 marks, Topics C and D. Section A 34 marks, Section B 34 marks, Section C a 12-mark and a 16-mark question covering both topics.
  • Each topic section ends with its own 8-mark question. Plan it before you write it.
  • AO weightings: AO1 35–40%, AO2 30–35%, AO3 30–35%.
  • An 8-mark question is AO1 4 marks plus AO3 4 marks. Equal emphasis on knowledge and evaluation is required.
  • Each question uses one command word only. Answer the verb you were given.
  • “Generic answers score 0 marks” is printed in real mark schemes. Name the theory’s actual machinery and keep it inside the scenario.
  • Levels-based marking: Level 4 (7–8) thorough knowledge plus balanced logical evaluation with a conclusion; Level 1 (1–2) isolated knowledge with a generic conclusion.
  • Timing: Unit 1 — 10 minutes per 8-mark, 15 for the 12-mark. Unit 2 — 12 minutes per 8-mark, 16 for the 12-mark, 20 for the 16-mark.
  • Research methods appear in both papers. Methods learned with Topics A and B can be re-examined in the Unit 2 paper.
