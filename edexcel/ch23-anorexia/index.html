1. Learning Objectives & Getting Started（学习目标与导入）

🎯 Learning Objectives（学习目标）

By the end of this chapter you should be able to describe and/or evaluate:

  • Symptoms and features of anorexia nervosa（神经性厌食症的症状和特征）
  • One biological explanation of anorexia nervosa — the role of genes（生物学解释：基因的作用）
  • One non-biological explanation of anorexia nervosa — social learning theory（非生物学解释：社会学习理论）
  • Two treatments/therapies for anorexia nervosa: drug therapy and cognitive behavioural therapy（两种疗法：药物治疗和认知行为疗法）

💭 Getting Started（课堂导入）

Anorexia nervosa is an eating disorder in which people become dangerously underweight due to restricted calorie intake（卡路里摄入限制）. Between 1 and 6 per cent of women and less than 0.5 per cent of men will experience anorexia nervosa at some point in their lives.

Psychiatrist Tony Jaffa (2007) described the cases of three teenage patients who developed anorexia nervosa following dental treatment, such as getting a fixed brace to straighten their teeth. In each case the dentist had advised the young people not to eat sugary snacks between meals and this restriction had triggered them to begin restricting other foods.

The dentist's advice was clearly not the cause of the disorder, but it merely correlated with the onset. Dane and Bhatia (2023) state that eating disorders like anorexia nervosa result from interactions between numerous biological, psychological and sociocultural risk factors.

Discussion questions:

  • What factors might have caused the young people in Jaffa's case study to develop such an unhealthy relationship with food?（哪些因素可能导致 Jaffa 案例中的青少年与食物产生不健康关系？）
  • Do you think anorexia nervosa is determined more by nature or nurture? How could you investigate this?（神经性厌食症更多由先天还是后天决定？如何研究？）
📝 中文要点：Jaffa 的临床案例提醒我们：同一个环境事件（如牙医建议少吃甜食）对不同个体的影响可能完全不同。只有本身就具备易感性（vulnerability）的青少年，才会把单一限制扩展为广泛的饮食控制。这体现了素质-压力模型（diathesis-stress model）的核心思想。
▶ Section 1: Getting Started — Exam Key Points AO1
  1. AN = dangerous underweight from restricted calorie intake（限制热量摄入导致危险的低体重）
  2. Lifetime prevalence: 1–6% women, <0.5% men（女性 1–6%，男性不足 0.5%）
  3. Jaffa (2007): 3 teenagers developed AN after dental advice to avoid sugary snacks — the trigger merely correlated with onset（牙医建议只是相关，不是原因）
  4. Dane & Bhatia (2023): AN results from interactions between biological + psychological + sociocultural risk factors（多因素交互）

2. Symptoms of Anorexia Nervosa（症状）

🎯 Quick Summary（本章要点）

  1. DSM-5 三主症: Restricted energy intake → low weight（少吃→低体重）+ intense fear of gaining weight（强烈怕胖）+ body weight/shape disturbance（体型认知扭曲）
  2. ICD-11 补充: purging（清除行为）、increase energy requirements（过度运动/冷暴露）、BMI < 18.5 或儿童第五百分位
  3. ⚠️ BMI caveat: 不直接测脂肪、基于白人男性数据、跨文化效度有限

People with anorexia nervosa (AN) experience a range of physical, cognitive, affective and behavioural symptoms. In the DSM-5 this disorder is classified as a feeding and eating disorder（喂养与进食障碍）. To receive a diagnosis, a person must demonstrate the following symptoms:

  • Restricted energy intake relative to requirements, leading to a significant low body weight in the context of the person's age, gender, physical health（低于正常/预期体重）
  • Intense fear of gaining weight or participating in persistent behaviour that will interrupt the gaining of weight（对体重增加或变胖的强烈恐惧）
  • Disturbed by one's body weight or shape, self-worth influenced by body weight or shape, or persistent lack of recognition of seriousness of low bodyweight（对身体体重或体型的认知扭曲；自我价值受体重/体型影响；或不承认低体重的严重性）

The symptoms listed in the ICD-11 are similar to those in the DSM-5. They also include various ways in which people restrict calorie intake, for example:

  • Choosing low-calorie foods
  • Excessive slow eating of small amounts of food
  • Hiding and/or spitting out food

ICD-11 also lists purging（清除行为）, for example the use of laxatives（泻药） or skipping insulin doses in people with diabetes, and ways that people increase their energy requirements in order to burn more calories, for example excessive exercise, use of certain medications and herbal products and deliberately exposing themselves to the cold.

🔑 Key Terms:
  • feeding and eating disorder: a group of disorders in which the primary symptoms are abnormal eating behaviour and negative attitudes towards food, eating and body shape and size.
  • laxatives（泻药）: medications used to regulate and encourage bowel movements（用于调节和促进排便的药物）。
  • purging（清除行为）: intentional behaviours which serve to remove food or calories from the body including self-induced vomiting, misuse of laxatives or excessive exercise（故意清除食物或热量的行为，包括自我催吐、滥用泻药或过度运动）。

BMI and ICD-11

ICD-11 gives guidance about how low body weight should be to be classed as significantly low, e.g. a body mass index (BMI，身体质量指数） of less than 18.5 in adults or a BMI which is under fifth percentile in children and adolescents (only 5 per cent of children weigh this much or less). Losing more than 20 per cent of body weight in six months would be a symptom of AN under ICD-11. ICD-11 also clarifies that low body weight must not be due to other medical conditions or lack of food availability (WHO, 2013).

Important caveat about BMI: It should be noted that advice for weight gain/loss may not be valid if based solely on body mass index. This is because BMI does not directly measure fat. A person with a lot of muscle and little body fat may still have a BMI that is above average but they do not need to lose weight to be healthy. Also, the initial body mass index statistics were based on white European men (Katella, 2023). For this reason, BMI may not be as valid as a measure of health in women and people from other areas of the world, where distribution of body fat may be different. For example, WHO has released specific advice for interpreting BMI with Asian people (Tan, 2004). The relationship between weight and health is also affected by age and lifestyle.

⚠️ 考试易错点：BMI 只是辅助指标，不是诊断 AN 的金标准。ICD-11 明确指出低体重不能由其他躯体疾病或食物匮乏导致；同时 BMI 基于白人男性数据建立，对亚洲人群、女性、肌肉发达者可能不够准确。

  2. ICD-11: BMI < 18.5（成人）或 第五百分位以下（儿童青少年）；6 个月减重 > 20%
  3. Restriction 方式：low-calorie foods / slow eating / hiding-spitting out food

  5. Burn calories：excessive exercise / cold exposure / herbal products

3. Features of Anorexia Nervosa（特征）

🎯 Quick Summary（本章要点）

  1. 2 亚型: restricting type（限制型）vs binge-purging type（暴食-清除型）——看最近 3 个月有无暴食/清除
  2. 患病率: 终身 2.4–4.3%；女性最高 4%，男性 0.3%（女高 10 倍以上）
  3. 发病年龄: 10–24 岁为主（青春期/成年早期）；常与重大生活压力事件吻合
  4. 文化差异: 高收入工业化国家更高；亚洲部分患者无"怕胖"主诉（改为胃痛）
  5. 预后: 多数 5 年内恢复；18 个月内复发常见；共病抑郁/焦虑/强迫

In this section you will learn about five features of anorexia nervosa, including the fact that there are different subtypes.

3.1 Subtypes（亚型）

DSM-5 lists two subtypes of AN: restricting type（限制型） and binge-purging type（暴食-清除型）. These refer to whether the person has or has not engaged in binging（暴食） or purging in the last three months:

Restricting Type（限制型）

Has not engaged in binge-purging in the last three months. Weight control relies on dieting and exercise（节食与运动）.

Binge-Purging Type（暴食-清除型）

Meets all AN criteria and experiences periods of binge eating, followed by purging behaviours（暴食后催吐/泻药/过度运动清除）.

🔑 区分：Restricting type 靠节食和运动控制体重；binge-purging type 会周期性暴食再通过催吐/泻药/过度运动清除。两者都满足 AN 的核心诊断标准，但行为模式不同。

3.2 Prevalence and Age of Onset（患病率与发病年龄）

Anorexia nervosa is usually diagnosed during adolescence or early adulthood, e.g. between the ages of 10 and 24 years. Early and late onset is less common, e.g. before puberty or after age 40.

Between 2.4 to 4.3 per cent of people will be affected by AN at some point in their lives but these figures vary significantly by gender and culture. Lifetime prevalence may be up to 4 per cent in women and 0.3 per cent in men (Tessema et al., 2023).

Onset of the disorder often appears to coincide with a significant life stressor such as starting university or leaving home. Prevalence is also higher in people with autism and ADHD compared with people without these diagnoses (Marucci et al., 2018).

3.3 Cultural Differences（文化差异）

  • Prevalence seems to be higher in high-income countries that are highly industrialised, such as the USA, Europe, Australia and New Zealand.
  • However, globalisation means that cultural values, roles, food availability and lifestyle are becoming increasingly similar in many areas of the world. This has led to an increased prevalence of AN in some countries.
  • Prevalence is still lower in Africa and Latin America, and for people from these cultural groups who are residents in the United States; however, this may be linked more to decreased help-seeking from medical practitioners rather than a genuinely lower rate of AN (van Hoeken et al., 2016).
  • AN may still be present in people from countries in Asia but often there is no indication of the fear of gaining weight that is central to the diagnosis in the minority world (e.g. Europe and the United States). Instead, people may complain of stomach aches and pains as a reason for reduced food intake. However, if the person is still very preoccupied with weight loss or avoidance of weight gain they may still be diagnosed with AN.
🌍 文化差异考试点：西方诊断标准强调"怕胖"，但在亚洲一些地区，患者可能以胃痛、食欲差为主诉，对体重增加的恐惧不明显。这会影响跨文化诊断的效度，也提醒我们 DSM-5/ICD-11 的文化局限性。

3.4 Prognosis（预后）

Some people recover almost entirely from episodes of AN; however, for many it becomes a chronic condition which may last many years. For those with dangerously low weight, e.g. BMI of 14 or less, hospitalisation may be necessary to prevent physical complications and preserve life.

Full remission becomes less likely under these circumstances and physical health problems resulting from malnutrition are more common. Typically, people recover within five years although they may still exhibit some symptoms such as lower than average body weight and preoccupation with perfectionism. Relapse is common especially within the first 18 months following treatment (Berends et al., 2016). Common comorbid disorders include unipolar depression, anxiety disorders and obsessive-compulsive disorder (Marucci et al., 2018).

📝 Exam Tip：If asked to "describe one feature of anorexia nervosa", pick the feature you can expand with a statistic or comparison. For example: "One feature is prevalence — lifetime prevalence is up to 4 per cent in women but only 0.3 per cent in men, meaning it is more than ten times more common in women."

4. Biological Explanation: The Role of Genes（生物学解释：基因的作用）

🎯 Quick Summary（本章要点）

  1. 一级亲属 → 风险 ×10（Pinheiro et al., 2009）；双生子遗传率 48–74%（Yilmaz et al., 2015）
  2. 连锁研究: Grice (2002) 192 家庭 → 亚组 37 家庭 → 1 号染色体易感区域；现已过时
  3. GWAS: Nakabayashi (2009) 日本 → SPATA17；Scott-van Zeeland (2013) 152 基因 → EPHX2
  4. 多基因: Johnson (2022) 53 个基因 → MHC + CLIC1（氯离子通道 → 突触修剪）
🧠 AO1 Zone — Biological Explanation（知识点区） describe & recall

Evidence suggests that anorexia nervosa may be a highly heritable disorder. For example, Andrea Poyastro Pinheiro and colleagues (2009) state that people with at least one first-degree relative with anorexia nervosa are ten times more likely to develop the disorder than people with no such relative. Also, estimates based on twin studies suggest a heritability rate between 48 per cent and 74 per cent (Yilmaz et al., 2015).

4.1 Linkage Studies（连锁研究）

Linkage studies（连锁研究） compare the genomes of family members who both share the same disorder and provide another way of exploring the role of genetic factors in mental health.

🧬 Grice et al. (2002) — Linkage Study Linkage

Studied 192 families including a relative with anorexia nervosa and at least one other relative either with AN or another eating disorder, e.g. bulimia nervosa（神经性贪食症）.

  • Initial findings: failed to identify any areas of the genome specifically associated with eating disorders.
  • Subgroup of 37 families (≥ 2 relatives with restrictive-type AN): evidence of a specific region of chromosome 1 that may make people susceptible (vulnerable) to developing AN.

→ Linkage studies are now seen as outdated; researchers have moved to genome-wide association studies (GWAS).

💡 打个比方理解 linkage study（连锁研究）：连锁研究就像警察查案——病人亲属们患有同样的病，研究者就去看他们的 DNA 里有没有"共同区域"。但它的精确度只到"这个家族里患病的人都在 1 号染色体附近有相似片段"，相当于警察只知道嫌疑人住在哪个片区，还不知道具体是哪一栋楼、哪一户（哪个具体基因）。所以后来科学家改用更精确的 GWAS 去"逐户排查"。

4.2 Polygenic Explanations（多基因解释）

Since these early studies, more and more genes have been identified that appear to increase the risk of developing anorexia nervosa.

🔬 Nakabayashi et al. (2009) — GWAS (Japan) GWAS

Identified the specific area of a gene called SPATA17 on chromosome 1 which seemed to differ in people with anorexia nervosa.

🧪 Scott-van Zeeland et al. (2013) — Candidate Gene Comparison GWAS

Compared 152 different genes in a sample of women with anorexia nervosa and another group without the disorder. Found significant differences in and around the EPHX2 gene.

The EPHX2 gene is associated with production of an enzyme (epoxide hydrolase 2) that breaks down cholesterol（分解胆固醇）. This suggests that disruption to the breakdown of this substance may be linked to the development of anorexia nervosa.

💡 打个比方理解 EPHX2：EPHX2 基因负责生产一种"分解胆固醇的酶"，你可以把它想成细胞里的垃圾回收系统——专门处理胆固醇这种"废料"。如果回收系统出了问题，废料堆积，细胞环境变差，大脑里与食欲、情绪相关的区域就可能受影响。所以一个看似跟进食无关的基因，也可能通过这种方式间接与 AN 扯上关系。
🔑 Key term: major histocompatibility complex (MHC，主要组织相容性复合体） — a group of genes that code for proteins that are critical to the correct functioning of the immune system.

However, it is important to remember that like many other disorders, AN is polygenic, meaning variants of many genes increase the risk of developing this disorder.

💡 打个比方理解 polygenic（多基因）：多基因就像很多人一起抬一块大石头——53 个基因里，每个基因只出一点点力（增加一点点风险），谁都不是全部原因，但大家一起用力，石头就被抬起来了。所以不能说"是某个基因让人得厌食症"，只能说"很多基因的微小贡献加在一起，让这个人更容易得病"。
🧠 Johnson et al. (2022) — 53 Genes & CLIC1 Polygenic

Identified 53 genes associated with anorexia nervosa. One interesting and original finding was a link between AN and a set of genes called the major histocompatibility complex (MHC) — genes which code for proteins coordinating the body's immune system.

Specifically, they identified the possible role of the CLIC1 gene: upregulation of this gene (increased expression) was associated with lowest body weight BMI (Johnson et al., 2021). This gene codes for chloride ion channels and is linked to synaptic pruning and neural connectivity（突触修剪与神经连接）. Abnormalities of this gene could therefore affect the way neurons communicate with each other in areas of the brain linked to eating and appetite.

🧬 中文理解：AN 的遗传解释已经从"单基因"走向"多基因（polygenic）"。Johnson 等人发现 53 个相关基因，其中 CLIC1 基因与最低 BMI 相关，它影响神经突触修剪和神经连接。这说明基因可能通过影响大脑食欲调控回路来增加 AN 风险，而不是直接"决定"一个人会得厌食症。

💡 打个比方理解突触修剪：大脑的神经连接不是越多越好——它像一座花园，会定期"修剪"：用得多的连接（小路）保留并加固，用不到的连接（杂草）被剪掉。这个过程就叫突触修剪（synaptic pruning）。CLIC1 基因就与负责修剪的机制有关；如果"园丁"出了问题，修剪乱了，大脑里管食欲和进食的区域之间的"信号道路"就会变得不正常。

  2. Grice (2002): 192 家庭 → 亚组 37 家庭 → 1 号染色体易感区域（连锁研究已过时）

  4. Scott-van Zeeland (2013): 152 基因对比 → EPHX2（epoxide hydrolase 2，分解胆固醇的酶）

5. Evaluation of Biological Explanation（生物学解释的评价）

⚖️ AO3 Zone — Evaluation（评估区） evaluate & analyse

Research into the role of genetics in anorexia nervosa has advanced as new methodologies for studying the role of genes have emerged. However, every research method has its limitations.

5.1 Limitations of Family and Twin Studies（家庭研究与双生子研究的局限）

⚠️ Family & Twin Study Limitations

  • Family studies are limited as it is impossible to determine whether the increased risk in first-degree relatives is due to transmission of certain genes or a result of observational learning（观察学习）. Family members share genes and environments, so correlation does not prove causation.
  • Twin studies can also be problematic due to the shared environment fallacy（共享环境谬误）. The greater similarity in concordance rates for monozygotic twins, compared with dizygotic twins, may be due to their identical appearance which means they are also treated more similarly than dizygotic twins (who are non-identical). This means that the heritability of AN may be overestimated.
Eval: shared environment · correlation ≠ causation · heritability may be overestimated
💡 打个比方理解 shared environment fallacy（共享环境谬误）：同卵双胞胎不仅基因 100% 相同，还因为长得一模一样，从小被家人、老师、朋友用几乎相同的方式对待——同样的夸奖、同样的期待、同样的饮食要求。就像两只一模一样的杯子，被倒了同样的水。所以双胞胎在体重、饮食行为上相似，不一定是基因的功劳，也可能是"被同样对待"的结果。这就让"遗传率 48–74%"可能被高估。

5.2 Limitations of Linkage and GWAS Studies（连锁研究与 GWAS 的局限）

⚠️ Linkage & GWAS Limitations

  • Linkage studies have been criticised for lacking specificity as they are only able to identify loci (regions) on chromosomes that are similar in people with AN and not identify specific candidate genes.
  • As they map the genomes of relatives with a specific disorder (e.g. anorexia nervosa) they may not be helpful in small families as there may not be enough affected people to conduct the study. In addition to this, they may miss including relatives with relevant gene abnormalities which have not been expressed, due to favourable environmental circumstances for example.
  • Although GWAS studies are a popular way of identifying potential candidate genes, these are also limited in that they require very large sample sizes in order for findings to reach statistical significance. Also they only identify candidate genes but do not actually explain how these genes make a person more vulnerable to developing the disorder.
Eval: lacks specificity · small families · very large samples needed · no mechanism explained

5.3 Strength of Biological Research（生物学研究的优势）

✅ Strength — reducing stigma（去污名化）：A strength of current research into genetic underpinning of anorexia nervosa is that it may help to change societal attitudes towards this potentially fatal disorder. If this research is shared publicly through the media, e.g. news items, television and social media, it may help to change people's attitudes to decrease prejudice and stigmatisation of this disorder. Research cited by Lisa Brelet and colleagues (2021) suggests that such prejudices even exist within medical professionals — such attitudes certainly are unhelpful to families trying to cope with their relatives' symptoms and any research which helps to overcome such negative opinions may be beneficial to individuals and their families.

💡 核心评价逻辑：遗传研究的价值不只是"找病因"，还能去污名化——如果公众理解 AN 有生物学基础，就可能减少对患者的指责。但研究方法的局限（共享环境、样本量、相关性≠因果性）意味着我们不能把 AN 简单归结为"基因决定"。
▶ Section 5: Evaluation of Biological — Exam Key Points AO3
  1. Family studies: shared genes and environments → 无法区分遗传与观察学习（correlation ≠ causation）
  2. Twin studies: shared environment fallacy（同卵双胞胎被同样对待）→ 遗传率可能被高估
  3. Linkage: 只能定位染色体区域（lacking specificity）；小家庭样本不足；无法捕捉未表达的基因异常
  4. GWAS: 需要极大样本才能显著；只找到候选基因，不解释机制
  5. ✅ Strength: 去污名化——媒体传播遗传研究可减少对患者的偏见（Brelet et al., 2021）

6. Non-Biological Explanation: Social Learning Theory（非生物学解释：社会学习理论）

🎯 Quick Summary（本章要点）

  1. 核心机制: 通过观察和模仿榜样（role models）学习身材规范——模特/演员/网红
  2. Bandura (1977): prevalence（行为出现频率）+ incentives（预期奖励）解释模仿
  3. 替代强化/惩罚: 瘦榜样获得名气点赞 → vicarious reinforcement；胖榜样被嘲笑 → vicarious punishment
  4. 研究证据: Harrison & Cantor (1997) 杂志 > 电视；Schwartz (1982) 美国小姐越来越瘦 vs 真实女性变胖；Dane & Bhatia (2023) 50 项研究
👥 AO1 Zone — Social Learning Theory（知识点区） describe & recall

Social learning theory suggests that we learn social norms for body size and shape through observation and imitation of role models. These are people with whom we identify, because they are attractive, powerful and/or popular such as fashion models, actors, presenters, musicians, vloggers, influencers and other famous people — all of whom may be seen as role models, especially by younger men and women. The physical appearance of these role models may influence attitudes about physical appearance.

6.1 Vicarious Reinforcement and Punishment（替代性强化与惩罚）

Vicarious reinforcement（替代性强化） may also encourage behaviours such as dieting and excessive exercise in an effort to lose weight, as role models are seen to be rewarded for their appearance via increased fame and popularity, including 'likes' and 'shares' on social media. When public figures gain weight this is often punished through negative media attention. Vicarious punishment（替代性惩罚） may also encourage media consumers to avoid behaviours which could lead to weight gain.

💡 打个比方理解 vicarious reinforcement（替代性强化）："替代（vicarious）"的意思是"替你看、替你感受"。就像你看直播带货：主播瘦、美、晒身材，然后收获点赞、夸赞、品牌代言——你虽然没有亲自拿到这些奖励，但亲眼看到"瘦 = 被奖励"，节食的念头就被悄悄强化了。反过来，如果某个明星变胖被全网嘲笑，你看到"胖 = 被惩罚"，就更不敢做会增重的事。奖励和惩罚都不是发生在你身上，但你都"看在眼里"，行为照样被影响——这就是"替代"的含义。

6.2 Bandura: Prevalence and Incentives（班杜拉：流行度与诱因）

Bandura (1977) uses the terms prevalence and incentives to explain why certain norms are established and behaviours imitated.

  • Prevalence refers to the frequency of a certain behaviour; the more often a behaviour is observed the greater the probability that it will be imitated. The wealth of media imagery idealising thinness means this specific body image becomes a readily available template to which people compare their perception of their own body.
  • Incentives refer to motivations to achieve the same body shape or size, i.e. anticipated rewards such as social acceptance and admiration of others.

6.3 Research Evidence for Social Learning Theory（社会学习理论的研究证据）

📰 Harrison & Cantor (1997) — Thinness Depicting/Promoting Correlational

Use the terms 'thinness depicting' and 'thinness promoting' to refer to the types of media associated with the development of anorexia nervosa.

Key finding: engagement with women's magazines was a stronger predictor for disordered eating than television viewing（女性杂志比电视更强的预测指标）.

👑 Schwartz et al. (1982) — Miss America Contestants Longitudinal

Review of Miss America beauty pageant competitors from 1959 to 1978 (20 years):

  • The average weight of contestants decreased over the period.
  • At the same time in America, the actual average weight of females was slightly increasing.

→ The 'ideal' body type hailed as beautiful was incongruent with reality, which could make women see themselves as unfairly 'fat' compared with these images.

📖 Garner et al. (1980) — Diet Articles Correlational

Over a 10-year period from 1970 to 1980 the number of diet articles in women's magazines hugely increased, suggesting a societal preoccupation with the need to lose weight.

6.4 Social Media and Body Image（社交媒体与身体意象）

Today, people receive messages about body ideals from social media apps such as Instagram and TikTok. Although research in this area is increasing, the research is sometimes poor quality which means conclusions must be cautious.

📱 Dane & Bhatia (2023) — Systematic Review Review

Conducted a systematic review of research in this area, including 50 studies from 17 countries.

They stated that the relationship between social media and body image is moderated by:

  • the type of content being accessed (e.g. pro-eating disorder content), and
  • social media literacy（社交媒体素养）.

Existing body dissatisfaction strengthens the relationship between social media usage and disordered eating. However, this dissatisfaction, in itself, may result from exposure to unrealistic body ideals and images which promote fitness and thinness (Dane & Bhatia, 2023).

🔑 Key term: social media literacy（社交媒体素养） — the extent to which a person has the ability to access, understand, evaluate and interact with social networking sites; includes understanding of the intentions of the individuals and/or organisations that share content on such platforms and the use of algorithms to target users with specific content.
📱 中文理解：社交媒体不是单向"导致"AN，而是通过内容类型和个体社交媒体素养调节影响。已经对身体不满意的人，更容易受到宣扬瘦身/健身内容的影响；而具备批判性媒介素养的人，可能更能识别和抵抗不现实的审美标准。

7. Evaluation of Social Learning Theory（社会学习理论的评价）

⚖️ AO3 Zone — Evaluation（评估区） evaluate & analyse

7.1 Supporting Evidence（支持证据）

✅ Supporting evidence: Evidence to support the social learning theory explanation comes from the fact that diagnoses of anorexia nervosa have increased steadily since the 1950s, which marked the beginning of the change towards slimmer models and the preoccupation with body image and dieting in the media. Likewise, research has found that anorexia nervosa is more common in dance and modelling students compared with other female university students due to over-adherence to social norms around weight loss in pursuit of a very slim body image (Garner and Garfinkel, 1980). In recent years, more males have been diagnosed with anorexia nervosa, which coincides with changes in media targeted at men, e.g. diet, fitness and body image content on social media. Both of these factors would suggest that there is a relationship between changes in cultural views and increased diagnoses of anorexia nervosa.

Eval: temporal & occupational evidence link media norms to diagnosis rates

7.2 Limitations（局限）

⚠️ Limitations

Although there is compelling evidence for the effect of cultural ideals on body image, and that this can possibly lead to the development of disorders such as anorexia nervosa, there are also many people who are exposed to these ideals that do not develop eating disorders. This suggests that sociocultural factors, and specifically social learning, may be one of a number of factors that combine to increase the risk of developing anorexia nervosa.

For example, sociocultural factors may only influence the body image of people with a genetic predisposition to develop the disorder. It is likely that certain personality traits encourage certain people to become more obsessive in terms of their media intake, thus increasing prevalence and incentives. This may be particularly true for people with problematic internet use (PIU) whose exposure to thinness depicting and promoting imagery via social media may be particularly high, putting them at greater risk. This demonstrates that it is not simply heavy media consumption that increases the risk of anorexia nervosa but that individual differences, including personality traits that predispose heavy media use, may also underpin disordered eating.

Eval: cannot explain why only a minority develop AN — SLT is one factor among many

7.3 Cross-Cultural Evidence: Hoek et al. (2005)（跨文化证据）

🌍 Hoek et al. (2005) — Curaçao: Evidence demonstrated that anorexia nervosa was non-existent in the indigenous population of Curaçao (a Caribbean island in the Netherlands Antilles) where it is culturally desirable to be overweight. This was despite exposure to US and Netherlands television channels.

However, social learning theory is still supported as the indigenous islanders may have failed to identify with characters in these programmes and continued to conform to the traditional cultural norms of the island. Rates of AN in white and mixed race women and girls were similar to those found in the United States and the Netherlands. However, the majority of the diagnosed individuals had visited one or other of these countries and may have been more likely to identify with Western standards regarding body size and shape.

Eval: identification matters — exposure alone is not enough
⚖️ 评价核心：社会学习理论能解释 AN 的文化分布和媒体影响，但无法解释为什么同样接触瘦身理想的人只有少数人患病。这说明它更像一个"风险因素"而非充分原因，需要和基因、人格等因素结合——这又回到了素质-压力模型。

  2. ⚠️ Limitation: 同样暴露于瘦身理想的大多数人并不患病 → SLT 只是风险因素之一

8. Wider Issues and Debates（更广泛议题与辩论）

8.1 Nature versus Nurture（先天与后天）

🧬 Nature vs Nurture

Despite mounting evidence that anorexia nervosa has a genetic component, like many other disorders, it is important to remember that the risk is only fractionally increased by possessing one or more of these genes. Gene expression is affected by environmental (or epigenetic) factors which can lead to overexpression or underexpression. Inheritance (genotype) may therefore create a genetic diathesis, however lifestyle choice and life events determine the effect of genes on behaviour (phenotype). This means that one cannot ignore the role of the environment when considering the role of genes.

Also, anorexia nervosa is a heterogeneous disorder — not everyone with this diagnosis shares the exact same traits or symptoms. There are different subtypes and degrees of severity, some of which are more heritable than others. On the one hand, the identification of multiple genes which have differing effects on the body is a strength — as it helps to explain the diversity in symptoms. However, cultural differences in prevalence and range of symptoms, e.g. no reported 'fear of fatness', indicate the important role that cultural factors play in this disorder.

💡 打个比方理解 Nature vs Nurture（先天 vs 后天）：把基因想象成种子里的"程序"，环境是土壤、阳光和雨水。同样的种子，种在贫瘠干旱的土里长成小苗，种在肥沃湿润的土里就长得高大——种子（基因）决定了"可能性"，环境决定了"最终长成什么样"。AN 的基因只是给了人一个"容易对节食上瘾"的倾向（diathesis，易感基础），但要不要表达出来，取决于生活经历、文化环境这些外部因素。所以"有 AN 基因"不等于"一定会得 AN"。

8.2 Psychology as a Science（心理学作为科学）

🔬 Psychology as a Science

Typically, drug therapies are only approved if there is a strong evidence base to support their safety and credibility. Studies should be large-scale, double-blind randomised control trials. This means that the drug has performed better than a placebo in reducing symptoms, that possible side-effects are well recognised and that the risk of their development can be assessed for each individual offered the treatment.

9. Treatments for Anorexia Nervosa（治疗方法概述）

Due to the medical complications associated with malnourishment, the primary goal of treatment for anorexia nervosa is weight gain. However, psychological therapies are also used to treat anxiety associated with weight gain and to help improve mood regulation, social skills, body image and self-esteem, and to prevent relapse.

💡 治疗顺序：AN 治疗必须先处理营养不良和体重过低的医学风险（有时需住院），然后才是心理干预。不能单纯用谈话治疗解决危及生命的躯体问题。

10. Biological Treatment: Drug Therapy（生物治疗：药物治疗）

🎯 Quick Summary（本章要点）

  1. NICE 立场: 药物不能作为 AN 的一线或唯一治疗（严重低体重时副作用风险 > 获益）
  2. 四类药: appetite stimulants（食欲刺激剂）/ SSRIs（抗抑郁）/ atypical antipsychotics（非典型抗精神病药）/ hormone-based
  3. 证据: dronabinol +0.73kg（Andries 2013）✅ · SSRIs 疗效不一致（Ferguson 1999 无效 / Kaye 2001 降复发）✅⚠️ · olanzapine 结果矛盾 ⚠️
  4. 统计坑: 小样本（n=10）不显著可能 = Type II error（假阴性）
💊 AO1 Zone — Drug Therapy（知识点区） describe & recall

The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) state that drugs should not be used as a primary or sole treatment for patients with anorexia nervosa — costs may outweigh the benefits owing to physical problems resulting from taking medications when seriously underweight. For example, heart problems and other physical health issues are not uncommon and some medications may lead to side-effects which put additional pressure on the body and could put the patient at risk. This demonstrates the importance of weighing potential risks and benefits carefully before making decisions.

This said, researchers have investigated a variety of options including appetite stimulants, anti-anxiety drugs, antidepressants and atypical antipsychotics as well as a range of hormone-based treatments. Some of the most commonly used drugs are SSRIs and atypical antipsychotics.

Why use any drugs at all? As comorbid conditions (e.g. depression, anxiety, obsessive-compulsive disorder) are common in people with anorexia nervosa, this means that any medications that can be used to treat these symptoms may enable the person to benefit more from psychological therapies used to treat symptoms of anorexia nervosa. For example, a patient who is anxious about their weight gain, which is necessary to treat their eating disorder, is less likely to drop out of a therapy programme if their anxiety can be treated with drugs.

10.1 Appetite Stimulants（食欲刺激剂）

Restricted energy intake may result from poor appetite, meaning drugs that stimulate appetite may be helpful. Research has also suggested that such drugs may disrupt sleep and increase dysphoria.

💊 Andries et al. (2013) — Dronabinol RCT Double-blind RCT

In a double-blind randomised control trial, women with AN who took the appetite stimulant drug dronabinol gained an average of 0.73 kg more than the placebo group.

Caveat: non-compliance is extremely problematic in people with AN and drugs which increase appetite in many populations tend to be less effective with people with AN.

10.2 Antidepressants: Targeting Mood and Anxiety（抗抑郁药：针对情绪与焦虑）

Tricyclic antidepressants have not proved effective for people with AN (Frank et al., 2016), however there is some evidence that SSRIs can be more effective than placebos in terms of encouraging weight gain. However, larger controlled trials indicated that although SSRIs may have some impact on body dissatisfaction and mood, they did not significantly improve weight gain, weight maintenance and relapse prevention comparison with placebos (Frank, 2020).

⚖️ Ferguson et al. (1999) — SSRI Comparison Quasi-experiment

Compared 24 patients taking SSRIs and 16 patients treated on the same ward who were not taking SSRIs.

There was no significant difference between them in terms of age or body weight, but most importantly there was no difference in their clinical symptoms or self-reported anxiety. This suggests that the use of the drugs had no significant impact on their treatment outcomes.

🔁 Kaye et al. (2001) — Fluoxetine Double-blind Double-blind

Double-blind study comparing outcomes for patients given fluoxetine with patients given a placebo:

  • Those on fluoxetine were much more likely to stay on the medication up to a year into their outpatient treatment.
  • Those who continued taking fluoxetine had much lower relapse rates, measured by increased body weight and improvement in symptoms.

10.3 Atypical Antipsychotics: Targeting Distorted Body Image（非典型抗精神病药：针对扭曲的身体意象）

Distorted body image and beliefs about the lack of seriousness of weight loss often border on delusional, suggesting that antipsychotics may be a useful treatment. These drugs also often lead to significant weight gain in patients with schizophrenia, suggesting they may be an effective treatment for AN. However, results from various trials of the atypical antipsychotic olanzapine have been contradictory.

  • Occasionally, olanzapine has led to superior outcomes in comparison with placebos, such as faster and greater weight gain (e.g. Attia et al., 2019).
  • However, there have been many studies that have shown no significant difference between olanzapine and placebos (e.g. Kafantaris et al., 2011).
  • If drugs such as olanzapine are tried, it is important to remember that the first two months or so may be difficult as it can take this long for the drug to take effect and reported side-effects include hunger and weight gain, which are likely to lead to additional stress for many patients.

🧮 Maths Tip: Type II Error

In his review of drug treatments for anorexia nervosa, Guido Frank (2020) comments that one very small study (n = 10) showed 'no significant difference' in weight gain between the group taking an atypical antipsychotic called quetiapine and the placebo group (Powers et al., 2012). In studies with very small sample sizes, it is much more difficult to obtain a significant result. This does not necessarily mean that the genuine drug is no more effective than the placebo, just that there is insufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis. However, Frank (2020) states that the findings of this study may be an example of a type II error, meaning the null hypothesis was retained when it should have been rejected (i.e. the real drug really was more effective than the placebo). This is an example of a 'false negative'.

💡 打个比方理解 Type II error（假阴性）：就像手机信号差——对方其实给你发了消息（药物真的有效），但因为你这边信号弱（样本太小 n=10），消息没收到，你就以为对方没发（误判为"没有效果"）。"没收到消息" ≠ "对方没发消息"；同理，"没测出差异" ≠ "真的没差异"。小样本研究说"无显著差异"，很可能只是没本事测出来而已。

🧮 中文解释：Type II error（二类错误）= 假阴性——药物其实有效，但因为样本太小（n=10），统计检验没能达到显著水平，所以错误地保留了"药物无效"的零假设。这提醒我们：小样本研究的"不显著"不等于"无效果"。

10.4 Evaluation of Drug Therapy（药物治疗的评价）

When evaluating treatment and therapies, use REAP: Research evidence, Ethical issues, Alternatives and Practical issues.

  • Research evidence — what studies are there to support whether it works or not and is the evidence credible? For example, think — control group, sample size, blinding, follow up, etc.
  • Ethical issues — is the person receiving treatment in control of what happens to them, are they empowered or disempowered by the therapy? Are they and the therapist/doctor equal partners or does one person have more power over the other?
  • Alternatives — how does the therapy compare with other options? For example, how does drug therapy compare with CBT?
  • Practical issues — this might include financial costs attached to delivering treatment, (e.g. does it need to be delivered by qualified/trained professionals?), how long are the sessions, where will it take place, is it accessible, could it be offered online? Practical issues might also cover side-effects and other factors affecting compliance.

11. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy: CBT-E（认知行为疗法）

🎯 Quick Summary（本章要点）

  1. 定义: Enhanced CBT（Fairburn, 2003），一对一，20–40 次；专门针对进食障碍相关的想法与行为
  2. 四阶段: Starting well（快速改变+每周称重）→ Taking stock（回顾+个性化计划）→ 3 个维持因素（体型/饮食规则/事件情绪）→ Ending well（防复发+5 个月复查）
  3. 疗效: 非严重低体重者约 2/3 完全康复；Pike (2003) 复发率 CBT 22% vs 营养咨询 73%
  4. 局限: 需要动机；严重低体重者难完成；完美主义/低自尊/人际问题需用改良版
🧠 AO1 Zone — CBT-E（知识点区） describe & recall

Cognitive behavioural therapy was initially developed for use with people with mood disorders but has been adapted to tackle a range of disorders, including anorexia nervosa. Enhanced cognitive behaviour therapy (CBT-E) was developed by Christopher Fairburn, initially as a treatment for bulimia nervosa, but it was later adapted for use with all eating disorders (Fairburn, 2003). It specifically targets thoughts and behaviours associated with disordered eating behaviour. The therapy is conducted on a one-to-one basis and people will usually attend 20 to 40 sessions, depending on the severity of the disorder. It has been adapted for use with inpatients and outpatients as well as young people. However, every therapist will adapt the programme to suit the individual needs of their client.

11.1 Initial Assessment（初步评估）

Initially, a detailed interview takes place, usually over two separate sessions. This allows the therapist to assess the patient's suitability for treatment using CBT-E, at the current time. The therapist will also explain the treatment process and ask whether the person has any questions. For the therapy to be effective, any possible barriers to treatment must be removed at the outset. This means if there are other factors in the person's life that might affect the success of the treatment, these should be dealt with before therapy begins.

11.2 The Four Stages of CBT-E（CBT-E 的四个阶段）

Treatment takes place through four defined stages as shown in Table 23.1. However, for people with anorexia nervosa who need to gain weight to achieve an average BMI for their height, they will be encouraged to make the decision themselves to start making changes which will help them to gain weight rather than having a timetable imposed on them by the therapist.

Table 23.1: The Four Stages of Enhanced Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT-E)

StageDurationPreparation for treatment and change
1 Four weeks, with two sessions per week

To encourage rapid change in the client's behaviour; two important procedures are implemented in this phase:

  • weekly weighing with the therapist
  • regular eating; help to develop healthy eating habits

Clients are educated about the disorder, including explaining the risks of malnutrition and low body weight and providing information about good nutrition. They will also learn about the treatment programme so that they know what to expect, e.g. they will have to complete homework activities between sessions, including monitoring daily intake and associated thoughts and feelings, putting into practice advice and skills taught during sessions.

Together with the therapist, the person with AN will be helped to set goals, so that progress can be monitored over the coming weeks.

It is important during this first stage that the patient is positive about the treatment and motivated to progress because the therapy will only be effective if they are willing to make the necessary changes.
2 Two sessions, one week apart

The therapist and the person with AN will meet to discuss the progress made in stage one; good progress can be praised to boost motivation, poor progress can be discussed to uncover possible reasons for why things are not going well.

Together the therapist and person with AN will create a personalised treatment plan focusing on any factors which have maintained the person's eating problems. The plan can be modified at any point.
3 Eight sessions, once a week

This is the main stage of the treatment phase where the person with AN usually has to tackle the factors involved in maintaining their eating disorder. This will involve dealing with issues relating to body image, dietary rules, and any event-related changes in eating.

Issues relating to body image will be managed through behaviours likely to lead to body dissatisfaction, such as constant body checking, as well as the triggers that make the person 'feel fat'.

Dietary rules are explored to consider the impact that rigid and restrictive rules are having on the client's quality of life, and any foods they currently avoid are gradually introduced to the diet.

It is also important that external events that impact on major changes in eating are considered and tackled at this stage in treatment.

The therapist may use techniques such as cognitive restructuring, to help the person to change irrational beliefs about eating and food to become more objective and logical. They may challenge the person to test their beliefs between sessions in behavioural experiments.
4 Three sessions, about two weeks apart

Clients are encouraged to look to the future and consider factors that need to be managed to prevent relapse. The therapist and person with AN will draw up an agreed plan that is personalised for their specific circumstances. Individuals are also encouraged to consider their mindset so that they do not see any relapse as 'failure', and instead think of it as a 'lapse' that they can address. A post-treatment review appointment will then be made about five months later where the individual will be able to discuss any setbacks or issues.

11.3 Evaluation of CBT-E（CBT-E 的评价）

CBT-E is an evidence-based therapy, meaning it has been tested in various studies and consistently shown superior outcomes in comparison with control groups. The therapy is effective with adults from a variety of cultures including the UK, Australia, Denmark, Italy and the United States and also with young people (Dalle Grave et al., 2013). When CBT-E is delivered by well-trained therapists about two-thirds of people make a full recovery and results are maintained over time. However, this statistic applies only to people who are not significantly underweight although significant improvements can also be made by people with severe symptoms. However, this group is less likely to complete the full treatment programme.

📊 Pike et al. (2003) — CBT vs Nutritional Counselling Comparison

Compared the effectiveness of CBT and nutritional counselling as outpatient treatments given to people with anorexia nervosa following hospitalisation. In the 33 patients followed by the researchers, the relapse rates of those receiving CBT was considerably lower (22 per cent) than those receiving nutritional counselling (73 per cent).

🌏 More CBT-E Evidence Evidence
  • Byrne et al. (2011): CBT-E in an Australian outpatient clinic — two-thirds of patients showed significant improvement in symptoms (eating behaviour + general psychopathology).
  • Fairburn et al. (2015): demonstrated the superiority of CBT-E in comparison with interpersonal psychotherapy (IPT).
  • Poulsen et al. (2014): CBT-E was more effective than 100 hours of psychoanalytic therapy.

11.4 Limitations of CBT-E（CBT-E 的局限）

⚠️ Limitations

  • One possible issue related to the use of CBT-E is that the client has to be motivated to change, so this is a form of therapy that will not be effective for everyone.
  • The initial stage of the treatment does allow for the client to spend time learning about their disorder before they enter the full treatment phase so it will be important that the therapist recognises signs that the patient is not yet ready.
  • The benefit of any form of CBT is that it is flexible and can be adapted to suit the needs of the patient. For example, there is a version of CBT-E that is designed specifically for patients with extremely low body weight.
  • The most commonly used form of CBT-E is only effective for patients with anorexia nervosa whose eating disorder is not maintained by clinical perfectionism, low self-esteem or interpersonal problems. However, there are other forms of CBT-E that can be used with these patient groups and this can be identified early on in the treatment process.
  • The strategies taught in CBT-E are probably more suited to treating older patients with anorexia nervosa who have more opportunity to access treatment independently and who may be living away from their family. For patients who still live at home, family therapy may be more effective as it deals with the effects that anorexia nervosa can have on the family, not just the individual.
Eval: motivation barrier · severely underweight drop-out · perfectionism/low-esteem need adapted CBT-E · older patients only

12. Checkpoint & Exam Practice（检测与考试练习）

📝 Checkpoint（知识检测）

  1. What are the three main symptoms of anorexia nervosa?
    Restricted energy intake leading to low body weight; intense fear of gaining weight; disturbance in body weight/shape perception or lack of recognition of seriousness.
  2. What are the two subtypes of anorexia nervosa?
    Restricting type and binge-purging type.
  3. What is the BMI quoted in the ICD-11 that would mean someone was classified as underweight?
    Less than 18.5 in adults; or under fifth percentile in children/adolescents.
  4. What does the EPHX2 gene code for and why is this a candidate gene for anorexia nervosa?
    Epoxide hydrolase 2, an enzyme that breaks down cholesterol; disruption may be linked to AN.
  5. Which two words did Bandura (1977) use to explain why some behaviours are imitated and not others?
    Prevalence and incentives.
  6. How do the findings of Hoek et al. (2005) in Curaçao differ from those of Becker et al. (2002) in Fiji?
    Hoek: AN virtually absent in indigenous Curaçao where overweight is valued, despite Western TV exposure; Becker: Fijian girls developed disordered eating after Western TV introduced thin ideals.
  7. Why are drug treatments not recommended as a primary treatment for anorexia nervosa?
    Physical risks when seriously underweight; side-effects may outweigh benefits; NICE guidelines.
  8. Which drugs were better than placebos in increasing weight gain?
    SSRIs (some evidence) and appetite stimulant dronabinol (+0.73 kg); olanzapine results are contradictory.
  9. What happens in stage 2 of CBT-E?
    Review progress and create a personalised treatment plan.
  10. Who might not benefit from CBT-E?
    People not motivated to change; those with extremely low body weight; those whose AN is maintained by clinical perfectionism, low self-esteem or interpersonal problems (unless adapted CBT-E used).

🎯 Exam Practice（考试练习）

Q1. Explain one strength of one biological explanation for your chosen disorder. (2 marks)

Model Answer：

One strength of the genetic explanation of anorexia nervosa is that it is supported by twin-study evidence. Yilmaz et al. (2015) reported heritability estimates between 48 and 74 per cent, suggesting a strong genetic component. This is a strength because the high concordance, especially in monozygotic twins, is difficult to explain purely by shared environment. (1 mark for study detail + 1 mark for why it supports)

Q2. Describe one non-biological explanation for your chosen mental health disorder. (4 marks)

Model Answer（Social Learning Theory）：

One non-biological explanation of anorexia nervosa is social learning theory. Bandura (1977) suggested that people learn behaviours through observation and imitation of role models. Young people may observe attractive celebrities, influencers or models who display thin body ideals and receive rewards such as fame, likes and social admiration. This provides vicarious reinforcement for dieting and excessive exercise. The concepts of prevalence (how often the behaviour is seen) and incentives (anticipated rewards) help explain why thinness-promoting media can establish body-size norms. For example, Harrison and Cantor (1997) found that women's magazines were a stronger predictor of disordered eating than television. (AO1: up to 4 marks for clear description of observation, imitation, role models, vicarious reinforcement and application to AN)

Q3. Zainab is a cognitive behavioural therapist. She is designing a study to assess the effectiveness of CBT for treating people with your chosen mental health disorder.

(a) Suggest one way that Zainab could collect quantitative data in her study and one way that she could collect qualitative data. (4 marks)

Model Answer：

  • Quantitative: Measure BMI or weight change before and after CBT-E using a standard scale; calculate mean weight gain and use a t-test to compare groups. (1 mark method + 1 mark linked to CBT/AN)
  • Qualitative: Conduct semi-structured interviews asking participants about their experience of CBT-E stages, such as challenging dietary rules; analyse themes. (1 mark method + 1 mark linked to CBT/AN)

(b) To get ideas for her research, Zainab looks at an online forum where people have posted comments about their experiences of CBT; she decides to analyse some of the qualitative data that she finds there. Suggest one way that Zainab could analyse the online comments about CBT. (2 marks)

Model Answer：

Zainab could use thematic analysis. She would familiarise herself with the comments, generate initial codes, search for themes, review and define themes, and then write up patterns such as "barriers to completing CBT-E" or "motivational changes". (1 mark for named method + 1 mark for brief description)
