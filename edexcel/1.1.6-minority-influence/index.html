1.1.6 MINORITY INFLUENCE
How a few people change what everyone thinks · 少数派影响 · Moscovici (1976)

Social Psychology · Majorities keep society running. Minorities change where it is going.
🧠 Core idea: behavioural style beats numbers 🔁 Ends in internalisation 📖 Textbook: Chapter 3

What this section is really asking

  1. Majority influence keeps social order; minority influence is how social change happens — a few people rejecting the established norms and pulling the majority towards them.
  2. Minorities have no power in numbers, so their influence comes from their behavioural style: above all consistency, supported by flexibility.
  3. It works through informational social influence and ends in internalisation — people genuinely come to believe the new view, not just go along with it.
  4. It is slow: the process of conversion is a gradual shift in majority opinion, seen in feminism, civil rights and environmental campaigns.

1. Getting Started

Picture the first day a classmate — on their own — starts carrying a reusable water bottle and picking up litter that nobody asked them to touch. For weeks, nothing changes. Some people smirk. Then a second person joins. Then a third. A term later, half the class does it, and the school puts recycling bins in the corridor.

Nobody ordered anyone to change. There was no punishment for ignoring it. And yet the behaviour of the whole group moved — because a small number of people kept doing the same thing, in the same way, for a long time.

The question this chapter answers

We already know how majorities pressure individuals (Asch). But history shows the opposite happening: individuals and small groups changing the whole of society. How does a minority — with no numbers, no authority, no punishment — ever win?

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

Think of a big real-world change — recycling, seatbelts, civil rights. What made the early campaigners (the minority) effective?

The answer is the middle one — and it is the whole topic in a sentence. Minorities do not win by being big. They win by how they behave.

中文理解：为什么少数派能赢？
多数派影响（majority influence）靠的是人多——你不从众就不合群；
少数派影响（minority influence）靠的是行为风格——你说的观点始终如一（consistency），别人就会开始想：「他们是不是真的有道理？」
一句话：多数派让人「不得不跟」，少数派让人「想了以后愿意跟」。

2. Two Directions of Influence

Every kind of social influence pushes in one of two directions, and each direction does a different job for society.

Majority influence

Keeps social order running. People accept the established norms so that groups and institutions function — queueing, following rules, agreeing on how to behave. You have met it in Asch's line-judging task.

Minority influence

Creates social change. Throughout history, minorities have challenged the established social order — and those challenges are how societies move: feminism, civil rights, the 'right to education', environmental change.

Notice the symmetry. Both are forms of social influence; both can change a person's behaviour and beliefs. What differs is who is doing the pushing — and that difference changes everything about how the pushing works.

中文理解：两个箭头，两个结果
左图：多数 → 个人，大家把你「压」进来，维持秩序（Asch 讲的就是这个）。
右图：少数 → 多数，几个人把整个群体「拉」过去，产生变化（本章讲的就是这个）。
考试如果问「minority influence 和 majority influence 有什么不同」，先答方向，再答机制（人多 vs 行为风格）。

3. What Minority Influence Is

minority influence（少数派影响）
A form of social influence where people reject the established norms of the majority and move towards the behaviour and opinions of a minority group.

Read the definition slowly, because every clause is doing work:

Part of the definition
What it means
Example
reject the established norms
The majority's current way of thinking is treated as the "normal" one — the minority steps away from it.
In 1950s America, racial segregation was the norm. Civil-rights protesters rejected it publicly.
move towards the minority
Members of the majority actually shift — their behaviour and opinions start to match the minority's.
Ten years later, equal-rights laws pass and attitudes have moved with them.

Who said it, and when

Serge Moscovici (1976) is the name attached to this theory. His argument: by definition minorities do not have power in numbers — so their influence must be explained by their behavioural style. Successful minorities show consistent conviction in their views.

中文理解：名字要挂在 Moscovici 身上
考试写「Explain minority influence」时，第一句就点名：Moscovici (1976) argued that…。
核心逻辑只有一句：少数派没有「人多」这个武器，所以只能靠「行为风格」（behavioural style）——尤其是始终坚持同一立场（consistency）。

4. The Engine Room: Where the Power Comes From

Two facts work together to explain how the trick is done — one about why people listen, one about how deeply they change.

Fact 1: it is informational, not normative

You already know the two channels of social influence from Asch's topic. Minority influence travels through informational social influence (ISI): the minority provides information — a viewpoint, an argument, a way of seeing the issue — that the majority starts to take seriously. Nobody is afraid of being excluded by two campaigners; they listen because the message seems worth listening to.

Fact 2: the result is internalisation

Because people accept the information as true, the outcome is internalisation — the deepest form of conformity, where the belief becomes part of the person. They carry it with them even when the minority is not watching, and they will hold it in private as well as in public.

Compliance (recall)

Go along publicly, disagree privately. Shallow change. That is what majority pressure usually buys (NSI).

Internalisation

Public and private acceptance. Deep change. This is what minority influence produces, because people are convinced by information.

🎯 Quick check No notes

Why does minority influence end in internalisation rather than mere compliance?

中文理解：信息 → 真信 → 内化
少数派改变你，靠的不是吓你（NSI），而是说服你（ISI）：给你一个你以前没想到的角度。
你一旦觉得「他说得有道理」，这个观点就住进了你脑子里——这就是 internalisation（内化）：当人不在场时你也照样这么认为。
连起来记：少数派 → ISI → internalisation，三个词是一条链。

5. Style 1: Consistency

This is the headline factor — the one Moscovici's name is tied to.

consistency（一致性）
The minority keeps saying and doing the same thing — the same viewpoint, maintained over time, by all members of the minority. Successful minorities show consistent conviction in their views.

Why does sameness create influence? Two reasons:

Mechanism 1 · It signals certainty

If the campaigners never waver — same message in week one and week fifty — observers start to think: they must really believe this. Maybe they know something we don't.

Mechanism 2 · It creates doubt

A consistent challenge makes the majority's own position look shakier by comparison. People begin to re-examine a view they had never questioned before.

This is why big social movements could not have happened without it: such change required a consistent viewpoint being established and maintained, and minority individuals being dedicated to their cause — showing up again and again, year after year.

中文理解：重复本身就是说服力
第一次听到有人说「要回收」，你觉得多事；第十次、第一百次听到不同的人说同样的话，你就会开始想「是不是真有问题」。
consistency 的本质：把「一个奇怪的想法」重复成「一个值得认真对待的想法」。
注意 dedication 一词：投入/坚持也常和 consistency 一起考——既相同又坚持，缺一不可。

6. Style 2: Flexibility

Consistency alone is not enough — and here is the subtlety students miss. Consistency carried to extremes backfires.

flexibility（灵活性）
Minorities are rarely effective unless they show flexibility in their viewpoint and are not rigid in their beliefs — accepting alternative opinions and negotiating change.
Minority style
How the majority sees them
Result
Rigid & uncompromising
We perceive an uncompromising minority as extreme — dogmatic, unreasonable, not worth engaging with.
Less likely to be believed; the message is dismissed with the messenger.
Flexible & accepting
Viewed as less extreme and more reasonable — people who will listen as well as talk.
Their viewpoint is more likely to be considered — and a flexible style can cast doubt or uncertainty over the beliefs of the majority, as change is negotiated.

Hold both at once

The best-argued minority is consistent about its core aim but flexible about how to get there — never moving the destination, but willing to negotiate the road. That combination is what makes a minority hard to dismiss.

中文理解：像谈判，不像呐喊
想象和父母谈判要养猫：
死硬派：「必须养！不养绝食！」→ 父母觉得你偏激，话题终结。
灵活派：「我很想养。不过可以先试寄养两周，我负责铲屎，费用我出。」→ 目标没变（要猫），姿态有弹性（让步方案），父母反而开始认真考虑。
少数派的秘诀：终点坚定，路线灵活。

7. The Process of Conversion

Minority influence is rarely a quick process. Nobody wakes up one morning having been converted by two campaigners. What actually happens is slower — and it has a name.

conversion（转化）
The process where individuals in the majority group gradually come to accept the minority viewpoint — a gradual shift in majority opinion.

That final stage matters most. When the two lines meet, the majority hasn't just copied a behaviour — the belief itself has moved. That is why the endpoint of minority influence is internalisation, and why changes it produces tend to stick even when the original campaigners are long gone.

中文理解：温水式的改变
conversion（转化）的关键词是 gradual（渐进）：从「这想法好怪」→「好像也有点道理」→「他们是对的」。
类比烧水：不是点火就开，而是温度一格一格爬升，某个时刻突然沸腾——社会观念的变化也是这样，看起来「突然」，其实一直是 minorities 在持续加热。

8. Social Change: The Theory in the Wild

Every major social movement the textbook names follows the same script. Check each one against the theory:

Movement
Started as…
The minority style on display
Feminism
A small group arguing for rights the majority considered settled.
Consistent message maintained for decades; flexible tactics (petitions, marches, legal test cases) negotiating each step.
Civil rights
Activists rejecting segregation, an established norm.
Dedication sustained through years of resistance; consistency made the moral claim impossible to keep ignoring.
'Right to education'

Environmental change
A few scientists and campaigners on carbon, recycling and food waste.

Notice what all four have in common: the change could not have occurred without a consistent viewpoint being established and maintained, and individuals dedicated to the cause. That sentence is worth memorising — it is the theory applied, not just described.

中文理解：把「理论」套到「新闻」上
考试若要求举例说明 minority influence，四个现成例子任选：feminism、civil rights、right to education、environmental change。
答题公式："This change could not have occurred without a consistent viewpoint being established and maintained." —— 一句话同时覆盖 consistency + dedication 两个考点。

9. Evidence 1: Moscovici et al. (1969)

The theory has direct experimental support from the man who proposed it.

The study in brief

Moscovici et al. (1969) conducted a series of experiments to investigate minority influence on a colour perception judgement task. The task involved participants in a majority being swayed by incorrect judgements made by a minority in the group. They found that minorities can exert some influence over majority opinion — particularly if the minority were consistent in their viewpoint.

Three things to notice about the design, because each one is doing theoretical work:

Design choice
Why it matters here
Colour judgement task
A simple perception task — answers are easy to check, so if the majority starts agreeing with the minority's wrong answers, that shows influence at work.
Minority gives wrong answers
The influence is measured directly: agreeing with answers known to be wrong means the minority has moved the majority.
Consistency is the headline
Chapter 3's key finding: minorities exerted influence particularly if they were consistent. (The full procedure — including the consistent vs inconsistent condition — appears in the textbook's study section on pp. 59–62.)

📖 Link: the full procedure and results of Moscovici et al. (1969) — the classic study for this topic — get their own treatment when we build the 1.3.1 study page. For the theory, remember the headline: consistent minorities move majorities.

中文理解：这个实验在「证明」什么
颜色判断题的答案本来毫无争议——所以如果多数人开始跟着少数人给出明显错误的答案，唯一的解释就是被影响了。
而「坚持重复同一答案的少数派」影响最大——这正是 consistency 的直接证据。

10. Evidence 2: Nemeth & Brilmayer (1987)

The second study tests the other half of the behavioural style: flexibility.

The study in brief

Nemeth and Brilmayer (1987) used a mock jury study. 72 male students were asked to decide how much compensation to award a victim of a ski accident due to the negligence of a ski company. When a compensation offer suggested by a minority was consistent and uncompromising, it was less effective than when the offer was negotiated.

Question
Answer from the study
Who?
72 male students, acting as a mock jury.
Deciding what?
The amount of compensation for a ski-accident victim injured through the ski company's negligence.
What varied?
The minority's style: an unchanging, uncompromising offer versus an offer that was negotiated — adjusted in response to the discussion.
Finding?
The negotiated (flexible) offer was more effective than the rigid one. Flexibility beat stubbornness.

This is direct support for the idea that minority flexibility is an important factor in social influence — the courtroom version of "you catch more flies with honey".

中文理解：两个实验各管一半理论
Moscovici (1969)：颜色任务 → 证明 consistency 有效。
Nemeth & Brilmayer (1987)：模拟陪审团 → 证明 flexibility（会谈判）比死顶更有效。
两个一起记，evaluation 就有两个现成的 named study 可写。

11. Who Listens to a Minority?

Minority influence does not work equally on everyone. The textbook points to a third layer of the explanation — the audience.

Minority influence can be affected by individual differences and culture. Some individuals and cultures are more or less likely to be swayed by the opinions of others. And whether we accept a minority viewpoint also depends on whether we identify with the minority group:

social identity theory & the ingroup（社会认同理论与内群体）
According to social identity theory, we are more likely to identify with a minority if they form part of our ingroup — the group we feel we belong to. A minority that feels like "people like us" gets a hearing; one that feels like "people like them" often doesn't.

Why this matters for your exam

This sentence is a bridge. It tells you that the style of the minority (consistency, flexibility) is only half the story — the characteristics of the audience (personality, situation, culture) do the rest. That is exactly the content of the next page, 1.1.7 Factors affecting conformity and minority influence.

中文理解：同一句话，不同人听，效果不同
同样的环保倡议，本校环保社团的学长说 → 你觉得「自己人」说的，愿意听；陌生城市的陌生人说 → 你可能无感。
ingroup（内群体）= 「我们这一边的」。少数派如果能让你觉得「他和我们是一伙的」，说服就成功了一半。
这一条连接了 1.1.6（少数派说什么）和 1.1.7（听的人是谁、在什么情境、来自什么文化）。

12. Evaluation · Strengths

Strength 1 · Experimental support for consistency（一致性有实验直接支持）

Moscovici et al. (1969) found that minorities can exert some influence over majority opinion, particularly if the minority were consistent. Because the task was colour perception, an area with a clear right answer, the shift towards the minority's incorrect judgements is strong evidence that behavioural style, not ambiguity, did the persuading.

Strength 2 · Experimental support for flexibility（灵活性有实验直接支持）

Nemeth and Brilmayer (1987) found that a consistent but uncompromising compensation offer was less effective than a negotiated one. This is evidence that minority flexibility matters in social influence, and it shows the theory is more than "be stubborn for long enough".

Strength 3 · Explains real social change（能解释真实社会变革）

Movements such as feminism, civil rights, the right to education and environmental change all began with a minority viewpoint, and all required a consistent position maintained by dedicated individuals. The theory's predictions match how change actually happens: gradually, through conversion, ending in internalisation.

Strength 4 · Fits the deeper channel of influence（解释改变为何持久）

Because minority influence operates through informational social influence, its end point, internalisation, explains why converted majorities keep the new belief in private and pass it on, rather than snapping back the moment the minority stops watching.

中文理解：四条优势其实是一条线
两个实验各证一半（consistency / flexibility）→ 理论又正好解释真实历史（社会运动）→ 结果层面还说得通（内化 = 改变持久）。
AO3 写的时候记住配对：consistency ↔ Moscovici (1969)，flexibility ↔ Nemeth & Brilmayer (1987)，别张冠李戴。

13. Evaluation · Limitations

Limitation 1 · Narrow, artificial evidence base（证据基础窄且人工）

The two supporting studies used contrived situations: judging coloured slides, awarding hypothetical compensation in a mock jury. Real social influence happens over months and years, in messy public debates. A colour-slide task cannot capture decades of campaigning, so caution is needed when generalising from the lab to society.

Limitation 2 · Effect depends on the audience（效果看听众）

Minority influence is affected by individual differences and culture: some people and some cultures are simply more or less likely to be swayed. Acceptance also depends on ingroup identification. The same consistent, flexible minority will succeed in front of one audience and fail in front of another.

Limitation 3 · Consistency and flexibility pull against each other（坚持与灵活互相拉扯）

The theory asks the minority to hold firm and bend at the same time. Nemeth and Brilmayer's finding shows why this is delicate: uncompromising consistency was less effective. A minority that bends too far may lose the very consistency that earned it attention.

Limitation 4 · Slow process, hard to study（过程太慢难研究）

Conversion is by definition gradual, and real-world change takes years. Experiments can compress consistency into a single session, but they cannot compress decades. This makes the theory's long-term claims harder to test directly in a laboratory.

中文理解：三条限制各打一个点
① 实验太「人工」（颜色滑片、模拟陪审团）→ 不敢直接推广到几十年真实社会运动；
② 效果看听众（个体差异、文化、ingroup）→ 同样的少数派换个观众就失灵；
③ 坚持与灵活本来就互相拉扯 → 太硬被当偏激，太软丢掉一致性。
加上「过程太慢难研究」，四条正好凑成一组能写的 AO3。

Learn it as a unit — it contains the definition, the process and the required style all at once.

The 8-mark paragraph that usually scores

AO1 (about 4 marks). Minority influence (Moscovici, 1976) is a form of social influence in which people reject the established norms of the majority and move towards the behaviour and opinions of a minority group. Because minorities lack power in numbers, their influence is explained by their behavioural style: they show consistent conviction in their views — the same viewpoint established and maintained by dedicated individuals — supported by flexibility, since a rigid minority is perceived as extreme. It operates through informational social influence and results in internalisation of the minority's beliefs. 16. Exam Lab

Question · The recycling campaign (8 marks)

A small group of students starts a campaign to reduce food waste in their school. Most students ignore them at first, but the group keeps raising the same arguments at every assembly, month after month, and is willing to negotiate practical steps like smaller portion options in the canteen. Within a year, food waste has fallen and most students say they believe reducing waste matters. Discuss minority influence, using the recycling campaign as an example. (8 marks)

AO1 4 marks · AO3 4 marks. Use the campaign in your answer — a generic essay scores lower.

Model answer

AO1. Minority influence (Moscovici, 1976) is a form of social influence in which people reject the established norms of the majority and move towards the behaviour and opinions of a minority group. Since minorities lack power in numbers, their influence comes from their behavioural style: consistent conviction in their viewpoint, maintained over time by dedicated individuals, together with flexibility so they are seen as reasonable rather than extreme. Minority influence operates through informational social influence, so it results in individuals internalising the new beliefs — accepting them publicly and privately. It is rarely quick: through the process of conversion, members of the majority gradually come to accept the minority viewpoint.

AO3. The campaign displays exactly this style: the students keep raising the same arguments (consistency) yet negotiate practical steps such as smaller portions (flexibility), and the outcome — most students privately believing waste matters — is internalisation, not mere compliance. This fits Moscovici et al. (1969), where a consistent minority swayed a majority's colour-perception judgements, and Nemeth and Brilmayer (1987), where a negotiated offer was more effective than an uncompromising one. However, both studies used artificial tasks, so caution is needed when applying them to a year-long real campaign; and acceptance also depends on the audience — students may comply because the campaigners are part of their ingroup, so individual differences and culture, not style alone, helped the minority succeed.

17. Key Terms

minority influence 少数派影响
A form of social influence where people reject the established norms of the majority and move towards the behaviour and opinions of a minority group.
behavioural style 行为风格
The way a minority acts — its consistency, dedication and flexibility. Since minorities lack numbers, style is their source of power.
consistency 一致性
Keeping the same viewpoint over time and across members; consistent conviction makes the majority doubt its own position.
dedication 投入与坚持
Commitment to the cause over the long term, shown by minority individuals keeping the campaign alive.
flexibility 灵活性
Being willing to accept alternative opinions and negotiate change; a rigid minority is perceived as extreme and less believable.
conversion 转化
The gradual process by which individuals in the majority group come to accept the minority viewpoint.
internalisation 内化
Public and private acceptance of a belief — the deepest form of conformity, and the typical end point of minority influence.
informational social influence 信息性社会影响
Changing because the source provides information or argument that seems true — the channel through which minorities persuade.
social change 社会变革
Large-scale shifts in society's norms, begun by minorities — e.g. feminism, civil rights, environmental change.
mock jury 模拟陪审团
A research set-up where participants act as jurors in a fictional case, used by Nemeth and Brilmayer (1987).
social identity theory 社会认同理论
The theory that we derive identity from group membership; we more readily accept a minority that forms part of our ingroup.
ingroup 内群体
The group a person feels they belong to ("us"), as opposed to the outgroup ("them").
