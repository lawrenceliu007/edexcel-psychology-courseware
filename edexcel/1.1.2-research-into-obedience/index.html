1.1.2 RESEARCH INTO OBEDIENCE
服从的研究

Social Psychology · Milgram (1963) 基线研究 + 三个变式实验（7 / 10 / 13）
🧠 Core idea: the situation, not the person ⚡ 65% to 450 V 📖 Textbook: Chapter 1

1. Getting Started

⚡ You are in the chair Think first

You have volunteered for what you were told is a study about memory. You are the teacher. Every time the learner in the next room gets an answer wrong, you press a switch that gives him an electric shock — and each wrong answer means a slightly bigger shock than the last one.

By now he has shouted that the shocks hurt, then screamed that he wants to stop, then said his heart is bothering him. At the next wrong answer you reach 300 volts — and he starts banging on the wall and demanding to be let out.

A man in a grey lab coat stands beside you and says: “The experiment requires that you continue.”

中文理解
先看清楚这个场景里没有什么：没有人拿枪指着你，没有威胁，没有好处可拿。只有一个人，穿着白大褂，说了一句话。
1963 年，真有 40 个人坐在这把椅子上。这就是本章要讲的研究。它想回答的问题是：一个普通人，会不会仅仅因为被命令，就去伤害另一个人？

2. Why Milgram Did It

Stanley Milgram was a social psychologist at Yale University. He began his study in 1961 — sixteen years after the end of the Second World War.

The question behind the study

During the Nuremberg trials, Nazi officers accused of mass murder used the same defence again and again: “I was only following orders.” The world’s reaction was that this was an excuse — that the people who did these things must have been unusually cruel, or mentally ill, or somehow a different kind of person.

Milgram was not convinced. He wanted to test a different idea: that ordinary, mentally healthy people would obey orders to hurt a stranger, and that the cause lay in the situation rather than in their personality.

Two words you must not confuse

TermWhat it means
obedience（服从）Following a direct order from someone perceived to have authority.
conformity（从众）Changing your behaviour to match a majority or group — no order is given. Covered in 1.1.4–1.1.7.

In Milgram’s study there is no group to match and no vote to join. There is one man giving one order. That is why it is obedience.

为什么这一点会决定整章？
如果服从来自人格（personality），那么只有“某种人”才会做坏事。如果服从来自情境（situation），那么任何人都可能 —— 只要情境对。
Milgram 的三个变式实验就是为了证明第二种：他不停改情境，看服从会不会跟着变。

3. Nobody Predicted It

Before running the study, Milgram described the procedure to 40 psychiatrists — doctors whose job is to understand disturbed behaviour — and asked them how far participants would go.

🔮 Your prediction Guess first

There are 40 participants. The machine goes up to 450 volts, labelled “Danger: severe shock” and then “XXX”. The victim has been screaming and has stopped responding at all. How many do you think will go all the way to 450 V?

moral strain（道德压力） — the distress a person feels when what they are told to do clashes with what they believe is right. In Milgram’s terms it is the conflict between obeying the authority and avoiding harm to the victim.
中文理解
精神科医生预测的比例是 千分之一 —— 也就是 40 个人里，大概 0 个。
记住这个预测，因为它说明了 Milgram 的真正发现：连专家都低估了情境的力量。评价题里写“专家预测与实际结果差距巨大”是很好的一条。

4. How It Worked

Seven steps. Read them in order — this is the version you have to be able to describe in the exam.

Figure 1 — the seven steps of the baseline study. Memorise the sequence, not the wording.

Four details examiners look for

  • 40 male participants from the New Haven area, recruited by newspaper advert and paid $4.50 for turning up.
  • The learner was a confederate — an actor, Mr Wallace, working with Milgram. He received no shocks at all.
  • The draw was fixed, so the real participant was always the teacher. This is deception.
  • The experimenter wore a grey lab coat and gave no threats and no rewards — only verbal prods.

5. The Shock Machine

The generator had 30 switches, from 15 V up to 450 V in 15 V steps. Crucially, the switches were not just numbers — they carried written labels, so participants knew how serious each level was supposed to be.

Voltage rangeLabel on the panelWhy it matters
15–60 VSlight shockParticipants were given a real 45 V sample so the machine felt genuine.
75–120 VModerate shockThe learner begins to grunt and then complains.
135–180 VStrong shockHe demands to be released — the first clear refusal.
195–240 VVery strong shockScreaming begins.
255–300 VIntense shock300 V: he bangs on the wall and then stops responding.
315–360 VExtreme intensity shockSilence from the learner — no answer, no protest.
375–420 VDanger: severe shockContinuing past this point is continuing against a written warning.
435–450 VXXXThe top of the scale. 450 V is the maximum — and the number in the results.
中文理解：机器是“道具”，但学生答题时最容易写错的是它
三件事必须写准：① 30 个开关；② 每档 15 伏；③ 最高 450 伏。
很多学生写“电击是真的” —— 错。电击是假的，只有那一下 45 伏的“样品电击”是真的，用来让参与者相信机器。学习者（Mr Wallace）是演员，从没受过电击。

6. What the Learner Said

The learner’s reactions were scripted — every participant heard exactly the same sequence at exactly the same voltages. This is what makes the study a controlled comparison.

VoltageWhat the teacher heard
75 VA grunt.
120 VShouts that the shocks were becoming painful.
150 V“Get me out of here! I refuse to go on. My heart is starting to bother me.”
180 V“I can’t stand the pain!”
270 VAn agonised scream.
300 VRefuses to answer; bangs on the wall and demands to be released.
315 V onwardSilence. No further answers and no further protests.

🔇 The hardest moment Check yourself

After which voltage did the learner stop responding completely — so the teacher heard nothing at all, not even a protest?

为什么“沉默”比“尖叫”更可怕？
尖叫至少还说明那个人活着、在反应。300 伏之后什么都没有了 —— 参与者完全不知道隔壁是晕过去了，还是更糟。
考试里写这个细节，能说明参与者是在极大的不确定和不安下继续按开关的，而不是因为麻木。

7. The Four Prods

When a participant hesitated or said they wanted to stop, the experimenter replied with one of four standard prods, always in this order. He was not allowed to add anything else.

OrderThe prodWhat is doing the work
1“Please continue.” / “Please go on.”A polite request.
2“The experiment requires that you continue.”The study needs it — not the man personally.
3“It is absolutely essential that you continue.”Escalation, but still no threat.
4“You have no other choice, you must go on.”The strongest prod. After this, refusal ended the session.

Two things students get wrong

Wrong: “Milgram ordered them or threatened them.” — He did not. There was no punishment for stopping and no reward for continuing.

Wrong: “They could not leave.” — They could. The door was not locked, the money was already theirs, and 14 of the 40 did stop. The pressure was entirely verbal.

中文理解
把四句话读一遍，你会发现它们没有一句是威胁。没有“不然我报警”，没有“不给钱”。
这正是 Milgram 最想证明的：服从不需要暴力，只需要一个被认可的权威在旁边持续说话。

8. What Happened

100% reached 300 V

Every single one of the 40 participants went at least as far as the point where the learner banged on the wall.

65% reached 450 V

26 of the 40 went to the very top of the scale, past “Danger: severe shock” to “XXX”.

35% defied

14 participants refused to complete the series. Five stopped at 300 V, four at 315 V.

368 V average

The mean maximum shock delivered was around 368 V — well into the danger zone.

1.2% predicted

The psychiatrists had estimated that barely one person in a thousand would reach 450 V.

What Milgram concluded

Ordinary people will follow the orders of an authority figure even when the orders conflict with their own conscience. The behaviour was produced by the situation — the setting, the lab coat, the prestigous university and the step-by-step escalation — and not by any unusual cruelty in the participants.

中文理解：三个数字必须背
100% 到 300 伏 · 65%（26/40）到 450 伏 · 35%（14/40）反抗。
答题时不要只写“很多人服从”，要写具体数字。Edexcel 的评分标准奖励具体证据。

9. Moral Strain

The important part is how participants obeyed. Milgram recorded that they did not obey calmly.

Signs of distress observed

  • Sweating, trembling and stuttering
  • Biting their lips until they bled
  • Digging their fingernails into their own flesh
  • Nervous laughing fits
  • Three participants had full-blown, uncontrollable seizures

Why this detail wins marks

It shows they were not indifferent or sadistic. They were caught in a conflict between two pressures — obey the authority and stop hurting the victim. That conflict is exactly what the term moral strain names, and it is the reason obedience was so uncomfortable for them.

中文理解
如果参与者都面无表情地按到底，那 Milgram 的结论会是“有些人天生残忍”。
但他们出汗、发抖、咬破嘴唇 —— 说明他们不想做，却停不下来。这就是 moral strain：心里知道不对，手却继续按。这直接支持“情境压过了良心”的说法。

10. Did They Believe It?

The criticism: demand characteristics

Orne and Holland (1968) argued that participants did not really believe the shocks were real. If they guessed the learner was an actor, then their behaviour was not obedience at all — it was going along with what they thought the researcher wanted. That is demand characteristics（需求特征）: cues in a study that tell participants what is expected of them.

Evidence they DID believe it

  • Participants showed unmistakable distress — trembling, sweating, seizures.
  • In post-experiment interviews, most said they believed the shocks were real.
  • 84% said they were glad to have taken part; only about 1% regretted it.
  • A follow-up a year later found no lasting psychological harm.

Evidence some did NOT

  • Re-analysis of Milgram’s archived tape recordings (Perry, 2013) found that a number of participants openly questioned whether the shocks were real during the session.
  • Some hesitated in ways that suggest they were testing the experimenter rather than obeying him.
中文理解：答题怎么写才不偏？
不要只写“研究无效”或“研究完全真实”。最好的写法是两边都写，再给判断：
“有批评者认为参与者看穿了（Orne & Holland），但参与者的痛苦反应和事后访谈支持他们相信了电击。Perry (2013) 重新听录音带后发现部分人确实怀疑过 —— 因此这个争议到今天没有完全解决。”

11. Was It Ethical?

Milgram’s study is the most famous ethics debate in psychology. You need both sides.

Ethical issueWhat happenedHow Milgram defended it
Deception（欺骗）The draw was fixed; the learner was an actor; the shocks were fake.Deception was necessary — without it there is no obedience to study.
Informed consent（知情同意）Participants did not know the real aim and could not truly consent.Presumptive consent: Milgram asked other people whether they would agree to take part; most said yes.
Right to withdraw（退出权）The four prods made stopping feel difficult.Participants could leave, and 14 of them did.
Protection from harm（免受伤害）Severe distress; three seizures.Full debriefing, a reunion with the unharmed learner, and a one-year follow-up showing no lasting harm.

What happened in the debriefing

Afterwards, each participant was told the truth, introduced to Mr Wallace to see he was unharmed, and interviewed about the experience. Milgram reported that 84% were glad to have participated and only around 1% regretted it. A questionnaire a year later found no evidence of long-term harm.

Exam habit. When a question asks you to “evaluate” Milgram, an ethical point on its own scores little. You must say why it matters: e.g. “the deception means we cannot be certain participants were responding to real obedience rather than demand characteristics, which weakens the conclusion.”

12. The Plan Behind the Variations

Milgram ran more than thirty variations of the same basic procedure. The specification asks you to know three of them — Experiments 7, 10 and 13.

中文理解：这一节是整章的骨架，先建立坐标
别把三个变式当成三个独立的故事去背。它们其实是同一个实验，每次拆掉一样东西：
权威靠三条腿站着 —— 在不在场（presence）、有没有名望（prestige）、是不是真权威（legitimacy）。
Milgram 的做法是：一次抽掉一条腿，看服从会不会塌。
Figure 2 — one study, three removals. Each variation takes away one thing that made the authority powerful.

13. Experiment 7 · Telephonic Instructions

What changed

The experimenter left the room and gave the orders over a telephone from another room. Everything else — the machine, the learner, the prods — stayed exactly the same. 40 participants.

Result: 20.5%

Only about 9 of the 40 participants went all the way to 450 V — down from 65% in the baseline.

Something else happened

Several participants cheated: they gave shocks smaller than they were told to, or claimed to be following instructions when they were not.

What it shows

Take the authority figure out of the room and obedience drops by two thirds — even though the words spoken are identical.

中文理解：作弊行为反而证明了结论
有趣的是：当实验者看不见他们在做什么时，很多人偷偷不按要求按 —— 说明他们在乎学习者，只是不敢当面违抗。
考试里这是一条很漂亮的证据：它说明参与者不是冷血，而是被在场的人压住了。权威一走，他们的真实想法立刻回来了。

14. Experiment 10 · Rundown Office Block

What changed

The whole study moved out of Yale. It was run in a rundown office block in Bridgeport, Connecticut, under the made-up name “Research Associates of Bridgeport” — with no university connection at all. 40 participants.

Result: 47.5%

19 of the 40 still went to 450 V. Lower than Yale’s 65%, but far above half of the drop seen in Experiment 7.

The comparison that matters

Removing the authority’s body cost 44.5 points. Removing the university’s name cost only 17.5.

What it shows

Prestige matters, but much less than presence. Nearly half still obeyed a stranger in a shabby office.

Do not over-read this one

A tempting wrong conclusion is “people only obeyed because it was Yale.” The data says otherwise: 47.5% still obeyed with Yale’s name completely removed. The honest conclusion is that prestige contributes but does not create obedience.

中文理解
想象一下：你在一个破旧写字楼里，一个自称“Bridgeport 研究协会”的人让你电击陌生人。近一半人还是照做了。
这说明服从不是耶鲁的招牌造出来的。这是评价题里可以用来反驳“Milgram 的结论只是名校光环”的那一类批评。

15. Experiment 13 · Ordinary Man Gives Orders

What changed

The experimenter was called away on a pretended telephone call. An “ordinary man” — a confederate pretending to be another participant — then took over and suggested raising the shock by 15 V after each wrong answer. No uniform, no lab coat, no recognised position. 20 participants.

Result: 20%

Only 4 of the 20 participants obeyed fully — the lowest of the three variations.

Typical response

Many used the lowest possible shocks, argued with him, or went through the motions without really following his suggestions.

What it shows

Obedience depends on who is seen as entitled to give orders. The same words from someone with no authority have very little power.

中文理解：这一条最贴近“我不是听你的，我是听那个位置的”
“普通人”说的话完全一样，但他没有制服、没有头衔、不代表任何机构。
结果：几乎没人听他的。这说明服从的对象是那个被认可的权威身份，而不是任何一张嘴。

17. What They Prove

dissent（不从 / 抗拒） — refusing to obey an order or resisting the pressure to continue. The specification asks you to treat these three variations as evidence about situational factors that encourage dissent.

The single most important sentence in this chapter

In all three variations the participants were the same kind of people, recruited the same way, doing the same task on the same machine. Only the situation changed — and obedience changed with it. That is why the conclusion is about situations, not personalities.

🔗 Match the factor Check yourself

Each scenario removes one of the three supports. Choose which one.

A prison officer is delivering a reprimand by loudspeaker from the control room rather than standing in front of the prisoner. Compliance is noticeably lower.
The same training course is delivered by a world-famous university instead of an unknown local college. Attendees follow the instructions far more closely.
A passenger with no uniform or badge starts directing traffic at a junction during a power cut. Almost nobody follows him.
中文理解：把三个变式收成一句话
权威的“三条腿”被抽掉任何一条，服从就下降；因此鼓励 dissent 的方法就是削弱这些情境因素：
让权威不在场（7）、去掉机构的名望（10）、让下令的人没有合法性（13）。
这正好接上 1.1.1 的代理理论：进入代理状态需要一个被认可的权威——三个变式证明了这一点。

How to phrase it in an exam answer

“Milgram’s variations show that dissent is encouraged by removing the supports of the authority figure. When orders were given by telephone (Experiment 7), obedience fell from 65% to 20.5%, showing that the physical presence of the authority is a powerful situational factor. When the study moved to a rundown office block (Experiment 10) obedience fell to 47.5%, showing that institutional prestige contributes but is not essential. When an ordinary man gave the orders (Experiment 13) obedience fell to 20%, showing that obedience depends on the perceived legitimacy of the person giving them.”

18. Evaluating Milgram’s Research

▶ Strengths — why the research is still worth taking seriously
  • CONTROL 高度控制：A laboratory experiment with a standardised script — every participant heard the same protests at the same voltages, so comparisons are meaningful.
  • REPLICATION 可重复：Burger (2009) replicated a modified version and found 70% continued at 150 V, close to Milgram’s 82% at the same point — the effect has not simply disappeared with time.
  • CULTURE 跨文化的元分析：Blass (1999) analysed replications in several countries outside the USA and found obedience rates broadly similar, suggesting the finding is not uniquely American.
  • INTERNAL 内部效度：The variations change one feature at a time, which lets us identify which situational feature is doing the work.
▶ Limitations — what the research cannot tell us
  • ETHICS 伦理：Deception, no informed consent, pressure against withdrawal, and severe distress including three seizures. Baumrind (1964) argued the costs to participants were unacceptable.
  • VALIDITY 生态效度：Pressing switches on a machine in a laboratory is not the same as carrying out orders in a real institution, so the findings may not generalise to real-world atrocities.
  • SAMPLE 样本：40 American men, all volunteers from one city who replied to an advert and were paid. Androcentric and unlikely to represent women or other cultures.
  • DEMAND 需求特征：Orne and Holland (1968) argued participants were play-acting; Perry (2013) found archive recordings in which participants voiced doubts about the shocks.
  • INTERPRETATION 另一种解释：Haslam, Reicher and Birney (2014) re-examined Milgram’s own data and argued participants obeyed because they identified with the scientific goals of the research, not because they switched off their conscience — a different explanation of the same behaviour.
How to get top marks on “Evaluate Milgram”. Do not list criticisms. Take each point and say what it does to the conclusion. For example: “Because the sample was 40 male volunteers, we cannot generalise the 65% figure to women, which limits the claim that everyone would obey.”

20. Exam Lab

How to read the question

  • Describe — say what happened. Aim for who, where, what they did, and the key numbers.
  • Explain — say why, or say what something means. One point plus one reason per mark.
  • Evaluate — give strengths and limitations, and say what each one does to the conclusion.
Q1 · Describe Milgram’s (1963) research into obedience. 4 marks

Write your answer first, then compare.

Milgram recruited 40 male participants through a newspaper advert, telling them the study was about memory and learning. At a Yale laboratory each participant was assigned the role of teacher through a rigged draw, while a confederate played the learner. The teacher read word pairs and gave the learner an electric shock for every wrong answer, increasing the shock by 15 V each time, from 15 V up to 450 V. If the teacher hesitated, the experimenter used four standard prods such as “the experiment requires that you continue”. 100% of participants reached 300 V and 65% continued to the maximum 450 V.

Q2 · Explain what is meant by moral strain, and give one piece of evidence from Milgram’s study. 2 marks

Moral strain is the distress a person experiences when following an order conflicts with their own judgement about what is right. In Milgram’s study participants showed it through sweating, trembling, stuttering, nervous laughter and biting their lips, and three participants experienced full-blown seizures — showing they continued while deeply uncomfortable rather than obeying calmly.

Q3 · From Milgram’s variation studies, explain ONE situational factor that encourages dissent. 2 marks

One situational factor is the physical presence of the authority figure. In Experiment 7 the experimenter gave his orders by telephone instead of standing in the room, and obedience fell from 65% to 20.5%. Because nothing else changed, the drop shows that removing the authority figure’s presence makes dissent far more likely — people are much readier to refuse someone they do not have to face.

Q4 · Evaluate Milgram’s (1963) research into obedience. 8 marks

Plan four paragraphs first:

ParagraphJob of that paragraph
¶1 MethodStandardised laboratory procedure — and why that is both a strength and a limitation.
¶2 EthicsDeception, consent, distress — and what the deception does to the conclusion.
¶3 Sample & validity40 male volunteers; androcentric, ethnocentric, ecological validity.
¶4 ConclusionAlternative explanations and replications; say whether the conclusion survives.

¶1. A strength is that the procedure was highly standardised: every participant heard the same protests at the same voltages and faced the same four prods, so the study is replicable and the variations are genuinely comparable. However, this control was bought at the cost of ecological validity — pressing switches in a laboratory is not the same as carrying out orders inside a real institution.

¶2. The study raises serious ethical problems. Participants were deceived about the aim, the draw and the shocks, could not give informed consent, and were pressured not to withdraw; several showed extreme distress and three had seizures. This matters beyond welfare: the deception means we cannot be sure participants were showing real obedience rather than responding to demand characteristics, as Orne and Holland (1968) argued. Milgram replied that participants were distressed precisely because they believed it — and 84% later said they were glad to have taken part.

¶3. The sample limits the conclusion: 40 male volunteers from one American city, recruited by advert and paid $4.50. This makes the research androcentric and culturally narrow, so the 65% figure cannot be generalised to women. Blass (1999) partly answers this, finding broadly similar rates in other countries.

¶4. The conclusion is contested. Haslam, Reicher and Birney (2014) argue participants obeyed because they identified with the scientific goals rather than because they abandoned their conscience. Yet Burger (2009) found 70% still continued at 150 V decades later, and the three variations all show obedience shifting with the situation alone. On balance the situational explanation is well supported for the laboratory task, but should not be stretched to explain real-world atrocities on its own.

21. Key Terms

obedience服从
Following a direct order from someone perceived to have authority.
conformity从众
Changing behaviour to match a majority — no order is given. Different from obedience.
dissent不从 / 抗拒
Refusing to obey or resisting the pressure to continue.
moral strain道德压力
Distress from the clash between obeying an order and not wanting to cause harm.
confederate同谋者
An actor working for the researcher — in Milgram’s study, the learner Mr Wallace.
deception欺骗
Misleading participants about the true aim or nature of a study.
informed consent知情同意
Agreeing to take part after being told what the study really involves.
presumptive consent推定同意
Asking a similar group whether they would consent, used when the real participants cannot be asked.
right to withdraw退出权
The right to leave a study at any point without penalty.
debriefing事后说明
Telling participants the true aim afterwards and checking their wellbeing.
demand characteristics需求特征
Cues that tell participants what is expected, leading them to act accordingly.
ecological validity生态效度
Whether findings from a laboratory setting apply to real-life settings.
population validity总体效度
Whether the sample allows the findings to be generalised to a wider population.
androcentric男性中心
Based on men only, so the findings may not apply to women.
situational factor情境因素
A feature of the surroundings or setting that influences behaviour, rather than a feature of the person.
prestige名望
The status of the institution behind the authority — tested in Experiment 10.
legitimacy合法性
Whether the person giving orders is seen as entitled to do so — tested in Experiment 13.
standardised procedure标准化程序
