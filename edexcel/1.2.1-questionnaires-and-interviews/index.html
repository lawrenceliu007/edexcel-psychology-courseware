1.2.1 QUESTIONNAIRES AND INTERVIEWS
Asking people about themselves · 问卷与访谈 · self-report data

Social Psychology · Methods · You cannot watch an attitude. Sometimes the only way to learn what people think is to ask them.
🗣️ Core idea: self-report data 🔢 Quantitative vs qualitative 📖 Textbook: Chapter 4

What this section is really asking

  1. Surveys are self-report methods: they gather information about how people feel — attitudes, opinions, personality traits — through questionnaires or interviews.
  2. Questions come in two families: closed questions (fixed options, quick answers, quantitative data) and open questions (free answers, rich detail, qualitative data) — with Likert and ranked scales in between.
  3. Asking is not the same as measuring well: questionnaires must survive checks of reliability (consistency) and validity (measuring what it intends to measure), and avoid traps like social desirability and response bias.
  4. The asker matters too: researcher effects — the interviewer's sex, age, manner and expectations — can shape what respondents say, so they must be predicted and controlled.

1. Getting Started

Think about the last time someone asked you a question and you edited your answer. Maybe a teacher asked "did you revise for this test?" Maybe a relative asked "do you like my cooking?" You did not lie exactly. You just chose the version of the truth that made you look better.

Now imagine a psychologist who wants to know whether students would obey an unfair rule. They cannot follow a thousand students around all day. So they do something simpler — and something much more dangerous: they ask.

The question this chapter answers

Asking people about themselves is one of psychology's most powerful tools — and one of its most fragile. How do you design questions so people answer honestly, in a way you can measure and trust? That single question unpacks into question types, scales, reliability, validity, interviews and researcher effects.

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

A researcher wants to know whether students would admit to cheating in an exam. Which answer will they probably get?

The answer is the middle one. Admitting to cheating clashes with what society expects, so respondents give the answer that depicts them in a more favourable light. Psychologists have a name for this — social desirability — and it is one of the reasons "just ask them" is harder than it sounds.

中文理解：self-report（自我报告）为什么好用又危险？
好用：态度、观点、性格这些东西看不见，实验测不出来，只能开口问；
危险：人回答时会自动「美化」自己（social desirability 社会赞许性）——没人愿意承认自己作弊、不服从、不善良。
一句话：问出来的答案，是「对方想让你听到的版本」，研究者要设计方法把真话挤出来。

2. What Is Self-Report?

A survey is a self-report method, used to gather information about how people feel, their attitudes and opinions, personality types and other traits. Surveys are typically designed to gather a large amount of information, and they do it through two main tools:

Questionnaires

Written questions, answered in writing. Can be administered by post, email, face to face or online, and often consist of questions about attitudes, opinions, lifestyles — indeed any aspect of a person's life. Designed to reach a large sample at low cost.

Interviews

Questions answered directly, in conversation, rather than in writing. Can be a free-flowing conversation or a fixed set of preset questions asked by the researcher. Reaches depth that paper cannot.

survey 调查 — a self-report method gathering information about feelings, attitudes, opinions and traits. Carried out via questionnaire（问卷）or interview（访谈）.

One warning before we go further. Self-report data is what people say about themselves — not necessarily what they actually do. A student can tick "I never cheat" on a questionnaire and then cheat the next morning. Every claim built on self-report data carries this shadow, and every evaluation question about surveys lives in this gap between saying and doing.

3. Two Kinds of Data

Every question you write produces one of two kinds of data — and the kind it produces decides how you can analyse it.

Quantitative data（数量数据）

Information that is numerical or can be converted into numbers. "7 students said yes." "A rating of 4 out of 5." Numbers are easy to compare, count and graph.

Qualitative data（质量数据）

Information that is non-numerical prose — words, sentences, explanations. "I don't cheat because I saw how badly my friend was punished." Rich in meaning, but harder to summarise.

Exam tip — objective vs subjective

Ask two people to estimate the length of a classroom table and they will disagree — that is a subjective interpretation. Ask them to measure it with a ruler and they will agree — that is an objective measure. Quantitative data is objective because numbers are not open to interpretation; qualitative data can be subjective because the meanings found in prose are open to interpretation.

中文理解：quantitative vs qualitative 怎么记？
quantity = 数量 → quantitative = 能变成数字的（几个、几分、几成）；
quality = 质量/性质 → qualitative = 用文字描述的（为什么、什么感受）。
记忆口诀：能算数的是 quantitative，要读懂的是 qualitative。闭卷选择题给 quantitative，开放题给 qualitative。

4. Closed & Open Questions

The type of question decides the type of data. The textbook splits questions into two families:

Closed questions（封闭式问题）

Questions that have preset fixed answers which a respondent selects from by circling or ticking the closest match. Yes/no response questions, or a list of options ("fixed choice"). Closed questions yield quantitative data.

Open questions（开放式问题）

Questions that do not involve preset answers — respondents answer freely. This allows them to elaborate and justify their opinions, producing qualitative data that needs qualitative analysis.

Closed: what you gain
Closed: what you lose
Quick and easy for respondents; data is easy for researchers to analyse and compare across hundreds of people.
Frustrating if none of the preset options match what the person really thinks — and the researcher cannot know why someone answered that way, so detail is limited.
Open: what you gain
Open: what you lose
Depth and honesty — respondents explain why, in their own words, revealing reasons no preset option could capture.
More time and effort for the respondent, and the answers require qualitative analysis, which can lead to subjective interpretation on the part of the researcher.

🎯 Spot the type Quick check

"How regularly do you send text messages? Daily / A few times a week / Occasionally / Never (please circle)" — what kind of question is this, and what data does it yield?

The preset list of options makes it closed, and closed questions yield quantitative data — you can count how many people circle each option.

中文理解：怎么判断一个问题是 closed 还是 open？
只看一件事：答案是不是出题人预先写好的。
是 → closed（勾选、圈选、Yes/No、选项列表、Likert 打分、排序）；
不是、答题人自己写 → open（「为什么」「请解释」「描述一下」）。
再推一步：closed → 数字 → 好分析但浅；open → 文字 → 有深度但难分析。

5. Attitude Scales

A plain yes/no answer is fast but shallow. Attitude scales were designed to increase the level of detail achieved in a questionnaire: instead of a yes/no response, they offer respondents a range of options so that their strength of opinion can be gauged. The textbook gives two kinds:

Likert-style scale（李克特量表）

The respondent selects from a fixed set of options to rate their agreement with a series of statements — e.g. "I am happy with my purchase today": Strongly agree · Agree · Neutral · Disagree · Strongly disagree. (A Likert scale was used in the Adorno et al. survey on authoritarianism.)

Ranked scale（排序量表）

The respondent ranks their choices relative to other options. Each preference is given a weighting: to rank animals by how fearful people are of them, the most feared animal gets the highest score.

Odd or even? A design decision with consequences

When designing Likert-style statements, the number of options matters. An odd number of answers gives a middle value, which respondents may select more frequently — a comfortable escape hatch. An even number forces respondents to make a choice rather than select a 'neutral' or 'neither agree nor disagree' option.

Exam tip — response bias and reversed statements

Response bias can occur with Likert-style scales: if all the statements in a set are worded favourably (or all unfavourably), respondents can slip into just agreeing or just disagreeing with all of them — ticking without reading. To resolve this, statements should be reversed and mixed up:

Example statementReversed statement
Employment helps society to function.Employment does not help society to function.
Pets make people happy.Pets do not make people happy.
Politicians help the economy thrive.The economy can thrive without politicians.

If someone truly agrees that employment helps society, they should also disagree that it does not. Someone ticking "agree" all the way down is exposed as a response-bias responder.

🎯 Odd or even? Design decision

A researcher is worried that people will take the easy middle option instead of committing to a view. What should they do?

中文理解：Likert 与 ranked 怎么区分？
Likert = 给一句话打分（非常同意 → 非常不同意）——测「你对这句话有多同意」；
ranked = 给一堆东西排序（最怕的动物排第 1，最不怕的排第 4）——测「相对偏好」。
记忆口诀：Likert 竖着打分，ranked 横着排队。注意细节：odd 选项留了中立逃生门，even 逼人站队；全正向的题目会诱发 response bias，要混入反向陈述。

6. Design Issues

Questionnaires can be a useful way of finding out about attitudes, beliefs and opinions — but designing them well is tricky. Three issues come up again and again:

1 · Social desirability（社会赞许性）

The respondent does not give a genuine answer, but one which depicts them in a more favourable light — an answer that fits prevailing social norms. Almost nobody admits to agreeing with cheating in an exam. This is especially problematic for research into socially sensitive issues or attitudes that go against social norms. (Many questionnaires are designed to help researchers detect socially desirable responses — e.g. with reversed statements.)

2 · Question construction（题目措辞）

The researcher is often not present when the questionnaire is completed, so questions must not be too technical, ambiguous or complex. They must not lead or mislead the respondent into a particular answer. And they must respect the right to privacy: participants should not be asked personal questions they would find intrusive, and the researcher must not obtain personal data a participant would not voluntarily disclose.

3 · Response bias（反应偏差）

Covered above — all-favourable wording invites blanket agreement. Fix: reverse and mix up statements. Related fix: even-numbered scales remove the neutral hideout.

中文理解：三个坑怎么记？
坑一在人：social desirability——答题人自我美化，不给真话；
坑二在题：question construction——题目太技术、太模糊、有引导性，或侵犯 privacy；
坑三在格式：response bias——全同向陈述让人无脑全勾「同意」。
对策：人 → 用检测设计（反向陈述）；题 → 简单、中立、守隐私；格式 → 反向混排 + 偶数选项。

7. Reliability

Reliability refers to the consistency of a measure or finding. A questionnaire is reliable if it produces the same result every time it should. The textbook splits this into two directions:

Type
What consistency means here
External reliability（外部信度）
Consistency of a measure over time. If the same questionnaire is given to the same people on a different occasion, do they score the same?
Internal reliability（内部信度）
Consistency of a measure within itself. If several questions all measure the same trait, do they all point the same way?

Each type has its own test — and the two tests are a classic exam pairing:

Test-retest method → external reliability

The same people are given the same questionnaire to complete again on a different occasion. If their responses are the same or very similar, external reliability is established. (Note the textbook's warning: some scales lose external reliability exactly because people remember or change.)

Split-half method → internal reliability

Splitting the questions into two halves and comparing the findings from both halves during analysis. If all the questions measure the same concept, both halves should achieve the same score. If they do not, some questions may be measuring a different concept.

🎯 Name the method Quick check

A researcher splits their 20 anxiety questions into two sets of 10 and compares the scores from each half. What are they checking, and how?

中文理解：信度 = 「每次测都一样」吗？
external = 隔一段时间再测，结果稳不稳 → 用 test-retest（同一批人再测一次）；
internal = 问卷内部那几道题，是不是真的都在测同一个东西 → 用 split-half（拆两半对答案）。
记忆口诀：对外看时间（retest），对内看拆半（split-half）。

8. Validity

Validity refers to the extent to which something is measuring what it intends to measure. If you design a questionnaire intending to measure attitudes about education, your questions must measure that attitude — and nothing else. Reliability asks "is it consistent?"; validity asks the harder question: "is it the right thing that's consistent?"

Type of validity
How it is established
Face validity（表面效度）
Simply looking at each question and deciding whether it makes sense in terms of the construct being measured. Can be confirmed by asking an expert in the field to review the questions.
Predictive validity（预测效度）
The extent to which results from a test or study can predict future behaviour. A valid intelligence test should correlate with later educational success, such as A level or degree grading.
Concurrent validity（共时效度）
Comparing evidence from several studies testing the same thing to see if they agree — or comparing your questionnaire to another test that measures the same construct.
中文理解：三种效度一句话记忆
face = 「看脸」——读一遍题目，像不像在测这个东西；
predictive = 「预言」——今天测出的分数，能不能预测以后的行为（智力分 → 学业成绩）；
concurrent = 「对照」——和别的同主题测试比一比，结果一致才说明测的是同一个东西。
别和信度搞混：信度问「稳不稳」，效度问「准不准」。一支尺子每次都量出同一个错的数——信但不住，稳但不准。

9. Three Types of Interview

An interview is when respondents answer questions directly, rather than writing them. The three types differ in one thing: how tightly the researcher holds the script.

What the unstructured interviewer needs

Unlike a structured interviewer, an unstructured interviewer must be analytical during the interview — probing and seeking meaning from respondents. They need to be skilled at achieving a good rapport, responsive to the information offered, a good listener, and careful to use non-judgemental language.

🎯 Which interview is it? From the textbook

"A researcher wants to investigate what pets people own. They want to know what type of pets people have and how long they have owned them, but also want to explore the reasons for owning pets."

There are preset facts to collect (pet type, ownership time) but also room to explore reasons — preset questions plus permission to deviate. That is the textbook definition of a semi-structured interview.

中文理解：三种访谈 = 三种「剧本」
structured：照剧本念，一句不改 → 好重复、好比较，但浅；
semi-structured：有提纲，能跑题 → 又有结构又有深挖空间；
unstructured：只有一个大方向，纯聊天 → 最深，但费时间、没法复制。
记忆口诀：剧本越死越好比，剧本越活挖越深。

10. Researcher Effects

When asking people questions, the interviewer's own characteristics can influence the respondent: biological sex, age, manner and personality can all affect how a person responds, whether they are truthful, and whether they disclose information at all. It is therefore important to predict what characteristics might influence respondents and control them.

Who the researcher is

Example from the textbook: you can predict that a male interviewer will be unlikely to obtain detailed information from a female participant about her view of friendships within her peer group. Control: employ a female interviewer.

What the researcher expects

Researcher effects also include unintended influence from the researcher's expectations — smiling in approval at an answer, or inadvertently asking questions that serve to confirm the expected outcomes. This is confirmation bias: conducting research in a way that confirms the intended outcomes.

The fix

Researcher effects can be reduced by using an independent researcher who is trained in interviewing techniques, but is not aware of the study aims. No expectations → nothing to accidentally confirm.

confirmation bias 确认偏差 — when a researcher conducts research in a way that confirms the intended outcomes. Related: researcher effects（研究者效应）— any influence the researcher's characteristics or expectations have on respondents' answers.

Ethics: privacy matters most in interviews

Ethical issues matter in any questionnaire or interview, but are critical in unstructured interviews: the qualitative data can make direct reference to quotes from respondents. All respondent details must be anonymised and personal details disguised. Because the conversation is reflexive and personal, the interviewer must ask for personal information sensitively, to ensure they do not breach the respondent's right to privacy.

中文理解：researcher effects 为什么存在？
面对面提问时，提问者本身就是环境的一部分：你的性别、年龄、语气、表情都在影响对方敢不敢说真话。
两条腿：你是谁（male interviewer 问 female participant 闺蜜话题 → 问不出细节）+ 你盼什么（expected outcomes → confirmation bias，点头微笑把答案「钓」出来）。
对策一句话：换一个不知道研究目的的独立访谈者。

11. Primary vs Secondary Data

Where does your data come from? The textbook draws one clean line:

Dimension
Primary data（一手数据）
Secondary data（二手数据）
Source
Gathered directly by a researcher from participants or sources — using an experiment, observation, survey or content analysis.
Gathered from existing studies conducted by other researchers — e.g. during a literature review or meta-analysis.
Fit to the aim
First-hand and therefore gathered specifically according to the aims of the study.
Second-hand — may be slightly different and not specific to the aims of the researcher.
Cost
Time consuming to collect: the researcher must gather participants and design and conduct the research themselves.
Already collected — just needs to be analysed and compared.
Freshness & scale
Current — collected now, for this question.
May be out of date, but useful because large amounts of data from many studies give an overview of one research area.
literature review 文献综述 — when a researcher reads, summarises and draws conclusions from other people's research.
中文理解：primary vs secondary 怎么判断？
只问一句：数据是不是你自己直接向参与者收集的？
是 → primary（实验、观察、问卷、访谈都算）；
不是、是从别人的研究里拿的 → secondary（文献综述、meta-analysis）。
一句话：primary 贴合目的但费时；secondary 省时且量大，但可能过时、不对口。

12. Alternative Hypotheses

In addition to an overall research aim, a study also makes a prediction about what is likely to occur. This prediction is known as the alternative hypothesis.

What a good hypothesis must be

A hypothesis should contain the variables under investigation and be a clear, testable and precise statement made at the beginning of a study. The prediction is often guided by previous research in the topic area.

But what if previous research is thin — or the findings are mixed? Then the prediction may have to state only that a difference or relationship might be found between the variables, without saying which direction it will take:

Directional prediction

Previous research points one way, so the hypothesis names the direction: "Students who score higher on a conformity scale will report more agreement with the majority." (You will meet the full directional / non-directional language in Topic B, Chapter 11.)

Non-directional prediction

Limited or mixed previous research, so the hypothesis predicts only that a difference or relationship exists — not whether it goes up or down.

中文理解：alternative hypothesis 是什么？
研究开始前，把「我觉得会发生什么」写成一句明确、可检验、精确的话，并包含要研究的变量。
有把握方向 → 写明方向（「X 越高 Y 越高」）；研究少、结论乱 → 只写「会发现差异/关系」，不指方向。
记住它是「备择」：它是和「什么都没发生」相对立的那种预测——名字在 Topic B 还会展开。

13. Exam Focus

This topic appears constantly in Paper 1 — especially quantitative data, the single most frequent theme in past papers. Three textbook exam tips do most of the work:

Tip 1 — strengths/weaknesses must be social-psychology specific

You may be asked to give a strength or weakness of questionnaires as they are used in social psychology. A generic answer scores little; the answer must relate to social psychology specifically. Model:

"One weakness with using questionnaires in social psychology is that participants may answer in a way that portrays them in the best possible light: social desirability bias may occur. This means that people may say that they are more obedient than they actually are because they don't want people to think they are disobedient."

Tip 2 — interviews earn their depth in social psychology

Model strength: "One strength of using interviews in social psychology is that in-depth answers contain detailed information and reasons why individuals may consider others to have authority or power. This increases the validity of the data gathered by social psychology researchers about topics such as obedience." Notice the structure: feature → because → so what for social psychology.

Method
Signature strength
Signature weakness
Questionnaire
Reaches large samples cheaply; closed questions give quick quantitative data that is easy to analyse.
Social desirability bias — respondents may claim to be more obedient (or less conforming) than they really are.
Interview
In-depth answers reveal why people grant authority or power — increasing the validity of data on obedience.
Researcher effects: the interviewer's characteristics and expectations can distort what respondents disclose.
Structured interview
Standardised and easily repeated; no rapport needed.
Superficial data; respondents feel stifled and cannot fully express opinions.
Unstructured interview
Depth, rapport, and responsive probing follow the respondent's meaning.
Time consuming; not replicable; sensitive ethics around privacy and quotes.
中文理解：考试万能公式（Point → Because → So what）
1）先报术语：One weakness is social desirability bias…（点出术语名）；
2）解释机制：…because people don't want to be seen as disobedient…（说清为什么）；
3）落回社会心理学：…so they may say they are more obedient than they actually are.（绑定本学科场景）。
缺第 3 步 = 泛泛而谈 = 低分。考纲明说 Generic answers score 0。

15. Exam Lab

These are the actual question patterns from the textbook's exam practice for this chapter. Attempt each before revealing.

中文理解：这章考什么
两类题：一类让你自己设计（写一道封闭式问题，注意给出固定选项；区分开放式和封闭式）；一类考概念对比（structured 与 unstructured interview 的差别，从问题顺序和追问自由度两点说）。先自己写再点开对照。

Q1 — Carlos and the 'keep off the grass' signs (from the textbook)

Carlos wants to test how compliant pedestrians are to signs which ask them to 'keep off the grass'. He designs a questionnaire to gather quantitative data from pedestrians who visit a local park.

(a) State one closed question that Carlos could use in his questionnaire about obedience. (1 mark)

Any fixed-option question, e.g. "Would you walk on the grass if a sign asked you not to? Yes / No (please circle)." The key is preset answers that can be circled or ticked — that is what makes it closed and quantitative.

(b) State one open question that Carlos could use in his questionnaire. (1 mark)

Any free-answer question, e.g. "Explain why you would or would not walk on the grass when the sign asks you not to." No preset options — the respondent writes freely.

(c) Carlos decided to recruit participants using an opportunity sampling technique. Explain how Carlos would go about recruiting participants for his study. (2 marks)

Opportunity sampling uses participants who are readily available at the time. Carlos would go to the local park (where his target population — pedestrians — are) and ask the people who are there and willing to take part in his questionnaire.

(d) Explain one weakness of Carlos using an opportunity sampling technique for his questionnaire. (2 marks)

Opportunity sampling gives the researcher limited control over who is recruited: only pedestrians available at that time and place can be selected, so not everyone in the target population has an equal chance of being chosen. The resulting sample may be unrepresentative, limiting how far conclusions about compliance to 'keep off the grass' signs can be generalised. (Also worth knowing: asking people directly can be unethical as it puts pressure on them to participate.)

Q2 — the 'how' question: describing a stratified sample (from the textbook exam tip)

Crystal has been asked by her employer to send out questionnaires asking opinions about whether the food served in the office canteen is good. There are 100 employees who use the canteen at lunchtime and work in the office building. Describe how Crystal might gather a stratified sample of the employees in the office building. (3 marks)

The textbook's rule: if asked to describe 'how' a sampling technique is used, treat it as a process — firstly, secondly, thirdly. This ensures you access all the available marks.

Firstly, Crystal would decide which subgroups exist in the office building, such as managers, clerks, cleaners. Secondly, she would calculate the percentage of managers, clerks and cleaners that represents the whole target population in the office building. Thirdly, she would randomly select the proportionate number of employees in each subgroup and ask if they would complete the questionnaire on how good the food was in the office canteen.

The full stratified sampling walkthrough is in 1.2.5 Sampling Techniques — this page previews the exam pattern so the two connect.

16. Key Terms

survey（问卷调查）
A self-report method gathering information about feelings, attitudes, opinions, personality and traits, via questionnaire or interview.
quantitative data（量化数据）
Information that is numerical or can be converted into numbers. Objective — not open to interpretation.
qualitative data（质性数据）
Information that is non-numerical prose. Can be subjective — meanings in prose are open to interpretation.
closed questions（封闭式问题）
Preset fixed answers the respondent selects by circling or ticking. Yield quantitative data.
open questions（开放式问题）
No preset answers; respondents answer freely, elaborate and justify. Yield qualitative data.
attitude scale（态度量表）
More than yes/no: offers a range of options so the strength of opinion can be gauged.
Likert-style scale（李克特式量表）
Respondent rates agreement with statements from a fixed set of options (Strongly agree → Strongly disagree).
ranked scale（排序量表）
Respondent ranks choices relative to other options; each preference is given a weighting.
social desirability（社会赞许性）
Responding in a way that depicts oneself in a more favourable light according to prevailing social norms.
response bias（反应偏差）
Agreeing (or disagreeing) with every statement in an all-same-direction set. Fixed by reversing and mixing statements.
privacy（隐私）
Participants should not be asked intrusive personal questions, and the researcher must not obtain personal data a participant would not voluntarily disclose.
reliability（信度）
The consistency of a measure or finding. External = over time; internal = within itself.
test-retest method（重测法）
Same people, same questionnaire, different occasion — establishes external reliability.
split-half method（分半法）
Splitting questions into two halves and comparing the findings — establishes internal reliability.
validity（效度）
The extent to which something measures what it intends to measure.
face validity（表面效度）
Looking at each question and deciding whether it makes sense for the construct being measured.
predictive validity（预测效度）
The extent to which results predict future behaviour (e.g. intelligence scores ↔ degree grading).
concurrent validity（共时效度）
Comparing evidence from several studies testing the same thing to see if they agree.
structured interview（结构化访谈）
Standardised: same preset questions, same way, for everyone. Easy to repeat; can be superficial.
semi-structured interview（半结构化访谈）
Preset questions but no standardised format; the researcher can deviate. Gathers quantitative and qualitative data.
unstructured interview（非结构化访谈）
A loose research aim explored through conversation; needs rapport, listening skills, non-judgemental language; not replicable.
researcher effects（研究者效应）
Influence of the interviewer's characteristics (sex, age, manner, personality) or expectations on respondents' answers.
confirmation bias（确认偏差）
Conducting research in a way that confirms the intended outcomes — e.g. asking questions that confirm expected results.
primary data（一手数据）
Gathered directly by the researcher from participants, fitted to the study's aims; time consuming but current.
secondary data（二手数据）
Gathered from existing studies (literature review, meta-analysis); quick and large-scale but possibly out of date and not aim-specific.
literature review（文献综述）
Reading, summarising and drawing conclusions from other people's research.
alternative hypothesis（备择假设）
A clear, testable, precise prediction containing the variables under investigation, made at the beginning of a study.
