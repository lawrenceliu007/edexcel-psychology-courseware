1.2.5 SAMPLING TECHNIQUES
Who gets to be in the study? · 抽样技术 · random · stratified · opportunity · volunteer

Social Psychology · Methods · You cannot study everyone. So the small group you do study has to carry the weight for everyone else.
🎯 Core idea: representativeness ⚖️ Four techniques, four trade-offs 📖 Textbook: Chapter 4

What this section is really asking

  1. A whole population is too big to study, so researchers recruit a sample — and the aim of any sampling method is a representative sample: one that reflects the characteristics of the target population well.
  2. Stratified sampling is the most representative: divide the population into subgroups, calculate each subgroup's percentage, and randomly select proportionate numbers from each.
  3. Random, opportunity and volunteer each trade representativeness for speed — random gives everyone an equal chance, opportunity takes whoever is available, volunteer lets people self-select.
  4. Every technique has a failure mode: if a type of participant is over- or under-represented, sampling bias occurs and conclusions cannot be generalised back to the target population.

1. Getting Started

Your school has 1,200 students. You want to know how students feel about the new canteen menu. You cannot ask all 1,200 — there is no time. So you ask 60.

Now the real question, the one this whole topic lives inside: which 60?

Option A

The first 60 students who walk into the canteen at 12:30. Fast. Convenient. And notice something: they are all there because they eat at the canteen — hardly a neutral panel for judging its food.

Option B

Names pulled from a hat — every student's name in the draw, no exceptions. Slower. But nobody's presence or absence was decided by convenience.

Option C

A poster: "Share your canteen opinions — scan here." Only the students who care enough to respond. Some will have very strong views indeed.

The question this chapter answers

All three options produce 60 respondents. But they produce very different 60s — and every conclusion the researcher draws is only as good as the group they asked. How do psychologists choose people so that the small group can stand in for the whole population?

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

Which of the three options is most likely to give you students with extreme opinions about the canteen?

Option C. People who respond to an advert self-select — they chose to take part, usually because the topic matters to them. A student who feels nothing about the canteen never scans the poster. That is why volunteer samples so often over-represent the passionate, the opinionated and the available.

中文理解：抽样到底在解决什么问题？
研究者想说的是「全体学生怎么想」，但手里只有「被选中的 60 人」的数据。
这中间隔着一座桥：样本能不能代表总体（representativeness）。
桥搭得好 → 60 人的结论可以推广给 1,200 人；桥搭歪了 → 60 人只代表 60 人。
一句话：抽样技术 = 决定「谁来代表大家」的规则。

2. Population & Sample

Before naming techniques, the textbook builds the vocabulary:

Term
Meaning
Sampling（抽样）
The way in which participants are selected for a study.
Target population（目标总体）
The population of people being investigated by a study. If you are investigating attitudes about health services at a local hospital, the target population is people at the hospital. A sample is recruited from this group.
Representative sample（代表性样本）
A sample that represents the characteristics of the population well — so conclusions drawn from the research can be generalised back to explain the behaviour of the target population as a whole.
Sampling bias（抽样偏差）
Occurs if the sample is not representative — because of an over- or under-representation of a particular type of participant.

The technique used depends on the type of research and the availability of participants — but the aim is always the same: select a representative sample, so findings can be generalised back to the target population.

中文理解：population vs sample 一图记牢
target population = 你想下结论的那群人（全校 1,200 人）；
sample = 你真正问到的人（60 人）；
sampling bias = 样本里某类人太多或太少（问的全是天天去食堂的人）。
检验标准一句话：样本长得像不像总体。

3. Stratified Sampling

The most likely way to recruit a representative sample of a target population is by using a stratified sampling technique. If the target population has certain subgroups that need to be represented in the sample, stratified sampling recruits the correct number of participants from each subgroup.

The process — three steps (learn these as a chain)

  1. Divide the target population into its subgroups (strata).
  2. Calculate the percentage of members of each subgroup. If the target population is 50% children and 20% teenagers, the sample must represent those percentages.
  3. Randomly select a proportionate number of each subgroup.

When it earns its keep — the workplace example

Researching stress in the workplace at a company: find out how many staff occupy different roles — office clerks, managers, canteen staff, cleaners. There may be more clerks than managers, so more clerks need to be recruited than managers to represent the company staff fairly.

Strength
Catch
The most representative sampling method — every subgroup appears in the sample in its true proportion.
Participants selected this way can still refuse to take part, which can distort the final sample. It is also time consuming to organise.
中文理解：stratified 三步口诀
一分（divide）→ 二算（calculate percentages）→ 三抽（random select proportionally）。
例：总体 50% 儿童 / 20% 青少年 → 样本也必须是 50% / 20%。公司研究：职员多、经理少，就多抽职员、少抽经理。
为什么它最公平？总体里各占几成，样本里就各占几成——总体的小型复制品。但要记住「三抽」后面还藏着一句：被选中的人仍可能拒绝参加。

4. Random Sampling

A random sampling technique should ensure that everyone has an equal chance of being selected. The textbook gives two concrete ways to achieve this:

Computer-generated numbers

Computers can produce random sequences of numbers. Every person in the target population is assigned a number, and the computer-generated numbers select the sample where the numbers correspond.

The hat method

A simpler way: place the names of every member of the target population into a hat, shuffle them, and draw at random. Then contact everyone drawn to see if they wish to take part.

What it promises
Where it breaks
Random sampling should result in a representative sample — no researcher's hand ever picked who got in.
You can select an unrepresentative sample at random, too — chance has no memory. And even a representative list needs consent: anyone who declines to take part leaves you with an unrepresentative final sample.

Exam tip — time cost

Similar to stratified sampling, random sampling is time consuming — the researcher must obtain a full list of the target population, select randomly, and then contact each person selected to ask for consent.

中文理解：random = 抽签，为什么还不够？
每人机会均等，研究者手不沾——听起来最公平。但两个漏洞：
运气会歪：抽签也可能恰好抽出一屋子同款人（representative 不保证）；
拒绝会歪：抽中的人有权说不来，最后到场的样本就变了形。
一句话：随机保证的是「机会」，不是「结果」。

5. Opportunity Sampling

An opportunity sample makes use of participants who are readily available by asking them to take part. Two textbook pictures of this:

The student common room

A researcher walks into a student common room and asks the people there to take part. Whoever happens to be in the room at the time is the sample.

The street

Or investigating passers-by in a street: whoever walks past and agrees becomes a participant. Again — availability decides, not the population's characteristics.

Why researchers use it
What it costs
It is the quickest and easiest technique — no lists, no strata, no follow-up letters. The people are simply there.
The researcher has limited control over who is recruited: not everyone in the target population has an equal chance of being selected, because they may not be available at the time. Asking people directly can also be unethical, as it puts pressure on them to participate.
中文理解：opportunity = 「顺手抓人」
谁在 common room、谁路过街头，就问谁。省时省力是它唯一的王牌。
代价两个：样本歪（在场的 ≠ 总体的缩影）+ 道德压力（当面开口，人家不好意思拒绝）。
一句话：方便换来的，都是代表性欠的债。

6. Volunteer Sampling

Self-selected participants can be recruited by placing an advert in a newspaper or a student common room. Volunteers are self-selecting: they choose to take part — they are not approached and asked by a researcher.

Strength
Weakness
Considered a more ethical sampling method — nobody is approached and put under pressure; people decide for themselves.
The researcher has no control over who volunteers: a certain type of participant may choose to take part, and only those who see the advert and have the time will respond. This can result in a sample bias which limits the generalisability of the study conclusions.
中文理解：volunteer = 「贴广告等人报名」
道德上最舒服：没人被当面施压，报名全凭自愿。
但报名的人自带滤镜：有空的、看到广告的、对话题有强观点的——往往是「某一类人」。
对比记忆：opportunity 是研究者走向人（顺手抓），volunteer 是人走向研究者（看广告）。方向相反，代价各有不同。

7. The Four Compared

Technique
How it works
Main weakness
Stratified
Divide into subgroups → calculate percentages → randomly select proportionate numbers.
Time consuming; those selected may still refuse to take part.
Random
Everyone has an equal chance — computer-generated numbers or names in a hat.
Chance can still produce an unrepresentative sample; refusals distort it; time consuming.
Opportunity
Ask whoever is readily available — common room, street.
Unequal chance of selection; direct asking can be unethical (pressure to participate).
Volunteer
Advertise; participants self-select by responding.
Only certain types of people volunteer — sample bias limits generalisability.

Read the table twice and a pattern appears: every move away from stratified buys convenience and pays in representativeness. Stratified is the gold standard; random keeps fairness but leaves results to chance; opportunity and volunteer trade the most representativeness for the least effort.

中文理解：一张表压住整章
考试时按需取用：问「how」→ 背 stratified 三步；问「define」→ 方法名 + 例子；问「weakness」→ 从最后一列取一条并落到社会心理学场景。
记忆主线一句话：公平度：stratified > random > opportunity ≈ volunteer；方便度正好反过来。

8. Spotting Sampling Bias

The textbook's Activity 4 asks you to identify the sampling bias in each scenario — and to suggest its likely impact on the conclusions. This is exactly the skill the exam rewards. Work through them, then reveal:

(a) A researcher recruits student participants in the canteen at lunchtime to find out about attitudes towards A level reforms.

Opportunity sampling: only students who happen to be in the canteen at lunchtime are recruited, so students who avoid the canteen have no chance of selection. Impact: the sample over-represents one type of student, so conclusions about all students' attitudes to A level reforms may not generalise.

(b) Participants volunteer to take part in a study about career choices by responding to an advert in a women's magazine.

Volunteer sampling with a heavily skewed readership: the advert reaches the magazine's readers, and only they can respond. Impact: one demographic (e.g. by gender and interests) is over-represented, so conclusions about career choices in the wider population are limited.

(c) A researcher uses the telephone directory to gather a sample of participants for a study on health-related behaviour.

Random-ish selection from a flawed frame: people without listed numbers (or with no landline) have no chance of selection. Impact: households unrepresented by the directory may differ systematically in age, income or lifestyle — biasing conclusions about health-related behaviour.

(d) Attitudes towards a new school uniform policy are gathered from students recruited from a school common room.

Opportunity sampling again: common-room users are over-represented; students who spend breaks elsewhere are missed. Impact: if common-room users share habits or year groups, the sample's attitudes may not represent the whole school's.

中文理解：诊断抽样的三连问
① 谁有机会被选中？（opportunity → 在场的人；volunteer → 看到广告的人；random → 名单上的人；stratified → 各子群按比例）
② 谁没有机会？（没去食堂的、不看那本杂志的、没登记电话的）
③ 缺的那批人，观点会不会系统性不同？会 → 结论推广受限（limited generalisability）。

9. Exam Focus

Sampling questions come in two shapes, and the textbook gives a scoring rule for each:

Shape 1 — "Describe how…" (the how question)

If asked to describe 'how' a sampling technique is used, treat it as a process: firstly, secondly, thirdly. This ensures you access the available marks. The model answer is the Crystal canteen question in 1.2.1's Exam Lab — stratified in exactly three steps.

Shape 2 — "Define…" (the definition question)

If asked to define a sampling method, you gain more marks by giving an example. Model from the textbook: "Opportunity sampling uses participants who are available at the time, for example gathering students who are available in the common room at the time of the study and who agree to take part when asked." Structure: definition → "for example" → concrete scene.

Why sampling questions dominate social psychology papers

Every classic social study made a sampling decision you can be asked about: Milgram's newspaper advert (volunteer sampling — which is why his sample was self-selected), Asch's student participants (opportunity sampling). When you evaluate a study, sampling is one of the fastest evaluation points available: name the technique, state the bias, limit the generalisability.

Universal strength
Universal weakness
Stratified: most representative, so conclusions generalise best to the target population.
Opportunity and volunteer: a certain type of person is over-represented — sampling bias limits generalisability.
中文理解：两种题型两套模板
Describe how（过程题）：firstly 分子群 → secondly 算百分比 → thirdly 按比例随机抽并邀请参加——三步拿满分。
Define（定义题）：定义 + for example + 具体场景——多一个例子多一分。
评价题万能链条：技术名 → 偏差 → 推广受限。

11. Exam Lab

Q1 — Carlos's pedestrians, part two (from the textbook)

Carlos wants to test how compliant pedestrians are to 'keep off the grass' signs in a local park.

(a) Explain how Carlos would recruit participants using opportunity sampling. (2 marks)

Carlos would go to the local park and ask the pedestrians who are there at the time to take part in his questionnaire. Opportunity sampling uses whoever is readily available — the people in the park when he is — rather than selecting from a full list of the target population.

(b) Explain one weakness of opportunity sampling for Carlos. (2 marks)

Carlos has limited control over who is recruited: only pedestrians available at that time are selected, so not everyone in the target population has an equal chance of being chosen. The sample may be unrepresentative — for example, people who visit the park at other times or who avoid it — limiting the generalisability of his conclusions about compliance.

Q2 — the Crystal stratified question, replayed for sampling (from the textbook)

Crystal must send out questionnaires asking opinions about the office canteen food. There are 100 employees who use the canteen. Describe how Crystal might gather a stratified sample of the employees. (3 marks)

Firstly, Crystal would decide which subgroups exist in the office building, such as managers, clerks, cleaners. Secondly, she would calculate the percentage of managers, clerks and cleaners that represents the whole target population in the office building. Thirdly, she would randomly select the proportionate number of employees in each subgroup and ask if they would complete the questionnaire.

Three steps, three marks — "firstly / secondly / thirdly" is the scoring frame.

Q3 — definition with example (the textbook's exam tip, applied)

Define volunteer sampling. (2 marks)

Volunteer sampling recruits self-selected participants who choose to take part — for example by placing an advert in a newspaper or a student common room, and recruiting those who respond. Nobody is approached and asked by the researcher.

12. Key Terms

sampling
The way in which participants are selected for a study.
target population
The population of people being investigated by a study; the sample is recruited from it.
representative sample
A sample that represents the characteristics of the population well, allowing conclusions to be generalised back.
generalisability
The extent to which conclusions from the sample can explain the behaviour of the target population as a whole.
sampling bias
Over- or under-representation of a particular type of participant, making the sample unrepresentative.
stratified sampling
Divide the population into subgroups, calculate each subgroup's percentage, randomly select proportionate numbers. Most representative.
strata
The subgroups within the target population (e.g. managers / clerks / cleaners; 50% children / 20% teenagers).
random sampling
Everyone has an equal chance of selection — via computer-generated numbers or names in a hat.
opportunity sampling
Using participants who are readily available by asking them to take part (common room, street passers-by).
volunteer sampling
Self-selected participants recruited through an advert; more ethical but a certain type of person tends to volunteer.
self-selecting
Participants who choose to take part rather than being approached by the researcher.
consent (in sampling)
Even selected participants must agree to take part — refusals can leave the final sample unrepresentative.
