CI The two memory foundations · 两大记忆原理ANSWERS → 课件 §3
The chapter's hinge 章节枢纽
The cognitive interview wasn't invented by accident. It is built on two cognitive theories about how memory works — and understanding those two theories is what makes the four techniques make sense (rather than feel like four arbitrary tricks). Memorise these two foundations before reading §4.
Foundation 1 — Bartlett (1932) reconstructive memory · 重构记忆
What does it claim?Memory is not a recording 不是录像, but an active re-building 重新构建. When we recall, we fill in gaps using our schemas 图式 — pre-existing expectations about how the world works.
Key studyBartlett (1932) — "War of the Ghosts" story. Participants mis-remembered 误记 details to fit their own culture (e.g. "canoe" → boat 船).
Witness riskA witness's recall may be contaminated by what they expect to have seen 他们期待看到的, not what they actually saw.
Interview fixBreak the witness out of story mode 故事模式 — reverse the order, change perspective → prevents schema from filling in details.
Foundation 2 — Tulving (1974) context-dependent memory · 情境依赖记忆
What does it claim?Memory is best retrieved in the same context 同等情境 in which it was encoded. Mood, surroundings, sounds, and weather 天气 at encoding act as retrieval cues.
Key studyGodden & Baddeley (1975) — divers recalled words better on land if learned on land and better underwater if learned underwater (the textbook uses Tulving's principle; Godden & Baddeley is the most cited test).
Witness riskA witness is being interviewed in a quiet police station 安静的警局 — nothing like the noisy, chaotic crime scene. Cues are gone, retrieval drops.
Interview fixAsk the witness to mentally rebuild the original scene — what they could see, hear, smell, how they felt 感受 at the time. Cue the context back.
Exam tip · 自己回答"designed from which two theories?"
The Checkpoint answer is exactly: Bartlett's (1932) reconstructive memory theory and Tulving's (1974) context-dependent memory. Don't say "Loftus & Palmer" (that's misleading questions — tested elsewhere) and don't say "Godden & Baddeley" (it tested Tulving but isn't the theory itself).

CI The four techniques of the cognitive interviewANSWERS → 课件 §4
Fill in the four techniques — what they do and why 对抗哪个记忆毛病
Technique 技巧What the officer does 警官做什么Fights which foundation problem 对抗哪个毛病
1. Report everything
报告一切		 Let the witness give a free / uninterrupted 自由 / 不打断 account. Even trivial details ("the man had a red shoelace") can act as memory cues 记忆线索. Fights Bartlett's 巴图效应 — by saying "report everything" you invite more raw detail that the schema would otherwise skip.
2. Reinstate the context / mental state
重建情境与心境		 Ask the witness to mentally rebuild 心理重建 the crime scene — sights, sounds, smells, weather, how they felt. Fights Tulving's 塔文宁效应 — restores the encoding context so retrieval cues come back.
3. Change the order
改变顺序		 Ask the witness to recall the event backwards / in a different sequence 倒序或换序 — start from the end, then work backwards. Fights Bartlett's 巴图效应 — breaks story mode; the schema can't "fill in" because the narrative is rearranged.
4. Change the perspective
改变视角		 Ask the witness to report the event from another person's standpoint 另一个人的视角 — e.g. the security guard's, the cashier's. Fights Bartlett's 巴图效应 — forces a different retrieval route, recalling details normally filtered out by the witness's own view.

§7 — Four pieces of evidence · 四项证据（必背）
Study 研究Design & sample 设计/样本Finding 结果
Geiselman et al. (1985) Lab study, detectives / students 侦探 / 学生 watched a video of a crime, recalled 1 day later. CI: correct items vs standard: . Confabulations roughly equal 大致相同.
Fisher et al. (1989) Field study — real crimes 真实案件 in Florida, USA. Real detectives using CI recovered +47% more correct info than standard.
Köhnken et al. (1999) Meta-analysis of studies. CI reliably outperforms 胜过 standard across many contexts — but the size of the gain varies.
Holliday (2003) Field study on child witnesses, ages 5–10 vs 11+. CI works well for older children (11+), but only modest gains for younger ones.
§8 — Evaluating the CI · 评价（三段：strength + evidence + why it matters）
Point 评价点Evidence 证据Why it matters 为什么重要
Strength 1 — more accurate recall Geiselman 1985: 41.15 vs 29.40 correct. Witness testimony is the most common form of evidence in court; CI makes it more reliable 更可靠.
Strength 2 — works in real field settings Fisher 1989: +47% in real crimes. Lab results generalise 推广 to real policing — supports ecological validity.
Limitation 1 — time-consuming CI typically takes ~ 60 min vs 15 min for standard. Officers under time pressure in active investigations may not be able to apply the full protocol 跑完完整流程.
Limitation 2 — training demands Dando et al. (2008): 221 officers reported feeling inadequately trained 训练不足. Poorly applied CI can still mislead witnesses — applicability depends on training 应用取决于训练.
Limitation 3 — limited for young children Holliday 2003: only modest gains under age 11. CI is not a one-size-fits-all tool — children below ~11 may need other methods.

"One strength is that [point], as shown by [named study], which found that [numbers]. This matters because [real-world consequence]." — always name the study, always use the number, always finish with the "so what".

FORM What is formulation? · 什么是心理/个案概念化ANSWERS → 课件 §10–§11
§10 — Case formulation: definition & purpose 定义与目的
One-line definitionA psychological formulation is the process by which a psychologist gathers information 收集信息 about an offender's life (relationships, social circumstances, life events) and then applies psychological theory 应用心理学理论 to form a hypothesis 假设 about the function of their offending.
When it happensAfter an offender is caught and convicted. It is not 不用于 used to catch the offender — that's the police's job, using the CI and other clues.
Who does itA qualified forensic psychologist 司法心理学家 (often in prison or community probation), not a police officer.
What it informsrisk of reoffending 再犯风险 · appropriate rehabilitation programme 康复方案 · safe release planning · explaining the offending to the offender themselves (first step toward change).
§11 — Offence analysis · 罪行分析 (different from formulation)
Offence analysis 罪行分析Case formulation 个案概念化
What it studies The facts of the crime 案件事实本身 — what happened, where, when, how, who was killed/harmed. The person who did it 罪犯本人 — their life, relationships, beliefs, what the offence functioned for.
Used for Investigating similar cases, linking crimes. Treatment, risk assessment, release decisions.
Done by Police / investigators. forensic psychologist 司法心理学家.
Is it objective? Closer to objective — describing events 事件. Subjective 主观 — involves the psychologist's interpretation and theory choice.
The form of a formulation · 概念化的结构
A good formulation connects three layers: ① triggers / antecedents (what was happening just before) → ② function (what need the offending met: control? intimacy? status?) → ③ consequences (what the offender got out of it: relief? excitement? respect?). The psychologist then chooses a treatment that targets the function.

§13 — Strengths & limitations of formulation · 优缺点
Point 评价点WhyMark-scheme echo 评分要点
Strength 1 — tailored to the individual Unlike statistical risk tools (e.g. actuarial risk scales), a formulation captures the offender's unique life history 独特的人生经历 and the specific function 功能 their offending served. AO3 — evaluation.
Strength 2 — improves staff understanding Prison staff who understand why an offender behaves a certain way are better placed to manage them safely 安全管理 and build rapport. AO3 — application.
Strength 3 — first step to change Explaining the formulation to the offender themselves gives them a non-judgemental account of what led them to offend — can be the first step in rehabilitation 康复. AO3 — application.
Limitation 1 — subjective The psychologist interprets 解释 the information; different psychologists may emphasise different things. There is inter-rater variability 评分者间差异. AO3 — evaluation.
Limitation 2 — hard to measure effectiveness You cannot simply count "did they reoffend?" because many reoffenders are never caught 从未被抓. So reoffending rate underestimates real reoffending. AO3 — evaluation (a Checkpoint answer too).
Limitation 3 — depends on information given An offender may hide / distort 隐瞒 / 歪曲 their history. The formulation is only as good as what the offender (and the files) reveal. AO3 — evaluation.

Answer key · 答案速查Check & tick
p.3 — Two memory foundations

Bartlett (1932): memory is not a recording but an active re-building; recall is filled in using schemas. Risk: a witness's recall may be contaminated by what they expect to have seen. Fix: break the witness out of story mode — reverse the order, change perspective.

Tulving (1974): memory is best retrieved when the context at retrieval matches the context at encoding (mood, surroundings, sounds, weather). Risk: a witness in a quiet police station has lost the cues. Fix: ask the witness to mentally rebuild the original scene.

p.4 — Four techniques

Report everything — let the witness give a free, uninterrupted account; trivial details can act as memory cues. Fights Bartlett — invites raw detail schemas would skip.

Reinstate context — ask the witness to mentally rebuild the scene. Fights Tulving — restores encoding cues.

Change order — recall the event in reverse / a different sequence. Fights Bartlett — breaks story mode.

Change perspective — recall from another person's standpoint (security guard, cashier). Fights Bartlett — different retrieval route, different detail.

p.5 — Interview flow (5 steps)

① Transfer rapport from first officer → ② Open question / free recall → ③ Apply 4 CI techniques → ④ Specific follow-up questions → ⑤ Return witness to a normal emotional state.

p.5 — Geiselman (1985) numbers

CI = 41.15 correct items; Standard = 29.40. Confabulations roughly equal — extra items are real, not guesses.

p.6 — Evidence table

Fisher (1989): field study on real crimes in Florida; real detectives recovered +47% more correct info with CI.

Köhnken (1999): meta-analysis of 42 studies; CI reliably outperforms standard.

Holliday (2003): children aged 5-10 vs 11+; CI works well for 11+, only modest gains for younger.

p.7 — Formulation & offence analysis

Formulation: psychologist gathers information about offender's life (relationships, social circumstances, life events) and applies psychological theory to form a hypothesis about the function of offending — informs risk assessment, rehab programme, release.

Offence analysis studies the facts of the crime; formulation studies the person who did it. Offence analysis is closer to objective; formulation is subjective.

p.8 — Kaia marks & Darian

Kaia criminogenic factors (5): k1 financial / k2 Robin Hood / k3 chaotic childhood / k4 father imprisoned / k5 visits father. Traps (not factors): k6 diamond value / k7 alarm bypass / k8 airport arrest.

Darian 3 marks: ① gather info on relationships, social circumstances, life events · ② interview the offender directly · ③ apply theory to form a hypothesis about the function of offending, to inform risk / rehab decisions.
