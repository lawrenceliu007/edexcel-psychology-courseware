4.1.6 PSYCHOANALYSIS AND THE OBJECT RELATIONS SCHOOL
Making the unconscious conscious · 精神分析与客体关系学派

Topic D · Learning theories and development Therapy: psychodynamic Spec 4.1.6
Freud's therapy does not fight the symptom. It follows the clues — free speech, dreams, slip-ups, even missed appointments — down to the unconscious conflicts underneath.

What this section is really asking

  1. The aim is self-insight: make the unconscious conscious, so the client can finally see what has been driving their thoughts and feelings.
  2. The therapist never pushes. This is a non-directive therapy — the client speaks, dreams and drifts; the therapist interprets.
  3. Four windows into the unconscious: free association, dream analysis, transference, and resistance — each one is a leak the defence mechanisms cannot fully plug.
  4. Object relations takes it further: our earliest relationships are carried inside us as an internal working model, and therapy helps the client tell where old patterns end and the self begins.

1. Getting Started

Meet two people who keep failing in the same way.

The first dates partners who are cold and critical — again and again, different person, same story. The second cannot keep a close friend; every friendship somehow ends in the same argument. Both are intelligent. Both can describe exactly what goes wrong. Neither can stop it.

The puzzle

If they can see the pattern, why can't they change it? Freud's answer: because the real reason is not visible to them. It lives in the unconscious — and what we consciously believe about ourselves is the story the mind tells afterwards.

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

So what would a therapy built on Freud's theory try to do?

The answer is the second one. And notice what this therapy is not: it gives no instructions, sets no exercises, builds no hierarchy. The client talks; the therapist listens and interprets. That is why it is called non-directive — the mirror image of systematic desensitisation from 4.1.5.

中文理解：为什么「看见了却改不掉」？
Freud 认为你看到的模式（总遇错的人、总吵一样的架）只是表面，真正的动力藏在潜意识里——那里有你自己都不承认的愿望、冲突和记忆。
所以治疗不能靠「给建议」，而要把潜意识的内容翻到意识层面（making the unconscious conscious）。看见真正的理由，改变才有可能。

2. From Theory to Therapy

Psychoanalysis is Freud's theory of mind, turned into a treatment. The textbook names three concepts from Freud's psychodynamic theory that the therapy is built on — you met them all in 4.1.4.

Concept from the theory
What it says
What the therapy does with it
The unconscious mind
A large part of the mind is hidden from awareness, yet still drives thoughts, feelings and behaviour.
The whole therapy is a journey down into it — every technique is a way of hearing what the unconscious is saying.
Intrapsychic conflict
The id demands, the super-ego forbids, the ego is squeezed in the middle.
Unresolved conflicts from the past keep causing trouble; the client relives them safely and resolves them as an adult.
Ego defence mechanisms
The ego protects itself by pushing painful material out of awareness.
The techniques work by weakening these defences, letting unconscious content slip through.
Figure 4.1.6a · Freud's iceberg. The visible tip is the conscious mind; psychoanalysis is a deliberate attempt to haul material from below the waterline into view.

One more idea completes the picture. The aim of psychoanalysis is to give the client self-insight by making the unconscious conscious. Through exploring the unconscious, the client understands its impact on their thoughts and emotions — and, crucially, they get to relive past experiences in a safe environment. Reliving them as an adult, with adult resources, they can respond more appropriately and resolve the psychic conflicts and defences they have been carrying.

中文理解：三个理论概念 = 三个治疗动作
潜意识 → 治疗就是「下去捞」；内心冲突 → 治疗让来访者在安全的环境里重新经历一遍，用成年人的方式回应它；防御机制 → 各种技术都是在削弱防御，让被压住的东西漏出来。
冰山图考试能直接用：水面上的叫 conscious，水面下的叫 unconscious，治疗就是把下面的东西拉上来。

3. What Psychoanalysis Is

psychoanalysis（精神分析）
A non-directive psychological therapy developed by Sigmund Freud. Its aim is to give the client self-insight by making the unconscious conscious, so that unconscious conflicts can be understood and resolved.

Put it next to systematic desensitisation and the contrast explains both:

4. The Setting

Walk into a traditional psychoanalysis session and the first thing you notice is the furniture.

The classic arrangement

The client lies on a couch facing away from the therapist, and the therapist remains anonymous — no judgements, no visible emotional reactions. Freud's own clients lay on a chaise longue facing away from him while they spoke.

Why so strange? Every detail has a job:

Facing away

The client cannot read the therapist's face, so they cannot edit what they say to please or annoy them. The flow of thought stays unfiltered.

The anonymous therapist

Not reacting means not judging. Without approval or disapproval to chase, the client is safe to say the embarrassing things — which are usually the important ones.

The therapeutic relationship

All of this establishes trust — an essential process in psychoanalysis. Nothing else in the therapy works without it.

中文理解：躺椅不是 Freud 的怪癖，是技术设计
你试着对着朋友说一个难以启齿的想法——你一定会盯着他的脸，看他皱不皱眉，然后当场删改。看不到脸，才敢说真话。
治疗师保持匿名也是同一逻辑：我不评判你，所以你不用讨好我或防着我。

5. Session One: Assessment

Before any technique begins, psychoanalysis starts with an initial assessment of:

  • the client's current concerns — what is troubling them now;
  • their personal history — childhood, family, significant events;
  • their relationships — past and present;
  • any symptoms they may be experiencing.

This is not a formality. Psychoanalysis is interested in the whole person, not one behaviour — the assessment is where the map of the client's life is first drawn, and every later interpretation is checked against it.

Connect this to the evaluation

Remember this breadth: it is why psychoanalysis is called a holistic therapy later in this chapter, and it is the first strength you can quote in an exam.

6. Free Association

free association（自由联想）
The client is encouraged to speak freely about their thoughts, memories, feelings and dreams — and asked not to censor themselves at all, no matter how embarrassing or irrelevant the information seems.

Why would rambling help? Because censorship is exactly what the defence mechanisms do. Every time you swallow a thought (“that's silly”, “I shouldn't say that”), a defence has done its job. Free association aims to access the unconscious by weakening the ego defence mechanisms that normally censor it.

As the client becomes more relaxed in the non-judgemental environment, they begin to express themselves without filtering or distorting unconscious thoughts. What leaks out — the pauses, the odd word, the story that arrives out of nowhere — is material for interpretation.

A two-minute experiment

Try saying honestly whatever comes to mind for one minute, forbidding yourself to censor. Notice how quickly an internal editor appears — “don't say that”, “that's irrelevant”. That editor is the defence mechanism at work; the therapist is listening for the moments it fails.

中文理解：自由联想 = 让「内心审稿人」下班
我们平时说话前都会自我审查：这个不体面、那个太尴尬。这些「审稿人」就是防御机制。
自由联想的规则只有一条：想到什么说什么，不许删改。越放松、越不评判的环境，审稿人越松懈，潜意识的内容就漏得越多。

7. Dream Analysis

For Freud, dreaming is not random noise. When we dream we are fulfilling our wishes and desires. But the wish almost never appears in the dream wearing its own face — it arrives in disguise.

manifest content（显性内容）
The dream as we experience and report it — the story we remember in the morning.
latent content（隐性内容）
The actual wish or desire underneath — the real meaning the dream is hiding.
dreamwork（梦的工作）
The process that alters and disguises the latent content into the manifest content — turning a forbidden wish into an acceptable story.
Figure 4.1.6b · Dreamwork turns the hidden wish into the remembered dream. Interpretation is the therapist reading the disguises backwards.

Displacement: the disguise itself

During dreamwork the latent content is disguised through displacement: we dream of something which is actually symbolic of something else. A dream about a snarling dog may carry feelings that actually belong to a frightening parent; a dream about missing an exam may carry anxieties about a job.

Why does the disguise work at night? Because during sleep our ego defence mechanisms are weaker, allowing unconscious content to slip into the dream. The defences are still strong enough to demand a disguise — but not strong enough to block the dream entirely. That gap is what the therapist works in: the role of the therapist is to interpret these symbols to uncover the actual unconscious wish.

🎯 Quick check No notes

A client dreams of being chased by a large black dog. In the dream, the dog is…

中文理解：梦 = 加了伪装的愿望
latent＝真正想说的（藏起来的）；manifest＝你早上讲出来的那个故事；dreamwork＝中间那道「伪装工序」，常用 displacement（转移）：把情绪挪到一个替身身上。
为什么睡觉时能漏出来？因为睡着时防御机制值班松了。治疗师的工作 = 顺着伪装往回破译。

8. Transference

Sit with anyone long enough, in an atmosphere of trust and anonymity, and something remarkable happens: the client's relationships walk into the room.

transference（移情）
During therapy, the client may transfer their feelings and thoughts about a person or situation onto the therapist — positive feelings, hostile feelings, romantic feelings, or dependence — as if the therapist were a parent.
Figure 4.1.6c · Transference runs from client to therapist; counter-transference is the return current. Both are interpreted, not suppressed.

The feelings are not really about the therapist — the therapist is a stand-in. That is what makes transference valuable: the client's deepest patterns are being reenacted live, in a place where someone trained is watching. It is the therapist's role to interpret the transference to gain insight into the client's unconscious mind.

中文理解：把治疗师「当成」某个重要的人
你对权威老师又敬又怕、总想讨好——这模式不会因为进了治疗室就消失。你会不知不觉用对待父亲/母亲的方式对待治疗师：撒娇、敌意、依赖、甚至爱慕。
这不是事故，是素材：你最重要的人际模式正在现场直播，治疗师正好现场解说。

9. Counter-transference

counter-transference（反移情）
The therapist's emotional response to the client's transference. The therapist may feel warmth, frustration or irritation, feel protective towards the client, or even develop romantic feelings.

Read the definition twice: the therapist is a person too. Week after week, a client may pour affection, hostility or helplessness into the room — and the therapist's own feelings respond. Those responses can include:

Positive feelings

Warmth towards the client, a wish to help beyond the professional role.

Negative feelings

Frustration or irritation — perhaps echoing the client's own hostility.

Protective feelings

Wanting to shield the client, as a parent might shield a child.

Romantic feelings

A response to the client's romantic transference — the most dangerous kind if unexamined.

Why the therapist's feelings matter

The textbook is clear: both transference and counter-transference are important features of the therapeutic relationship, and the therapist should work to interpret them and explain their significance to the client. The therapist's feelings are not an obstacle to the therapy — handled properly, they are another source of information about the client's unconscious.

中文理解：治疗师也是人，也会有情绪
来访者对你撒娇依赖，你可能莫名想照顾他；来访者 week after week 攻击你，你可能开始烦他。
这些情绪本身不是错误——关键是别被它牵着走，而是拿它当数据：「我为什么突然想保护这个人？也许他在我面前重现了和母亲的关系。」
考试考点：transference＝来访者的情绪投向治疗师；counter-transference＝治疗师的情绪反应。方向别记反。

10. Resistance

Psychoanalysis is about revealing the contents of the unconscious — and some of those contents are painful. A common response is for the client to show resistance: they become reluctant to explore certain distressing thoughts and emotions.

resistance（阻抗）
The client's reluctance to explore distressing unconscious material, shown indirectly — missing or arriving late to sessions, changing the topic of conversation, falling silent at the crucial moment.

Resistance is easy to misread as rudeness or laziness. In this therapy it is neither: it is the defence mechanisms defending, exactly where the defences are needed most. That is the clue:

Resistance is information

Understanding the client's resistance becomes part of the therapy. The therapist can analyse it — because the client does not resist randomly. They resist precisely where it hurts, so the shape of the resistance traces the outline of the unconscious conflict underneath.

中文理解：迟到和跑题不是态度问题，是地图
来访者平时守时，唯独聊到父亲那周迟到半小时；平时健谈，唯独提到童年就换话题。避开哪里，哪里就有东西。
治疗师不指责阻抗，反而研究它——因为阻抗画出的「禁区」，正是潜意识冲突的藏身处。

12. Object Relations: The Idea

One school of thought grew directly out of psychoanalysis — and changed what therapy looks at.

object relations school of thought（客体关系学派）
A psychodynamic approach that focuses on the relationships we have with others, and the internal representations of others — known as “objects” — in the development of the human psyche. It emphasises the importance of early childhood relationships with parents.
human psyche（人类心智）
The human mind with all its conscious and unconscious aspects: thoughts, feelings, memories, and wishes.
internal working model（内部工作模型）
A mental construct formed from our attachment experiences, which influences our perception of relationships as stable or unstable, trusting or untrusting, secure or insecure.

The word object sounds cold, but it just means a person as represented inside your mind — not the real person, but your internal copy of them. The theory's chain of reasoning runs like this:

Figure 4.1.6d · Object relations in one line: early attachments become internal objects, and internal objects become the working model the adult lives by.

These early relationships shape our understanding of the world. A child whose first attachments were reliable builds a model in which people can be trusted; a child whose first attachments were frightening builds a model in which closeness means danger. Either way, the model was built before the child had any say in it — which is why it operates so invisibly in adult life.

中文理解：object 不是「物体」，是「住在你心里的那个人」
客体关系学派的「客体」＝你头脑中对某个重要他人的内部表征（internal representation）。
链条：早期和父母的依恋 → 心里留下「人大概是什么样的」内部模型（internal working model）→ 成年后按这个模板去建立关系。
类比：小时候学会的母语口音，你自己察觉不到，却决定你一生的口音——早期关系就是关系的「母语」。

13. Object Relations Therapy

Object relations therapy is a non-directive therapy. Its aim is to understand the client's relationships and the internal representations of objects — the people carried inside.

What happens in the therapy

Early relationships on the table

Because the school focuses on early attachments, the therapy emphasises experiences in early childhood and the quality of the attachments that were formed. The client is encouraged to talk about these early relationships and the feelings and thoughts they have about them.

The psychoanalytic toolkit, reused

Like psychoanalysis, the therapist analyses incidents of transference and counter-transference. There is also analysis of symbols and exploration of the unconscious.

A different centre of gravity

But the focus stays on relationships with others and how they have been internalised — not on drives or wishes, as in classical psychoanalysis.

The goal

Differentiate self from others, and build boundaries

The goal of the therapy is to help clients differentiate between their self-identity and others, and to establish boundaries between them. If your model of relationships was written by someone else in your first years of life, therapy helps you see which parts are truly you — and where other people end and you begin.

中文理解：治疗目标为什么是「分清你和别人」
如果早期关系一团糟，来访者心里的「你」「我」边界常常是模糊的：要么过度依赖（分不清自己要什么），要么过度隔离（不敢让人靠近）。
治疗做的事＝陪着来访者把心里那些「别人的声音」一个个认出来：这个害怕是我自己的，还是我妈妈塞给我的？认出来，边界就立起来了。

14. Evaluation · Psychoanalysis

Strengths

It treats the whole person

Psychoanalysis takes into account many factors about a person that may have led to mental health problems — history, relationships, unconscious conflict. Unlike systematic desensitisation, which focuses on learned behaviour, it is a holistic therapy.

It has been productive

It has led to the development of other therapies, most notably the object relations school of thought. A therapy whose ideas keep generating new treatments is doing something right.

Limitations

1. Interpretation is subjective — so the therapy is unreliable

Much of the therapy's principles are derived from psychodynamic theory, and Freud built that theory from case studies of his own patients. The interpretations of symbols and behaviours — in free association, dream analysis and transference — are made by the therapist about the nature of the unconscious, and they are subjective. A different therapist may have a completely different interpretation of the same dream. If the diagnosis depends on who is listening, psychoanalysis is an unreliable therapy.

2. It is slow, expensive and inaccessible

Sessions last around an hour, once a week, and therapy can go on for many years. It is a talking therapy that requires a degree of self-insight and understanding to take part in at all — which makes it a therapy that is inaccessible for many people, both financially and psychologically.

3. The effectiveness evidence is uncomfortable

Hans Eysenck (1962) reviewed research into the effectiveness of psychoanalysis compared with other therapies in the treatment of neurosis. Psychoanalysis appeared effective in around half of the patients treated — but more patients seemed to recover after no treatment at all. Later research suggests different rates of effectiveness and of spontaneous remission, but the deeper problem stands: there is no consistent measure of “cure”, and individual differences between patients, therapists, and the type and duration of therapy make conclusions unreliable.

spontaneous remission（自发缓解）
Recovery without treatment — the awkward benchmark any therapy must beat. If people recover anyway, an apparent cure may not be a cure.
中文理解：Eysenck 这刀为什么狠
治好一半听起来不错？但 Eysenck 说：不治疗的自己好了更多。这就好比一种药吃好转了一半的人，不吃药好了一半以上——药的价值在哪？
再加上「治愈」没有统一标准、来访者/治疗师/疗程都差异巨大，效果研究几乎没法做干净。这是 AO3 里最锋利的一条 limitation。

15. Evaluation · Object Relations

Object relations therapy inherits both the strengths and the problems of its parent.

Limitation — the same subjectivity

The same criticism made of psychoanalysis can be made here: the therapist analyses symbols in the client's internal world and the impact of early relationships on the internal working model, and that analysis is subjective.

However — and this is the textbook's important qualification — therapists are trained to acknowledge and minimise subjective interpretation. Instead of pronouncing meanings from above, they work with the client in a collaborative way to facilitate a shared understanding of the client's experiences. Two minds checking each other is still not objectivity, but it is a step towards it.

Strength — a solid developmental base

Object relations therapy is built on extensive work in developmental psychology demonstrating the importance of early childhood relationships for later development. Its central claim — that early attachments matter — is one of the best-supported ideas in the whole discipline. (You will meet it again in Topic E, with Bowlby's theory of attachment and Ainsworth's attachment types.)

Limitation — case studies again

In the same way as psychoanalysis, object relations therapy is built on case study evidence, and it is difficult to measure its effectiveness. The therapy is also long-term, which raises the same issues of cost and accessibility.

中文理解：一个「但书」值得背
考试最容易拿分的写法是先给批评再给转折：「主观性依旧存在，但是治疗师受过训练，会承认并尽量减少主观解释，与来访者合作达成共同理解。」——这句话同时展示你 knows the weakness 和 knows the defence，比单写一边高级。

💡 From the textbook

When asked to explain or discuss how a therapy is conducted, it is a process — like following a recipe. Frame the answer with firstly, secondly, thirdly so you write enough points and the examiner can see you know the sequence.

The 8-mark paragraph that usually scores

AO1 (about 4 marks). Psychoanalysis is a non-directive therapy which aims to give the client self-insight by making the unconscious conscious. The therapist would begin with an assessment of the client's concerns, history, relationships and symptoms. In sessions, the client would free-associate — speaking without censoring anything — so that weakened defences allow unconscious material to surface. Dreams would be analysed: the therapist interprets the manifest content, looking through displacement to the latent wish. Finally, the therapist would interpret transference — the client's feelings towards the therapist as if they were a parent — and any resistance, building a picture of the unconscious conflict and explaining it.

AO3 (about 4 marks). A strength is that the therapy is holistic: it considers many factors in the person's history rather than one behaviour, and it has generated further therapies such as object relations. However, interpretations are subjective — a different therapist might interpret the same dream differently — which makes the therapy unreliable. It is also time-consuming and costly, with weekly sessions for years, and Eysenck (1962) found only about half of patients improved while more recovered with no treatment at all, with no consistent measure of cure.

19. Exam Lab

Question 1 · Saisha and Omal (8 marks)

Saisha is a psychoanalyst. She has a new client, Omal, who has come to her for psychoanalysis because he has a phobia of flying. Discuss how Saisha might use psychoanalysis to help Omal with his phobia of flying. (8 marks)

AO1 4 marks · AO3 4 marks. Keep referring back to Omal — a generic answer scores 0 in many mark schemes.

Model answer

AO1. Psychoanalysis is a non-directive therapy which aims to give the client self-insight by making the unconscious conscious — the idea being that Omal's fear of flying is a surface symptom of an unconscious conflict. Saisha would begin with an assessment of Omal's current concerns, personal history, relationships and symptoms. In sessions, Omal would lie on the couch and free-associate, speaking without censoring anything, so that his weakened defences allow unconscious material to surface. Saisha would analyse his dreams: the frightening flight is the manifest content, and through displacement it may disguise a latent wish or fear with no obvious link to planes. She would also interpret transference — if Omal begins to treat her as he once treated a parent, his deepest relationship patterns are being re-enacted in the room — and any resistance, such as missed sessions, as a map of what he cannot yet face. By explaining these interpretations, Saisha helps Omal relive the conflict safely as an adult and resolve it.

AO3. A strength is that psychoanalysis is holistic — it considers many factors in Omal's history rather than treating the symptom alone, so if his flying phobia is rooted in something deeper, the therapy may reach it. It also has generative value: it produced the object relations school. However, the interpretations Saisha offers are subjective — another analyst might read Omal's dog dream completely differently — so the therapy is unreliable. It is also slow and costly (weekly sessions, potentially for years) and Eysenck (1962) found only about half of neurotic patients improved, while more recovered with no treatment at all, with no consistent measure of cure. For a specific phobia, systematic desensitisation would be faster and better evidenced — which is a comparison the examiner rewards.

Question 2 · Object relations therapy (4 marks)

Explain one strength and one weakness of the object relations school of thought therapy. (4 marks)

Model answer

Strength. The therapy rests on extensive work in developmental psychology showing that early childhood relationships shape later development, so its central assumption is well supported by research — unlike therapies whose assumptions come from a single theorist's cases. Its therapists are also trained to acknowledge and minimise subjective interpretation, working collaboratively with the client towards a shared understanding, which partly answers the charge of subjectivity.

Weakness. Like psychoanalysis, it is built on case study evidence, so its effectiveness is difficult to measure: there is no consistent measure of cure, and interpretations of the client's internal world remain subjective. A different therapist could draw different conclusions from the same material, making the therapy unreliable.

20. Key Terms

psychoanalysis 精神分析
Freud's non-directive therapy: making the unconscious conscious so the client gains self-insight and resolves unconscious conflicts.
non-directive therapy 非指导性治疗
Therapy focused on making sense of the client's experiences; the client leads rather than follows instructions.
free association 自由联想
Speaking freely without censoring anything, however embarrassing or irrelevant, to weaken defences and surface unconscious material.
manifest content 显性梦境
The dream as experienced and reported — the disguise, not the wish.
latent content 隐性梦境
The actual unconscious wish or desire the dream conceals.
dreamwork 梦的工作
The process altering and disguising latent content into the manifest dream.
displacement 置换
Dreaming of something symbolic of something else — the feelings move to a substitute object.
transference 移情
The client transfers feelings about a person or situation onto the therapist — positive, hostile, romantic or dependent — as if the therapist were a parent.
counter-transference 反移情
The therapist's emotional response to the client's transference — warmth, irritation, protectiveness, romantic feelings — to be interpreted, not suppressed.
resistance 阻抗
Reluctance to explore distressing material, shown by lateness, missed sessions or changing the topic; itself evidence of where the conflict lies.
object 客体
In object relations theory, the internal representation of another person carried in the mind.
object relations school 客体关系学派
A psychodynamic approach focusing on internal representations of others and the role of early childhood attachments in shaping the psyche.
internal working model 内部工作模型
A mental construct from attachment experiences shaping how relationships are perceived — stable or unstable, trusting or untrusting, secure or insecure.
human psyche 人类心智
The mind with all its conscious and unconscious aspects: thoughts, feelings, memories and wishes.
spontaneous remission 自发缓解
Recovery without treatment — the benchmark any therapy must beat, central to Eysenck's critique.
neurosis 神经症
The category of milder mental disorders Eysenck (1962) studied when reviewing psychoanalysis's effectiveness.
