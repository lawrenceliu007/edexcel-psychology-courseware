1.1.4 TYPES AND EXPLANATIONS OF CONFORMITY
从众的类型与解释

Social Psychology · Kelman 三类型 + Deutsch & Gerrard 双解释 + 社会角色 + Latané 社会影响理论
🧠 Core idea: the group, not an order 👥 Three types, two explanations 📖 Textbook: Chapter 2

1. Getting Started

🫙 The jar of beans Try it on yourself

Your teacher holds up a jar full of coloured beans and asks everyone to write down privately how many beans it holds. You write your estimate. Then the class discusses it and agrees on a group estimate — say, most people are saying “about 850”.

Now the teacher asks everyone to write a second private estimate. Your first guess was 600. What happens to your second one?

中文理解
没有人命令你改答案。没有老师施压、没有惩罚、没有奖励 —— 你只是听到别人怎么想，然后自己悄悄改了。这就是从众（conformity）和服从（obedience）最大的区别：服从有人下命令，从众没有人下命令，只有一群人和他们的影响。
1932 年心理学家 Jenness 用真的豆子瓶做过这个实验 —— 结果大多数人的第二次估计都向小组的估计靠近了。

2. What Conformity Is

The definition 背下来

Conformity（从众） is when individuals change their behaviour or attitudes to align with those of the majority. The pressure to conform may be real or imagined — nobody has to say anything out loud.

Two words you must not confuse（接 1.1.2）

TermWhat it means
obedience（服从）Following a direct order from someone perceived to have authority. One man, one order.
conformity（从众）Changing your behaviour to match a majority or group — no order is given.
private acceptance（私下接受） — genuinely adopting the behaviours or values of the majority; you really believe it.
public acceptance（公开顺从） — simply going along with the majority even if you do not agree with them.
中文理解：先记住这两根轴
判断任何一种从众，问两个问题：
① 你心里真的信了吗？（私下接受 vs 公开顺从）
② 离开这个群体后改变还在吗？（持久 vs 暂时）
Kelman 的三种类型（下一节）就是这两根轴组合出来的。

3. Kelman's Three Types

Herbert Kelman (1958) proposed that there are three types of conformity. Before you learn the names, look at the map — the three types differ in how deep the change goes and how long it lasts.

Figure 1 — the three types, arranged by how deep and how lasting the change is.
中文理解：一张图记住三种
compliance = 表面跟着走（心里不信，人群散了就变回去）；identification = 想当“自己人”（在这个圈子里就那样做，离开圈子就不做了）；internalisation = 真的信了（变成自己的观点，最持久）。

4. Compliance

Compliance（表面顺从） involves going along with the majority to gain social approval or avoid social rejection, even if we do not agree with the majority opinion or behaviour. It is public acceptance only: the individual publicly goes along with the majority while privately holding different views.

Key features 考点特征

  • The change in behaviour is likely to influence the person only while majority pressure exists.
  • It will not have a lasting effect on behaviour — remove the group, and the behaviour reverts.
  • Driven by normative social influence (§9): we want approval, we fear rejection.
中文理解 + 例子
课本里的例子：全组人都在听一个新歌手，其实你不喜欢，但你也买了演唱会门票 —— 为了合群，不是为了音乐。
再一个：同事都给服务员小费，你其实不想给，但你也给了。人一走，下次你就不会给了 —— 这就是“不持久”。

5. Internalisation

Internalisation（内化） occurs when you adopt the behaviours or values of the majority because you genuinely accept them and internalise them as your own — private acceptance. This may be because the majority seems to hold knowledge or information about how to behave, so you look to them for guidance.

Key features 考点特征

  • It results in the most permanent behaviour change, because there is a genuine change in belief or behaviour.
  • Driven by informational social influence (§8): we believe the majority knows better.

The evidence: Sherif (1935) — the autokinetic effect

Muzafer Sherif used the autokinetic effect: a spot of light projected onto a screen in a dark room appears to move, even though it is stationary. Participants first estimated how far the light moved alone, then in groups.

Finding: individual estimates converged to match the group estimate. Because there was no real correct answer — the light never moved — this conformity was probably internalisation: with no facts to check against, people genuinely adopted the group's answer.

中文理解：为什么“没有正确答案”是关键
黑屋里光点其实没动，但你看起来它在动 —— 每个人都不知道真相。在模糊情境（ambiguous situation）里，别人就是我们唯一的信息来源，所以“跟大伙儿一致”是理性的。信了，就是 internalisation。
对比记忆：1.1.2 里 Milgram 的实验有明确对错（线有多长、答案是什么），那是另一回事 —— Asch 要在答案明显的情况下测从众，下一章 1.1.5 讲。

6. Identification

Identification（认同） occurs when we change our behaviour while in the company of a majority in order to fit in. We do it because we want to define ourselves as a member of a group, so we adopt their beliefs and behaviours.

Key features 考点特征

  • The change is temporary: it exists because of group membership.
  • Classic example: adopting a style of dress when with a friendship group — and dressing differently elsewhere.
  • Driven by normative social influence: the reward is belonging.
中文理解：compliance 和 identification 的区别在哪？
两者都不是真信。区别在动机：compliance 是怕被拒绝 / 想被认可（我被迫跟着走）；identification 是想成为这个群体的一员，把群体的做法当成“我们做的事”（我愿意跟着走）。
两者都是公开顺从、都不持久 —— 考试里最容易混的就是这一对。

8. Informational Social Influence

Morton Deutsch and Harold Gerrard (1955) proposed two explanations for why we conform: informational and normative social influence.

Informational social influence（信息性社会影响，ISI）

In a situation where we are unsure how to act or think, we look to others to guide us. We conform to the majority because we believe it is the correct way to behave — and we accept it.

  • Explains conformity in ambiguous situations（模糊情境）.
  • Involves a rational judgement based on the information available.
  • Leads to internalisation — we genuinely believe the majority view (private acceptance).

The textbook example 课本例子

In a high-end restaurant, a diner looks at other guests to see which cutlery to use for the starter, because they do not know which fork is correct. The other diners are the best available source of information.

中文理解：一句话抓住 ISI
“我不知道，所以他们大概知道。” —— 触发条件是不确定；结果是真的信（internalisation）。关键词：ambiguous、rational、information、correct。

9. Normative Social Influence

Normative social influence（规范性社会影响，NSI）

Sometimes we conform because we have a need to be accepted and to fit into a group — or to avoid rejection. We do not necessarily believe the majority is right; we just want to belong.

  • Peer pressure is a common form, especially in adolescence when belonging matters most.
  • Leads to compliance and identification — public acceptance without genuine belief.

The two textbook examples

NSI example: in a friendship group, many members start listening to a new musician. Not all of them like the music — but they all buy tickets to the live show.

Evidence: Asch (1956) demonstrated NSI in a series of experiments where participants sometimes selected an answer that was obviously incorrect because of social pressure to go along with the group judgement. (Full detail in 1.1.5.)

Figure 2 — the two routes into conformity. The situation decides which explanation, and the explanation decides which type.
中文理解：一句话抓住 NSI
“我知道他们不对，但我不想被孤立。” —— 触发条件是想被接受 / 怕被拒绝；结果是公开顺从但心里不信（compliance / identification）。关键词：approval、rejection、peer pressure、belonging。
考试连招：ISI → internalisation；NSI → compliance + identification。写反了就丢分。

10. Evaluating ISI & NSI

▶ Supporting evidence — and where it comes from
  • STUDY Jenness (1932) — the beans experiment: participants judged how many beans were in a glass bottle alone, then produced a negotiated group estimate, then made a second individual estimate. Individuals shifted their second estimate toward the group estimate. This demonstrates ISI: the situation was ambiguous, and the group perhaps had more accurate information.
  • STUDY Sherif (1935) — participants adjusted their estimate of the light's movement to the group estimate because they were unsure of their own answer — again ISI.
  • STUDY Asch (1956) — participants gave an obviously incorrect answer when the whole group gave the wrong one, even though the correct answer was clear — NSI.
▶ Criticisms — what the two explanations miss
  • LAB ONLY 支持研究都是实验室实验：They demonstrate the process of majority influence, but tell us little about social influence in real-life situations such as peer pressure at school or work.
  • MOTIVATION 忽略动机：The explanation does not account for the underlying motivation to conform or not. For example, we might keep to speed limits simply for personal safety — not because of missing information or group pressure.
  • INDIVIDUAL 忽略个体差异：It ignores personality, gender and cultural values that influence conformity, and does not fully explore the situational factors known to affect whether we conform or resist.
Exam shape. A 4-mark evaluation answer = two points, each with a consequence. “Jenness (1932) supports ISI because second estimates moved toward the group, which shows people use others as information in ambiguous situations.” The which shows… clause is where the second mark lives.

11. Conformity to Social Roles

Roles and norms

Social situations are governed by social norms — unwritten rules about how to behave in a situation. Individuals adopt or are assigned social roles（社会角色）, and behave according to the norms of that role.

Conformity to a social role is a type of identification. We conform to the norms of the role while we are in that role — but not when we are out of it.

The textbook example

Being a teacher is a role that carries norms: a teacher is expected to be an upstanding citizen and role model. A teacher conforms to the role at school — and may behave quite differently during the school holidays. We all play multiple social roles and adjust our behaviour to each.

中文理解
你在学校是“学生”，在家是“孩子”，在球场上是“队友” —— 每个身份自带一套行为规范，你切换身份时行为也跟着切换。这就是 identification：身份在，改变就在；身份摘掉，改变就消失。

12. The Prison Studies

Zimbardo et al. (1973) — the Stanford prison study

Philip Zimbardo demonstrated the power of conformity to social roles in a study at Stanford University. College students were recruited to play the role of either prison guard or prisoner in a simulated prison in the basement of the university.

What happened: over a short period, the ‘prisoners' became passive, and the ‘prison guards' became aggressive and controlling. They had adopted the social roles they had been given.

The replication: Reicher and Haslam (BBC prison study)

Steve Reicher and Alex Haslam's BBC prison study replicated Zimbardo's — and showed that conformity to social roles was not simply unthinkingly playing a role you are given. It is dynamic and influenced by factors such as identification with the group you represent.

中文理解：为什么复制的结论不一样也很重要
Zimbardo 说“角色太强，一穿上就变了”；BBC 复制版说“没那么简单，关键看你是否认同这个群体”。考试里这是一对现成的 evaluate 素材：Zimbardo 是证据，Reicher & Haslam 是边界条件 —— 说明角色从众不是自动发生的。

13. Social Impact Theory

Latané (1981) — the theory

Social impact theory explains many forms of social influence, including both conformity and obedience. We are greatly influenced by the actions of others: we can be persuaded, inhibited, threatened and supported by other people. Latané called this social impact — the effect others, real or imagined, have on us.

He distinguished the source（影响源）— the influencer, in conformity the majority — from the target（目标）— the person feeling the pressure, the individual.

Three social forces determine the impact

ForceWhat it means
Strength 强度The real or perceived status, legitimacy or authority of the source. The stronger the majority, the more likely someone is to conform.
Immediacy 即时性The proximity or distance between source and target. A physically close majority has more influence.
Number 数量How many sources and targets there are. A greater majority exerts more influence — but only up to a point.

Latané expressed the combined effect as a formula: I = f(S·I·N) — impact is a function of Strength × Immediacy × Number. The effect is multiplicative: the combination matters more than any single factor.

Figure 3 — the light bulb analogy for the force of Number: each added source contributes less than the one before.
中文理解：S·I·N 三个力一起作用
记法：强不强（Strength）、近不近（Immediacy）、多不多（Number）。三者是相乘不是相加 —— 有一项为零，整体影响就大打折扣。比如人数再多，但如果权威毫无地位（Strength 弱）、离得很远（Immediacy 弱），影响也有限。

14. SIT Applied

SIT links back to the three types

Type of conformityWhat SIT says
ComplianceExplained by normative influence: a majority with strength, immediacy and number pressures the individual to conform publicly while privately disagreeing.
IdentificationAlso normative: the majority's perceived power motivates the individual to identify with the group and its norms.
InternalisationExplained by informational influence: a majority with strength, immediacy and number is seen as credible, so the individual internalises its beliefs.

SIT also explains obedience

Authority figures who are perceived to be legitimate (strength), who are immediate to the individual, and who are greater in number will be more likely to command obedience. Notice how neatly this maps onto Milgram's variations from 1.1.2: remove presence (immediacy) and obedience fell to 20.5%.

social engineering（社会工程） — Latané's term for using knowledge of social impact to design better social settings: choosing leaders for their strength of influence, deciding how many students belong in a classroom, managing crowds. Others might consider it a form of social control.
中文理解：一个理论管两章
SIT 是 Topic A 里少见的通用理论：既能解释从众（1.1.4）也能解释服从（1.1.1/1.1.2）。考试问 “describe a theory of conformity or obedience”，SIT 是性价比最高的选择 —— 一套 S·I·N 用两处。

15. Evaluating SIT

▶ Supporting evidence
  • STUDY Berkowitz, Bickman and Milgram (1969) — the street-corner study. Between 1 and 15 confederates stood on a New York street craning their necks to look up at the sixth floor of a university building; Milgram recorded from a sixth-floor window and counted how many passers-by stopped to look up too. Finding: more confederates produced more copying — but the effect levelled off, exactly as the force of Number predicts.
  • STUDY Asch (1956) — conformity rose as the majority grew from 1 to 3 confederates, but larger majorities produced no significant further increase. The presence of just one dissenter reduced conformity. Both findings support the diminishing effect of Number.
▶ Criticisms
  • REDUCTIONIST 简化论：SIT views individuals as passive receivers of others' behaviour, disregarding the active nature of social interaction and what the target themselves brings to the situation.
  • INDIVIDUAL 忽略个体差异：Everyone is different — some of us are more independent and therefore more resistant to majority influence. SIT oversimplifies human interaction by ignoring this.
Exam tip from the textbook. “Describe a theory” questions need no evaluation — give an account of the key elements and stop. For SIT, revise with prompts: strength, number, immediacy, multiplicative. Each prompt should trigger one sentence.

17. Exam Lab

How to read the question

Describe — give an account. No evaluation, no judgement. For a theory: name the theorist, list the key elements, stop.
Explain — give the reason or mechanism. One point plus one reason per mark.

Q1a · Fezile — compliance (2 marks)

Fezile is asked by her teacher to volunteer at a local primary school to help younger children learn to read. She agrees to help, but has no interest in teaching young children and would rather canoe with her friends.

Fezile is showing compliance: she publicly goes along with her teacher's request to appear helpful, but she does not privately agree with it — she would rather be canoeing. Because compliance is only public acceptance, Fezile's helpful behaviour is likely to last only while the teacher's pressure exists, and she is unlikely to keep volunteering once she is free to choose.

Q1b · Fezile — internalisation (2 marks)

Fezile is also asked to volunteer to train young children to canoe. She chooses not to go canoeing with her friends so that she can teach the children instead.

Fezile is showing internalisation: she has genuinely accepted the value of helping children learn to canoe, so it has become her own belief — private acceptance. This is why she gives up something she actually wants (canoeing with friends) with no majority present to see her sacrifice: the change is real and self-sustaining, not dependent on the group.

Q2 · Kasim — conformity (2 marks)

Kasim is with a large group of friends at a youth club. His friends start ripping the material on the seat covers and drawing on tables. Kasim asks them to stop, but his friends laugh at him and hand him a pen to join in. Kasim does not want to join in, but he writes on the table.

Kasim conforms because of normative social influence: he changes his behaviour to match the majority of his friendship group even though he privately disagrees — he actually asked them to stop. His need to be accepted by the group and to avoid rejection is stronger than his own opinion, so he complies publicly. Because this is compliance rather than genuine belief, the behaviour depends on the group being present.

Exam tip from the textbook. For any named study you may be asked how it could be improved. Take a weakness, turn it into an improvement, then justify why it is an improvement: “Asch could include female participants instead of just 50 male students — this would make his findings about conformity more generalisable to a wider target population.”

18. Key Terms

conformity 从众
Changing behaviour or attitudes to align with those of the majority — no order is given.
compliance 表面顺从
Going along with the majority for approval or to avoid rejection, without genuine agreement (public acceptance only).
internalisation 内化
Genuinely accepting the majority's values as your own (private acceptance) — the most permanent change.
identification 认同
Changing behaviour to fit in because you want to belong to a group — temporary, tied to membership.
private acceptance 私下接受
Genuinely adopting the majority's behaviours or values as your own.
public acceptance 公开顺从
Going along with the majority outwardly while privately disagreeing.
informational social influence 信息性社会影响
Conforming because we believe the majority knows the correct answer — ambiguous situations, rational, leads to internalisation.
normative social influence 规范性社会影响
Conforming because we need to be accepted and fear rejection — leads to compliance and identification.
social role 社会角色
A position in a social situation with its own norms of behaviour — conforming to a role is identification.
social norms 社会规范
Unwritten rules about how to behave in a given social situation.
social impact theory 社会影响理论
Latané (1981): impact on a target is a function of the source's Strength, Immediacy and Number — I = f(S·I·N).
source / target 影响源 / 目标
The source is the influencer (the majority); the target is the person feeling the pressure (the individual).
multiplicative 相乘的
The combined effect of strength, immediacy and number is greater than their individual effects.
autokinetic effect 自主运动效应
Optical illusion where a stationary spot of light in a dark room appears to move — used by Sherif (1935).
social engineering 社会工程
Latané's idea of applying social impact theory to design settings — leadership, classroom size, crowds; seen by some as social control.
visual estimation 目视估计
Estimating the value or size of something using sight alone — the basis of Jenness's beans study.
