1.1.7 FACTORS AFFECTING CONFORMITY AND MINORITY INFLUENCE
影响从众与少数派影响的因素 · individual differences · situation · culture

Social Psychology · Same experiment, different people, different situations, different cultures — different results.
🧠 Core idea: three lenses — person, situation, culture 📊 Packed with named studies 📖 Textbook Book 1 · Ch.3 · pp.32–35

What this section is really asking

  1. Who conforms and who resists? Personality matters: an internal locus of control and a high desire for control both make people harder to sway.
  2. When is conformity strongest? Situation matters: group size, a united majority, ambiguous situations and high-status sources all raise it; a broken majority collapses it (33% → 5.5%).
  3. Where is conformity strongest? Culture matters: collectivistic cultures conform more than individualistic ones (Smith & Bond, 1996 — 133 studies, 17 countries), and Berry (1967) traced the difference to how a culture gets its food.
  4. The same three lenses also decide whether a minority gets heard — which is why 1.1.6 ended with this page.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Explain how the situation (group size, unanimity, task difficulty) changes conformity rates.
  • Explain cultural differences between individualist and collectivist societies.
  • Explain individual-difference factors such as locus of control and expertise.
  • Use these factors to explain why conformity rates differ across studies and populations.
  • Apply the factors to a scenario and predict whether conformity rises or falls.

1. Getting Started

In Asch's baseline study, about three in four participants went along with the wrong answer at least once; one in four never did. Some people caved in minutes; others held out from the first trial.

Stop and think about what that means. The situation was identical for everyone — same room, same task, the same seven confederates. So the difference between those who yielded and those who resisted has to come from something the situation didn't contain: the person. Their personality. What they believe about who controls their life.

The question this chapter answers

Asch and Moscovici told us that people yield to influence. This page tells us how much — and what makes the difference: the individual differences (personality) we bring, the situation we are placed in, and the culture we grew up in.

🎯 Have a guess first No notes

Two students hear the same viral rumour. One checks the facts and shrugs it off; the other shares it within minutes. Which difference best explains this?

Answer B — and that belief about control has a technical name, which is where we start.

中文理解：一个实验，两种人
Asch 的实验里条件完全相同，但结果不同：说明除了情境，人的内部还有变量。
本章就是一套「三层滤镜」：你是谁（personality）→ 你在什么情境（situation）→ 你来自什么文化（culture）。三层滤镜叠在一起，才决定一个人从众多少。

2. Three Lenses on Influence

The specification splits the factors into three groups. Keep them as three drawers — every fact on this page belongs in exactly one drawer, and the exam questions map onto them one by one.

1 · Individual differences (personality)

What you bring into the room: locus of control, desire for control, self-esteem, need for social approval, anxiety, insecurity.

2 · Situation

What the room contains: size of the majority and of the minority, unity of the majority, how ambiguous the situation is, and the status/credibility of the source.

3 · Culture

What the room sits inside: collectivistic vs individualistic societies, and even how a culture gets its food (Berry, 1967).

💡 Key point

The exam practice for this chapter is literally three questions: "Explain one individual difference… one situational factor… one factor associated with culture… which may influence conformity and/or minority influence. (2 marks each)". Three drawers, three questions. Organise your revision the same way.

中文理解：三个抽屉，各放各的
人：控制点（locus of control）与控制欲（desire for control），再加上低自尊、需要被认可、焦虑和不安；
情境：多数派/少数派规模、多数派是否团结、情境是否模糊，以及信息来源的地位与可信度；
文化：集体主义 vs 个人主义、Berry 的农耕 vs 狩猎。
答题时先判断题目问的是哪个抽屉，再从对应抽屉里拿证据，不会乱。

3. Individual Difference 1: Locus of Control

Similar to obedience, there are individual differences between us that mean some people are more likely to conform than others. The first is a belief system about control itself.

locus of control（控制点）
A belief system which determines our perception of control over events that occur. The "locus" is the location of control — inside you, or outside you.

Internal locus of control

"What happens to me depends on what I do." Such a person may feel less pressure from others, is less likely to comply with group norms or feel a need to fit in — and is therefore less susceptible to normative social influence.

External locus of control

"What happens to me depends on luck, fate and other people." Such a person tends to look to others to help guide their decisions and rely on others' opinions — and is therefore more susceptible to normative social influence.

中文理解：命运的方向盘握在谁手里？
Internal（内控）＝方向盘在我手里：考试没考好，是「我没复习够」→ 既然结果由我决定，别人的意见只是参考。
External（外控）＝方向盘在别人/运气手里：没考好是「题目太偏、运气差」→ 那就多听听别人怎么说吧，反正我自己说了不算。
记法：外控 → 依赖他人意见 → 更容易从众（NSI）；内控 → 反之。

4. Locus of Control: The Evidence

Three studies — and a meta-analysis to tie them together. Learn them as a chain: two early experiments, one big summary.

Study
What they did
What they found
Biondo et al. (1971)
Compared how students with internal vs external loci of control responded to attempts to change their attitudes.
Externals were more influenced by overt attempts to persuade attitude change than internals. Internals reacted against overt influence and ignored subtle influence.
Ritchie & Phares (1969)
Tested how persuasion worked depending on who delivered it.
Externals were more persuaded by communication from a source of high prestige than internals were.
Avtgis (1998)
Conducted a meta-analysis of studies on locus of control and social influence — gathering and analysing other people's data to compare effect sizes.
Most research indicated that higher external locus of control scores went with more conformity. But the evidence is mixed: the link depends on the type and source of the persuasion.
meta-analysis（元分析）
When a researcher gathers and analyses other people's data to look at effect sizes, to draw conclusions about research in a topic area. One big conclusion from many small studies.

🎯 Quick check No notes

An internal student receives an obvious, direct attempt to change their opinion. Based on Biondo et al. (1971), what do they do?

Keep the nuance

Avtgis's meta-analysis supports the general trend — but evidence in this area is mixed and the link depends on the type and source of the persuasion. Many different factors play a role in social influence; locus of control is one of them, not the whole story.

中文理解：三个证据连成一条线
Biondo（外控的人挡不住正面说服）＋ Ritchie & Phares（外控的人特别吃「权威来源」那一套）＝ 两个实验；
Avtgis (1998) 元分析＝ 把一摊研究合起来看，结论仍是 外控 → 更从众，但要补一句 but 证据并不一致，取决于说服的类型和来源，这句 "but" 在 AO3 里值钱。

5. Individual Difference 2: Desire for Control

Where locus of control is about belief, the second difference is about motivation.

desire for control（控制欲）
Proposed by Jerry Burger and Harris Cooper (1979): individual differences in our motivation to control events in our environment.

High desire for control

Tends to be assertive, decisive and to take leadership roles within a group. More likely to resist other opinions and beliefs.

Low desire for control

Tends to be passive and influenced by others.

Burger (1987): putting the idea to the test

Fifty-five student participants were placed in groups of four and asked to rate the funniness of a cartoon on a scale of 1–100. Each participant gave their rating publicly, after three other participants — who were confederates of the study — had given theirs. Burger found that participants with a high desire for control were less likely to conform to the group norm by agreeing with the confederates' judgements.

Design detail
Why it matters
Cartoon ratings, 1–100
There is no correct answer — this is an opinion task, so conformity to the group's "norm" of funniness is easy to measure against the confederates' numbers.
Speaking after three confederates
Each real participant always answered last, so the group "norm" was already established before their turn — the classic Asch set-up, in a modern disguise.
Public rating
Saying the number out loud adds normative pressure — you cannot quietly disagree; the group hears your answer.
中文理解：belief vs desire：两个「控制」别混
Locus of control ＝ 你相信控制权在哪（belief/信念）；
Desire for control ＝ 你多想掌控一切（motivation/动机）。
类比：一个人可能相信「成绩靠努力」（内控），但并不特别想当班长（低控制欲），两者是不同的东西。考试最爱考的就是把这两个词互换着骗你。

6. Other Characteristics That Matter

Personality does not stop at control. Four more characteristics can explain variations in social influence:

Characteristic
Typical link
Low self-esteem（低自尊）
If you do not trust your own judgement, other people's judgements fill the gap — more room for influence.
High need for social approval（高社会赞许需要）
If being liked matters above all, agreeing with the group is the safest route — more normative conformity.
Anxiety（焦虑）
Anxious situations and anxious people look to others for cues about what to do.
Insecurity（不安全感）
Feeling unsure of your standing makes the group's opinion feel heavier.

These four are "also on the list" rather than deeply studied here — the examinable personalities are locus of control and desire for control, each with their named studies.

中文理解：一个共同主题
低自尊、需要认可、焦虑与不安全感，这四个词说的是同一件事：对自己的判断不够自信。
对自己越没把握的人，越需要「跟着别人走」这份安全感：这就是它们推高从众的共同逻辑。

7. Situational Factor 1: Size of the Groups

The first situational factor is simply how many people are on each side — and it works differently for majorities and minorities.

Size of the majority

As the Asch experiments demonstrated, the size of the majority changes the odds of conformity. Asch found that a majority of three was optimal — conformity rose as majority members were added, up to three — and any greater number did not exert greater influence. The pressure saturates.

Size of the minority

The size of a minority can also influence whether a majority can be persuaded — however, research is mixed, and the arguments point both ways:

Case for a large minority

A large minority has more visual presence — it is harder to ignore five campaigners than one.

Case for a small minority

A small minority can still be effective if their argument is strong and consistent — a tiny consistent group stands out against the crowd, which is where attention begins.

Risk for a small minority

A small minority could be discounted as extreme or eccentric — "it's just those two again".

Risk for a large minority

A large minority risks being seen as "just another group" rather than a cause — size alone persuades nobody.

中文理解：多数派有「最优人数」，少数派没有定论
多数派：3 个人就是上限，再加人也白加（Asch 的饱和效应）。
少数派：研究结论不一（research is mixed），大有大的显眼，小有小的聚焦。
AO3 现成素材：写到少数派规模时，别只写一边，把 "however, research is mixed" 这句亮出来。

8. Situational Factor 2: Unity of the Majority

Majority unity can also influence conformity — and this is one of the most quotable numbers in the topic.

The broken-majority effect

As found in the Asch experiments: when a majority is broken — one dissenting confederate starts giving different answers — conformity fell from 33% to 5.5%.

Why such a collapse? A united majority says "everyone agrees, so you must be wrong." A broken majority says "reasonable people can disagree." The social cost of disagreeing suddenly drops — the participant is no longer alone against the world, they are one of two.

中文理解：33% → 5.5%，一句话记忆
多数派「团结」时，反对一个人 = 对抗全世界，33% 的人从众；
只要有一个同盟军开口，反对的人不再孤单，从众率跌到 5.5%，只剩原来的 六分之一。
这也是 33% 这个数字的考点：考「unity」必配这组数据。

9. Situational Factors 3 & 4: Ambiguity and the Source

3 · Ambiguity and uncertainty

If a situation is ambiguous or uncertain, we are more likely to conform to the opinions of others around us — because their opinions provide a source of information we need to make sense of the situation.

Which channel is this?

This is a form of informational social influence — and because people are accepting information, not just going along, it results in a more persistent change in attitude or behaviour. Compare with normative pressure: that buys public agreement, which evaporates when the group leaves the room.

4 · The source of the influence

The final situational factor is who is doing the influencing. If a source has status, reputation, credibility or expertise — whether the source is a majority or a minority — it will affect its ability to influence others.

Source quality
Effect on influence
High status / credibility / expertise
The message gets taken seriously — the classic example is environmental campaigners, whose policy has scientific support and is led by prominent activists such as Greta Thunberg. This also helps minorities: credibility is how a small group earns a hearing (and links straight back to Ritchie & Phares — prestige sources persuade externals especially).
Low credibility
The same argument falls flat — the content is the same, the messenger is not.

🎯 Quick check No notes

A confused tourist in an unfamiliar station follows the crowd towards an exit. Which factor and channel explain this best?

中文理解：越不懂，越跟人
看不懂出口指示牌（情境模糊）→ 别人走的方向就成了「信息」（ISI）；
记住后半句：ISI 造成的是「更持久」的改变，因为你真的把信息当作答案接受了，不像 NSI 只是表面合群。
「来源」这条和 1.1.6 呼应：少数派要被听见，也得有 credibility。

10. Cultural Factor 1: The Cross-Culture Scoreboard

The third drawer is the biggest: culture. Research has found that some cultures show greater conformity than others. Cross-cultural replications of Asch-type experiments have shown different levels of conformity in different countries — here is the full scoreboard.

Study
Who was tested
Finding
Neto (1995)
Female Portuguese university students
A higher rate of conformity than Asch found.
Whittaker & Meade (1967)
Students in Brazil and Zimbabwe
Brazilians: similar rates to Asch; Zimbabweans showed greater conformity.
Chandra (1973)
Native Fijians vs Indians
Native Fijians were more conforming than Indians; the Fijians displayed 36%, comparable to Asch's American students.
Timaeus (1968)
German students
Less conforming than Americans.
Milgram (1961)
Norwegians (and French)
Higher conformity in Norwegians.
Perrin & Spencer (1980)
British students (England)
A virtual absence of conformity in England.
Triandis et al. (1988)
Japanese vs US students
Japanese students reported conforming less than US students in general, but differentiated in-groups from out-groups more sharply — more likely to conform to fellow students than to strangers.
中文理解：别背国家，背「对比」
七条研究不要当七个孤立知识点背，按三组对比记：
① 更高：葡萄牙女生（Neto）、津巴布韦（W&M）、挪威人（Milgram）、斐济土著 36%（Chandra）；
② 更低：德国（Timaeus）、英国「几乎无人从众」（Perrin & Spencer）、日本人对陌生人（Triandis）；
③ 持平：巴西 ≈ Asch（W&M）。
考试写得出 1–2 个具体研究＋方向，就够 2 分。

11. Cultural Factor 2: Berry (1967) — Two Ways of Living

The most interesting cultural study does not compare countries at all. It compares two subsistence cultures — cultures defined by how they get their food.

The Temne（塞拉利昂）

The Temne from Sierra Leone exist on a single crop of food. There is an annual effort to ensure the crop is harvested — and harvesting requires cooperation. Everyone's survival depends on everyone working together.

The Canadian Inuit（因纽特人）

The Canadian Inuit are hunter-gatherers, so they rely less on collective effort in food collection. A good hunt depends on skill and judgement, not on coordinating a whole village.

The hypothesis and the result

John Berry (1967) hypothesised that the Temne would display more conformity than the Canadian Inuit. Using an adaptation of Asch's line study, he found exactly that: the Temne showed higher rates of conformity than the Canadian Inuit — which could be explained by the cooperative values instilled in the culture because of food production.

中文理解：饭碗怎么来，性格就怎么长
种单一作物（Temne）＝全村人必须配合农时 → 文化里灌输 cooperation → 从众高；
狩猎采集（Inuit）＝各凭本事 → 不依赖集体 → 从众低。
这是「文化影响从众」最漂亮的因果链：生存方式 → 文化价值观 → 从众行为，答题时把三环都写上。

12. Cultural Factor 3: Collectivist vs Individualist

Berry's two cultures point at a wider pattern. As discussed earlier in the book, collectivistic cultures place a greater emphasis on group cooperation and interdependence, so social norms are expected to be adhered to; individualistic cultures nurture greater independence and are more tolerant of dissent to social norms.

Smith & Bond (1996) — the big test
A meta-analysis of 133 conformity studies using an Asch-type procedure, across 17 collectivistic and individualistic countries. They found that collectivistic countries tended to show higher levels of conformity than individualistic ones.

The important caveat

While we can draw general conclusions from this research, many variables influence conformity, so the exact influence of socio-cultural factors cannot be determined. "Collectivistic countries conform more" is a trend across 133 studies — not a law that predicts any individual.

中文理解：三个文化研究递进
① 一堆国家各测一次（第 10 节记分板）→ 发现方向不一；
② Berry (1967) → 给出因果解释：生存方式塑造合作价值观；
③ Smith & Bond (1996) → 用 133 项研究、17 国的元分析做总结：集体主义 > 个人主义，但注明变量太多，确切影响无法确定。
三步正好是「现象 → 解释 → 总检验」。

13. Evaluation · Strengths and Limits

Strength 1 · Each factor is backed by named evidence（每个因素都有实证）

The individual-difference claims rest on experiments (Biondo et al., 1971; Ritchie & Phares, 1969; Burger, 1987), the situational claims on Asch's own manipulations, and the cultural claims on a meta-analysis of 133 studies across 17 countries (Smith & Bond, 1996). （三层证据各管一摊，结论不是拍脑袋。）

Strength 2 · The three lenses complement each other（三视角互补）

Because personality, situation and culture each explain variance the others cannot, the account is more complete than any single-factor explanation: the same situation produces different results in different people, and the same person conforms differently in different cultures.

Limitation 1 · The evidence is mixed and context-dependent（证据混杂、看情境）

Avtgis (1998) found the locus-of-control link depends on the type and source of persuasion; minority size research is mixed; and Perrin & Spencer found a virtual absence of conformity in England, showing Asch's effect is not universal even within one culture. Tidy rules ("externals conform") come with so many exceptions that the honest summary is "it depends".

Limitation 2 · Socio-cultural influence cannot be isolated（社会文化因素无法单独拆出）

The caveat: there are many variables that influence conformity, so the exact influence of socio-cultural factors cannot be determined. Culture is entangled with education, era, task and sample; cross-cultural replications differ in more than one thing at a time, so no single cause can be pinned down.

中文理解：AO3 的两把刀
这页的 evaluation 有两把最锋利的刀：
① "evidence in this area is mixed"（LOC、少数派规模都是）→ 任何「某因素导致从众」的说法都要加条件；
② "the exact influence of socio-cultural factors cannot be determined"（变量太多）→ 文化结论只能谈趋势，不能下断言。
优势面就写「三层证据 + 三视角互补」。

14. Exam Focus

The practice questions set the pattern: three short questions, one per drawer. Each is worth 2 marks — a named factor plus a correct explanation.

Question
What earns the 2 marks
1 · Explain one individual difference
Name it (e.g. internal locus of control) + explain the mechanism: they feel less pressure from others, so they are less susceptible to normative social influence and less likely to comply with group norms.
2 · Explain one situational factor
Name it (e.g. unity of the majority) + evidence: when the majority is broken, conformity fell from 33% to 5.5% — a dissenter lowers the cost of disagreeing.
3 · Explain one cultural factor
Name it (e.g. collectivism) + explanation: collectivistic cultures emphasise group cooperation and interdependence, so norms are expected to be adhered to — Smith & Bond (1996) found higher conformity across 133 studies.

💡 The 2-mark formula

Point + because. "An internal locus of control, because people who believe they control their own outcomes feel less pressure to fit in, so they resist normative social influence." One sentence of factor, one sentence of mechanism — no more is needed for 2 marks, and less is too thin.

The 8-mark paragraph that usually scores

AO1 (about 4 marks). Conformity and minority influence vary with individual differences, situation and culture. Internally-controlled people, who believe outcomes depend on their own actions, resist normative social influence, while externally-controlled people rely on others' opinions and conform more (Avtgis, 1998, meta-analysis). People with a high desire for control (Burger & Cooper, 1979) are assertive and resist: Burger (1987) found high desire-for-control participants conformed less when rating cartoons publicly after three confederates. Situationally, Asch found a majority of three was the optimal size and that a broken majority cut conformity from 33% to 5.5%; ambiguous situations raise conformity through informational social influence, and high-status, credible sources (like Greta Thunberg for environmental campaigns) influence more. Culturally, Berry (1967) found the cooperative Temne conformed more than hunter-gatherer Inuit, and Smith & Bond's (1996) meta-analysis of 133 studies in 17 countries found collectivistic countries conform more than individualistic ones.

AO3 (about 4 marks). However, the evidence is mixed and context-dependent: Avtgis notes the locus-of-control link depends on the type and source of persuasion, and minority-size research is inconclusive. Perrin and Spencer (1980) found a virtual absence of conformity in England, showing the Asch effect is not universal even within one culture. One caution applies to the cultural claims: because many variables influence conformity, the exact influence of socio-cultural factors cannot be determined. The three lenses are best treated as complementary trends, not laws.

16. Exam Lab

Question · Three factors, three students (6 marks)

Three students join the same school club. Amara rarely changes her mind no matter what the group thinks; Ben goes along with whatever the loudest members say; Chi keeps her opinions even when visiting her cousin's school abroad, but notices that students there agree with each other much more than students at her own school. Explain one individual difference, one situational factor and one cultural factor that may influence conformity, using the students as examples. (6 marks)

Model answer

Individual difference — Amara. Amara may have an internal locus of control: she believes outcomes depend on her own actions, so she feels less pressure from those around her, is less susceptible to normative social influence, and resists the group's opinions. (Her assertiveness could also reflect a high desire for control — Burger, 1987, found such participants conform less.)

Situational factor — Ben. Ben's conformity is shaped by the situation: the loudest members form a united majority, which raises conformity — in Asch's research a broken majority reduced conformity from 33% to 5.5%, so unity is powerful. The loud members may also be a high-status source, and sources with status, reputation, credibility or expertise influence others more.

Cultural factor — Chi's observation. The cousin's school may sit in a more collectivistic culture, which emphasises group cooperation and interdependence, so social norms are expected to be adhered to. Smith and Bond (1996) found in a meta-analysis of 133 Asch-type studies across 17 countries that collectivistic countries show higher conformity than individualistic ones — though the exact influence of socio-cultural factors cannot be determined, as many variables are at work.

17. Key Terms

locus of control 控制点
A belief system determining our perception of control over events: internal (my actions) versus external (luck, fate, others).
internal locus of control 内控
Feeling less pressure from others; less likely to comply with group norms; less susceptible to normative social influence.
external locus of control 外控
Looking to others to guide decisions and relying on others' opinions; more susceptible to normative social influence.
desire for control 控制欲
Burger & Cooper's (1979) individual difference in motivation to control events; high desire = assertive, decisive, resistant to influence.
meta-analysis 元分析
Gathering and analysing other people's data to look at effect sizes and draw conclusions about a topic area.
majority unity 多数派团结
Whether the dissenting majority all agree; a broken majority cut Asch's conformity from 33% to 5.5%.
ambiguity 情境模糊
Unclear situations make us conform for information — informational social influence, producing a more persistent change.
source credibility 信息来源可信度
Status, reputation, credibility or expertise of the influencer — raises influence for majorities and minorities alike.
cross-cultural replication 跨文化重复验证
Repeating Asch-type experiments in other countries, revealing different conformity rates across cultures.
subsistence culture 生计文化
A culture defined by how it obtains food — Berry (1967) compared crop-farming Temne with hunter-gatherer Inuit.
collectivistic culture 集体主义文化
Emphasises group cooperation and interdependence; norms are expected to be adhered to; shows higher conformity.
individualistic culture 个人主义文化
Nurtures independence and tolerates dissent from social norms; shows lower conformity.
