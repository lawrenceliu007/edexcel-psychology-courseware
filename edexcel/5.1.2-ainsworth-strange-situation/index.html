5.1.2 AINSWORTH'S WORK ON ATTACHMENT
Caregiver sensitivity · Strange Situation · Three types · Cross-cultural research · 陌生情境与依恋类型

Topic E · Developmental psychology Content Spec 5.1.2 📖 Textbook Book 2 · Ch.1 · pp.15–22

What this page is really asking

  1. Calm is not the same as secure. The nursery head praised the baby who never cried; Ainsworth's procedure shows that the "easy" baby may be the insecure-avoidant one, and the clingy crying one may be securely attached.
  2. Sensitivity is the hypothesis. Caregivers who read the baby's signals and respond appropriately tend to raise secure infants — though later meta-analyses shrink the effect (from .78 to .24) and temperament shares the credit.
  3. The Strange Situation is a graded stress test. Eight episodes, two separations, two reunions: safe-base behaviour, stranger response, separation behaviour and reunion behaviour are the four things scored.
  4. Secure attachment is universal, but the insecure split is cultural. German independence can masquerade as avoidance; Japanese infants rarely left alone can find the same episodes far more stressful.

Learning Objectives 学习目标

  • Describe Ainsworth's two observational studies (Uganda ethnographic, Baltimore) and the caregiving hypothesis.
  • Reproduce the Strange Situation procedure: eight episodes, the four measured behaviours, time sampling and rating scales.
  • Classify infants into Types A/B/C using safe-base, stranger, separation and reunion behaviour.
  • Evaluate the procedure on standardisation, inter-rater reliability, demand characteristics, ethnocentrism and social sensitivity.
  • Interpret cross-cultural findings (Grossman 1991, Takahashi 1990) as a test of universality and of the procedure's validity.

1. From Uganda to Baltimore: The Sensitivity Hypothesis

Mary Ainsworth worked with Bowlby for several years. Her major contribution was a standardised way to measure security of attachment and the identification of three attachment types: secure, insecure-avoidant（焦虑-回避型）and insecure-resistant（焦虑-矛盾型，也称反抗型）.

Her evidence began with ethnographic research（人种志研究：研究者深入当地社区，用长期自然观察和访谈理解一种文化的日常实践）in a village near Kampala, Uganda: a nine-month longitudinal study observing mother-child interactions in 26 families, visiting twice a month for two hours with a local social worker interpreting. She recorded qualitative field notes and open-question interviews, and noticed that securely attached babies explored more with their mothers present and cried less, while insecure babies were fussier and explored less. She identified three factors affecting security (Ainsworth, 1967): the amount of care mothers provided, the detail mothers shared in interviews, and their enjoyment of breastfeeding.

From these observations she developed the caregiving hypothesis（照顾假说：照顾者的敏感性与回应性决定婴儿依恋的安全性）: security depends on caregiver sensitivity（照顾者敏感性：能否读懂婴儿不同的哭声与表情信号，并恰当、及时地回应）. Caregivers who can tell a cry for food from a cry for comfort, and respond quickly and effectively, tend to form secure attachments; caregivers who struggle to read the cues tend to form insecure ones.

Evaluation. The Strange Situation results and naturalistic home observations supported a strong correlation (.78) between attachment type and maternal sensitivity. However, replications have not been so strong: a meta-analysis by de Wolff and van IJzendoorn (1997) found correlations of only 0.24 for mothers and 0.13 for fathers, although some included studies observed for very short periods. Fuertes et al. (2009) studied 48 Portuguese mother-infant pairs and found that infant temperament in the first months was also a significant predictor of attachment type at 12 months, so the caregiving hypothesis is not a complete explanation.

Back to the nursery: Duoduo's mother may show sensitivity in bursts but inconsistency across occasions, while Lele's mother's signals may be getting ignored at home. Keep both in mind for Section 4.

中文理解：Ainsworth 的证据链分两步：先在乌干达 26 个家庭里做了九个月的人种志观察（每月两次、每次两小时），发现安全型宝宝在妈妈在场时更敢探索、哭得更少；由此提出照顾假说，依恋安全与否取决于照顾者能否读懂信号并恰当回应。评价记三个数字：她自己的数据相关高达 .78；但 1997 年元分析掉到母亲 .24、父亲 .13；Fuertes 2009 发现婴儿气质也是预测因素。一句话：敏感性重要，但不是全部解释。

2. The Strange Situation: The Procedure

In 1963 Ainsworth began a second observational study in Baltimore, USA, with middle-class families recruited before their babies were born. She observed parent-child interactions for four hours each month from the first weeks of life, again finding that sensitive-responsive parenting went with happier, less crying children. At 12 months, families were invited into a structured observation（结构化观察：在人工布置的环境中观察并记录行为，刻意操纵情境以引发目标行为）: the Strange Situation.

  • Aim: to investigate individual differences in attachment by observing infant behaviour in an unfamiliar environment, with and without an unfamiliar adult present.
  • Method: structured observation (non-participant, controlled).
  • Sample: the initial pilot included 23 middle-class American mothers and their infants; other studies with slightly larger samples were ongoing as the procedure was developed.
  • Setting: a 9 × 9-foot laboratory playroom marked into 16 squares so the baby's location and movement could be monitored, watched through a one-way mirror by two observers describing behaviour into a voice recorder.

The 20-minute procedure has eight episodes. A timer clicked every 15 seconds so each comment matched its moment; observers scored one point for every 15-second period containing movement, toy play, looking at the mother or crying (12 points maximum per three-minute episode: time sampling). They also used 1–7 rating scales for intensity of proximity and contact-seeking, contact-maintaining, avoidance, resistance, and searching during separations. Four attachment behaviours were measured:

  • Safe-base behaviour（安全基地行为：妈妈在场时敢不敢探索房间与玩具）
  • Stranger response（陌生人反应：妈妈在场与不在场时对陌生人的反应）
  • Separation behaviour（分离行为：妈妈离开时的反应）
  • Reunion behaviour（重聚行为：妈妈回来时的反应）

中文理解：陌生情境是「故意制造轻微压力」的标准流程：8 幕、约 20 分钟，关键节点是两次分离（第 3 幕尾和第 5 幕尾）和两次重聚（第 5 幕和第 8 幕）。记录分两层。定量层：每 15 秒打一次点（时间取样，每幕满分 12）。量表层：1 到 7 的强度评分。判型看四件事：敢不敢探索（安全基地行为），怕不怕陌生人，分离时多难受，重聚时怎么表现。记住一个设计要点：压力是逐级加码的，不是一下子把宝宝扔进陌生人怀抱。

3. Three Types of Attachment

From her detailed observations, Ainsworth identified three types (Table 1.4 in the textbook). The distribution in her US studies was approximately 70 per cent secure, 20 per cent insecure-avoidant, 10 per cent insecure-resistant.

Ainsworth concluded that attachment security depends on the attachment figure's emotional availability, including sensitivity and responsiveness.

Back to the nursery: Duoduo's pattern (huge separation distress, contact-seeking on reunion mixed with angry pushing away) maps onto Type C with its inconsistent mothering. Lele's calm (no separation protest, no reunion response, stranger accepted) maps onto Type A. The head teacher's "best adapted" verdict now looks questionable.

4. Evaluating the Strange Situation

Strengths

  • Highly standardised, therefore scientific. Every episode lasts the same time and the mothers come and go the same way (leaving quietly the first time, saying "bye-bye" the second), so other researchers can repeat the study, for example with fathers (Main & Weston, 1981).
  • High inter-rater reliability. For 14 randomly selected infants (Ainsworth & Bell, 1970), observers trained on the rating scales agreed strongly, for example 0.93 for proximity- and contact-seeking behaviours, which raises the scientific status of the findings.

Weaknesses

  • Validity threat from demand characteristics. Although a one-way mirror was used, the mothers knew they were being observed and may have behaved unnaturally, for example being more attentive than usual.
  • Ethnocentric sample. All infants came from middle-class US families in Baltimore. In cultures where children are cared for by multiple people from an early age, or are rarely separated from caregivers, the distribution of types could look very different (see Takahashi, Section 5).
  • Socially sensitive. The procedure highlights the mother's role and implies that a mother's parenting style causes insecure attachment with negative consequences for the child, which can feed blame and guilt.

中文理解：评价记两绿三红。绿：高度标准化所以可重复（连妈妈离开的方式都有脚本，第一次安静走、第二次说 bye-bye）；评分者信度 .93（14 名婴儿、观察者受过详细培训）。红：妈妈知道被观察，可能出现需求特征，行为不自然；样本全是巴尔的摩中产家庭，民族中心主义；「妈妈养育方式决定依恋类型」这个暗示对社会敏感，容易变成对妈妈的指责。

5. Cross-Cultural Research: Does the Procedure Travel?

Cultural variations matter beyond curiosity: they test Bowlby's claim that attachment behaviour is innate and universal（普遍性：不论文化养育方式如何都成立）. If attachment is biology, babies worldwide should react similarly in the Strange Situation; if nurture shapes it, distributions should differ, because cultures differ in beliefs about how children should be raised. Two replications in cultures with very different values (Table 1.5 in the textbook):

Interpreting the differences. At face value the results suggest lower parental sensitivity in Germany and Japan, but read them with caution: the procedure may simply not be valid for cultures whose child-rearing practices differ sharply from the US. In Germany, children are taught self-reliance from an early age, and this independence may have been wrongly interpreted as avoidance: maternal sensitivity of the Type A German mothers did not differ significantly from that of the Type B American mothers, so what looked like insecure-avoidant attachment reflected cultural norms about interpersonal distance rather than insensitive parenting. In Japan, infants are traditionally rarely left alone, so the mother's exit in episode 3 provoked far more intense reactions; many Japanese babies became so upset they could not be comforted and their trials had to be ended. Extreme distress is coded as resistant behaviour, which explains the high Type C rate, and suggests the procedure is not a valid measure for infants raised in more traditional Japanese families.

Overall. Secure attachment dominates across many cultures, which supports Bowlby's evolutionary account (nature). But when infants do form insecure attachments, the type of insecurity is influenced by experience (nurture), supporting Ainsworth's caregiver sensitivity ideas.

Close the loop: back to the two babies at the nursery door

You now have a full, evidence-based answer to the head teacher's judgement. Duoduo's reunion mix of clinging and angry pushing is the signature of Type C, usually linked to inconsistent caregiving, not to a badly attached child. Lele's flat calm is not strong security: a securely attached baby uses the mother as a safe base and seeks her on reunion, while Lele ignored her, which matches Type A, linked at home to insensitive or rejecting mothering. The nursery verdict got it exactly backwards, and the cross-cultural evidence explains why calm behaviour misleads us: in some cultures independence looks like avoidance without being insecure, and extreme separation distress looks like resistance without being a broken bond. The behaviour you see in a strange room depends on what "normal" separation means at home.

Reflect before the Checkpoint: if the Strange Situation were run in your own city, which cultural norms might change how babies behave in episodes 3 and 6, and would that make the classifications wrong or right?

7. Exam Lab

Q1 · Explain one strength 2 marks

Explain one strength of Ainsworth's Strange Situation Procedure.

One strength is that the procedure is highly standardised: each episode lasts the same amount of time and the mothers come and go in the same way, for example leaving quietly the first time and saying "bye-bye" the second. Because every infant experiences the same sequence, other researchers can repeat the study to check the findings, for example testing children with their fathers (Main & Weston, 1981), which makes the procedure scientific and replicable.

Marking guidance: 1 mark for identifying the strength (standardisation / replicability / high inter-rater reliability), 1 mark for explaining how it improves the scientific status or comparability of the findings.

Q2 · Explain two ways 4 marks

Bodhana and Jovita are 8 months old. Bodhana is cared for by her childminder Olena when her mother is at work. Olena has worked with the family since Bodhana was born and knows exactly how to help when Bodhana is upset. Jovita's mother works from home. Although she feeds and changes Jovita regularly, she pays no attention to her daughter. Explain two ways that Bodhana and Jovita's parenting experiences might affect their development. You should refer to caregiver sensitivity in your answer.

Way 1: Bodhana's sensitive care should promote secure attachment. Olena knows exactly how to help when Bodhana is upset, which is caregiver sensitivity: perceiving the baby's signals and responding promptly and appropriately. According to Ainsworth's caregiving hypothesis, this sensitive, responsive care is associated with secure attachment (Type B), so Bodhana is likely to use Olena as a safe base, explore confidently and be easily comforted.

Way 2: Jovita's unresponsive care risks insecure attachment. Jovita's mother meets physical needs (feeding, changing) but pays no attention to her, so the baby's social and emotional cues go unread and unanswered. Low sensitivity is associated with insecure attachment, likely insecure-avoidant (Type A) given the consistent ignoring of cues, meaning Jovita may not use her mother as a safe base and may show little comfort-seeking, which could affect her later relationships through her internal working model.

Marking guidance: up to 2 marks per way: 1 for identifying how the caregiving differs (sensitive vs unresponsive), 1 for explaining the developmental consequence via caregiver sensitivity and attachment security.

Q3 · Assess 8 marks

Assess the Strange Situation Procedure in terms of validity and ethics.

Validity: strengths. The procedure is highly standardised, with fixed episode durations and scripted maternal departures, so it measures attachment behaviours consistently across infants and can be replicated (Main & Weston, 1981). Inter-rater reliability was very high (0.93 for proximity- and contact-seeking; Ainsworth & Bell, 1970), meaning the classifications are not one observer's opinion but agreed measurements, supporting the validity of the type assignments.

Validity: weaknesses. The mothers knew they were being observed, so demand characteristics are a threat: they may have behaved more attentively than usual, making the observed behaviour unnatural. More seriously, the procedure may lack cross-cultural validity: Takahashi et al. (1990) found 32 per cent resistant classifications in Japan, but Japanese infants are rarely left alone, so the separation episodes provoked extreme distress that had to be ended for some babies, and the distress was coded as resistance. Grossman et al. (1991) found 49 per cent avoidant in Germany, yet maternal sensitivity did not differ from American Type B mothers, so German self-reliance was misread as avoidance. Behaviour in the strange room depends on cultural norms about separation, so the same behaviour may not mean the same attachment type everywhere.

Ethics: strengths and safeguards. The stress was designed to be mild and brief: episodes are about three minutes, the stranger first interacts with the mother rather than the baby, and mothers return as soon as the sequence requires, so the distress is within everyday experience.

Ethics: weaknesses. The infants could not give consent, so consent came from mothers who did not know the procedure's aim of inducing separation stress. Takahashi's data show the procedure can push infants beyond mild distress: some became inconsolable and trials were terminated, which suggests the risk of psychological harm is real, especially for infants whose upbringing makes separation unusual. Confidentiality is easier to protect than wellbeing here, because the core ethical problem, deliberately stressing an infant who cannot withdraw meaningfully, is built into the design itself.

Marking guidance: AO1 (4): accurate description of validity strengths/weaknesses and ethical issues. AO3 (4): linked evaluation, e.g. demand characteristics vs standardisation, Takahashi and Grossman as evidence on cultural validity, consent and harm for infants. Both strands (validity and ethics) must be addressed for full marks.
